Executive summary · Electron–Phonon–Photon Primer

Electrons, phonons and photons: the short version

Giustino's 2017 review builds first-principles electron–phonon theory on one instantaneous Coulomb interaction, folded into screened parameters. The EPP formalism keeps the whole electromagnetic field and, as $c\to\infty$, returns Giustino's long-range (polar) formulas: the RPA $W$, plus the Gonze–Lee LO–TO term and Verdi–Giustino vertex that DFPT adds by hand, now derived. Bottom line: EPP changes the numbers only inside the light cone (polaritons, infrared response, radiative widths) or where dynamical screening matters (doped polar semiconductors); transport and zero-point renormalization stay electrostatic.

Seven things to take away

Tags such as (G.53) and (E.454) number equations in Giustino's review and the EPP manuscript.

  1. Giustino folds every Coulomb effect into three sets of numbers.Part A

    Eq. (1) of the review has no electron–electron or ion–ion interaction and no photons: the electron–electron Coulomb energy sits in the band energies $\varepsilon_{n\bk}$, the screened ion–ion forces in the phonon frequencies $\omega_{\bq\nu}$, and the electron–ion interaction in the coupling $g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)$. DFPT computes $g$ as the bare electron–ion perturbation screened by a static dielectric matrix, your GW or BSE $\epsilon=1-v\chi^0$ plus $f^{xc}$:

    $$\partial v^{\rm KS}=(\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc})^{-1}\,\partial v^{\rm en}\tag{G.53}$$

    Static screening is the adiabatic approximation; the frequency dependence it drops is what Giustino calls retardation.

    Read more: §A.2 Eq. (1) and g · §A.3 DFPT

  2. The lattice is a second channel of the screened interaction.Part A

    With clamped nuclei, Hedin's equations give $W_{\rm e}=v+vP_{\rm e}W_{\rm e}$, the $W$ of GW and BSE. Moving nuclei add a phonon-mediated term ($\mathcal D$ the nuclear density correlator) whose vertices are dynamically screened nuclear-potential gradients, $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$:

    $$W=W_{\rm e}+\underbrace{W_{\rm e}\,\mathcal D\,W_{\rm e}}_{W_{\rm ph}}\tag{G.104–105}$$

    In a polar insulator at long wavelength $W_{\rm ph}$ is Fröhlich's coupling squared times the LO propagator, with a screened vertex at each end. The phonon self-energy (G.141) is different: one bare and one screened vertex around the irreducible bubble $P_{\rm e}$, because screening both ends would double count; EPP has the same structure (item 6).

    Read more: §A.5 Hedin–Baym · §A.6 phonon self-energy

  3. Coulomb is the longitudinal photon, and the light cone is tiny.Bridge

    In the temporal gauge the longitudinal field has no dynamics of its own; by Gauss's law its energy is the instantaneous Coulomb energy of electrons and ionic bound charges. Charge conservation turns every density vertex into a current vertex and $v$ into $D^{0l}$, so the free propagator is the vacuum Maxwell propagator (Gaussian units, $P^L=\hat\bq\hat\bq$, $P^T=1-P^L$):

    $$D^0=\underbrace{-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,P^L}_{D^{0l}}+\underbrace{\frac{4\pi}{c}\,\frac{P^T}{q^2-\omega^2/c^2}}_{D^{0t}}$$

    The Coulomb part has no light cone. The transverse part matters only for $q\lesssim q_c=\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega/c$: $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ Å$^{-1}$ for phonons, about $10^{-3}$ Å$^{-1}$ for a 2 eV exciton in vacuum, against a Brillouin zone of about 1 Å$^{-1}$.

    Read more: §X.1 the light cone · §B.4 quantized fields

  4. One Dyson equation for light and matter, with $W$ as its longitudinal corner.Part B

    All matter enters through one current–current self-energy, $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm e}+\Pi^{\rm ion}$, and the field obeys a single $3\times3$ Dyson equation:

    $$D=D^0+D^0\,\Pi_{jj}\,D\tag{E.379}$$

    Giustino's Dyson equations for $W_{\rm e}$ and $W$ are its longitudinal sector: at $c\to\infty$ the electron-dressed photon reduces to $D^{\rm e}\to-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\bq\bq\,W^{\rm e}$ (E.441), with $W^{\rm e}$ the RPA $W$ without local fields. Your $\chi^0$, times $e^2$ (EPP's Gaussian $v=4\pi/q^2$ carries no charge), is the longitudinal projection of the electronic $\Pi_{jj}$.

    Read more: §B.5 photon self-energy · §B.9 electrostatic limit

  5. Phonons dressed by photons give the LO–TO splitting and phonon polaritons.Part B

    From the lattice side, the same series is Giustino's phonon Dyson equation (G.135) with photons; its self-energy (E.399) exchanges one electron-screened photon between two Born-charge current vertices. With $c\to\infty$ taken first and the electrons screening the field, only the longitudinal sector survives: the shift (E.454) is the electrostatic LO–TO splitting, valid for $q\gg q_c$, the Gonze–Lee term of DFPT, now derived. For a cubic diatomic crystal ($Z^*$ in units of $|e|$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}$ the cell volume, $\mu$ the reduced mass) it reads

    $$\omega_{\rm LO}^2=\omega_{\rm TO}^2+\frac{\omega_p^2}{\epsilon_\infty},\qquad \omega_p^2=\frac{4\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\mu}$$

    (E.410) is the zone-centre polariton point of an electron-free crystal, where all polar modes share one unscreened frequency; it differs from (E.454) by electronic screening and by the order of limits. In GaAs screening alone takes it from 55.6 to 35.9 meV, and the order of limits moves only the TO modes: 35.9 meV inside the light cone, on the upper polariton with the LO, and 33.3 meV outside (figure below).

    Read more: §B.7 phonon self-energy · §B.9 electrostatic limit

  6. The Fröhlich vertex is derived, and it becomes dynamical.Part B

    Opening the electron loop inside $\Omega^{\rm e}$ yields an electron–phonon vertex nobody put in by hand, a photon exchanged between ionic and electronic currents:

    $$g^{\rm dyn}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=i\,J^{*}D^{\rm e}J^{\rm e}\tag{E.416}$$

    The LO channel is pure Coulomb at every $q$; TO phonons couple to electrons only near the light cone. At $c\to\infty$ the vertex reduces to the $\bG=0$ Verdi–Giustino long-range vertex with $\epsilon^\infty$ replaced by the dynamical $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$ (complex conjugated, for real $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}$): nonadiabatic screening, which matters with free carriers. The electron-induced part of the phonon self-energy, $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$, pairs it with a bare vertex around the irreducible electronic bubble (EPP's electron–hole propagator, not the Lindhard $\chi^0$ of item 4): Giustino's bare-times-screened structure (item 2).

    Read more: §B.8 Fröhlich vertex · §A.4 polar materials

  7. In 2D there is no LO–TO splitting at Γ for in-plane modes.Part B

    The in-plane polar modes of a sheet feel the strictly two-dimensional screened interaction $W^{\rm e,2D}=v_Q/\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}$ with $v_Q=2\pi/Q$, which cancels only one of the dipole vertex's two powers of $Q$, so the LO shift grows linearly from zero. For a gapped layer $\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}=\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q$, the Rytova–Keldysh form of 2D BSE ($r_0=2\pi\alpha_{\rm 2D}$; papers that write $\epsilon_{\rm env}(1+r_0'Q)$ use $r_0'=r_0/\epsilon_{\rm env}$). The LO branch of a diatomic sheet follows, with $C=2\pi e^2Z^{*2}\hbar^2/(S_{\rm uc}\,\mu)$ ($S_{\rm uc}$ the cell area; $Z^*$ and $\mu$ as in item 5),

    $$(\hbar\tilde\omega_{\rm LO})^2=(\hbar\omega_{\rm T})^2+\frac{C\,Q}{\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q}$$

    The environment sets the initial slope, $r_0$ alone sets the plateau $(\hbar\omega_{\rm T})^2+C/r_0$, and the knee sits at $Q^*=\epsilon_{\rm env}/r_0$. So your BSE workflow's $r_0$, plus $Z^*$ and the mode eigenvector, gives the LO dispersion.

Where the electromagnetic field changes the answer

Two kinds of retardation sort the observables: a finite speed of light, which acts only inside the light cone, and frequency-dependent electronic screening, which acts wherever $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$ has structure near phonon frequencies (EPP keeps it in the vertex). Outside both windows the electrostatic theory suffices.

What is still open

  • Short-range coupling. The crystal potential is frozen at equilibrium, so deformation-potential coupling (acoustic and non-polar optical phonons) is absent.
  • Electron self-energies. No Fan–Migdal or Debye–Waller counterpart yet, hence no temperature-dependent gaps or lifetimes.
  • Excitons. $\Pi^{\rm e}$ is the RPA bubble, without local fields, $f^{xc}$ or an electron–hole ladder. The BSE response in its place, minus its $(\bG=0)$ exchange (already the longitudinal photon), would give exciton polaritons.
  • Anisotropic polaritons. L–T mixing off the principal axes is treated only formally, so the hyperbolic phonon polaritons of hBN remain to be done.
