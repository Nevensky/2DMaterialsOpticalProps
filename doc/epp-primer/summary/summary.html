Executive summary · Electron–phonon–photon primer, second edition

Electrons, phonons and photons: the short version

Giustino's 2017 review builds first-principles electron–phonon theory on one instantaneous Coulomb interaction, folded into screened parameters. The EPP formalism keeps the whole electromagnetic field and, as $c\to\infty$, returns Giustino's long-range (polar) formulas: the RPA $W$, plus the Gonze–Lee LO–TO term and Verdi–Giustino vertex that DFPT adds by hand, now derived. Bottom line: EPP changes the numbers only inside the light cone (polaritons, infrared response, radiative widths) or where dynamical screening matters (doped polar semiconductors); transport and zero-point renormalization stay electrostatic.

The idea in one picture

The same electron–phonon vertex in both theories. Toggle the speed of light: the photon line $D^{\rme}$, dressed by the electrons, collapses to the screened Coulomb line $W^{\rme}$.

Seven things to take away

Tags such as (G.53) and (E.454) number equations in Giustino's review and the EPP manuscript. Symbols and Gaussian units follow the primer's conventions.

  1. Giustino folds every Coulomb effect into three sets of numbers.Part A

    Eq. (1) of the review has no electron–electron or ion–ion interaction and no photons: the electron–electron Coulomb energy sits in the band energies $E_{n\bk}$, the screened ion–ion forces in the phonon frequencies $\omega_{\bq\nu}$, and the electron–ion interaction in the coupling $g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)$. DFPT computes $g$ as the bare electron–ion perturbation screened by a static test-electron dielectric matrix, $\varepsilon^{\rme}=1-(v+f^{\mathrm{xc}})\chi^0$, which is your GW or BSE $\varepsilon^{\rme}_{\mathrm{RPA}}=1-v\chi^0$ plus $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$:

    $$\Delta V^{\mathrm{KS}}=(\varepsilon^{\rme})^{-1}\,\Delta V^{\mathrm{en}}\tag{G.53}$$

    Static screening is the adiabatic approximation; the frequency dependence it drops, dynamical screening, is what Giustino calls retardation.

    Read more: §A.2 Eq. (1) and $g$ · §A.3 DFPT

  2. The lattice is a second channel of the screened interaction.Part A

    With clamped nuclei, Hedin's equations give $W^{\rme}=v+v\chi^{\mathrm{irr,e}}W^{\rme}$, the $W$ of GW and BSE. Moving nuclei add a phonon-mediated term ($\chi^{\ion}$ the nuclear density correlator) whose vertices are dynamically screened nuclear-potential gradients, $\mathcal V_{\bq\nu}=(\varepsilon^{\rme})^{-1}\Delta_{\bq\nu}V^{\mathrm{en}}$ (exact $\varepsilon^{\rme}$):

    $$W=W^{\rme}+\underbrace{W^{\rme}\chi^{\ion}W^{\rme}}_{W^{\ph}}\tag{G.104–105}$$

    In a polar insulator at long wavelength $W^{\ph}$ is Fröhlich's coupling squared times the LO propagator, with a screened vertex at each end. The phonon self-energy (G.141) is different: one bare and one screened vertex around the irreducible bubble $\chi^{\mathrm{irr,e}}$, because screening both ends would double count; EPP has the same structure (item 6).

    Read more: §A.5 Hedin–Baym · §A.6 phonon self-energy

  3. Coulomb is the longitudinal photon, and the light cone is tiny.Bridge

    In the temporal gauge the longitudinal field has no dynamics of its own; by Gauss's law its energy is the instantaneous Coulomb energy of electrons and ionic bound charges. Charge conservation turns every charge vertex into a current vertex and $v/e^2$ into $D^{0\mathrm L}$, so the free propagator is the vacuum Maxwell propagator ($\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$ and $\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$ are the longitudinal and transverse projectors):

    $$\begin{gathered}D^0_{\alpha\beta}=D^{0\mathrm L}\,\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta+D^{0\mathrm T}\big(\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta\big),\\ D^{0\mathrm L}=-\frac{4\pi c}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2},\\ D^{0\mathrm T}=-\frac{4\pi c}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-c^2q^2}\end{gathered}$$

    The Coulomb part has no light cone. The transverse part matters only for $q\lesssim q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega)=\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}\,\omega/c$: $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹ for phonons, about $10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹ for a 2 eV exciton in vacuum, against a Brillouin zone of about 1 Å⁻¹.

    Read more: §X.1 the light cone · §B.4 quantized fields

  4. One Dyson equation for light and matter, with $W$ as its longitudinal corner.Part B

    All matter enters through one current–current self-energy, $\Pi=\Pi^{\rme}+\Pi^{\ion}$, and the field obeys a single $3\times3$ Dyson equation:

    $$D=D^0+D^0\,\Pi\,D\tag{E.379}$$

    Giustino's Dyson equations for $W^{\rme}$ and $W$ are its longitudinal sector: for large $c$, $D^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}\simeq-\frac{c}{e^2\omega^2}\,q_\alpha q_\beta\,W^{\rme}$ (E.441), with $W^{\rme}$ the RPA $W$ without local fields. Your $\chi^0$ in charge units, $e^2\chi^0=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rme}\cdot\bq$, is the longitudinal projection of the electronic $\Pi^{\rme}$.

    Read more: §B.5 photon self-energy · §B.6 total propagator · §B.9 electrostatic limit

  5. Phonons dressed by photons give the LO–TO splitting and phonon polaritons.Part B

    From the lattice side, the same series is Giustino's phonon Dyson equation (G.135) with photons; its self-energy $\Pi^{\ph}$ (E.399) exchanges one electron-dressed photon between two Born-charge current vertices. With $c\to\infty$ taken first and the electrons screening the field, only the longitudinal sector survives: the shift (E.454) is the electrostatic LO–TO splitting, valid for $q\gg q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega_{\mathrm{TO}})$, the Gonze–Lee term of DFPT, now derived. For a cubic two-atom crystal ($Z^*$ the dimensionless Born charge, $\OV$ the cell volume, $M_{\mathrm r}$ the reduced mass) it reads

    $$\begin{gathered}\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2=\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2+\frac{(\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p})^2}{\varepsilon_\infty},\\ (\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p})^2=\frac{4\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{\OV M_{\mathrm r}}\end{gathered}$$

    (E.410) is the zone-centre polariton point of an electron-free crystal, where all polar modes share one unscreened frequency; it differs from (E.454) by electronic screening and by the order of limits. In GaAs screening alone takes it from 55.6 to 35.9 meV (measured LO: 36.2 meV), and the order of limits moves only the TO modes: 35.9 meV inside the light cone, on the upper polariton with the LO, and 33.3 meV outside (figure below).

  6. The Fröhlich vertex is derived, and it becomes dynamical.Part B

    Opening the electron bubble inside $\Pi^{\ph}$ yields an electron–phonon vertex nobody put in by hand, a photon exchanged between the ionic and electronic current vertices $\bJ_\nu$ and $\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$:

    $$g^{\mathrm{dyn}}_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=\ii\,\bJ_\nu^*\cdot D^{\rme}\cdot\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\tag{E.416}$$

    The LO channel is pure Coulomb at every $q$; TO phonons couple to electrons only near the light cone. At $c\to\infty$ the vertex reduces to the $\bG=0$ term of the Verdi–Giustino vertex $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$, with $\varepsilon_\infty$ replaced by the dynamical RPA $\varepsilon^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)$ (complex conjugated, for real $\varepsilon^{\rme}$): nonadiabatic screening, which matters with free carriers. The electron-induced part of the phonon self-energy, $\Delta\Pi^{\ph}$, pairs it with the bare vertex $g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}$ around one electron–hole bubble (its pair factor, EPP's electron–hole propagator, is not a term of the $\chi^0$ of item 4): Giustino's bare-times-screened structure (item 2).

    Read more: §B.8 Fröhlich vertex · §A.4 polar crystals

  7. In 2D there is no LO–TO splitting at Γ for in-plane modes.Part B

    The in-plane polar modes of a sheet feel the strictly two-dimensional screened interaction $W^{\mathrm{e,2D}}=v^{\mathrm{2D}}/\varepsilon^{\mathrm{2D}}$ with $v^{\mathrm{2D}}=2\pi e^2/Q_\parallel$, which cancels only one of the dipole vertex's two powers of the in-plane wavevector $Q_\parallel$, so the LO shift grows linearly from zero. For a gapped layer $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{2D}}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+r_0Q_\parallel$, the Rytova–Keldysh form of 2D BSE ($r_0=2\pi\chi^{\mathrm{2D}}$, with $\chi^{\mathrm{2D}}$ the sheet polarizability; papers that write $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}(1+r_0'Q_\parallel)$ use $r_0'=r_0/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$). The LO branch of a two-atom sheet follows ($\OS$ the cell area; $Z^*$ and $M_{\mathrm r}$ as in item 5):

    $$\begin{gathered}\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2(Q_\parallel)=\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2\\ +\frac{2\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{\OS M_{\mathrm r}}\,\frac{Q_\parallel}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+r_0Q_\parallel}\end{gathered}$$

    The environment sets the initial slope, $r_0$ alone sets the plateau, $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2=\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2+2\pi e^2Z^{*2}/(\OS M_{\mathrm r}r_0)$, and the knee sits at $Q_\parallel=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}/r_0$. So your BSE workflow's $r_0$, plus $Z^*$ and the mode eigenvector, gives the LO dispersion.

Where the electromagnetic field changes the answer

Two delays sort the observables; the review calls the first retardation, the EPP manuscript the second. Dynamical screening, the frequency dependence of $\varepsilon^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)$, acts wherever $\varepsilon^{\rme}$ has structure near phonon frequencies (EPP keeps it in the vertex). A finite speed of light acts only inside the light cone. Outside both windows the electrostatic theory suffices.

What is still open

  • Optics beyond RPA. $\Pi^{\rme}$ is an RPA bubble without local fields, $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$ or an electron–hole ladder: no excitons, no phonon-assisted absorption.
  • 2D beyond in-plane modes. One planar, freestanding, electrostatic sheet (§B.10 adds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$ and finite $c$): no out-of-plane dipoles, atomic heights or multilayers.
  • Anisotropy and hBN. Tensor equations, isotropic evaluation: no L–T mixing or hyperbolic phonon polaritons at finite $c$.
  • Electron self-energies. No Fan–Migdal or Debye–Waller counterpart: no band renormalization, lifetimes or polarons.
  • Short-range coupling. A frozen crystal potential and Born dipoles only: no deformation potentials, no quadrupoles.
  • Ordered phases. A normal-state theory, without anomalous propagators: no pairing.
  • Plasmon–phonon hybrids. (E.453) contains them; the manuscript screens statically (E.454). Models: §B.9, §B.10; first principles: Lihm and Park, Phys. Rev. Lett. 133, 116402 (2024).
