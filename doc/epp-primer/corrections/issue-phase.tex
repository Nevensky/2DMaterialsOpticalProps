\subsection*{The missing basis phase: Born dipoles sit at $\mathbf R_i^0+\mathbf r_a^0$, not at the cell origin}

\paragraph{Problem.}
In Sec.~3 (pp.~24--25) Eqs.~(121)--(123) place the dipole $\mathbf p_{ia}$ of atom $a$ at the cell origin $\mathbf R^0_i$. The atom itself sits at $\mathbf R^0_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r^0_a$, Eq.~(94). Section~2 keeps the corresponding factor, $V_a(\mathbf q')=e^{-i\mathbf q'\mathbf r^0_a}v_a(\mathbf q')$ in Eq.~(101), but $V^{\mathcal L}_a$ in Eq.~(124) does not. As a result Eq.~(131) lacks the factor $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}$ inside the atom sum, which Eq.~(180) of F.~Giustino, Rev.\ Mod.\ Phys.\ \textbf{89}, 015003 (2017) carries. Giustino's footnote~7 notes that Eq.~(4) of C.~Verdi and F.~Giustino, Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{115}, 176401 (2015) lacked it too. The same placement recurs in Eqs.~(134), (141)--(143), (167), (183), (186) and the Hamiltonians between (184) and (186), (237), (241), (270)--(272), (274), (286)--(311) and the displays between them, (314)--(315), and (352)--(354) with the displays on pp.~76--77. Through the ionic vertex $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ it also reaches every equation of Secs.~6.1--8. There the vertex is written out explicitly in the two displays after (417), in (418), (430), (447) and (448), and in the displays on pp.~95, 96 and 98.

\paragraph{Phase convention.}
Equations~(7), (83) and (242) attach the Bloch phase to the cell, not to the atom: $Q_{ia\nu}\propto e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_i}$. The dipole $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}$ located at $\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r^0_a$ therefore has amplitude $\propto Z^*_{a\mu\nu}e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}$ at wave vector $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$, so the basis phase must appear explicitly in every vertex. The sign of this phase follows from (83) and the Fourier convention (117) alone. Only the product $e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}$ is independent of how the atoms are grouped into cells. Moving atom $a$ to a neighbouring cell gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r^0_a\to\mathbf r^0_a+\mathbf R_L,\qquad
e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\to e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R_L}e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q),\qquad
e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}\ \text{unchanged}.
\tag{131a}
\end{equation}
With atom phases instead, $Q_{ia\nu}\propto\tilde e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_{ia}}$, the factor reduces to $e^{-i\mathbf G\mathbf r^0_a}$. Eq.~(131) as printed is therefore correct only in that convention and only at $\mathbf G=0$.

The transformation law in (131a) and the cancellation in (131b) below assume that $e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ are the eigenvectors that solve the equations of motion for the pattern $e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_i}$, as in Giustino's Eqs.~(14) and (20), i.e.\ those of $(m_am_b)^{-1/2}\sum_{\boldsymbol\delta}e^{-i\mathbf q\boldsymbol\delta}k_{a\mu b\nu}(\boldsymbol\delta)$. Eqs.~(15) and (35) as printed have $e^{+i\mathbf q\boldsymbol\delta}$, and the step after Eq.~(43) uses $\tilde\Omega^2_{b\nu a\mu}=\tilde\Omega^2_{a\mu b\nu}$, which holds only for real $\tilde\Omega^2$. The eigenvectors of the printed matrix are the complex conjugates. For a long acoustic wave they carry $e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a}$, and the basis phase would then double the spurious acoustic term of (131b) instead of removing it. The sign in (15) and (35) must therefore be corrected together with the present issue.

\paragraph{Corrected equations, Sec.~3 (SI, as in the manuscript).}
The left-hand label of (121) is $\nu$, the direction of the displacement.
\begin{align}
\phi_{ia\nu}(\mathbf r)&=-i\sum_\mu\frac{Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\,e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf r^0_a)}\frac{q_\mu}{\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q},\tag{121$'$}\\
\phi_i(\mathbf r)&=-i\sum_{a\mu\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\,e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf r^0_a)}\frac{q_\mu}{\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q},\tag{122$'$}\\
\Delta V^{\mathcal L}_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)&=i\sum_{ia\mu\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{e\,q_\mu}{\epsilon_0\,\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q}\,e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf r^0_a)},\tag{123$'$}\\
V^{\mathcal L}_a(\mathbf q)&=e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a}\,\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0\,\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q}.\tag{124$'$}
\end{align}
Equations~(125)--(130) keep their form. With (124$'$) the identity between (128) and (102) now includes the basis phase of (101), as it should. Inserting (124$'$) into (130) gives
\begin{equation}
g^{\mathcal L}_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\frac{4\pi i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{e}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{\mathbf G}\sum_{a\mu\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}\;
\frac{(q_\mu+G_\mu)Z^*_{a\mu\nu}e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}}{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\hat\epsilon^\infty(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)}\;\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G),
\tag{131$'$}
\end{equation}
with $e/4\pi\epsilon_0\to e$ in Gaussian units. For an acoustic branch, with $M=\sum_am_a$,
\begin{equation*}
e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)=e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a}\Big[\sqrt{m_a/M}\,u_\nu+i\,w_{a\nu}(\mathbf q)+O(q^2)\Big],
\end{equation*}
where $\mathbf u$ is the common displacement and $\mathbf w_a(\mathbf q)=O(q)$ the internal-strain relaxation, which does not depend on the choice of cell. Then
\begin{equation}
\sum_a\frac{\hat Z^*_a\mathbf e_{a\alpha}(\mathbf q)}{\sqrt{m_a}}-\sum_a\frac{\hat Z^*_a\mathbf e_{a\alpha}(\mathbf q)}{\sqrt{m_a}}\,e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a}=\frac{i}{\sqrt M}\sum_a(\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a)\,\hat Z^*_a\mathbf u+O(q^2),
\qquad
\sum_a\frac{\hat Z^*_a\mathbf e_{a\alpha}(\mathbf q)}{\sqrt{m_a}}\,e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a}=\frac{\big(\sum_a\hat Z^*_a\big)\mathbf u}{\sqrt M}+i\sum_a\frac{\hat Z^*_a\mathbf w_a(\mathbf q)}{\sqrt{m_a}}+O(q^2)=i\sum_a\frac{\hat Z^*_a\mathbf w_a(\mathbf q)}{\sqrt{m_a}}+O(q^2).
\tag{131b}
\end{equation}
The printed (phase-less) vertex therefore equals the corrected one plus the spurious term $(i/\sqrt M)\sum_a(\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a)\hat Z^*_a\mathbf u$. Because $\omega_\alpha\propto q$, this term gives $|g^{\mathcal L}|^2\propto1/q$ for acoustic phonons, the scaling of a piezoelectric coupling. It depends on the choice of cell, and it survives in centrosymmetric crystals, where the physical term $\sum_a\hat Z^*_a\mathbf w_a$ vanishes. For GaAs the spurious term alone corresponds to a piezoelectric constant $|\sum_aZ^*_a(\hat{\mathbf q}\mathbf r^0_a)|/\Omega_{\rm uc}$ of 0.6--1.8~C/m$^2$ along [111], depending on the cell, and 1.04~C/m$^2$ along [100]. This is comparable to or larger than the internal-strain coupling of the corrected vertex, about 1.2~C/m$^2$ along [111] in a central-force model of GaAs (nearest- and next-nearest-neighbour springs fitted to $\omega_{\rm TO}$ and to the [100] sound velocity) and zero along [100]. The printed total can be larger or smaller than the corrected one, depending on the cell (1.8 or 0.6~C/m$^2$ along [111] in that model). It is also nonzero along [100], where the true coupling vanishes by symmetry. With the phase (right equation of (131b)), the leading term vanishes by the acoustic sum rule $\sum_a\hat Z^*_a=0$, and the remainder is the internal-strain dipole. The measured coupling, $(2/\sqrt3)|e_{14}|\approx0.19$~C/m$^2$ for LA[111], is much smaller still: the clamped-ion (electronic) piezoelectric response largely cancels the internal-strain term [S.~de~Gironcoli, S.~Baroni and R.~Resta, Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{62}, 2853 (1989)]. In the long-range vertex that response enters through the dynamical quadrupoles, so a consistent $O(q)$ theory needs them [Brunin \textit{et al.}, Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{125}, 136601 (2020); Jhalani \textit{et al.}, \textit{ibid.}\ 136602 (2020); Park \textit{et al.}, Phys.\ Rev.\ B \textbf{102}, 125203 (2020)].

\paragraph{Justification.}
The manuscript's normal-mode expansion (7), (83) gives every atom of cell $i$ the Bloch phase $e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_i}$ of its cell. The Fourier component at $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ of the dipole density of mode $(\mathbf q,\alpha)$ is then $\Omega_{\rm uc}^{-1}\sum_a\hat Z^*_a\mathbf e_{a\alpha}(\mathbf q)e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}/\sqrt{m_a}$ times the phonon amplitude. Section~2 already obtains exactly this structure for the pseudopotential through Eq.~(101). Dropping the factor has three consequences:
\begin{itemize}
\item the vertex depends on the arbitrary assignment of atoms to cells, Eq.~(131a);
\item it predicts a spurious, cell-dependent long-range coupling to acoustic phonons, $|g|^2\propto1/q$, even in centrosymmetric crystals;
\item in the ionic self-energy and in the dipole--dipole dynamical matrix (454a), the $\mathbf G$ sums of the printed form describe coincident dipoles. The on-site terms $a=b$ need regularization (removal of the Ewald self term) in both forms; with coincident dipoles the $a\neq b$ terms diverge as well. The vertex $g^{\mathcal L}$ itself stays finite, because coincident dipoles simply add into one dipole per cell.
\end{itemize}
With the factor, (131$'$) coincides with Giustino's Eq.~(180), and the ionic self-energy (297$'$)--(304$'$) reproduces the Ewald-summed dipole lattice of Gonze and Lee, Eq.~(454a). This was checked numerically: the $\mathbf G$ sum is independent of the Ewald parameter only with the sign $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r^0_a}$. All $\mathbf q\to0$, $\mathbf G=0$ results of the manuscript for optical modes are untouched.
