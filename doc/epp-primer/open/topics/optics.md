# Optics beyond RPA: excitons, local fields, xc and phonon-assisted absorption

Labels: **[est]** established literature; **[derived]** worked out here from EPP's equations, not printed in EPP; **[proposal]** suggested extension; **[spec]** speculation. Notation and units are those of primer v2 ([§C.0](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#c-conventions)): Gaussian units with $e>0$, $\hbar$ and $c$ explicit, so the Coulomb interaction $v=4\pi e^2/q^2$ and the screened $W$ carry $e^2$. "G" is Giustino's review, "E" the EPP draft; (A$n$.$m$) and (B$n$.$m$) are equations of primer v2.

## 1. What is missing in EPP

- **Independent-particle bubble.** E.278–E.279 (pp. 57–58) define the photon self-energy as the lowest-order bubble plus contact (diamagnetic) term per species (primer [§B.5](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-photon-se)). E.282 and E.383 (pp. 59, 85) evaluate it with single-particle energies $E_{n\mathbf{k}}$ and current vertices (E.284, E.384). No electron–hole ladder means no excitons; no self-energy insertions means no $GW$ gap and no phonon renormalization.
- **No local fields, no xc.** From E.380 on (p. 85) EPP drops the $\mathbf{G}$ indices, so only $\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}'=0$ is kept for electrons. For ions, printed E.293–E.295 has no basis phase at all (after E.293 the ionic self-energy is called "a constant in $GG'$ space"); the primer's ionic current vertex $j_{\alpha\nu}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})$, (B5.22), carries the structure factors $\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa}$, and a footnote of [§B.5](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-photon-se) states the correction. Hence $W^{\mathrm{e}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=4\pi e^2/(\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\mathbf{q})$ (E.445; (B9.7)), with $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{e}}$ the head of the RPA dielectric matrix without local fields (EPP's E.432 tensor, $\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{e}}$ in [§B.8](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-frohlich), has the same longitudinal element). The $\varepsilon_\infty$ implicit in $W^{\mathrm{e}}(\mathbf{q},0)$ of E.454, and the $r_0$ implicit in $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{2D}}(Q_\parallel,0)$ of E.472, are RPA numbers without local fields. No $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$ appears anywhere. DFPT screening, $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}}$ (G.54), is static but includes both ([§A.3](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a-dfpt), [§B.6](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-propagator)).
- **No non-RPA electronic insertions.** Mixed $\langle\mathbf{j}^{\mathrm{e}}\mathbf{j}^{\mathrm{ion}}\rangle$ bubbles vanish at lowest order (E.316). Phonon-assisted absorption (G.200) and exciton–phonon coupling are instead the first non-RPA electronic insertions: self-energy and vertex diagrams of $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ containing an internal $D^{\mathrm{ph}}$ line (the phonon part $D-D^{\mathrm{e}}$ of the photon propagator; v2 names only its Coulomb sector, $W^{\mathrm{ph}}$). EPP has no direct electron–ion vertex, so the phonon reaches the electrons only through this photon line. Because these diagrams are photon-irreducible, they live inside $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ and are not double counted by the Dyson resummation of E.379. They are not derived ([§A.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a-applications); [§X.2](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#x2-missing) lists phonon-assisted optics as not worked out).
- **No short-range electron–phonon coupling.** The Lagrangian freezes the crystal potential ([§B.2](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-lagrangian)), so $D^{\mathrm{ph}}$ carries only the dipolar (Fröhlich) part of $g$. Any phonon-assisted process built from EPP alone is its polar part only ([shortrange.md](shortrange.md)).
- **$T=0$ boson lines.** The $n_{\mathrm{B}}=0$ rule (E.249, §B.5) is safe for E.379. It fails once a phonon line sits inside $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$, which needs the weights $n_{\mathbf{q}\nu}+\frac12\pm\frac12$ of G.200.
- **2D.** The sheet bubble is $l\,\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}'=0}$ (p. 60). EPP writes a $z$-resolved $D^{\mathrm{e}}(\mathbf{Q}_\parallel,\omega;z,z')$ "including electron $z$-LFE" (p. 100; (B10.18)), but keeps only the independent-particle sheet bubble $e^2\chi^0(\mathbf{Q}_\parallel,\omega)\,\delta(z)\,\delta(z')$ (before E.461). It therefore has Rytova–Keldysh screening (E.469–E.470, without $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$) but no electron–hole ladder, so neither intralayer nor interlayer excitons.

## 2. Why it matters

- **Excitons dominate 2D optics [est].** Monolayer WS₂ has an exciton binding energy of 0.32 eV and a non-hydrogenic Rydberg series [5]. An RPA $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ misses the peaks that carry the oscillator strength.
- **Linewidths are meV [est].** hBN-encapsulated MoS₂ shows exciton linewidths of about 2 meV FWHM at 4 K, approaching the homogeneous limit [6]. What remains is radiative (transverse photon) plus exciton–phonon broadening. EPP covers the radiative channel and the polar (Fröhlich) part of the phonon channel. The acoustic and non-polar optical contributions need the short-range coupling that EPP lacks ([shortrange.md](shortrange.md)). In a MoS₂-like sheet the Fröhlich channel is weak ($r_0^{\mathrm{ion}}\approx0.5$ Å with the primer preset, §4.3) [derived].
- **Screening inputs propagate [est].** Giustino (§XII; primer §A.9) estimates that a 20% error in $\varepsilon_\infty$ means about 40% in band renormalization and 10% in phonon frequencies. EPP's $\varepsilon_\infty$ and $r_0$ currently lack local fields and xc.
- **Temperature [est].** Zero-point motion shifts and broadens excitons in Si and hBN [7]. The $GW$ zero-point renormalization of diamond's direct gap is −628 meV (Antonius *et al.* [16], via G Fig. 13), which moves every absorption edge.
- **Phonon screening of $W$ [est].** Adding phonon screening to the BSE kernel lowers exciton binding energies in halide perovskites [12]. In EPP this screening is already inside $D$ (§4.3).
- **Light cone [derived, §X.1].** A 2 eV exciton in vacuum has $q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega)\approx10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹, while an exciton of radius ~1 nm (illustrative) samples $Q_\parallel\sim0.1$ Å⁻¹. The kernel stays electrostatic; radiative decay and polaritons live in the transverse sector of $D$.
- **For your code.** Your BSE already builds the photon-irreducible response. EPP would combine it with phonons and light: IR and UV $\varepsilon(\omega)$ from one object, polaritons and radiative widths from $D^{\mathrm{T}}$, and BSE-quality $\varepsilon_\infty$, $r_0$ for E.454 and E.472.

## 3. What standard theory does

- **Giustino.** Hedin's polarization $\chi^{\mathrm{irr}}=GG\Lambda$ (G.89–G.92) contains excitons through the vertex $\Lambda$, but the review uses the RPA $\chi^{\mathrm{irr}}$ of the electrons; excitons appear only as BSE plus Allen–Heine (Marini [7]; G p. 42). Absorption is Hall–Bardeen–Blatt (G.200): classical field, independent particles, $n_{\mathrm{r}}$ put in by hand; first principles in [8]. Temperature enters through Williams–Lax (G.201), stochastically (Zacharias, Patrick, Giustino 2015, G p. 45) or with one deterministic configuration [9].
- **BSE [est].** $L=L^0+L^0(2v-W)L$. The absorption uses $\bar L$, with the long-range $v_{\mathbf{G}=0}$ removed from the exchange, and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{M}}=1-v_0\bar\chi_{00}$. The full $L$ gives the loss function [1].
- **TDDFT [est].** $\chi=\chi^0+\chi^0(v+f^{\mathrm{xc}})\chi$, with local fields from $\mathbf{G}\neq0$. A long-range kernel $f^{\mathrm{xc}}=-\alpha_{\mathrm{LRC}}e^2/q^2$ mimics continuum excitons [3].
- **Exciton–phonon [est].** Exciton self-energy [13]; exciton–phonon matrix elements and relaxation times (hBN) [11]; "elemental" (irreducible) versus reducible excitons [14]; exciton lifetimes and optical line-width profile from exciton–phonon interactions, monolayer MoS₂ [15]; phonon-assisted absorption and emission of bulk hBN by finite differences [10].
- **Polaritons [est].** Hopfield [2] mixes the exciton field with photons. In a BSE code this is the transverse pole of §4.1.
- **2D [est].** For a freestanding sheet $W^{\mathrm{e,2D}}=2\pi e^2/[Q_\parallel(1+r_0Q_\parallel)]$ with $r_0=2\pi\chi^{\mathrm{2D}}$ [4]. In a dielectric environment the standard Rytova–Keldysh generalization is $2\pi e^2/[Q_\parallel(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+r_0Q_\parallel)]$, used with ab initio $r_0$ for TMD excitons in [17]. EPP's E.469–E.470 give the sheet part; $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$ is added in [§B.10](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-2d) ((B10.8)–(B10.9)), which now also solves the Wannier equation with this interaction ((B10.22)–(B10.24)).

## 4. How it could enter the EPP formalism

**4.1 Photon-irreducible BSE as $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ [proposal].** With $t=(n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})$ and $L^0_t=(f_{n\mathbf{k}}-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})/(\hbar\omega+E_{n\mathbf{k}}-E_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}+\mathrm{i}\eta)$ as in E.282 ((B5.18)):

$$\Pi^{\mathrm{e,BSE}}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=-\frac{2}{c\,\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{SC}}}\sum_{tt'}j^{\,t}_{\alpha}\big[\bar L_{tt'}(\mathbf{q},\omega)-\bar L_{tt'}(\mathbf{q},0)\big]\big(j^{\,t'}_{\beta}\big)^*,\qquad \bar L=L^0+L^0\big(2\bar\Xi^{\mathrm{x}}-\Xi^{\mathrm{d}}\big)\bar L .$$

*Kernels.* Write all kernels in EPP's scalar + vector (4×4) form of E.317–E.319, $D^0=\mathrm{diag}(v/e^2,D^{0\mathrm{T}})$ ([§B.6](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-propagator)), which is Coulomb-gauge-like. The exchange is $\bar\Xi^{\mathrm{x}}_{tt'}=\rho_t^*\,\bar v\,\rho_{t'}$ (pair densities $\rho_t$, $\mathbf{G}\neq0$ only), plus a transverse (magnetic) part $\mathbf{j}^{\mathrm{T}*}\cdot D^{0\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{j}^{\mathrm{T}}$. That part is of relative size $(\hbar|\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}|/m_{\mathrm{e}}c)^2\approx10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ (§B.5: magnetic forces are weaker than Coulomb by the square of the electron speed over $c$), and we drop it [derived]. The temporal-gauge form $-\frac1c\,\mathbf{j}^*\cdot D^{0\mathrm{L}}\cdot\mathbf{j}$ gives $(\Delta_t\Delta_{t'}/\hbar^2\omega^2)\,\bar v_{tt'}$ by continuity and equals $\rho^*\bar v\rho$ only on shell ([selfenergy.md](selfenergy.md) §4.3), so it is not used inside the kernel. The current form is kept only for the two external vertices of $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$. The direct kernel is $\Xi^{\mathrm{d}}=\rho^*W\rho$, with $W=e^2D_{\rho\rho}$ the scalar (Coulomb) sector of the total $D$ (§4.3). With $\bar\Xi^{\mathrm{x}}=\Xi^{\mathrm{d}}=0$ this reduces to E.281. The subtraction $\bar L(\omega)-\bar L(0)$ stands in for the diamagnetic term ((B5.15)). This is exact only if the static BSE current response obeys the same Ward identity, which holds for the conserving pair of §7 step 1 but not in general for $G_0W_0$ + static-$W$ BSE.

*Length form.* At $\mathbf{q}\to0$ we define $\Pi^{\mathrm{e,BSE}}$ by its length-gauge form (what BSE codes compute):

$$\Pi^{\mathrm{e,BSE}}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\omega^2}{4\pi c}\big[\varepsilon^{\bar L}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)-\delta_{\alpha\beta}\big],\quad \varepsilon^{\bar L}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{8\pi e^2}{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{SC}}}\sum_S\Big[\frac{R^{\,S}_{\alpha}\big(R^{\,S}_{\beta}\big)^*}{E_S-\hbar\omega-\mathrm{i}\eta}+\frac{\big(R^{\,S}_{\alpha}\big)^*R^{\,S}_{\beta}}{E_S+\hbar\omega+\mathrm{i}\eta}\Big],$$

with $R^{\,S}_{\alpha}=\sum_{vc\mathbf{k}}A^S_{vc\mathbf{k}}\langle c\mathbf{k}|\hat v_\alpha|v\mathbf{k}\rangle/(\mathrm{i}\omega^{\mathrm{KS}}_{cv\mathbf{k}})$ for exciton $S$ (TDA; standard [1]; for $\omega^{\mathrm{KS}}$ see §5). The velocity-gauge expression above agrees with it only if the current vertex is dressed consistently with the kernel and $\Sigma$, $\hat{\mathbf{v}}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\nabla/m_{\mathrm{e}}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}[V^{\mathrm{NL}}+\Sigma^{\mathrm{nl}},\mathbf{r}]$ (Ward identity). With bare vertices the two differ. Since $\varepsilon^{\bar L}(0)$ is finite, $\Pi\propto\omega^2$, so gauge invariance ($\Pi(0)=0$) holds by construction for the length form. The independent check, the f-sum rule ($\Pi\to\Pi^{\mathrm{dia}}$ as $\omega\to\infty$), is then not guaranteed, and TDA violates it. Inserted into E.380 for a cubic crystal (§B.6):

- $\varepsilon^{\bar L,\mathrm{L}}=0$ gives the longitudinal excitons, which are the poles of the full-$L$ BSE [est].
- $c^2q^2=\omega^2\varepsilon^{\bar L,\mathrm{T}}$ gives exciton polaritons [2].
- For one exciton with oscillator strength $(\omega^{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{p}})^2$, $(\omega^{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{L}})^2-(\omega^{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{T}})^2=(\omega^{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{p}})^2/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}$, the algebra of $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2-\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2$. The exciton L–T splitting comes from the removed $\mathbf{G}=0$ exchange, which $D^{0\mathrm{L}}$ restores here [est; reproduced here in EPP form; 1, 2].

**4.2 Local fields and xc [proposal].** Fold the microscopic lines into a macroscopically irreducible self-energy, then keep E.380 at $\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}'=0$:

$$\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}=\Pi^{\mathrm{e},0}+\Pi^{\mathrm{e},0}\big(\bar D^0+D^{\mathrm{xc}}\big)\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{e}},\quad \bar D^0_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}'}=(1-\delta_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{0}})D^0(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G},\omega)\delta_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}'},\quad D^{\mathrm{xc}}_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{c}{e^2\omega^2}\partial_\alpha\partial'_\beta f^{\mathrm{xc}}.$$

$D^{\mathrm{xc}}$ is the E.364 rule applied to $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$, an energy like $v$, hence the $1/e^2$ of (B9.6). At this response-function level the current form is safe: both vertices carry the external $\omega$, and with the diamagnetic term in $\Pi^{\mathrm{e},0}$ the longitudinal projection reproduces the density Dyson equation. The on-shell problem of §4.1 arises only between transition vertices [derived]. The long-range kernel $f^{\mathrm{xc}}=-\alpha_{\mathrm{LRC}}e^2/q^2$ [3] becomes $D^{\mathrm{xc}}_{\alpha\beta,\mathbf{0}\mathbf{0}}=+\frac{c\,\alpha_{\mathrm{LRC}}}{\omega^2}\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$, a $q$-independent term that belongs in the kernel of the $\bar\Pi$ Dyson equation (the $\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}'=0$ element of $D^{\mathrm{xc}}$), not in the propagator $D$ [derived].

**4.3 Direct kernel from $D$: phonon screening [proposal].** Take $\Xi^{\mathrm{d}}=\rho^*W\rho$, with $W=e^2D_{\rho\rho}$ the scalar (Coulomb) sector of the total $D=D^{\mathrm{e}}+D^{\mathrm{ph}}$, $D^{\mathrm{ph}}=-D^{\mathrm{e}}JFJ^\dagger D^{\mathrm{e}}$ (selfenergy.md §4.2). Its scalar sector is $W=W^{\mathrm{e}}+W^{\mathrm{e}}\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\rho\rho}W/e^2$, i.e. $W=W^{\mathrm{e}}+W^{\mathrm{e}}\chi^{\mathrm{ion}}W^{\mathrm{e}}$, with $\chi^{\mathrm{ion}}$ the reducible ionic density response ($W^{\mathrm{ph}}=W^{\mathrm{e}}\chi^{\mathrm{ion}}W^{\mathrm{e}}$ of [§A.5](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a-hedin-baym)), whose poles are the screened LO frequencies of E.454 (G.104–105; §X.1). $\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}$ itself is irreducible and built from bare short-range phonons, with poles at the unscreened $\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}$. So EPP's kernel contains Fröhlich phonon screening [12], dynamically. With free carriers the poles of $W$ become coupled LO-phonon–plasmon modes ([§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-dynamic); [plasmon.md](plasmon.md)). In 2D the static sheet gives [derived from E.470–E.472]

$$\varepsilon^{\mathrm{2D}}_{\mathrm{s}}(Q_\parallel)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+\big(r_0^{\mathrm{e}}+r_0^{\mathrm{ion}}\big)Q_\parallel,\qquad r_0^{\mathrm{ion}}=\frac{2\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{S}}\,M_{\mathrm{r}}\,\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2}=\frac{C}{(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}})^2},$$

with $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{2D}}_{\mathrm{s}}$ the static sheet screening with the ions free (the sheet analogue of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}}$), $r_0^{\mathrm{e}}$ the $r_0$ of (B10.7), and $C=\hbar^2\cdot2\pi e^2Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\mathrm{S}}M_{\mathrm{r}})$ the coefficient in the table of [§B.10](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-2d). With the primer's illustrative presets: 2.9 Å against $r_0^{\mathrm{e}}=10$ Å for hBN (+29%), 0.5 Å against 40 Å for a MoS₂-like sheet (~1%). Unlike bulk, 2D ionic screening vanishes as $Q_\parallel\to0$: it changes $r_0$, not the $1/(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}r)$ tail. For $E_{\mathrm{b}}\gg\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ the ions cannot follow, so the static value is an upper bound and the dynamic kernel is needed. Monolayer hBN is this case: $E_{\mathrm{b}}$ is of order 2 eV (illustrative) against $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}\lesssim0.2$ eV, so the static +29% badly overestimates the effect on the bound exciton.

**4.4 Phonon-assisted optics as the first non-RPA electronic insertion [proposal].** In EPP the phonon line is $D^{\mathrm{ph}}$. At first order in $D^{\mathrm{ph}}$, $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ gains two self-energy diagrams and one vertex correction, $\Pi^{(1)}=\Pi^{\mathrm{SE}}[D^{\mathrm{ph}}]+\Pi^{\mathrm{VC}}[D^{\mathrm{ph}}]$, with the vertex $g^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ of §B.8 ((B8.6); E.415–E.416). Golden-rule cuts through $F$ should reproduce the long-range (dipole) part of G.200, with $g^{\mathrm{dyn}}\to$ its electrostatic limit ((B8.16)–(B8.17); E.448) (to derive). The full G.200 needs the short-range vertex of [shortrange.md](shortrange.md). Ladders on both sides give the excitonic form [est, schematic; cf. 10]:

$$\mathrm{Im}\,\varepsilon^{(1)}_{\alpha\alpha}(\omega)\propto\frac{1}{\omega^2}\sum_{S\mathbf{q}\nu s}\Big|\sum_{S'}\frac{J^{\,S'}_{\alpha}\,\mathcal{G}_{S'S\nu}(\mathbf{q})}{E_{S'}-\hbar\omega}\Big|^2\big(n_{\mathbf{q}\nu}+\tfrac12+\tfrac s2\big)\,\delta\big(E_S(\mathbf{q})+s\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}-\hbar\omega\big).$$

Here $J^{\,S'}_{\alpha}$ is the current of the bright exciton $S'$ and $\mathcal{G}$ the exciton–phonon vertex; from EPP alone $\mathcal{G}$ contains only the Fröhlich part. The light is now internal: $\alpha_{\mathrm{abs}}=2\,\mathrm{Im}\,q$ with $c^2q^2=\omega^2\varepsilon^{\mathrm{T}}$, so $n_{\mathrm{r}}$ becomes an output (§A.9). Luminescence needs the lesser $\Pi^<$ for a thermalized exciton population (detailed balance, as in [10]), which means a Keldysh extension of EPP. The same diagrams resummed in the exciton basis give $\Sigma^{\mathrm{X}}_S(\omega)$ for linewidths [13, 15].

**4.5 2D radiative widths and polaritons [proposal].** Replace $(\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}/\Omega_{\mathrm{S}})\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}$ in the finite-$c$ sheet algebra of §B.10 by the BSE sheet response. With $\zeta=\sqrt{Q_\parallel^2-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}\omega^2/c^2}$ ((B10.28)) one obtains exciton radiative widths with the $\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (p) and $1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (s) laws, which (B10.29) gives for the in-plane phonons. Out-of-plane and interlayer excitons need the $zz$ block and the $z$-resolved $D(\mathbf{Q}_\parallel,\omega;z,z')$ (p. 100; [twod.md](twod.md)).

## 5. Pitfalls

- **$\mathbf{G}=0$ exchange twice.** Keeping $v_0$ in the kernel while E.380 adds $D^{0\mathrm{L}}$ doubles the L–T splitting. The transverse $\mathbf{G}=0$ exchange belongs to $D$ only.
- **Temporal-gauge kernels.** Inside the BSE kernel use $\rho^*v\rho$ and $\rho^*W\rho$ (E.317–E.319), not $-\frac1c\mathbf{j}^*\cdot D\cdot\mathbf{j}$. The latter carries $(\Delta/\hbar\omega)^2$ factors and diverges for a static $W$ (§4.1).
- **Screening twice.** EPP needs bare short-range phonons ([§B.1](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b1-range), the range rule; [§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-electrostatic)). Phonons that already carry the Gonze–Lee LO–TO term, combined with $\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}$, count it twice. $Z^*$ and $\varepsilon_\infty$ from DFPT include local fields and xc, while EPP's $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ does not: keep them consistent, or E.454 shifts. Restoring the ionic $\mathbf{G}\neq0$ terms must match the Gonze–Lee Ewald split ([shortrange.md](shortrange.md); (B9.15)). Williams–Lax supercells already contain the static polar fields that $D^{\mathrm{ph}}$ would add again.
- **Polar-only phonon channel.** Without short-range $g$, EPP gives zero phonon-assisted absorption in a nonpolar crystal such as Si ($Z^*=0$), and misses zone-boundary processes such as those near T/K in bulk hBN.
- **Which excitons couple.** Compute $\mathcal{G}$ from $\bar L$ eigenvectors and let $D$ add the macroscopic field. In EPP terms, Paleari–Marini's elemental versus reducible distinction [14] is $\bar L$ versus $L$ [spec].
- **Vertex screening.** DFPT $g$ is screened by $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}}$, EPP's by $D^{\mathrm{e}}$: use one. Self-energies and phonon kernel must be a conserving pair, derived from one Luttinger–Ward functional.
- **Gauge and sum rules.** E.350 (length = velocity gauge) holds only if the current vertex is dressed consistently with the kernel and $\Sigma$ (Ward identity). Include $[V^{\mathrm{NL}},\mathbf{r}]$ and, with $GW$, the nonlocal-$\Sigma$ correction to velocities [est] (Del Sole–Girlanda 1993; Levine–Allan 1989; both unverified, §6). For $G_0W_0$ + static-$W$ BSE no exact Ward identity exists: the kernel is not $\delta\Sigma^{GW}/\delta G$ (the $\mathrm{i}G\,\delta W/\delta G$ term is dropped), and the static approximation breaks the frequency structure. The length–velocity mismatch must then be measured, not assumed away.
- **KS versus QP energies in $R$.** $R^{\,S}_{\alpha}$ must use KS transition energies $\omega^{\mathrm{KS}}_{cv}$ when $\langle\hat{\mathbf{v}}\rangle$ is the KS velocity (i.e. KS dipoles $\langle c|\mathbf{r}|v\rangle$), even when $E_S$ uses QP energies. Using $\omega^{\mathrm{qp}}$ rescales oscillator strengths by $(\omega^{\mathrm{KS}}/\omega^{\mathrm{qp}})^2$ [est].
- **f-sum rule and TDA.** Check the f-sum rule with the full (coupling) BSE or at the IP/RPA level; TDA violates it. Beyond TDA, keep the coupling block for $D^{\mathrm{L}}$ near plasmons [est: TDA loss spectra are unreliable there, e.g. the Si plasmon] (Olevano–Reining 2001; Sander–Maggio–Kresse 2015; both unverified, §6). Keep SE and VC together, or $\Pi^{(1)}(0)\neq0$ gives a fake Drude term (§B.5).
- **Numerics.** Compute $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ from $\varepsilon^{\bar L}-1$ times $\omega^2$, since the $1/\omega^2$ double pole of E.374 cancels only analytically. 2D BSE needs truncated Coulomb and dense $\mathbf{k}$ meshes. $\eta$ must be below meV [6], and Fröhlich $q\to0$ singularities need principal-value care (G p. 43).
- **$n_{\mathrm{B}}=0$** fails for internal phonon lines; use Matsubara or real-time occupations.

## 7. First steps

1. **Derive (1–2 weeks).** Write 4.1–4.2 with all prefactors, with kernels in the 4×4 scalar + vector form. For $G_0W_0$ + BSE, quantify the length–velocity mismatch numerically in your code. Show that the poles of $D^{\mathrm{L}}$ are the full-$L$ excitons and that $D^{\mathrm{T}}$ gives Hopfield polaritons.
2. **2D optics in your code (1–2 weeks).** This assumes that 2D-truncated Coulomb, SOC and $\mathbf{k}$ meshes of at least 30×30 are already available. For monolayer hBN and encapsulated MoS₂ (with SOC to resolve A and B; encapsulation via $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$ in the sheet propagator, not explicit hBN layers), build the sheet $\Pi$ from $\varepsilon^{\bar L}$. (a) Compare $r_0$ from BSE, RPA and RPA without local fields (what E.472 inherits). (b) Compute radiative widths and polariton dispersion from the sheet propagator. (c) Check length versus velocity gauge. Check the f-sum rule with the full (coupling) BSE or at the IP/RPA level, since TDA violates it.
3. **Phonon screening (weeks).** Use $W=W^{\mathrm{e}}+W^{\mathrm{e}}\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\rho\rho}W/e^2$ (bare short-range phonons, $Z^*$) as the kernel, first static and then with a first-order dynamic correction [12]. Test bulk MgO or GaN against $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}}-\varepsilon_\infty$ (Lyddane–Sachs–Teller) and the model of [12]; in 2D compare with $r_0^{\mathrm{ion}}$. For monolayer hBN go directly to the dynamical kernel, since $E_{\mathrm{b}}\gg\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$.
4. **Phonon-assisted optics (months).** Derive $\Pi^{(1)}$ at finite $T$ and recover the Fröhlich part of G.200, then the excitonic form. A Si comparison [8] requires the short-range vertex first.
5. **Local fields and xc (weeks).** Restore $\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}'$ and $D^{\mathrm{xc}}$; quantify $\varepsilon_\infty$ and E.454.
