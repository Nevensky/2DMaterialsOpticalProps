# Short-range coupling and higher multipoles

Labels: **[est]** established in the literature; **[derived]** algebra done for this note from EPP's own equations, not printed in EPP; **[proposal]** a suggested extension of EPP; **[spec]** speculation. Numbers marked *illustrative* come from the primer's toy models or literature presets. References marked † in §6 could not be re-verified in this revision. Notation and units are those of primer v2 ([§C.0](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#c-conventions)): Gaussian units with $e>0$, $\hbar$ and $c$ explicit, and $v=4\pi e^2/q^2$ and $W$ carrying $e^2$. $Z^*$ is dimensionless, so the dipole of a displaced atom is $e\mathbf{Z}^*\cdot\mathbf{u}$ (EPP's $Z^*$ carries the charge); the quadrupoles $Q$ and $\mathcal{Q}$ below are likewise in units of $e$. $Q^{\beta\gamma}_{\kappa\alpha}$ (or $Q$) is the dynamical quadrupole of the literature, not a normal coordinate, and $Q_\parallel$ is the in-plane wavevector of a sheet. A tilde on $\Phi$ means mass-scaled, as in primer v2. (A$n$.$m$) and (B$n$.$m$) are equations of primer v2. Primed and lettered E tags (E.295′, E.454a, …) are the corrected or added equations of [corrections/EPP_corrections.tex](../../corrections/EPP_corrections.tex); primer v2 builds those corrections into Part B and states them in footnotes.

## 1. What is missing in EPP

**No deformation-potential vertex.** EPP derives the rigid-ion vertex $g^0$ in §2 (E.100–E.108; primer (B1.4)), then drops it in §4. In the Lagrangian (E.134; (B2.6)) the electrons feel the frozen potential $V^{\mathrm{en}}_0(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\ell\kappa}V_\kappa(\mathbf{r}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\ell\kappa})$ (E.136; (B2.7)). The ions reach the field only through the Born-dipole current $\frac1c\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{\ell\kappa}\cdot\mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\ell\kappa})$, $\mathbf{p}_{\ell\kappa}=e\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\ell\kappa}$ (E.135; (B2.10)). This is E.134 with each dipole at its atom, as primer v2 writes it and states in a footnote of [§B.2](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b2-site); the draft prints $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{R}^0_i)$, the cell corner (p. 26). No term contains both an electron position $\mathbf{r}_s$ and a displacement $\mathbf{u}_{\ell\kappa}$ ([§B.2](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b2-noeph)), so "plausibly closed the scheme" (p. 26) holds only for the polar channel. As a result:

- In Si, diamond or graphene ($Z^*=0$), EPP's electron–phonon coupling vanishes.
- Non-polar optical modes (MoS₂ A$_1'$) and the acoustic deformation potential are absent.
- Intervalley coupling reduces to the screened dipole tail.

[§B.1](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b1-range) lists $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ as "not used", and [§X.2](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#x2-missing) lists short-range coupling as missing.

**Only the dipole moment.** Point dipoles at the sites (E.241′, 314′; (B2.10)) give the ionic current vertex $j_{\alpha\nu}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})=\sum_{\kappa\beta}eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}\,e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\mathbf{q})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$ (E.295′; (B5.22)). This is the zeroth moment of the current a moving atom carries.

- **Optical modes.** The vertex has the full $O(q^{-1})$ Fröhlich term.
- **Acoustic modes.** The $O(q^0)$ term is incomplete. With the basis phase (E.131b; (B1.17)–(B1.18) of [§B.1](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b1-acoustic)) only the internal-strain dipole survives. The clamped-ion response, carried by dynamical quadrupoles, is missing. Primer v2 now says so ([§A.4](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a4-limits), [§B.1](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b1-acoustic)); neither source includes the quadrupoles.
- **GaAs check.** The internal-strain term alone gives ≈1.2 C/m² along [111] in the primer's central-force toy, against a measured $(2/\sqrt3)|e_{14}|\approx0.19$ C/m² (*illustrative*). The toy has Kleinman parameter $\zeta=1$. Its next-nearest-neighbour springs join atoms of the same sublattice and do not couple strain to the internal displacement, so $\zeta$ is set by the central nearest-neighbour springs alone **[derived]**. In general $e^{\mathrm{int}}_{14}=eZ^*\zeta/a^2=1.04\,\zeta$ C/m² for GaAs ($Z^*=2.07$, $a=5.65$ Å), or $1.20\,\zeta$ C/m² along [111] **[derived]**. With a realistic $\zeta\approx0.5$–$0.75$ (literature range, unverified) the internal-strain term is about 0.6–0.9 C/m² along [111]. The clamped-ion (quadrupolar) term still has to cancel most of it. The split into the two parts should be quoted from de Gironcoli, Baroni and Resta (1989)† once that paper is checked.

**Force constants.** EPP p. 21 assumes force constants that vanish beyond one cell ($\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}$, EPP's $k^{\mathcal{S}}$; (B1.7)). DFPT force constants carry dipolar and multipolar tails, and EPP does not say what to subtract. Primer v2 states the rule for the dipolar tail, subtract $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$ ([§B.1](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b1-range), [§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-matrix)); the multipolar tails remain open.

**Local fields.** From E.380 on, only $\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}'=0$ is kept, and $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ is RPA without $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$. A $\mathbf{G}$-resolved ionic $\Pi$ (E.454a of the corrections) needs Ewald removal of the on-site self term. The draft has neither; primer v2 now writes that $\mathbf{G}$ sum, (B9.15), with the self term to be removed by Ewald's method ([§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-matrix)). Nor does the draft dress the electron's coupling to the macroscopic field with local fields (§3, P0).

## 2. Why it matters

- **Transport.** The room-temperature mobility of n-type monolayer MoS₂ (~410 cm²/Vs) is dominated by optical-phonon scattering through intra- and intervalley deformation potentials and the Fröhlich interaction (Kaasbjerg *et al.* 2012; figure and summary as recalled from the abstract, not re-checked here). EPP has only the Fröhlich part. Above the L-valley threshold, hot electrons in GaAs also relax by intervalley ($\Gamma\to$L, X) scattering, which needs short-range coupling. Ab initio hot-electron thermalization in GaAs is due to Sjakste, Vast and Tyuterev (2007) and Bernardi *et al.* (2015), who resolved carrier lifetimes valley by valley (G §X.A, p. 015003-46). Intervalley scattering times in Ge were computed the same way (Tyuterev *et al.* 2011).
- **Quadrupoles [est].**
  - Si, GaAs, GaP: neglecting quadrupoles changes the computed mobilities significantly (Brunin *et al.* 2020†; magnitude to be quoted from the paper).
  - GaN: quadrupoles reshape the piezoelectric (acoustic) scattering of electrons and holes (Jhalani *et al.* 2020†). The earlier draft of this note said the acoustic share falls from about half of the total rate to a negligible one for electrons, and from ~75% to below 50% for holes. Those numbers are not re-checked.
  - MoS₂: neglecting quadrupoles gives 23% and 76% errors in the electron and hole Hall mobilities (Poncé *et al.* 2023†; not re-checked in this revision).
- **2D power counting [derived].** In a sheet with electronic screening length $r_0$ the macroscopic interaction is $W^{\mathrm{e,2D}}(Q_\parallel)=2\pi e^2/[Q_\parallel(1+r_0Q_\parallel)]$ ((B10.9) with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}=1$; [§B.10](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b-2d)). Per unit displacement $u$, the macroscopic charges (in units of $e$) are:
  - optical dipole, $\rho^{\mathrm{dip}}\sim Z^*Q_\parallel u$;
  - quadrupole, $\rho^{\mathrm{quad}}\sim \mathcal{Q}\,Q_\parallel^2u$;
  - acoustic piezoelectric (internal strain plus clamped ion), $\rho^{\mathrm{piezo}}\sim Q_\parallel\cdot(e_{ij}\,Q_\parallel u)$, i.e. $Q_\parallel$ times a piezoelectric constant $e_{ij}$ times a strain $\sim Q_\parallel u$.

  The vertex is $g\sim W\rho$. For the optical dipole that gives $g\sim 2\pi e^2Z^*u/(1+r_0Q_\parallel)$. For $Q_\parallel\ll1/r_0$ this is $O(Q_\parallel^0)$, like the optical deformation potential. The acoustic piezoelectric and quadrupole terms are $O(Q_\parallel)$, like the acoustic deformation potential. So in 2D, unlike 3D, short-range coupling is leading order. For $Q_\parallel\gg1/r_0$ the dipole term is further suppressed by $1/(r_0Q_\parallel)$. In MoS₂, $r_0\approx40$ Å (the §B.10 MoS₂-like preset, *illustrative*; not checked against the literature), so $1/r_0\approx0.025$ Å⁻¹. Thermal electrons at 300 K with $m^*\approx0.5\,m_{\mathrm{e}}$ have $k_{\mathrm{th}}=\sqrt{2m^*k_{\mathrm{B}}T}/\hbar\approx0.058$ Å⁻¹, and emission of a 48 meV optical phonon (§B.10 preset, *illustrative*) needs $Q_\parallel\approx0.08$ Å⁻¹. Over the relevant 0.06–0.12 Å⁻¹, $r_0Q_\parallel\approx2.5$–$5$ and $1/(1+r_0Q_\parallel)\approx0.17$–$0.29$. The finite 2D Fröhlich vertex is that of Sohier, Calandra and Mauri (2016)†; primer v2 now writes its sheet form, finite as $Q_\parallel\to0$ and falling as $1/(r_0Q_\parallel)$ beyond $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}/r_0$ ([§B.10](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b10-coupling)). Short-range coupling therefore dominates even more, and EPP §8 alone cannot give 2D carrier or exciton scattering.
- **For an RPA/GW/BSE practitioner.** Exciton linewidths in monolayer TMDs come from intravalley phonon scattering. In W-based TMDs they also come from scattering into dark intervalley excitons, K–K′ and K–Λ, so the phonon momenta are near $q\approx\mathrm{K}$ and $q\approx|\mathrm{K}-\Lambda|$, both deep in the zone (Selig *et al.* 2016†; which materials were measured and which only modelled is to be checked against the paper). That is short-range territory, as is phonon-assisted absorption (G §IX.B, p. 015003-44). DFPT screens with local fields (G.53) and EPP's $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ has none. Reconciling the two is the $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{M}}$ / $\bar v$ split of your BSE code.

## 3. What standard theory does

- **DFPT vertex.** $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})=\langle\psi_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|\Delta_{\mathbf{q}\nu}V^{\mathrm{KS}}|\psi_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle$ (G.38; (A2.16)), with $\partial V^{\mathrm{KS}}=(\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}})^{-1}\partial V^{\mathrm{en}}$ (G.53). It contains all orders, statically screened ([§A.3](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a-dfpt); Baroni *et al.* 2001).
- **Decay in real space.** The potential of a displaced atom decays as $R^{-3}$ or faster in nonpolar insulators, a consequence of the analytic properties of the dielectric matrix (Pick, Cohen and Martin 1970). So $g$ is finite at $q\to0$ (Vogl 1976; G p. 015003-34). In polar insulators it decays as $R^{-2}$ and $g\sim1/q$ (G §VI.A.2–3). Vogl (1976) is also the original microscopic multipole treatment of the long-range vertex.
- **Split for interpolation [est].** Subtract (G.180) on the coarse grid, Wannier-interpolate $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ (G.177–G.179), and add $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$ back, Gaussian-filtered (footnote 7; Verdi and Giustino 2015; Sjakste *et al.* 2015). Force constants are treated the same way with the Ewald dipole–dipole term (Gonze and Lee 1997).
- **Quadrupoles [est].** Long-wave DFPT gives the dynamical quadrupoles $Q^{\beta\gamma}_{\kappa\alpha}$ (Royo and Stengel 2019†). With $\rho(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})=-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{p}-\tfrac12(q_\beta+G_\beta) (q_\gamma+G_\gamma)\,eQ^{\beta\gamma}$, the long-range vertex is, schematically,
  $$g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{mn\nu}\propto\sum_{\kappa}\sum_{\mathbf{G}\neq-\mathbf{q}}\frac{\big[\mathrm{i}(q_\beta+G_\beta) Z^*_{\kappa,\beta\alpha}+\tfrac12(q_\beta+G_\beta) (q_\gamma+G_\gamma) Q^{\beta\gamma}_{\kappa\alpha}\big]e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf{q})}{(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_\infty\cdot(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})}\,\langle\psi_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa)}[1+\dots]|\psi_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle .$$
  The quadrupole term is $O(q^0)$ and direction dependent. It spoils interpolation even in Si (Park *et al.* 2020†; Brunin *et al.* 2020†). The "$\dots$" stands for two further $O(q^0)$ corrections.
  - **Local fields of the macroscopic field.** The macroscopic field $E_\gamma\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}$ of the dipole induces a periodic Hxc potential $V^{\mathrm{Hxc},E_\gamma}(\mathbf{r})$, the Hxc potential per unit macroscopic field (Brunin *et al.*'s $V^{\mathrm{Hxc},\mathcal{E}}$ term†). From the dressed vertex P0b below **[derived]**, $1+\sum_{\mathbf{G}\neq0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{G}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\big((\bar v+f^{\mathrm{xc}})\bar\chi\big)_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{q},0)=1-\mathrm{i}q_\gamma V^{\mathrm{Hxc},E_\gamma}(\mathbf{r})+O(q^2)$. Times the dipole numerator, this adds $q_\beta q_\gamma Z^*_{\kappa,\beta\alpha}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}\langle\psi_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa)}V^{\mathrm{Hxc},E_\gamma}|\psi_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle/(\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_\infty\cdot\mathbf{q})$ to the $\mathbf{G}=0$ term: $O(q^0)$ and direction dependent, the same order as the quadrupole. Map the factor and sign to Brunin *et al.*'s convention before comparing.
  - **Berry connection.** In the Wannier gauge the overlap $\langle u_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle$ must be expanded to $O(q)$. The $O(q)$ term is the Berry connection, needed for gauge covariance at quadrupolar order (Poncé *et al.* 2023†).
- **Implementations.** The static subtraction with quadrupoles is available in ABINIT (Brunin *et al.*†), Perturbo (Park *et al.*†, Jhalani *et al.*†) and EPW (Poncé *et al.*†). This is as reported in those papers and was not re-checked here.
- **Multipolar force constants.** The same multipole orders in the force constants fix the sound velocity of piezoelectrics (Royo, Hahn, Stengel 2020†).
- **Martin relation.** The clamped-ion piezoelectric tensor is the sublattice sum of the first moments of the induced charge, that is, of the dynamical quadrupoles (Martin 1972†).
- **2D.** Truncated-Coulomb DFPT (Sohier *et al.* 2017†) and exact quasi-2D electrostatics (Royo and Stengel 2021†).

## 4. How it could enter the EPP formalism

**Route A** unfreezes $V^{\mathrm{en}}_0$ and replaces the Born dipoles by bare pseudo-ion charges. $Z^*$, $Q$, $\varepsilon_\infty$ and $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ must then emerge from a $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}'$-resolved $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ with $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$. This is Hedin–Baym (G.121; [§A.5](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a-hedin-baym)) in vector form: exact, but expensive. To avoid double counting, split $V_\kappa=V_\kappa^{\mathrm{sr}}-Z_\kappa e^2/r$. Only $V_\kappa^{\mathrm{sr}}$ moves with the ion in $V^{\mathrm{en}}$. The point charges $eZ_\kappa$ couple to $\mathbf{A}$, and the bare ion–ion Coulomb is removed from the force constants, because the longitudinal photon regenerates both. **Route B**, P0–P5 below, keeps EPP's structure and adds DFPT inputs under subtraction rules. All of it is **[proposal]** unless marked [derived].

**P0 · Partition.** Microscopic dressing ($\mathbf{G}\neq0$ Coulomb and $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$) goes into the inputs. EPP's lines carry only the macroscopic ($\mathbf{G}=0$) longitudinal and transverse photon, so everything attached to them must be macroscopically proper.

- **P0a, polarization.** Replace $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$ by $\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}$. This is the $\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}'=0$ element of the current–current response that is irreducible with respect to $\mathbf{G}=0$ photon lines, longitudinal and transverse, with $\bar v+f^{\mathrm{xc}}$ in the longitudinal kernel:
  $$\bar\chi=\chi^0+\chi^0(\bar v+f^{\mathrm{xc}})\bar\chi,\qquad \bar v_{\mathbf{G}}=v(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})(1-\delta_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{0}}).\tag{P0a}$$
  E.350 ((B6.7)) then gives $\Pi^{\mathrm{e}}_{\rho\rho}=e^2\bar\chi_{\mathbf{0}\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)$ automatically, so continuity and the L/T consistency of the photon self-energy are kept. Replacing only the $\rho\rho$ slot would break both. The transverse local-field kernel needs TDCDFT. Alternatively, it follows from the $q\to0$ equality of the longitudinal and transverse heads in cubic crystals.
- **P0b, vertex.** The electron's coupling to the macroscopic photon is dressed by the same kernel:
  $$\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle\;\to\;\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\Big[1+\sum_{\mathbf{G}\neq0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{G}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\big((\bar v+f^{\mathrm{xc}})\bar\chi\big)_{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big]|n\mathbf{k}\rangle .\tag{P0b}$$
  This is the EPP counterpart of DFPT's $V^{\mathrm{Hxc},\mathcal{E}}$ term (§3). Use the same dressed vertex in $g^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ and in the P3 subtraction.
- **Consequences.** Since $1-v(\mathbf{q})\bar\chi_{\mathbf{0}\mathbf{0}}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{M}}$ [est], EPP regenerates (via E.350) the $\varepsilon_\infty$ that DFPT uses. $Z^*$ and $Q$ are already zero-macroscopic-field quantities.

**P1 · Multipolar ionic current.** Replace term 6 of E.134 ((B2.6)) by $\frac1c\int\mathbf{j}^{\mathrm{ion}}\cdot\mathbf{A}$, with $\mathbf{j}^{\mathrm{ion}}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\ell\kappa\beta}\dot u_{\ell\kappa\beta}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}_{\kappa\beta}(\mathbf{r}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\ell\kappa})$. Here $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}$ is the current-density response to the atomic velocity (Stengel 2013†). Its moments are $\int\mathcal{J}_{\kappa\beta,\alpha}=eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}$ and $\int r_\gamma\mathcal{J}_{\kappa\beta,\alpha}=\frac e2\mathcal{Q}_{\kappa,\alpha\gamma\beta}$. Then E.295′ ((B5.22)) becomes
$$j_{\alpha\nu}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})=\sum_{\kappa\beta}e\Big[Z^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(q_\gamma+G_\gamma)\,\mathcal{Q}_{\kappa,\alpha\gamma\beta}+O(|\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}|^2)\Big]\frac{e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\mathbf{q})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa}}{\sqrt{M_\kappa}}.\tag{P1}$$
- **[derived]** $(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{j}$ reproduces the dipole plus quadrupole charge of §3, so the symmetric part of $\mathcal{Q}$ is fixed by $Q$.
- The antisymmetric part couples to $\mathbf{B}$ **[spec: negligible]**.
- **[derived]** P1 is linear in $\dot{\mathbf{u}}$, so E.183′, E.304′ and the gauge sum rule E.310 ((B4.7), (B5.24), (B5.25)) carry over with $\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa\to\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})=\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_\kappa$. This holds by construction (completeness of the eigenvectors), so it is not a test (§7).

**P2 · Bare phonons.** Take DFPT minus exactly what EPP regenerates statically:
$$\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}=\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{DFPT}}-\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{lr}},\qquad \tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{4\pi e^2}{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}\sqrt{M_\kappa M_{\kappa'}}}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}{}^{\mathrm{Ewald}}\frac{[(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})]^*_\alpha[(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{Z}^*_{\kappa'}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})]_\beta}{(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_\infty\cdot(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\kappa'})}.\tag{P2}$$
This is E.454a ((B9.15), mass-scaled) with $\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa\to\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})$: dipole–dipole, dipole–quadrupole and quadrupole–quadrupole terms, with the self term removed.

- **Order [derived].** Dipole–dipole is $O(1)$, dipole–quadrupole $O(q)$ and quadrupole–quadrupole $O(q^2)$. P2 is complete through $O(q)$ in all blocks. It is complete through $O(q^2)$ only in the acoustic–acoustic block after projection with the acoustic sum rule, which is enough for sound velocities. There, $\sum_\kappa\mathbf{Z}^*_\kappa=0$ makes the acoustic charge $O(q^2)$, and the dipole–octupole and $\varepsilon_\infty(q)$ corrections enter only at $O(q^3)$ and $O(q^4)$. The optical and mixed blocks at $O(q^2)$ also need octupoles and the $q^2$ dispersion of $\varepsilon_\infty$.
- **[derived]** EPP's $\mathbf{G}=0$-only term is not periodic in $\mathbf{q}$, so it jumps at the zone boundary. Keep the Gaussian-filtered $\mathbf{G}$ sum (as in G.180), in $\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{lr}}$ and in EPP alike. What the filter removes is analytic and stays in $\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}$.

**P3 · Vertex.** Fix $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ by matching to DFPT in the static, instantaneous limit:
$$g^{\mathrm{tot}}(\omega)=g^{\mathrm{sr}}+\delta g^{\mathrm{sr}}(\omega)+g^{\mathrm{dyn}}(\omega),\qquad g^{\mathrm{sr}}\equiv g^{\mathrm{DFPT}}-g^{\mathrm{dyn}}\big|_{c\to\infty,\,\omega=0}.\tag{P3}$$
$g^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ is E.416 ((B8.6)) with P0a, P0b and P1, in Giustino's labels with the E.448a sign convention ((B8.17)). Its static limit is (G.180) plus quadrupoles plus the local-field term of §3, which is the long-range part DFPT codes subtract. Then $g^{\mathrm{tot}}(c\to\infty,0)=g^{\mathrm{DFPT}}$ by construction. In the Wannier gauge, also expand the overlap to $O(q)$ (Berry connection, §3). If P0b is not used, $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ keeps the $O(q^0)$ nonanalytic remainder $q_\beta q_\gamma Z^*_{\kappa,\beta\alpha}\langle V^{\mathrm{Hxc},E_\gamma}\rangle/(\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_\infty\cdot\mathbf{q})$, and its Wannier interpolation fails.

**P4 · Dynamic screening of $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$.** Let $n^{\mathrm{ind}}_\nu(\mathbf{q},\omega)$ be the $\mathbf{G}=0$ charge induced by the proper vertex $\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}$, counted in electrons (charge $-en^{\mathrm{ind}}_\nu$): the bubble with one macroscopic charge vertex and one $\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ vertex, with $\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ screened by $\bar v+f^{\mathrm{xc}}$ only. The $Z^*$ and $Q$ in $J$ are total static (ionic plus electronic) charges, so $J$ already contains $n^{\mathrm{ind}}(0)$. Only the change $n^{\mathrm{ind}}(\omega)-n^{\mathrm{ind}}(0)$ may be added:
$$\delta g^{\mathrm{sr}}_{mn\nu}(\omega)=\bar\rho^*_{n\mathbf{k},m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\,W^{\mathrm{e}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\big[n^{\mathrm{ind}}_\nu(\mathbf{q},\omega)-n^{\mathrm{ind}}_\nu(\mathbf{q},0)\big],\tag{P4}$$
where $\bar\rho$ is the dressed vertex P0b. Equivalently, carry the dynamic part as a frequency-dependent ionic current $J\to J+\Delta J(\omega)$, where $\Delta J$ carries, by continuity, the charge $-e[n^{\mathrm{ind}}(\omega)-n^{\mathrm{ind}}(0)]$, and $\Delta J(0)=0$.

- **Check [derived].** The exact vertex is $\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}(\omega)+\bar\rho^*W^{\mathrm{e}}(\omega)[n^{\mathrm{ion}}+n^{\mathrm{ind}}(\omega)]$, with $n^{\mathrm{ion}}=-\rho^{\mathrm{ion}}/e$ the Born-dipole charge in the same units. P3 gives $g^{\mathrm{sr}}=\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}(0)$ and $g^{\mathrm{dyn}}=\bar\rho^*W^{\mathrm{e}}(\omega)[n^{\mathrm{ion}}+n^{\mathrm{ind}}(0)]$. Adding P4 reproduces the exact vertex, up to $\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}(\omega)-\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}(0)$, the microscopic dynamic screening, which is small in gapped systems.
- P4 matters in doped sheets ([§B.10](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b10-doped); bulk: [§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-dynamic)), where the carriers screen dynamically ([plasmon.md](plasmon.md)), not in gapped insulators.

**P5 · Dyson equations.**
$$F=F^0+F^0\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,tot}}F,\qquad \Pi^{\mathrm{ph,tot}}=-(J+\Delta J)^*D^{\mathrm{e}}(J+\Delta J)+\big[\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}(\omega)-\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}(0)\big].\tag{P5}$$
- The first term is E.399a (the $\mathbf{G}$-resolved form of (B7.5)) with P0 and P1 plus $\Delta J$. It enters in full, because P2 removed its static part and $\Delta J(0)=0$.
- $\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}$ contains only bubbles whose $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ ends are joined by $\bar v+f^{\mathrm{xc}}$, with no $\mathbf{G}=0$ photon line. Every $\mathbf{G}=0$ charge is in $J+\Delta J$ and every $\mathbf{G}=0$ line in $D^{\mathrm{e}}$. Its static part is already in the DFPT force constants, so it enters only nonadiabatically (EPP p. 21; G.125).
- Inside $\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}$ (E.352′; (B5.26)), use $J+\Delta J$ and dress the phonons only by $\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}(\omega)-\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}(0)$, so that $D$ (E.386–E.387; (B5.13)) adds the macroscopic dressing once.
- **[derived]** Both $\Delta J$ and $\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}(\omega)-\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}(0)$ vanish at $\omega=0$, so $\Pi^{\mathrm{para}}(0)$ and E.310 ((B5.25)) survive. Gauge invariance at finite $\omega$ with an $\omega$-dependent $\Delta J$ is not checked (§8).

## 5. Pitfalls

- **Inconsistent $\varepsilon$.** Suppose $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$ is subtracted with DFPT's $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}}$ but regenerated with EPP's $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA}}$. Then $g^{\mathrm{tot}}$ has a spurious Fröhlich tail $\propto(1/\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA}}-1/\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}})/q$. If it is subtracted with $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{RPA}}$, the same tail sits in $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ and spoils interpolation. Local fields lower the RPA head by about 10% in sp semiconductors (*illustrative*, [§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-survivor)), and ALDA $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$ raises it again. Measure the net difference (§7 step 3) instead of assuming 10%. P0a removes the mismatch.
- **Double screening.** DFPT's $g$ is already screened at $\mathbf{G}=0$, so routing it through $D^{\mathrm{e}}$ again screens it twice. A screened $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ on both sides of a bubble screens it once too often, a factor $1/\varepsilon$ too small. This is the G.145 ($g^0g^*$, one bare vertex) versus G.146 ($|g|^2$) issue (G p. 015003-25; primer [§A.6](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#a6-vertices)).
- **Routing and periodicity.** Every nonanalytic $\mathbf{G}=0$ charge (dipole, quadrupole, piezoelectric, local-field) goes through $\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{L}}$, never into $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ or $\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}$. A $\mathbf{G}=0$-only $g^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ makes $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ jump at the zone boundary, so use the filtered $\mathbf{G}$ sum.
- **Force constants.** Subtract the same multipole orders you regenerate. Keep the acoustic-sum-rule on-site block (missing from E.4) and check that results do not depend on the Ewald parameter.
- **Basis phase.** Without $\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa}$ and the phase convention of (A1.18) (Giustino's; the printed E.15 has the opposite sign), a spurious acoustic coupling appears (GaAs 0.6–1.8 C/m² along [111], *illustrative*). It is comparable to or larger than the clamped-ion (quadrupole) term itself, which is about 0.4–0.7 C/m² along [111] if $\zeta=0.5$–$0.75$ (§1; $\zeta$ unverified).
- **Convention mapping.** Map $\mathcal{Q}$ to ABINIT's $Q$ explicitly. Check the Fourier sign (the corrected EPP vertex and primer v2 use $\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_\kappa}$; this note writes $\rho=-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{p}-\frac12(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\,eQ$), the factor $\frac12$, the conversion from e·bohr, and the charge in $Z^*$: dimensionless here, in ABINIT and in primer v2, while EPP's carries it, $Z^*_{\mathrm{EPP}}=eZ^*$ ([§X.2](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#x2-srcmap)). A mismatch flips the sign of the dipole–quadrupole interference: the leading correction for optical modes and the clamped-ion piezoelectric term for acoustic modes. Check the sign of the dQ term against Brunin *et al.*'s published Si or GaAs $g$ before any physics run.
- **Nonlocal pseudopotentials.** Current vertices need the $\mathrm{i}[V^{\mathrm{NL}},\mathbf{r}]$ term, as in velocity-gauge optics (Dreyer *et al.* 2018†).
- **2D.** Use one sheet propagator in DFPT, in the subtraction and in EPP §8. Include $Z^*_{zz}$ and out-of-plane quadrupoles.

## 7. First steps

1. **Derive and check (days).** Use checks that can fail. Do them in 3D zincblende directly. The primer's three-atom chain ([§B.5](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b5-sumrule)) is 1D and longitudinal. It cannot hold the off-diagonal $Q^{yz}_x$-type components, the only ones the $T_d$ site symmetry of Si and zincblende allows, nor the antisymmetric part of $\mathcal{Q}$. E.310 with P1 holds by construction and is not a test.
   - (i) Continuity: $(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{j}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})/\omega$ from P1 reproduces $e\big(-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{Z}^*\cdot\mathbf{u}-\frac12(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})Q\cdot\mathbf{u}\big)$ in EPP's Fourier convention.
   - (ii) With the basis phase, the $O(q)$ acoustic vertex does not depend on the choice of cell ((B1.15)).
   - (iii) Martin relation: the acoustic $O(q)$ vertex equals internal strain plus the sublattice-summed $Q$. Compare it with ABINIT's clamped-ion plus relaxed-ion $e_{14}$ for GaAs, not only with the measured value.
   - (iv) For P2, compare $\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{lr}}$ with an independent long-range force-constant implementation for Si and GaAs. An example is ABINIT anaddb's dipole–quadrupole and quadrupole–quadrupole options; check the variable names in the current docs.
2. **Bulk benchmarks (1–2 weeks).**
   - (a) Reproduce Brunin *et al.*'s published Si and GaAs $g$ and mobilities with ABINIT. This is a regression test that fixes the conventions (units and signs of $Q$, the $V^{\mathrm{Hxc},\mathcal{E}}$ term).
   - (b) Then swap in EPP's $g^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ with P0 and P1, and measure the difference. Build $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ by P3 and check its real-space decay. An $O(q^0)$ direction-dependent remainder shows up as a slow $R^{-3}$ tail, instead of the faster decay a complete subtraction leaves.
   - (c) Compare LA[111] with the DFPT $e_{14}$ (clamped-ion plus relaxed) as well as with the measured one.
   - Before choosing pseudopotentials, check the long-wave DFPT restrictions on functional and pseudopotential. They have historically been LDA, norm-conserving, and no nonlinear core correction; check the current version.
3. **$\varepsilon$ consistency (1–2 weeks).** Compute the head with your RPA code, with and without local fields and $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$, and measure the net residual $1/q$ tail in $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$.
4. **2D (1–2 months).** First reproduce Poncé *et al.*'s published MoS₂ couplings and mobilities. Then treat monolayer MoS₂ (A$_1'$, LO, LA at $\mathrm{K}$ and at $|\mathrm{K}-\Lambda|$) with truncated DFPT and the §8 propagator ((B10.18)–(B10.19)), plus P4 for gated samples. Compare with Kaasbjerg *et al.*
5. **Hand-off (months).** Pass $g^{\mathrm{tot}}$ to the electron self-energy and exciton–phonon topics.

## 8. Open questions

- Is Route A tractable: does a $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}'$ RPA+$f^{\mathrm{xc}}$ self-energy reproduce $Z^*$, $Q$, $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$?
- Should $Q$ and $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ be screened dynamically by carriers, as the dipole term is ([plasmon.md](plasmon.md))? P4 says how, but $\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}(\omega)-\bar g^{\mathrm{sr}}(0)$ is dropped.
- Does an $\omega$-dependent $\Delta J$ (P4–P5) keep gauge invariance at finite $\omega$, beyond E.310 at $\omega=0$?
- How good is the transverse local-field kernel from the equal-heads argument in non-cubic and 2D crystals, or is TDCDFT needed?
- Which vertex, bare or screened, belongs on each side of $\bar\Pi^{\mathrm{ph,sr}}$ when the screening is dynamic?
- Acoustic modes never enter the light cone ($v_{\mathrm{s}}\ll c/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$) **[derived]**. Is finite $c$ ever relevant to short-range physics?
- **[spec]** Does the magnetic part of $\mathcal{Q}$ matter for chiral phonons in 2D?
