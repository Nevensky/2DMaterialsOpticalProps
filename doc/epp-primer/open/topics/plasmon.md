**Verdict.**

- **Does EPP support plasmon–phonon hybridization?** In its equations, yes: with carriers, the RPA bubble inside the phonon self-energy (E.398–E.399, E.388) carries the plasmon, and the pole condition E.453 contains the coupled LO-phonon–plasmon modes. In its results, no: the manuscript sets $\omega=0$ in the screening (E.454) and evaluates no doped crystal. The primer (§B.9, "Keeping $\omega$") supplies the carrier models and the $\mathrm L_\pm$ modes.
- **Should it be on the roadmap?** Yes, as a seventh topic. LP's self-energy is the electrostatic limit of EPP's once the electrons are split into bound electrons and carriers (§4.1), and LP solve it as a frequency-independent dynamical matrix with one extra row for the plasmon. So the bulk case needs no new Lagrangian terms and little new algebra. EPP can add finite $c$ (infrared near the light cone), 2D sheets with their environment, a split that cannot double count, and the same propagator in the electron self-energy and in pairing.

## 1. What is missing in EPP

- **The equations allow it.** $F=F^0+F^0\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}F$ with $\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\nu\nu'}=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\nu}D^{\mathrm e}_{\alpha\beta}J_{\beta\nu'}$ (E.398–E.399; primer (B7.5), (B7.7)) uses $D^{\mathrm e}$ dressed by the RPA bubble $\Pi^{\mathrm e}$, a sum over transitions weighted by $f_{n\mathbf k}$ (E.388, E.383). With carriers, $\Pi^{\mathrm e}$ gains a Drude term and $D^{\mathrm e}$ a plasmon pole ([§B.7](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b7-partA), "What this model leaves out"). For $c\to\infty$ the pole condition E.453, primer (B9.9), keeps $W^{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$; solved as it stands, it gives the $\mathrm L_\pm$ modes.
- **The manuscript stops at E.454.** It sets $\omega=0$ because "the 3D electronic screening is in the range of phonon energies practically static". True with a gap, or with a plasmon far above the phonons; false for $q\lesssim\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}$ when the plasmon lies near or below them ([§B.9](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-window), "When the static step is safe"). Its 2D result (E.471–E.472) is static too, and it evaluates no doped crystal.
- **Diagonal approximation.** §7.3 keeps only the diagonal $\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\nu\nu}$ (E.449). One plasmon couples to every polar mode with a current along $\hat{\mathbf q}$, so several polar modes need the matrix form (E.398; primer (B9.14)): in anatase TiO₂ one to three phonons hybridize, depending on $\hat{\mathbf q}$ [LP].
- **No damping beyond $\eta$, no electron side.** Landau damping would come from $\mathrm{Im}\,\chi^0$ once the bubble is evaluated; carrier scattering and anharmonic phonon widths are absent. Without an electron Dyson equation (roadmap topic 4) there is no self-energy from the hybrids, no satellite and no pairing kernel.

**What the primer adds.** [§B.9 "Keeping $\omega$"](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b9-dynamic): the Drude, Thomas–Fermi and Lindhard carrier response; (B9.16)–(B9.19); the window where the static step is safe; the non-commuting limits $q\to0$, $\omega\to0$; the figure `viz-b9-plasmon` (GaAs, GaN). Its footnotes credit Varga (1965) and Mooradian and Wright (1966) and note that the manuscript stops at E.454. [§B.8](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b8-dynamic) screens the polar vertex at the phonon frequency with $\mathbf S^{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$; [§B.10](https://claude.ai/artifact/VpqcSjXyAFNMcgBmiv3Gid#b10-doped) has a Drude sheet, (B10.25)–(B10.26). §A.6 and §A.7 point to these modes. All are one-band models at $T=0$.

## 2. Why it matters

**Magnitudes** [primer, LP]:

- **n-GaAs.** $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}=\omega_{\mathrm p}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$, with $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}^2=4\pi n_{\mathrm c}e^2/(\varepsilon_\infty m^*)$, gives $\hbar\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}=43.45$ meV at $n_{\mathrm c}=10^{18}$ cm⁻³ and crosses $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ at $6.9\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³. At $10^{18}$ cm⁻³ the modes sit at 47.8 and 30.3 meV (B9.18). Static Thomas–Fermi screening (B9.19) instead slides the LO mode to $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ for $q\lesssim k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}=0.021$ Å⁻¹ and has no plasmon, while the carriers cannot follow for $q\lesssim\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}=0.010$ Å⁻¹.
- **LP's benchmark.** At $q=8\times10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹ along [110] and 300 K, LP reproduce the Raman data of Mooradian and McWhorter (1967) without free parameters. Their static calculation loses the LO–TO splitting above $2\times10^{15}$ cm⁻³, where their Thomas–Fermi wavevector exceeds $q$, and at every density for infinitesimal $q$.
- **Anatase TiO₂** [LP]. Anti-adiabatic in both directions at $10^{18}$ cm⁻³; in-plane resonant at $10^{19}$; in-plane fully screened (no LO–TO splitting) and out-of-plane resonant at $2\times10^{20}$ cm⁻³. The plasmon at $\Gamma$ depends on $\hat{\mathbf q}$, like the LO mode through (B9.12) (Ahn and Das Sarma 2021).
- **Electrons** [LP; derived here]. Statically screened phonons give an $-\mathrm{Im}\,\Sigma$ that "can be significantly lower" than the hybrid one; adding a bare-plasmon self-energy overestimates it at low density, because the phonons screen the electron–plasmon coupling. The plasmon-like hybrid then couples with $(\varepsilon_\infty/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s})^{3/2}$ of the bare plasmon's strength (§4.4): 0.78 in GaAs, much less in strongly polar oxides.

**For an RPA/$GW$/BSE practitioner:**

1. **Doped and gated sheets.** A sheet plasmon rises from zero as $\sqrt{Q_\parallel}$ and crosses the LO branch above a modest density. In the primer's MoS₂-like Drude sheet at $n^{\mathrm{2D}}_{\mathrm c}=10^{12}$ cm⁻² the crossing lies near $Q_\parallel=0.05$ Å⁻¹, where the modes split by 4.3 meV against a 0.2 meV undoped LO shift (§B.10). Every dynamically screened $W$ of a doped sheet contains these hybrids.
2. **Static doped DFPT is the adiabatic limit** (Macheda et al. 2022, 2023): right for $q\gg\omega/v_{\mathrm F}$; at the small $q$ that optics probes, right only when the plasmon lies far above the phonons.
3. **Carrier screening of the polar vertex is dynamic** (§B.8). A static Thomas–Fermi factor removes coupling the carriers cannot screen at phonon frequencies. [spec] The hybrids should also appear in the dynamically screened $W$ of BSE calculations on gated samples.

## 3. What standard theory does

- **Models and experiment** [lit, via LP]. The coupled modes were treated by Yokota (1961), Varga (1965) and Singwi and Tosi (1966), and observed by Raman scattering in GaAs (Mooradian and Wright 1966; Mooradian and McWhorter 1967). LP's three regimes, by increasing doping, are the two limits of (B9.18) and the region between:

  | Regime | Energies | LO–TO splitting | Long-range field |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | anti-adiabatic | $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\ll\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ | yes | phonon |
  | resonant | $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\approx\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ | unclear: the LO phonons hybridize | hybrid |
  | adiabatic | $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\gg\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ | no | plasmon |

- **First principles before LP** [lit, as LP characterize it]. Verdi et al. (2017) and Caruso et al. (2018) screened with an electron-gas model and no feedback from the phonons to the plasmons; electron–plasmon coupling (Caruso and Giustino 2016) was computed apart from electron–phonon coupling. Static DFPT with doping (Macheda et al. 2022; 2023 in 2D) "cannot reproduce any nonadiabatic effects". For the carrier-induced phonon self-energy LP cite, among others, Berges et al. (2023, "To screen, or not to screen") and Marini (2023, "free from overscreening").
- **LP's method.** Undoped DFPT, rigid-band doping, RPA screening by the conduction-band carriers without local fields, and a plasmon-pole model (Hybertsen and Louie 1986; multipoles: Leon et al. 2021). Its three parameters are least-squares fits to that RPA response on a frequency grid (Supplemental Material, Secs. S1 A and S3). All this is turned into an $(N_{\mathrm{ph}}+1)$-dimensional dynamical matrix, $2(N_{\mathrm{ph}}+1)$-dimensional with linewidths (§4.2–4.3). Satellites and dilute superconductivity are left for later.

## 4. How it could enter the EPP formalism

**4.1 LP (1) is the electrostatic limit of EPP's phonon self-energy [derived here].**

1. **Start from (B9.8).** For $c\to\infty$,
$$\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\nu\nu'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{\hbar\,W^{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)}{2e^2\Omega_{\mathrm V}\sqrt{\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\omega_{\mathbf q\nu'}}}\,\big[\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\nu\big]^*\big[\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_{\nu'}\big],$$
with Part B's bare (short-range) modes $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ and the mode currents $\mathbf j_\nu$ of §B.9. The poles solve (B9.14), $\det\big[(\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu})\delta_{\nu\nu'}-\tfrac2\hbar\sqrt{\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\omega_{\mathbf q\nu'}}\,\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\nu\nu'}\big]=0$. *Why:* nothing is added to the primer.
2. **Split the electrons** into the bound electrons of the undoped crystal and the carriers. In the RPA the independent-particle responses add, $\chi^0=\chi^0_{\mathrm b}+\chi^0_{\mathrm c}$, and at phonon frequencies the bound part is $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$: $\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm e}\cdot\hat{\mathbf q}=\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q}-v\chi^0_{\mathrm c}$. The chain of (B9.6) then regroups exactly:
$$W^{\mathrm e}=W^{\mathrm e}_\infty+W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}W^{\mathrm e}_\infty,\qquad W^{\mathrm e}_\infty(\mathbf q)=\frac{4\pi e^2}{\mathbf q\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\mathbf q},\qquad \chi_{\mathrm c}=\chi^0_{\mathrm c}+\chi^0_{\mathrm c}W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}.$$
*Why:* with $x=W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi^0_{\mathrm c}$, $W^{\mathrm e}=W^{\mathrm e}_\infty/(1-x)$ and $\chi_{\mathrm c}=\chi^0_{\mathrm c}/(1-x)$, and $1/(1-x)=1+x/(1-x)$. The split assumes rigid bands and ignores Pauli blocking of interband transitions.
3. **The first term makes the undoped crystal.** With $W^{\mathrm e}_\infty$, $\tfrac2\hbar\sqrt{\omega\omega'}\,\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}$ is the static dipole–dipole matrix (B9.12) in the bare-mode basis, and with $\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}\delta_{\nu\nu'}$ it forms $\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}+\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$ (B9.14). Its eigenmodes are the DFPT phonons, LP's $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$; this note writes $\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$, and $\bar{\mathbf j}_\nu$ for their mode currents. *Why:* the self-energy in squared-frequency units, $\tfrac2\hbar\sqrt{\omega\omega'}\,\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\nu\nu'}=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\Omega_{\mathrm V}}\mathbf j^*_\nu\cdot D^{\mathrm e}\cdot\mathbf j_{\nu'}$ (B7.19), depends on the modes only through their currents, so a unitary change of mode basis transforms it like the dynamical matrix and leaves (B9.14) unchanged.
4. **The second term is LP (1).** In the undoped basis and in LP's units (squared frequency),
$$\frac2\hbar\sqrt{\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu'}}\;\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\mathrm c,\nu\nu'}=\frac{W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}W^{\mathrm e}_\infty}{e^2\Omega_{\mathrm V}}\,(\mathbf q\cdot\bar{\mathbf j}_\nu)^*(\mathbf q\cdot\bar{\mathbf j}_{\nu'})=\big(g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu}\big)^*\,\delta\chi\,g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu'}\Big|_{\mathrm{LP}}$$
with $U_{\mathbf q}=W^{\mathrm e}_\infty/\Omega_{\mathrm V}$ (their $4\pi e^2/(Vq^2\,\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q})$, $V$ the cell volume), $\delta\chi=\Omega_{\mathrm V}\chi_{\mathrm c}$ (per cell) and $g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu}|_{\mathrm{LP}}=\mathrm i\,W^{\mathrm e}_\infty(\mathbf q\cdot\bar{\mathbf j}_\nu)/(e\,\Omega_{\mathrm V})$: the potential energy of an electron in the macroscopic field of mode $\nu$'s polarization wave, per unit mass-weighted normal coordinate. *Why:* the product of the three is the middle expression.
5. **Propagators and units.** LP's $D$, with $D^0=1/[(\omega+\mathrm i0^+)^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}]$, is the propagator of mass-weighted normal coordinates $Q_{\mathbf q\nu}=(\hbar/2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})^{1/2}\phi_{\mathbf q\nu}$ (§A.1): $D_{\nu\nu'}=\hbar F_{\nu\nu'}/(2\sqrt{\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\omega_{\mathbf q\nu'}})$, and their self-energy is $\tfrac2\hbar\sqrt{\omega\omega'}\,\Pi^{\mathrm{ph}}$, with the frequencies of the modes whose fields define $F$ (the undoped $\bar\omega$ in their basis). *Why:* the primer's $F^0=\frac1\hbar\,2\omega/[(\omega+\mathrm i\eta)^2-\omega^2]$ is per energy (NOTATION §12.3); scaling rows and columns of $F^{-1}$ by $(2\omega/\hbar)^{1/2}$ gives $D^{-1}$, as for (B9.14). Then $|g^{\mathrm{lr}}|^2_{\mathrm{LP}}\delta\chi$ is (energy/(length·√mass))²/energy, a squared frequency. The primer's vertex is $g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=(\hbar/2\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})^{1/2}g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu}|_{\mathrm{LP}}\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$, the $\mathbf G=0$ term of (B8.15)–(B8.17); LP's has no band indices because the electrons sit in $\delta\chi$.

The mapping holds at $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$, in the RPA without local fields for the carriers, with the undoped $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$. LP take that from DFPT (the macroscopic tensor of $\varepsilon^{\mathrm e}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}}$ with local fields), EPP from its RPA head (§B.9, "What this $W^{\mathrm e}$ leaves out"). LP's $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$, Eq. (S5) of their Supplemental Material, is the expression of step 4: it keeps the Born dipoles only and omits the basis phase $\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\mathbf q\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$, which matters only away from $\Gamma$. Neither side has quadrupoles (topic 5). Since $W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}W^{\mathrm e}_\infty=W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi^0_{\mathrm c}W^{\mathrm e}$, LP (1) also has §B.8's count: one vertex screened by the bound electrons only, which are not in the bubble, one fully screened, and the carriers' independent-particle bubble between. It does not overscreen.

**4.2 Plasmon pole and dynamical matrix [derived here].** LP (2) reads
$$W^{\mathrm e}_\infty(\mathbf q)\,\chi_{\mathrm c}(\mathbf q,\omega)\simeq\frac{\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}^2}{(\omega+\mathrm i\gamma^{\mathrm{pl}}_{\mathbf q})^2-(\omega^{\mathrm{pl}}_{\mathbf q})^2}.$$
The f-sum rule, $\int_0^\infty\mathrm d\omega\,\omega\,[-\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mathrm c}]=\pi n_{\mathrm c}q^2/2m^*$ (the same for $\chi^0_{\mathrm c}$), fixes the strength at §B.9's $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}$. For anisotropic or nonparabolic bands, $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}^2(\hat{\mathbf q})=4\pi e^2n_{\mathrm c}\,\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot(1/m^*)\cdot\hat{\mathbf q}/(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q})$, with the carriers' average inverse-mass tensor; it is nonanalytic at $\Gamma$. Matching the static response fixes the frequency, $(\omega^{\mathrm{pl}}_{\mathbf q})^2=\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}^2(1+\varepsilon_\infty q^2/k^2_{\mathrm{TF}})$ for Thomas–Fermi carriers; non-degenerate carriers take the Debye $k^2_{\mathrm{TF}}=4\pi e^2n_{\mathrm c}/k_{\mathrm B}T$. LP impose neither condition: they fit all three parameters to the RPA response at 31 frequencies from 0 to 500 meV (TiO₂; GaAs "in a similar manner", Sec. S3), so their strength obeys the f-sum rule only as well as the fit does. $\gamma^{\mathrm{pl}}_{\mathbf q}$ is the imaginary part of the pole, a half width like §A.6's $\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu}$; in LP it is Landau damping. For $\gamma\to0$ the Schur complement on the plasmon row shows that LP's propagator, $\hbar F/(2\sqrt{\bar\omega\bar\omega'})$, is the phonon block of $(\omega^2-\mathsf C)^{-1}$:
$$\mathsf C(\mathbf q)=\begin{pmatrix}(\omega^{\mathrm{pl}}_{\mathbf q})^2&\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,1}&\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,2}&\cdots\\ \bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,1}&\bar\omega^2_{\mathbf q1}&0&\cdots\\ \bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,2}&0&\bar\omega^2_{\mathbf q2}&\cdots\\ \vdots&&&\ddots\end{pmatrix},\qquad \big(\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,\nu}\big)^2=\frac{4\pi\,|\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\bar{\mathbf j}_\nu|^2}{\Omega_{\mathrm V}\,\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q}}.$$
The index of $\mathsf C$ runs over $\nu=0$ (the plasmon) and $\nu=1,\dots,3N_\kappa$ (the undoped phonons). $\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,\nu}$ is the ionic plasma frequency of mode $\nu$ along $\hat{\mathbf q}$, screened by $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$; the eigenvector phases are chosen so that $\mathrm i\,\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\bar{\mathbf j}_\nu>0$. So LP's wings (6), $c_{\mathbf q\nu}=\Omega_{\mathbf q0}g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu}/\sqrt{U_{\mathbf q}}$, are products of two screened plasma frequencies, of the carriers and of the lattice. For the LO mode of a cubic two-atom crystal, $(\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p})^2=\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}$ (B9.11). Modes without a current along $\hat{\mathbf q}$ do not couple, as in LP's Fig. 2. Hybrid $s=0,\dots,3N_\kappa$ has frequency $\omega_{\mathbf qs}$ and eigenvector $X_{\nu,s}(\mathbf q)$ (LP (7)), and LP (8) reads $\hbar F_{\nu\nu'}/(2\sqrt{\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu'}})=\sum_sX_{\nu,s}X^*_{\nu',s}/[(\omega+\mathrm i\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf qs}]$. Three checks, all to machine precision:

- **$q\to0$, Drude.** With $\omega^{\mathrm{pl}}=\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}$, the $2\times2$ characteristic polynomial is $\omega^4-(\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}+\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p})\omega^2+\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p}\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}$: (B9.18). GaAs at $10^{18}$ cm⁻³ gives 47.75 and 30.30 meV both ways.
- **$\omega=0$, Thomas–Fermi.** The static hybrid problem gives $\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-(\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}})k^2_{\mathrm{TF}}/(\varepsilon_\infty q^2+k^2_{\mathrm{TF}})$: (B9.19). The pole model is exact in both of §B.9's "two corners" and interpolates between them.
- **Secular equation.** Apart from modes without a current along $\hat{\mathbf q}$, which stay at $\bar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$, $\det(\omega^2-\mathsf C)=0$ is $\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}(\mathbf q,\omega)=0$ for the total longitudinal dielectric function with pole-model carriers. It uses $\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q}/\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{undoped}}=1+\sum_\nu(\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,\nu})^2/(\omega^2-\bar\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu})$, exact as a rank-one update of $\tilde\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}$. The hybrids are the longitudinal poles of $D$ (§X.1, (X1.14)), the many-mode form of (B9.16), found by linear algebra instead of a root search.

**4.3 Damping [derived here].** With the half widths on a diagonal, $\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)=\mathrm{diag}(\gamma^{\mathrm{pl}}_{\mathbf q},\gamma_{\mathbf q1},\dots)$, LP (9) is the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}-\mathrm i\,\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)&\mathbb 1\\ \mathsf C&-\mathrm i\,\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)\end{pmatrix}$ on (coordinate, velocity). It linearizes $\det\{[\lambda+\mathrm i\,\mathrm{diag}(\gamma)]^2-\mathsf C\}=0$, with the denominators $(\omega+\mathrm i\gamma)^2-\omega_0^2$ of LP (2), and its eigenvalues are $\omega_{\mathbf qs}-\mathrm i\gamma_{\mathbf qs}$, frequency and half width. The damped oscillator of NOTATION §8, $\omega^2-\omega_0^2+\mathrm i\gamma\omega$, uses a full width: its $\gamma$ is twice LP's, and its $\omega_0^2$ is LP's plus $\gamma^2$.

**4.4 Electrons see the hybrids [derived here].** Beyond the bound electrons an electron feels $W-W^{\mathrm e}_\infty$, with $W=v/\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}$ for carriers and ions together. In the pole model
$$\frac{W(\mathbf q,\omega)}{W^{\mathrm e}_\infty(\mathbf q)}-1=\sum_s\frac{\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf qs}},\qquad \bar\omega_{\mathrm p,s}=\Big|X^*_{0,s}\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}+\sum_{\nu\ge1}X^*_{\nu,s}\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,\nu}\Big|,\qquad \sum_s\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}=\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p}+\sum_\nu\big(\bar\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p,\nu}\big)^2.$$
Each hybrid carries a share of the plasma frequencies of the plasmon and the polar phonons. Matching one Fröhlich mode, $W^{\mathrm{ph}}/\Omega_{\mathrm V}=|g^{\mathrm{lr}}|^2F^0$, gives the electron–hybrid vertex
$$\big|g^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{mns}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\big|^2=\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\mathbf qs}}\,\frac{W^{\mathrm e}_\infty(\mathbf q)}{\Omega_{\mathrm V}}\,\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}\,\big|\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle\big|^2 .$$
It reduces to $|g^{\mathrm{lr}}|^2$ of §A.4 for one undoped LO mode, by Lyddane–Sachs–Teller, and to the usual electron–plasmon coupling for the plasmon alone. It agrees with LP's Eq. (S66), whose eigenvectors (S20) carry the factor $1/\sqrt{2\tilde\omega_{\mathbf q\beta}}$ of the hybrid, once their short-range vertex $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ (S54) is dropped. LP (10) is then the Fan–Migdal form (A7.9) with hybrids for phonons: $E_{n\mathbf k}$ for their $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$, $1/N_\ell$ for $1/N_q$, $n_{\mathbf qs}=n_{\mathrm B}(\omega_{\mathbf qs})$ for $\tilde n_{\mathbf q\beta}$, and $f$, $1-f$ for $f^\pm$. Minus its imaginary part at the band energy is the golden-rule linewidth; at $E^{\mathrm{qp}}_{n\mathbf k}$ it is $\Gamma^\tau_{n\mathbf k}$ (A7.17). At low density the plasmon-like hybrid sits at $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}}$, screened by the lattice with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}$, and carries $\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}=\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p}(\varepsilon_\infty/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s})^2$. Its $|g^{\mathrm{hyb}}|^2\propto\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}/\omega_{\mathbf qs}$ is therefore $(\varepsilon_\infty/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s})^{3/2}$ of the bare plasmon's, which quantifies LP's remark on the ad hoc correction. In EPP's language this is the identity $D=D^{\mathrm e}+D^{\mathrm{ph}}$ of the self-energy note (§4.2 there) with a dynamic $D^{\mathrm e}$. $W^{\mathrm e}$ has the bare plasmon pole; $W^{\mathrm{ph}}=W-W^{\mathrm e}$ has the hybrid poles and minus the bare plasmon pole. Only the sum has the right poles, and a static $W^{\mathrm{ph}}$ plus a bare-plasmon $W^{\mathrm e}$ breaks that cancellation.

**4.5 What EPP could add beyond LP [proposal].**

- **Finite $c$.** The full $D$ of §B.6 with a Drude $\Pi^{\mathrm e}$ and the ionic $\Pi^{\mathrm{ion}}$ gives transverse modes $c^2q^2=\omega^2\varepsilon(\omega)$, $\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_\infty(1-\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p}/\omega^2)+(\omega^{\mathrm{ion}}_{\mathrm p})^2/(\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}-\omega^2)$. In an isotropic crystal both branches start at $\omega_{\mathrm L\pm}$ at $q=0$ [derived here]; the lower rises towards $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$, the upper joins the light line, and the longitudinal hybrids do not depend on $c$ (X1.14). Infrared probes $q\lesssim q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega)\approx1\times10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹ at 100 meV with $\varepsilon_\infty\approx6$ (illustrative), far below the $2\times10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹ of LP's Fig. 2 (their text calls these wave vectors infinitesimal), which they propose to check by infrared spectroscopy. In uniaxial anatase the modes mix there for $\hat{\mathbf q}$ off the axes (topic 3). One propagator gives hybrids and infrared response with the same $\varepsilon_\infty$ and $Z^*$.
- **2D.** LP's method carries over with a $\sqrt{Q_\parallel}$ plasmon pole and $W^{\mathrm{e,2D}}_\infty=2\pi e^2/[Q_\parallel(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+r_0Q_\parallel)]$ (B10.9). Topic 2's layered kernels add substrate phonons, and §B.10's finite-$c$ sheet propagator gives the radiative widths of the hybrids.
- **One field, no double counting.** Part B regenerates $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$ from the same $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ that screens the carriers, and builds in §B.8's count of bare and screened vertices, the question Berges et al. and Marini (2023) address for the general phonon self-energy.
- **Electrons, satellites, pairing.** Topic 4's $\Sigma^{\mathrm L}[W]$ with this $W$ is LP (10) without the ad hoc sum. A cumulant on the hybrid spectrum (§A.7) gives satellites [spec: plasmon satellites weaken at low density, §4.4]. The hybrid $W(\mathbf q,\mathrm i\omega)$ is the dielectric pairing kernel of dilute polar superconductors (topic 6).

**4.6 What EPP lacks [assessment].**

- **A first-principles carrier response.** No doped $\Pi^{\mathrm e}$ is evaluated; the primer's models are one parabolic band. Needed: bands, the average inverse-mass tensor, a pole or multipole fit of $\chi_{\mathrm c}$.
- **Damping and anisotropy.** Carrier scattering and anharmonicity are outside the RPA; LP's plasmon width is the Landau damping of their fit, and LP (9) takes phonon widths as inputs. Anatase needs $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}(\hat{\mathbf q})$ and an evaluated anisotropic propagator (topic 3).
- **A stated order of limits.** E.454 sets $\omega=0$ first. With carriers $\varepsilon^{\mathrm e}(\mathbf q,0)$ diverges as $q\to0$, so E.454 predicts no LO–TO splitting at $\Gamma$ at any density, against experiment.
- **Beyond rigid bands and $T=0$.** Doping changes $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ (Pauli blocking), the mass and the short-range force constants, which LP keep undoped. GaAs carriers turn non-degenerate at 300 K below about $3\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³, and $\Sigma$ needs the hybrids' Bose factors (topic 4).
- **Shared with LP.** No local fields for the carriers (EPP's head-only $\varepsilon_\infty$ is about 10% too large in sp semiconductors, §B.9); carriers that act on the phonons through $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$ only (LP's electron–hybrid vertex adds DFPT's short-range $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$, Eq. (S54), which EPP lacks: topic 5); the Migdal level, which fails when $E_{\mathrm F}\sim\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ (§A.7).

## 5. Pitfalls

**Double counting.**

- **Reference phonons.** Start from undoped DFPT phonons, which contain $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$, and add only $W^{\mathrm e}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}W^{\mathrm e}_\infty$, as LP do. Dressing DFPT phonons with the full doped $W^{\mathrm e}$ counts the dipole field twice. From Part B's bare lattice, use one $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ in $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$ and in $W^{\mathrm e}_\infty$.
- **Overscreening.** Pair $\chi_{\mathrm c}$ with vertices screened by $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ only, or $\chi^0_{\mathrm c}$ with one fully screened vertex (§4.1). Screening both vertices by the carriers and keeping $\chi_{\mathrm c}$ counts them twice. So do statically doped DFPT force constants plus a dynamic carrier self-energy.
- **Electrons.** Do not add a bare-plasmon self-energy to one from statically screened phonons (§4.4).

**Order of limits.** Compare the $q$ of the experiment with $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}$ and $k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$ (GaAs, $10^{18}$ cm⁻³: 0.010 and 0.021 Å⁻¹). Raman backscattering of visible light probes about 0.01 Å⁻¹ (§B.7); LP's Fig. 1 uses $8\times10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹.

**Pole model and widths.** A single pole has no continuum: inside $\hbar\omega\le\hbar qv_{\mathrm F}+\hbar^2q^2/2m^*$ use the full Lindhard function or a multipole fit (Leon et al. 2021). Fix the two parameters by the f-sum rule and the static response; otherwise (B9.18) or (B9.19) is lost. LP's widths are half widths (§4.3).

**Carriers.** Use $f(T)$ and Debye screening for non-degenerate carriers. Use the average inverse mass, not the band-edge mass, and the tensor for anisotropic bands. Sample $\hat{\mathbf q}$ at $\Gamma$, where $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}(\hat{\mathbf q})$ is nonanalytic like the LO–TO term.

**Unresolved factor 2.** With $\varepsilon_\infty=10.9$ and Debye screening at 300 K, $k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}=1.13\times10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹ at $2\times10^{15}$ cm⁻³; $8\times10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹ is reached at $1.0\times10^{15}$ cm⁻³, half LP's density, as if $\varepsilon_\infty$ were 21.9. Their bare plasmon also lies above §B.9's Drude value at low density (§9). Their Supplemental Material settles neither: it gives no GaAs $\varepsilon_\infty$ or carrier mass and takes the GaAs set-up from Poncé et al. (2021). Recompute both before using Fig. 1 as a benchmark.

## 8. Open questions

- **Which $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$?** DFPT's or EPP's RPA head; it must be the same in $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$, $W^{\mathrm e}_\infty$ and $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}$.
- **Carriers and the lattice.** How much do carriers change the short-range force constants and deformation potentials at $10^{20}$ cm⁻³, which LP (1) leaves out?
- **Anatase in the light cone.** How much $\hat{\mathbf q}$ dependence of the hybrids survives where infrared probes?
- **Gated 2D crystals.** Is one pole per $Q_\parallel$ enough where the plasmon meets the polar branches and the intraband continuum?
- **Dilute carriers.** When $E_{\mathrm F}\lesssim\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$, do vertex corrections move the hybrids or only the electron self-energy?
