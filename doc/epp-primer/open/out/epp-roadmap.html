Electrons, phonons and photons: six open topics

Six topics remain open in the EPP formalism, from excitons in the photon self-energy to ordered phases. The table rates each by impact on 2D optics and by difficulty and names a first step; each section says what is missing, why it matters, how it could enter EPP and where to start. EPP today is a normal-state theory of three bosonic propagators (field, electron-dressed field, phonons) with an RPA electron bubble and Born-charge ions. Equations tagged “proposal” are sketches, not derived results.

Full primer, with derivations and interactive figures: Electron–Phonon–Photon Primer · Executive summary: Electron–Phonon–Photon Brief

Tags such as (E.454) and (G.157) number equations in the EPP draft (v2, corrected as in primer §Z.3; primes mark corrected equations) and in Giustino's review. Primer section headers list their EPP equation ranges: §B.1 1–⁠131, §B.2 132–⁠143, §B.3 144–⁠165, §B.4 166–⁠237, §B.5 246–⁠311, §B.6 312–⁠385, §B.7 386–⁠410, §B.8 411–⁠432, §B.9 433–⁠454, §B.10 455–⁠472. “Derived in the note” marks algebra done for the research notes from EPP's equations, still to be checked independently; a dashed left rule marks a proposal, a solid one an equation derived in the note. Units are Gaussian; numbers marked illustrative are model or order-of-magnitude values, mostly the primer's presets, not computed results.

The six topics at a glance

The sections run from what sits closest to an RPA, GW or BSE optics code (optics, 2D sheets, anisotropy), through the electron and lattice ingredients those results need (self-energies, short-range coupling), to the most distant topic (ordered phases). Numbers follow closeness to your code, not the order of work; the table rates each topic and the paragraph below it suggests where to start.

Both ratings are this page's judgments, based on the notes; the notes themselves do not rate the topics.

Where to start. Three steps run in your own RPA, $GW$ or BSE code and need no theory beyond the notes: $\epsilon_M(\hat\bq)$ along $a$, $c$ and 45° with and without local fields (topic 3, step 2, 1–⁠2 weeks); $r_0$ from BSE, RPA and RPA without local fields, plus length versus velocity gauge, for monolayer hBN and encapsulated MoS₂ (topic 1, step 2, 1–⁠2 weeks); and the $G_\parallel=0$ block of your truncated $W$ projected on atomic heights (topic 2, step 3, 2–⁠4 weeks). A head-only lattice $W^{\rm ph}$ in the same code follows once its prefactors are derived (topic 4, steps 1 and 3). The quickest model steps are the sheet propagator (topic 2, days) and the bulk-hBN model (topic 3, 1–⁠3 days). Topic 5 has high impact but needs DFPT inputs and new Lagrangian terms; topic 6 is furthest from optics.

One pitfall recurs in every topic. DFPT and $GW$ inputs already contain parts of what EPP regenerates: the $\bG=0$ nonanalytic LO–⁠TO term of the force constants, $\epsilon_\infty$ and $Z^*$ with local fields and xc, the long-range vertex $g^{\rm L}$. Take bare phonons from short-range force constants and subtract exactly what EPP re-adds, with the same $\epsilon$ and $Z^*$, or LO–⁠TO and the Fröhlich tail count twice. Each section lists only its own case.

1Optics beyond RPA

Excitons, local fields, xc and phonon-assisted absorption

EPP's photon self-energy is an independent-particle bubble. With your BSE response in its place, one propagator would give exciton polaritons, radiative widths and $\epsilon(\omega)$ from the infrared to the ultraviolet.

Impact on 2D optics high Difficulty medium

What is missing

  • An independent-particle bubble. EPP's photon self-energy (E.278–⁠E.279, §B.5) is a bubble plus a diamagnetic term built from single-particle energies and current vertices. With no electron–hole ladder there are no excitons; with no self-energy insertions, no $GW$ gap.
  • No local fields, no xc. From the full propagator (E.380) on, only the macroscopic element $\bG=\bG'=0$ is kept, so EPP's electronic screening $W^{\rm e}$, its $\epsilon_\infty$ and the 2D $r_0$ are RPA numbers without local fields, and no $f^{xc}$ appears. DFPT screening has both (§A.3).
  • No phonon-assisted processes. Mixed electron–ion bubbles vanish at lowest order (E.316). Phonon-assisted absorption and exciton–phonon coupling would be diagrams of $\Pi^{\rm e}$ with an internal $D^{\rm ph}$ line; they are not derived (§A.9, §Z.1), EPP's zero-temperature rule for boson lines would drop their phonon occupations, and from EPP alone they would be polar only (topic 5).
  • 2D. The sheet bubble gives Rytova–Keldysh screening (E.469–⁠E.470) but no ladder, so neither intralayer nor interlayer excitons.

Why it matters

  • Excitons dominate. Monolayer WS₂ has an exciton binding energy of 0.32 eV and a non-hydrogenic Rydberg series (Chernikov et al. 2014).
  • Linewidths are meV. hBN-encapsulated MoS₂ shows about 2 meV FWHM at 4 K (Cadiz et al. 2017), radiative plus exciton–phonon. EPP's propagator has the ingredients for the radiative channel and the Fröhlich part, though neither is evaluated for a 2D sheet (topic 2); the Fröhlich part is weak in a MoS₂-like sheet ($r_0^{\rm ion}\approx0.5$ Å, primer preset, derived in the note).
  • Inputs propagate. A 20% error in $\epsilon_\infty$ means about 40% in band renormalization and 10% in phonon frequencies (Giustino §XII; §A.9). The zero-point renormalization of the gap (topic 4) shifts every absorption edge.
  • The kernel stays electrostatic. A 2 eV exciton has $q_c\approx10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹, while an exciton of radius about 1 nm (illustrative) samples $Q\sim0.1$ Å⁻¹ (§X.1). Radiative decay and polaritons live in the transverse sector of $D$.

How it could enter EPP

Photon-irreducible BSE as $\Pi^{\rm e}$. With transitions $t=(n\bk,m\bk+\bq)$ and $L^0_t$ as in E.282:

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm e,BSE}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)&=-\frac{2}{c\Omega}\sum_{tt'}j^{\mu}_{t}\,\big[\bar L_{tt'}(\bq,\omega)\\ &\qquad\quad-\bar L_{tt'}(\bq,0)\big]\,j^{\nu*}_{t'}\\ \bar L&=L^0+L^0\big(2\bar K^{x}-K^{d}\big)\bar L\end{aligned}$$

The kernels take EPP's scalar-plus-vector form (E.317–⁠E.319): exchange $\rho^*\bar v\rho$ with $\bG\neq0$ only (in a $z$-resolved sheet the whole $G_\parallel=0$ block, topic 2), direct $\rho^*D_{11}\rho$ with $D_{11}=W$ the Coulomb sector of the total $D$; with both zero this is E.281. The $\omega=0$ subtraction replaces the diamagnetic term exactly only if the static BSE obeys the same Ward identity, so at $\bq\to0$ the note uses the length form $\frac{\omega^2}{4\pi c}[\epsilon^{\bar L}(\omega)-1]$, which BSE codes compute. In E.380 for a cubic crystal (anisotropic ones mix L and T, topic 3), $\epsilon^{\bar L}_L=0$ gives the longitudinal excitons and $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon^{\bar L}_T$ Hopfield's polaritons; the exciton L–⁠T splitting comes from the removed $\bG=0$ exchange, which $D^{0l}$ restores.

Phonon screening and local fields (proposal). The scalar sector of the total $D$ is $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}W$ (cf. G.104–⁠105), so the proposed direct kernel $K^d=\rho^*D_{11}\rho$ would contain Fröhlich phonon screening (Filip et al. 2021), dynamically; EPP itself has no BSE kernel, only this screening inside $D$. For a static sheet:

Derived in the note from E.470–⁠E.472

$$\begin{gathered}\epsilon_{\rm 2D}(Q,0)=\epsilon_{\rm env}+\big(r_0^{\rm e}+r_0^{\rm ion}\big)Q\\ r_0^{\rm ion}=\frac{2\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{S_{\rm uc}\,\mu\,\omega_{\rm T}^2}\end{gathered}$$

With the primer presets, $r_0^{\rm ion}$ is 2.9 Å against $r_0^{\rm e}=10$ Å for hBN (+29%) and 0.5 Å against 40 Å for a MoS₂-like sheet (about 1%). In monolayer hBN ($E_B$ of order 2 eV, illustrative, against $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}\lesssim0.2$ eV) the ions cannot follow, and the static +29% badly overestimates. Local fields and xc would enter through a macroscopically irreducible $\bar\Pi^{\rm e}=\Pi^{\rm e,0}+\Pi^{\rm e,0}(\tilde D^0+F^{xc})\bar\Pi^{\rm e}$ (proposal).

Phonon-assisted optics (proposal). At first order in $D^{\rm ph}$, $\Pi^{\rm e}$ gains two self-energy diagrams and one vertex correction with the vertex of E.415–⁠E.416, whose golden-rule cuts should reproduce the dipole part of G.200. The light becomes internal, so the refractive index is an output; luminescence needs a Keldysh extension. The BSE sheet response in the finite-$c$ algebra of §B.10 gives exciton radiative widths with $\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (p) and $1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (s) laws.

Pitfalls

  • $\bG=0$ exchange twice. $v_0$ in the kernel plus $D^{0l}$ in E.380 doubles the L–⁠T splitting.
  • Kernel gauge. Use $\rho^*v\rho$ and $\rho^*W\rho$ inside the kernel, not the temporal-gauge $-\frac1c\bj^*\cdot D\cdot\bj$, which diverges for a static $W$.
  • Screening twice. Besides the rule shared by all topics (overview), Williams–Lax (thermal-displacement) supercells already contain the static polar fields that $D^{\rm ph}$ would add again.
  • Gauge and sum rules. For $G_0W_0$ plus static-$W$ BSE no exact Ward identity exists, so compare length and velocity gauge rather than assume they agree; TDA violates the f-sum rule. With KS velocity matrix elements, divide by KS transition energies even when $E_\lambda$ is quasiparticle; using $\omega^{\rm QP}$ there rescales oscillator strengths by $(\omega^{\rm KS}/\omega^{\rm QP})^2$.

First steps

  1. Derive (1–⁠2 weeks). The BSE $\Pi^{\rm e}$ and local-field $\bar\Pi^{\rm e}$ proposals with all prefactors; E.350 for the conserving static-SEX pair; the poles of $D_L$ (full-$L$ excitons) and $D_T$ (Hopfield polaritons).
  2. 2D optics in your code (1–⁠2 weeks). Monolayer hBN and encapsulated MoS₂ (with SOC, $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ in the sheet propagator): $r_0$ from BSE, RPA and RPA without local fields; radiative widths and polariton dispersion; length versus velocity gauge.
  3. Local fields and xc (weeks). Restore $\bG,\bG'$ and $F^{xc}$; quantify the change in $\epsilon_\infty$ and E.454. Shared with topic 5, step 3.
  4. Phonon screening (weeks). $D_{11}$ as the kernel, static, then dynamic; bulk MgO or GaN against Lyddane–Sachs–Teller; monolayer hBN directly dynamic.
  5. Phonon-assisted optics (months). The first-order $\Pi$ at finite $T$; the Fröhlich part of G.200 on GaN or MgO, then bulk hBN once short-range coupling exists.

22D beyond in-plane modes

Out-of-plane dipoles, environment and radiative widths

EPP §8 treats one planar, freestanding sheet in the electrostatic limit. The phonon kernel is your $W$: $r_0$, $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ and Coulomb truncation enter the LO dispersion exactly as they enter Rytova–Keldysh exciton binding.

Impact on 2D optics high Difficulty medium

Illustrative numbers use the primer's hBN preset ($S_{\rm uc}=5.43$ Å², $\mu=6.10$ u, $Z^*_\parallel=2.7$, $\hbar\omega_{\rm T}=170$ meV, $r_0=10$ Å). Out-of-plane numbers use placeholders, $Z^*_{zz}=0.5$ and $\hbar\omega_{\rm ZO}=0.1$ eV.

What is missing

  • Out-of-plane dipoles are dropped. The ionic vertices contain $Z^*_{zz}$, but before E.463 the “divergent local term $\sim\delta(z-z')$” is discarded and only $x,y$ are kept, so ZO-type modes (hBN ZO, MoS₂ A₂″) get no self-energy (§B.10).
  • Heights are lost. The corrected ionic vertices (§Z.3) carry atomic heights, but §8 sets them to zero: no multi-plane sheets (MoS₂, InSe), multilayers or heterostructures.
  • Strictly 2D, electrostatic, freestanding. A $\delta(z)$ bubble, $c\to\infty$ (E.460), no substrate; the primer's $\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q$ is a literature form, not EPP.
  • No 2D vertex. EPP p. 86 calls the step to 2D “trivial”, but the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex (E.415–⁠E.416) is never evaluated with the sheet propagator, whose kernel $v_Q=2\pi/Q$ comes from the $q_z$ integral. There is no 2D Fröhlich coupling, radiative width or 2D phonon polariton (§Z.1).

Why it matters

  • LO dispersion and environment. The LO slope scales with $Z^{*2}$, falls with environmental screening and grows linearly with layer number (Sohier et al. 2017). For illustrative freestanding hBN it is 0.245 eV Å, cut to 0.054 eV Å by a hypothetical non-dispersive $\epsilon_{\rm env}=4.5$, a scaling illustration only, since at 170 meV hBN sits in its own Reststrahlen band. EELS on Cu foil reports a reduced slope (Li et al. 2024). A metal is not a dielectric $\epsilon_{\rm env}$: in the perfect-conductor image model (derived in the note, $Qd\ll1$) the LO shift becomes quadratic in $Q$, so the comparison needs a metallic-substrate kernel (first step 4).
  • Out-of-plane dipoles. Omitting the 2D dipolar correction, which includes them, overestimates the 300 K hole mobility of (C₂F₂)ₙ by 99% (Deng et al. 2021, preprint numbers), a figure that does not isolate the out-of-plane part. Royo and Stengel (2021) showed that out-of-plane charges make ZO branches disperse linearly; the placeholders give −0.014 eV Å, which corrects the “$Q$-independent shift” of §B.10.
  • Radiative widths. In-plane polar modes of freestanding hBN have $\hbar\gamma_0\approx42$ μeV at $Q=0$ (primer, finite $c$); ZO modes are dark at normal incidence. Bright 2D excitons follow the same angular laws with meV-scale widths (Wang et al. 2018).
  • For your code. An untruncated supercell restores a spurious LO–⁠TO splitting (Sohier et al. 2017), the artifact that also spoils untruncated 2D BSE.

How it could enter EPP

Full sheet propagator. With $\tilde\partial=(iQ_x,iQ_y,\partial_z)$, $k^2=\epsilon\omega^2/c^2$ and $\beta=\sqrt{Q^2-k^2}$, ${\rm Re}\,\beta\gt0$:

Derived in the note from E.375

$$\begin{aligned}D^0_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf Q,\omega;z,z')&=\frac{4\pi c}{\epsilon\,\omega^2}\Big[k^2\delta_{\mu\nu}-\tilde\partial_\mu\tilde\partial'_\nu\Big]\\ &\quad\times\frac{e^{-\beta|z-z'|}}{2\beta}\end{aligned}$$

In-plane it is the primer's result; its $zz$ block holds $-\frac{4\pi c}{\epsilon\omega^2}\delta(z-z')$, EPP's divergent local term; $c\to\infty$ gives E.460. Generalizing E.458 to all components and heights:

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\mathbf Q,\omega)&=-\sum_{ab}\sum_{\alpha\beta\in\{x,y,z\}}J^{a*}_{\alpha\sigma}\\ &\quad\times D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf Q,\omega;z_a,z_b)\,J^b_{\beta\sigma'}\end{aligned}$$

with $D^{\rm e}$ from the $z$-resolved Dyson equation of EPP p. 100. Without a mirror plane (Janus, buckled) LO-like and ZO-like modes couple through the matrix Dyson equation E.398. For a planar, mirror-symmetric sheet the electrostatic limit recovers E.472 in-plane and gives

Derived in the note

$$\omega^2_{\rm ZO}(\mathbf Q)=\bar\omega^2_{\rm ZO}-\frac{2\pi Q}{S_{\rm uc}}\,|j_{z\sigma}|^2$$

where $\bar\omega_{\rm ZO}$ contains the contact shift and $Z^*_{zz}$ is the open-circuit 2D charge. At finite $c$ the ZO width grows as $x^2/\sqrt{1-x^2}$, with $\hbar\gamma_{0,z}\approx1.4$ μeV for the placeholder.

Environment, vertex, multilayers (proposals). Replace $D^0$ by the stack's dyadic Green's function, with separate s and p reflection; its static limit is the image kernel. Over a perfect metal (derived, illustrative; $d=3$ Å, $Q=0.05$ Å⁻¹) the LO shift drops from 8.0 to 2.8 meV and the ZO shift grows from −0.72 to −1.25 meV. E.416 with the sheet propagator gives a 2D Fröhlich vertex whose in-plane term is Sohier's, and a layer-resolved $W$ (as in the quantum electrostatic heterostructure, QEH, model) should reproduce the slope $\propto N$.

Pitfalls

  • Double counting. Cutoff DFPT already holds the $Q$-linear dipole terms and the ZO contact shift: subtract its 2D nonanalytic part before adding $\Omega^{\rm e}$. In a BSE $\Pi^{\rm e}$, drop the $G_\parallel=0$ exchange for all $(G_z,G_z')$, since that block is the photon line.
  • Which $Z^*_{zz}$. Open- and short-circuit charges differ by $\epsilon_{zz}$: the vacuum kernel needs the open-circuit one, a $W$ with the layer's own $z$ local fields the short-circuit one.
  • Sums and conventions. E.310 needs all $3s$ modes in the mode sum; enforce $\sum_aZ^*_{a,\alpha\beta}=0$. Gaussian $r_0=2\pi\alpha_{\rm 2D}$, or $L(\epsilon^{\rm sc}_\parallel-1)/2$ from a supercell of height $L$; take $\epsilon_{\rm env}$ of polar substrates at the phonon frequency.

First steps

  1. Derive and check (days). The sheet propagator and its limits: E.460 → E.472, the traceless bare kernel, the primer's 0.245 eV Å and 42 μeV; then the ZO branch.
  2. Freestanding benchmark (1–⁠2 weeks). Cutoff DFPT for $Z^*_\parallel$, the open-circuit $Z^*_{zz}$, $\epsilon$ and force constants; reproduce Royo–Stengel's BN and SnS₂ ZO slopes before Janus MoSSe; cross-check $r_0$ with your RPA code.
  3. Your GW code's $W$ (2–⁠4 weeks). The $G_\parallel=0$ block of the truncated $W$, projected on atomic heights and paired with the short-circuit charge; test MoS₂ (E′, A₂″).
  4. Environment (weeks). The stack's Green's function with $\epsilon(\omega)$ of SiO₂ or hBN; compare with Li et al. only through a metallic-substrate kernel.
  5. Later. InSe (quadrupoles, topic 5), bilayer hBN, then the full finite-$c$ sheet Dyson equation, which gives topic 1 the $z$-resolved photon line for out-of-plane excitons; the layered-substrate kernel of step 4 is the one interfacial pairing needs (topic 6).

3Anisotropy and hBN

Anisotropic crystals and polaritons beyond the zone centre

EPP's equations are tensors, but what it evaluates is isotropic. For hBN, the standard encapsulant, closing the gap gives the hyperbolic bands and the frequency-dependent environment that your $W$ sees.

Impact on 2D optics medium Difficulty low

hBN parameters (a widely used rounded set, partly the primer's presets; published fits differ by 5–⁠20 cm⁻¹): in-plane TO/LO 1370/1610 cm⁻¹ with $\epsilon^\infty_t=4.87$, out-of-plane 780/830 cm⁻¹ with $\epsilon^\infty_z=2.95$.

What is missing

  • The equations are tensors. The propagator is a $3\times3$ matrix, the ionic self-energy carries full $Z^*$ tensors and the electronic screening enters as $\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\bq$, so the directional LO–⁠TO term exists for $c\to\infty$ (E.454a, §B.9).
  • The evaluation is isotropic. EPP's dielectric model (§4.2.1, E.144–⁠E.165) assumes diagonal $Z^*$ and adds the cubic Lorentz field for all symmetries; in hBN that field is a tensor, and in the first-principles route it is already inside $Z^*$ (§B.3). EPP §7.1 evaluates only $\bq=0$ without electrons.
  • Nothing at finite $c$. No L–⁠T mixing, Fresnel dispersion or hyperbolic regime, no slab, no damping beyond $i\eta$, no low-symmetry crystal. §B.9 has a constant uniaxial $\epsilon$ but no $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ inside the tensor (§Z.1).

Why it matters

  • Hyperbolic bands. hBN has $\epsilon_z\lt0\lt\epsilon_t$ at 780–⁠830 cm⁻¹ (Type I) and $\epsilon_t\lt0\lt\epsilon_z$ at 1370–⁠1610 cm⁻¹ (Type II). Volume-confined modes reach $\lambda/86$ (Caldwell et al. 2014), the film dispersion tunes with thickness (Dai et al. 2014), and α-MoO₃ polaritons are up to 60 times shorter than light (Ma et al. 2018).
  • For 2D GW and BSE. hBN's effective environment $\sqrt{\epsilon_t\epsilon_z}$ is 4.74 below the 97–⁠200 meV window ($\epsilon^0$) and 3.79 above it ($\epsilon^\infty$), about 25% apart. Inside 97–⁠103 and 170–⁠200 meV $\epsilon_t\epsilon_z\lt0$ (−12.5 at 1500 cm⁻¹), so $W(\mathbf Q,\omega)$ has a hyperbolic-polariton continuum instead of a real effective $\epsilon$ (derived in the note).
  • Oblique modes (derived in the note). With $Z^*_t=2.80|e|$ and $Z^*_z=0.73|e|$ from the splittings, the LO-like modes for $q\gg q_c$ ($1.9\times10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹ in-plane) depend on the angle θ to the $c$ axis (T marks a TO endpoint):

How it could enter EPP

Closed-form propagator. At $\bG=\bG'=0$, E.380 inverts algebraically, with $\hat\epsilon=1+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}(\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj})$ and the ionic part from E.385:

Derived in the note; proposal for EPP

4Electron self-energies

Electron self-energies with photon and phonon exchange

EPP dresses photons and phonons but never the electron. A $GW$ gap is a clamped-nuclei gap, and the lattice part EPP could add to your $W$ is one pole per polar mode in its head.

Impact on 2D optics medium Difficulty medium

What is missing

  • No electron Dyson equation. EPP solves three bosonic Dyson equations: for the total field $D$, the electron-dressed field $D^{\rm e}$ and the phonons $F$, whose self-energy is $\Omega^{\rm e}=-J^*D^{\rm e}J$ (E.398–⁠E.399). Electrons appear only in bubbles, with no $\Sigma$ or dressed $G$ (§X.2).
  • No Fan–Migdal, no Debye–Waller. Nothing matches Giustino's electron–phonon self-energy (G.151–⁠G.161): no zero-point renormalization (ZPR), lifetimes or spectral functions (§A.7, §A.8). EPP's electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex appears only in the phonon self-energy (§B.8).
  • Linear coupling, zero-temperature bosons. The coupling is linear in the displacements on a frozen potential, so there is no Debye–Waller term. EPP's zero-temperature rule for boson lines, $n_B=0$ (E.246–⁠E.250), fails for $\Sigma$, whose numerators carry phonon occupations.

Why it matters

  • Magnitudes. Diamond's direct-gap ZPR is 628 meV with $GW$-corrected couplings, more than 40% above DFT (Antonius et al. 2014). ZPRs often exceed 0.3 eV and reach 0.7 eV in light-element materials, and in infrared-active crystals the nonadiabatic Fröhlich term gives the majority for many materials (Miglio et al. 2020).
  • Fröhlich scale. $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$ is about −2.5 meV for the conduction-band minimum of GaAs and −40 meV for that of GaN (model $\alpha\approx0.07$ and 0.44; §A.8); for the gap of zinc-blende GaN, Nery and Allen (2016) find a Fröhlich correction of −0.05 of −0.15 eV; it is an analytic small-$q$ correction to an adiabatic grid result, not the same quantity as $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$. Lattice screening in $W$ can lower $GW$ gaps by more than 15% in highly polar compounds (Botti and Marques 2013), although Lambrecht et al. (2017) object that the polar term was not confined in $q$.
  • Light cone. In GaAs the cone ($q_c\approx6\times10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹ against a polaron wave vector of 0.025 Å⁻¹) holds about $1.5\times10^{-3}$ of the Fröhlich-shift integrand.
  • For your code. Without the ZPR, $GW$ agreement at the 0.1–⁠0.5 eV level can be fortuitous, for instance in hBN. Temperature-dependent BSE needs electron–phonon energies and widths. In 2D the adiabatic Fröhlich integral diverges logarithmically while the nonadiabatic one converges (derived in the note).

How it could enter EPP

Electron Dyson equation (proposal). E.237 gives a paramagnetic self-energy from two $-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA$ vertices and an $A^2$ tadpole, $G=G^{\rm KS}+G^{\rm KS}(\Sigma^{\rm para}+\Sigma^{\rm dia}-v^{xc})G$. The temporal-gauge kernel differs from Coulomb off shell; on shell, with complete band sums and a local Hamiltonian (nonlocal pseudopotentials and scissors break it), para plus dia is gauge invariant up to a band-independent constant (derived in the note). Resumming the ions gives exactly (derived in the note) $D=D^{\rm e}+D^{\rm ph}$ with $D^{\rm ph}=-D^{\rm e}JFJ^\dagger D^{\rm e}$: screened vertices at both ends, as in G.156. The note proposes the Coulomb gauge:

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}\Sigma^{\rm EPP}&\simeq\Sigma^{\rm L}[W]\\ &\quad+\Sigma^{\rm T}\big[D^{t}-D^{0t}\big]\\ &\quad+\Sigma^{\rm dia,T}\big[D^{t}-D^{0t}\big]\\ W&=\frac{4\pi}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\end{aligned}$$

The transverse terms are of order $(v/c)^2\approx10^{-7}$–$10^{-5}$ for electronic exchange and $(q_c/q)^2\approx10^{-6}$–$10^{-4}$ at the polaron scale; the vacuum $D^{0t}$ is already in the electron mass. The lattice adds to the head of $W$

Derived in the note; LST check

$$\begin{aligned}W^{\rm ph}(\bq,\omega)&=\big[W^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\big]^2\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}F_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)\\ &\quad\times\frac{(\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma)(\bq\cdot\bj_{\sigma'})^*}{2\Omega_{\rm uc}\sqrt{\omega_\sigma\omega_{\sigma'}}}\end{aligned}$$

For one isotropic mode this is Fröhlich's $|g|^2$ times the LO propagator, consistent with the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller (LST) form, and $\Sigma^{\rm L}$ gives G.157 with the vertex of E.448a. What is new: $\omega_{\rm LO}$ is an output (E.454), the screening $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$ is dynamical, so doped crystals get the $L_\pm$ modes of E.453, and the residue $\omega_\sigma/\tilde\omega$ of $F$ fixes the normalization (§X.2). Bose factors then enter only in $\Sigma$, as in G.158. A real Debye–Waller term needs a coupling second order in the displacements (G.159), or its rigid-ion form (G.195–⁠198) built from a short-range vertex such as that of topic 5; neither is in EPP.

Pitfalls

  • Reference and screening. Subtract $v^{xc}$ with a KS reference, and never add Fan–Migdal to a $W$ screened with $\epsilon^0$.
  • The polar vertex. Subtracting $g^{\rm L}$ from a DFPT $g$ also removes the short–long cross term; keep it explicitly or from DFPT.
  • Vertices and screening. Fan–Migdal takes two screened vertices, the phonon self-energy one bare and one screened. EPP's RPA head without local fields differs from $GW$ and DFPT screening.
  • Convergence. In 3D the $1/q^2$ singularity needs nonadiabatic denominators and analytic small-$q$ integration (error $\propto1/N_q$); in 2D use truncated Coulomb and integrate $Q\lesssim\epsilon_{\rm env}/r_0$ analytically.

First steps

  1. Derive (1–⁠2 weeks). All prefactors and the $v^{xc}$ subtraction; the $D^{\rm ph}$ identity and on-shell gauge equivalence; $c\to\infty$ gives G.157 with E.448a.
  2. Analytic benchmark (days). Isotropic Fröhlich crystals (GaAs, GaN, MgO): $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$, the §A.8 rate, Bose factors; finite-$c$ corrections at most $10^{-3}$.
  3. 2D test (2–⁠4 weeks). A head-only $W^{\rm ph}(Q,\omega)$ in your $GW$ code from E.469–⁠E.472 with $\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q$, on monolayer hBN: adiabatic divergence versus nonadiabatic convergence, $|g(Q)|$ against Sohier et al.   4. Doped crystals (weeks). Dynamical $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}$ with $L_\pm$ modes in n-GaAs or gated MoS₂, compared with Kandolf et al. (2022).
  5. Toward BSE (months). A short-range channel with a Debye–Waller term, a cumulant, then energies and widths for temperature-dependent BSE (topic 1) and, in Nambu form, pairing (topic 6).

5Short-range coupling

Short-range coupling and higher multipoles

EPP keeps only the polar channel. In a nonpolar crystal its electron–phonon coupling is zero, and in 2D, where short-range coupling is leading order, EPP §8 alone cannot give carrier or exciton scattering.

Impact on 2D optics high Difficulty high

What is missing

  • No deformation-potential vertex. EPP derives a rigid-ion vertex $g^0$ early on, then drops it. In its Lagrangian (E.134) the electrons feel a frozen crystal potential, and the ions reach the field only through the Born-dipole current $\frac1c\dot{\bp}_{ia}\cdot\bA(\bR^0_{ia})$ with $\bp=\hat Z^*\bQ$ (the site $\bR^0_{ia}$ as corrected in §Z.3; the draft prints $\bA(\bR^0_i)$); no term contains both the electron coordinate and the displacement (§B.2). In Si, diamond or graphene ($Z^*=0$) the coupling vanishes; non-polar optical modes (MoS₂ A₁′) and the acoustic deformation potential are absent.
  • Only the dipole moment. The ionic vertex E.295′ is the zeroth moment of the current a moving atom carries. Optical modes get the full Fröhlich term; acoustic modes keep only the internal-strain dipole, without the clamped-ion (quadrupole) response. In GaAs that term alone is 0.6–⁠0.9 C/m² along [111] for a Kleinman (internal-strain) parameter of 0.5–⁠0.75 (unverified range), against a measured 0.19 C/m² (illustrative).
  • Force constants, local fields. EPP does not say what to subtract from DFPT's dipolar and multipolar tails, and its $\Pi^{\rm e}$ has neither local fields nor $f^{xc}$.

Why it matters

  • Transport. The 300 K mobility of n-type monolayer MoS₂, about 410 cm²/Vs, is set by intra- and intervalley deformation potentials and the Fröhlich interaction (Kaasbjerg et al. 2012). Above the L-valley threshold, hot electrons in GaAs also relax by intervalley scattering, computed ab initio by Sjakste, Vast and Tyuterev (2007).
  • 2D power counting (derived in the note). The sheet dipole vertex $g\sim2\pi e^2Z^*u/(1+r_0Q)$ is $O(Q^0)$, like the optical deformation potential, while piezoelectric and quadrupole terms are $O(Q)$, like the acoustic one: in 2D short-range coupling is leading order. In MoS₂ ($r_0\approx40$ Å, illustrative), at the thermal-carrier and phonon-emission wave vectors, 0.06–⁠0.12 Å⁻¹, $1/(1+r_0Q)\approx0.17$–$0.29$.
  • For your code. Exciton linewidths in W-based TMDs involve dark K–K′ and K–Λ excitons, hence phonons deep in the zone (Selig et al. 2016, not re-verified), as does phonon-assisted absorption. Reconciling DFPT's local-field screening with EPP's is the $\epsilon_M$ versus $\bar v$ split of your BSE code.

How it could enter EPP

Route A unfreezes $V_{\rm cr}$ so that $Z^*$, quadrupoles, $\epsilon^\infty$ and the short-range vertex emerge from a $\bG\bG'$-resolved $\Pi^{\rm e}$ with $f^{xc}$: Hedin–Baym (G.121) in vector form, exact but expensive. Route B keeps EPP's structure and adds DFPT inputs under subtraction rules (proposals P0–P5 in the note). The polarization becomes macroscopically proper,

Proposal (P0a)

$$\begin{gathered}\bar\chi=\chi^0+\chi^0\big(\bar v+f^{xc}\big)\bar\chi\\ \bar v_{\bG}=v(\bq+\bG)\,(1-\delta_{\bG0})\end{gathered}$$

so E.350 gives $\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}=e^2\bar\chi_{00}$ and EPP regenerates DFPT's $\epsilon^\infty=\epsilon_M$. The ionic current carries its multipoles:

Proposal (P1)

$$\begin{aligned}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\tilde\bq)&=\sum_{a\mu}\tilde Z_{a\alpha\mu}(\tilde\bq)\,\frac{e_{a\mu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{-i\tilde\bq\cdot\br^0_a}}{\sqrt{m_a}}\\ \tilde Z_{a\alpha\mu}&=Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}-\tfrac i2\tilde q_\beta\,\mathcal Q_{a,\alpha\beta\mu}+O(\tilde q^2)\\ \tilde\bq&=\bq+\bG\end{aligned}$$

In P2 the bare phonons are DFPT minus exactly what EPP regenerates (E.454a with the multipolar charges), complete through $O(q)$. The short-range vertex is matched to DFPT in the static limit,

Proposal (P3)

$$\begin{gathered}g^{\rm tot}(\omega)=g^{\mathcal S}+\delta g^{\mathcal S}(\omega)+g^{\mathcal L,{\rm dyn}}(\omega)\\ g^{\mathcal S}\equiv g^{\rm DFPT}-g^{\mathcal L,{\rm dyn}}\big|_{c\to\infty,\,\omega=0}\end{gathered}$$

so the total equals the DFPT vertex at $c\to\infty$, $\omega=0$ by construction. Pitfalls

  • Inconsistent $\epsilon$. Subtracting $g^{\mathcal L}$ with DFPT's $\epsilon$ and regenerating it with EPP's leaves a Fröhlich tail $\propto(1/\epsilon_{\rm EPP}-1/\epsilon_{\rm DFPT})/q$. Local fields lower the RPA head by about 10% in sp semiconductors (illustrative) and ALDA raises it.
  • Double screening. DFPT's $g$ is already screened at $\bG=0$; routed through $D^{\rm e}$ it is screened twice (G.145 versus G.146).
  • Routing. Every nonanalytic $\bG=0$ charge goes through the longitudinal photon; keep the filtered $\bG$ sum so nothing jumps at the zone boundary.
  • Phases and conventions. Without the basis phase, GaAs gets a spurious 0.6–⁠1.8 C/m² acoustic coupling (illustrative); a mismatched quadrupole convention flips the dipole–quadrupole interference.

6Ordered phases

Superconductivity, charge density waves and condensates

EPP as written cannot produce any ordered phase: it is a normal-state theory, and its static lattice self-energy only stabilises. It is, however, a natural language for pairing in polar and dilute superconductors.

Impact on 2D optics low Difficulty high

PhaseEPP hasEPP lacks
SuperconductivityThe polar part of $D=D^{\rm e}+D^{\rm ph}$, dynamically screened$G$ with anomalous (Nambu–Gor'kov) parts; short-range $g$
CDW, ferroelectric$F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F$ (E.398)Short-range electron–lattice coupling, the $\bG\neq0$ dipole sum, anharmonicity

What is missing

  • No fermion propagator. Electrons appear only as the RPA bubble (E.282–⁠E.283; §B.5): no $G$, no $\Sigma$, no pair amplitude, no gap equation.
  • Only the polar channel. Ions couple through $\frac1c\dot{\bp}\cdot\bA$ (E.134–⁠E.135) on a frozen potential and a harmonic lattice; deformation-potential coupling, which dominates $\lambda$ in conventional superconductors and CDW metals, is absent (§B.8).
  • Only hardening (derived in the note). In RPA $W^{\rm e}(\bq,0)\gt0$, so EPP's lattice Dyson equation (E.452–⁠E.454) can never drive $\omega^2$ to zero. Cochran softening (the polar TO frequency driven to zero) needs the $\bG\neq0$ dipole sum, which the Dyson route drops.

Why it matters

  • Dilute polar superconductors. SrTiO₃ superconducts down to $5.5\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³, where $E_F\approx1.1$ meV (Lin et al. 2013), far below its polar phonons: Migdal's condition and the scale separation behind $\mu^*$ (G.204) both fail. LO phonons (Gor'kov 2016), plasmons (Ruhman and Lee 2016) and soft TO modes have all been proposed.
  • Interfacial coupling. Monolayer FeSe on SrTiO₃ opens gaps near 70 K (bulk FeSe about 8 K), with replica bands about 100 meV below the main bands (Lee et al. 2014). Faeth et al. (2021) find $\lambda=0.19\pm0.02$; in model RPA the FeSe electrons screen the coupling almost completely (Zhou and Millis 2017).
  • Light cone. Pairing samples $2k_F\sim0.2$–$2$ Å⁻¹, so the Fermi-surface fraction inside the cone is $2.5\times10^{-11}$–$2.5\times10^{-7}$ in 3D (derived in the note).
  • For your code. The Coulomb part of pairing (G.209) is a Fermi-surface matrix element of your full $W_{\bG\bG'}(\bq,i\nu)$, with $W^{\rm ph}$ added to the head only. Excitonic insulators are BSE problems with an anomalous self-energy.

How it could enter EPP

Pairing kernel from $D$ (proposal). In Nambu form EPP's current vertex enters as $\tau_0$ and its charge and diamagnetic vertices as $\tau_3$; in Coulomb-gauge form (exact for $c\to\infty$) the pairing interaction is $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$, with an attractive lattice part:

Derived in the note

$$W^{\rm ph}(\bq,i\nu)=-\frac{4\pi}{q^2}\,\frac{\omega_p^2/\epsilon^2}{\nu^2+\tilde\omega_{\rm LO}^2}$$

The linearized gap equation G.209 then holds with

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}&\lambda(i\nu)-N_FV\\ &\quad\to-N_F\big\langle W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}\big\rangle(i\nu)\end{aligned}$$

where $\lambda$, $V$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle$ are matrix elements between the paired states $n\bk$ and $n'\bk'$, using the full $W(\bq,i\nu)$ without splitting. This is the dielectric formulation of pairing that Takada (1980) applied to n-SrTiO₃; EPP would add first-principles $Z^*$, $\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,i\nu)$ and anisotropy, but still lacks the short-range $g$ and vertex corrections for $\omega_{\rm LO}/E_F\gtrsim1$. A layered substrate Green's function turns the FeSe/SrTiO₃ coupling into a Fuchs–Kliewer (substrate surface-phonon) pole of $W$.

Meissner kernel, soft modes, condensates. With Nambu propagators $\Pi^t_{jj}(\bq\to0,0)\propto n_s/m$, so E.279 must replace E.281, with Nambu's Ward identity in the longitudinal sector. Reinstating $g^0$ (E.107) for soft modes (proposal) admits only two consistent partitions, screened-adiabatic or fully bare. And E.281 and E.311 set $\Pi_{jj}(\bq,0)=0$ at every $\bq$: this enforces the uniform no-go theorem (Andolina et al. 2019) but deletes the electrons' static orbital response, which a finite-$q$ photon condensate needs ($\chi_{\rm orb}\gt1/4\pi$ in 3D; Andolina et al. 2020).

Pitfalls

  • Double counting. No empirical $\mu^*$ on top of $W^{\rm e}$, and no separate $v$ besides $D^{0l}$.
  • Normal self-energy. Bare bands with the full normal part, or $GW$ bands with only $W^{\rm ph}$ and the anomalous part of $W^{\rm e}$.
  • Gauge. $\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$ is exact longitudinally at all $q$ but transversely only as $q\to0$; truncated band sums fake a Drude weight.
  • Matsubara. $T_c\approx0.1$–$0.4$ K with $\nu$ up to 0.1–⁠0.5 eV needs $5\times10^2$–$10^4$ positive frequencies: use a sparse basis.

First steps

  1. Derive (1–⁠2 weeks). Nambu vertices; reduction to G.208–⁠G.210 with $|g^{\rm L}|^2$ (E.448a); Morel–Anderson (the $\mu^*$ pseudopotential) only for $\omega_{\rm pl}\gg\omega_{\rm LO}$; the Meissner kernel with the Ward identity.
  2. Benchmark (2–⁠3 weeks). Reproduce Takada (1980) and Gastiasoro et al. (2019) from EPP's single propagator, then add $Z^*$ and $\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,i\nu)$ from your RPA code; for SrTiO₃ include the soft TO₁ mode (about 1–⁠2 meV) and LO modes near 21, 59 and 98 meV.
  3. Interface (1–⁠2 months). From Zhou and Millis, $W(\mathbf Q,i\nu;0,0)$ from §B.10 plus a layered substrate, compared with Faeth et al.
  4. Soft modes (about 1 week). Cochran softening for SrTiO₃ or BaTiO₃ from the $\bG\neq0$ dipole sum, a consistency check.
  5. Polaritons (months). MoSe₂ in a cavity with the $\Pi^{\rm BSE}$ of topic 1: no $q=0$ instability with $\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$.

Relation to the corrections already made

The six notes build on the corrected draft of §Z.3: the basis phase of the Born-dipole vertex carries the atomic heights used in 2D and removes the spurious acoustic coupling that the short-range and anisotropy work must avoid, the general-$q$ reading of E.410 (E.410a–h) is the starting point of the anisotropic propagator, and the restored factor $e$ in the continuity equation (E.428′) underlies the gauge arguments of the self-energy and pairing proposals. Those corrections fix what EPP already says, and E.410a–h complete its §7.1 as a proposed new §7.1.1; the topics here extend what it covers.
