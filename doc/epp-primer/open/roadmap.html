Electrons, phonons and photons: seven open topics

Seven topics remain open in the EPP formalism, from excitons in the photon self-energy to plasmon–phonon hybrids and ordered phases. The table rates each by impact on 2D optics and by difficulty and names a first step; each section says what is missing, why it matters, how it could enter EPP and where to start. EPP today is a normal-state theory of three bosonic propagators (field, electron-dressed field, phonons) with an RPA electron bubble and Born-charge ions. Equations tagged “proposal” are sketches, not derived results.

The seven topics at a glance

Topics 1 to 6 run from what sits closest to an RPA, GW or BSE optics code (optics, 2D sheets, anisotropy), through the electron and lattice ingredients those results need (self-energies, short-range coupling), to the most distant topic (ordered phases); the numbers follow closeness to your code, not the order of work. Topic 7, the free carriers of doped crystals, was added last; by the same measure it sits between topics 5 and 6. The table rates each topic and the paragraph below it suggests where to start.

Where to start. Three steps run in your own RPA, $GW$ or BSE code and need no theory beyond the notes: $\varepsilon_{\mathrm M}(\hat\bq)$ along the a and c axes and at 45° with and without local fields (topic 3, step 2, 1–2 weeks); $r_0$ from BSE, RPA and RPA without local fields, plus length versus velocity gauge, for monolayer hBN and encapsulated MoS₂ (topic 1, step 2, 1–2 weeks); and the $\bG_\parallel=0$ block of your truncated $W$ projected on atomic heights (topic 2, step 3, 2–4 weeks). One pitfall recurs in every topic. DFPT and $GW$ inputs already contain parts of what EPP regenerates: the $\bG=0$ nonanalytic LO–TO term of the force constants, $\varepsilon_\infty$ and $\mathbf Z^*$ with local fields and xc, the long-range vertex $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$. Take bare phonons from short-range force constants and subtract exactly what EPP re-adds, with the same $\varepsilon_\infty$ and $\mathbf Z^*$, or LO–TO and the Fröhlich tail count twice. 1Optics beyond RPA

Excitons, local fields, xc and phonon-assisted absorption

EPP's photon self-energy is an independent-particle bubble. With your BSE response in its place, one propagator would give exciton polaritons, radiative widths and $\varepsilon(\omega)$ from the infrared to the ultraviolet.

Impact on 2D optics high Difficulty medium

What is missing

  • An independent-particle bubble. EPP's photon self-energy (E.278–E.279, §B.5) is a bubble plus a diamagnetic term built from single-particle energies and current vertices. With no electron–hole ladder there are no excitons; with no self-energy insertions, no $GW$ gap.
  • No local fields, no xc. From the full propagator (E.380) on, only the macroscopic element $\bG=\bG'=0$ is kept, so EPP's electronic screening $W^{\rme}$, its $\varepsilon_\infty$ and the 2D $r_0$ come from the head of $\varepsilon^{\rme}_{\mathrm{RPA}}$, without local fields or $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$. DFPT's $\varepsilon^{\rme}_{\mathrm{RPA+xc}}$ has both (§A.3).
  • No phonon-assisted processes. Mixed electron–ion bubbles vanish at lowest order (E.316). Phonon-assisted absorption and exciton–phonon coupling would be diagrams of $\Pi^{\rme}$ with an internal $D^{\ph}=D-D^{\rme}$ line, the phonon part of the field propagator; they are not derived (§A.9, §X.2), EPP's zero-temperature rule for boson lines would drop their phonon occupations, and from EPP alone they would be polar only (topic 5).
  • 2D. The sheet bubble gives Rytova–Keldysh screening (E.469–E.470), and §B.10 binds a model exciton with it, but EPP's own response has no ladder, so neither intralayer nor interlayer excitons.

Why it matters

  • Excitons dominate. Monolayer WS₂ has an exciton binding energy of 0.32 eV and a non-hydrogenic Rydberg series (Chernikov et al. 2014).
  • Linewidths are meV. hBN-encapsulated MoS₂ shows about 2 meV FWHM at 4 K (Cadiz et al. 2017), radiative plus exciton–phonon. EPP's propagator has the ingredients for the radiative channel and the Fröhlich part; §B.10 evaluates the radiative widths of a sheet's polar phonons and the 2D Fröhlich coupling of its carriers, but neither for excitons (topic 2). The Fröhlich part is weak in a MoS₂-like sheet ($r_0^{\ion}\approx0.5$ Å with §B.10's preset, derived in the note).
  • Inputs propagate. A 20% error in $\varepsilon_\infty$ means about 40% in band renormalization and 10% in phonon frequencies (Giustino §XII; §A.9). The zero-point renormalization of the gap (topic 4) shifts every absorption edge.
  • The kernel stays electrostatic. A 2 eV exciton in vacuum has $q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega)\approx10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹, while an exciton of radius about 1 nm (illustrative) samples $Q_\parallel\sim0.1$ Å⁻¹ (§X.1). Radiative decay and polaritons live in the transverse sector of $D$.

How it could enter EPP

Photon-irreducible BSE as $\Pi^{\rme}$. With transitions $t=(n\bk,m\bk+\bq)$, current vertices $j^{\,t}_\alpha$ and $L^0_t$ as in E.282:

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\mathrm{e,BSE}}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)&=-\frac{2}{c\OVSC}\sum_{tt'}j^{\,t}_{\alpha}\,\big[\bar L_{tt'}(\bq,\omega)\\ &\qquad\quad-\bar L_{tt'}(\bq,0)\big]\,\big(j^{\,t'}_{\beta}\big)^*\\ \bar L&=L^0+L^0\big(2\bar\Xi^{\mathrm x}-\Xi^{\mathrm d}\big)\bar L\end{aligned}$$

The kernels take EPP's scalar-plus-vector form (E.317–E.319): exchange $\bar\Xi^{\mathrm x}=\rho^*\bar v\rho$ between pair densities, with $\bG\neq0$ only (in a $z$-resolved sheet the whole $\bG_\parallel=0$ block, topic 2), and direct $\Xi^{\mathrm d}=\rho^*W\rho$ with $W=e^2D_{\rho\rho}$, the screened Coulomb sector of the total $D$; with both zero this is E.281. The $\omega=0$ subtraction replaces the diamagnetic term exactly only if the static BSE obeys the same Ward identity, so at $\bq\to0$ the note uses the length form $\frac{\omega^2}{4\pi c}[\varepsilon^{\bar L}(\omega)-1]$, which BSE codes compute. In E.380 for a cubic crystal (anisotropic ones mix L and T, topic 3), $\varepsilon^{\bar L,\mathrm L}=0$ gives the longitudinal excitons and $c^2q^2=\omega^2\varepsilon^{\bar L,\mathrm T}$ Hopfield's polaritons; the exciton L–T splitting comes from the removed $\bG=0$ exchange, which $D^{0\mathrm L}$ restores.

Phonon screening and local fields (proposal). The Coulomb sector of the total $D$ is $W=W^{\rme}+W^{\rme}\Pi^{\ion}_{\rho\rho}W/e^2$ (cf. G.104–105), so the proposed direct kernel $\Xi^{\mathrm d}=\rho^*W\rho$ would contain Fröhlich phonon screening (Filip et al. 2021), dynamically; EPP itself has no BSE kernel, only this screening inside $D$. For a static sheet, with the ions included:

Derived in the note from E.470–E.472

$$\begin{gathered}\varepsilon^{\mathrm{2D}}_{\mathrm s}(Q_\parallel)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+\big(r_0^{\rme}+r_0^{\ion}\big)Q_\parallel\\ r_0^{\ion}=\frac{2\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{\OS\,M_{\mathrm r}\,\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2}\end{gathered}$$

With §B.10's presets, $r_0^{\ion}$ is 2.9 Å against $r_0^{\rme}=10$ Å for hBN (+29%) and 0.5 Å against 40 Å for a MoS₂-like sheet (about 1%). In monolayer hBN ($E_{\mathrm b}$ of order 2 eV, illustrative, against $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}\lesssim0.2$ eV) the ions cannot follow, and the static +29% badly overestimates. Local fields and xc would enter through a macroscopically irreducible $\bar\Pi^{\rme}=\Pi^{\rme,0}+\Pi^{\rme,0}(\bar D^0+D^{\mathrm{xc}})\bar\Pi^{\rme}$, with $\bar D^0$ the bare line without its $\bG=0$ element and $D^{\mathrm{xc}}$ the xc kernel written as a line (proposal).

Phonon-assisted optics (proposal). At first order in $D^{\ph}$, $\Pi^{\rme}$ gains two self-energy diagrams and one vertex correction with the vertex of E.415–E.416, (B8.6) in the primer, whose golden-rule cuts should reproduce the dipole part of G.200. The light becomes internal, so the refractive index is an output; luminescence needs a Keldysh extension. The BSE sheet response in the finite-$c$ algebra of §B.10 gives exciton radiative widths with $\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (p) and $1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (s) laws, where $x=cQ_\parallel/(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\omega)$.

Pitfalls

  • $\bG=0$ exchange twice. The $\bG=0$ term $v(\bq)$ in the kernel plus $D^{0\mathrm L}$ in E.380 doubles the L–T splitting.
  • Kernel gauge. Use $\rho^*v\rho$ and $\rho^*W\rho$ inside the kernel, not the temporal-gauge $-\frac1c\bj^*\cdot D\cdot\bj$, which diverges for a static $W$.
  • Screening twice. Besides the rule shared by all topics (overview), Williams–Lax (thermal-displacement) supercells already contain the static polar fields that $D^{\ph}$ would add again.
  • Gauge and sum rules. For $G_0W_0$ plus static-$W$ BSE no exact Ward identity exists, so compare length and velocity gauge rather than assume they agree; TDA violates the f-sum rule. With KS velocity matrix elements, divide by KS transition energies even when the exciton energies are quasiparticle; using $\omega^{\mathrm{qp}}$ there rescales oscillator strengths by $(\omega^{\mathrm{KS}}/\omega^{\mathrm{qp}})^2$.

First steps

  1. Derive (1–2 weeks). The BSE $\Pi^{\rme}$ and local-field $\bar\Pi^{\rme}$ proposals with all prefactors; E.350 for the conserving static-SEX pair; the poles of $D^{\mathrm L}$ (full-$L$ excitons) and $D^{\mathrm T}$ (Hopfield polaritons).
  2. 2D optics in your code (1–2 weeks). Monolayer hBN and encapsulated MoS₂ (with SOC, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$ in the sheet propagator): $r_0$ from BSE, RPA and RPA without local fields; radiative widths and polariton dispersion; length versus velocity gauge.
  3. Local fields and xc (weeks). Restore $\bG,\bG'$ and $D^{\mathrm{xc}}$; quantify the change in $\varepsilon_\infty$ and E.454. Shared with topic 5, step 3.
  4. Phonon screening (weeks). $W=e^2D_{\rho\rho}$ as the kernel, static, then dynamic; bulk MgO or GaN against Lyddane–Sachs–Teller; monolayer hBN directly dynamic.
  5. Phonon-assisted optics (months). The first-order $\Pi^{\rme}$ at finite $T$; the Fröhlich part of G.200 on GaN or MgO, then bulk hBN once short-range coupling exists.

22D beyond in-plane modes

Out-of-plane dipoles, environment and radiative widths

EPP §8 treats one planar, freestanding sheet in the electrostatic limit. The phonon kernel is your $W$: $r_0$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$ and Coulomb truncation enter the LO dispersion exactly as they enter Rytova–Keldysh exciton binding.

Impact on 2D optics high Difficulty medium

Illustrative numbers use the hBN preset of §B.10 ($\OS=5.43$ Å², $M_{\mathrm r}=6.10$ amu, $Z^*_{xx}=2.7$, $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}=170$ meV, $r_0=10$ Å). Out-of-plane numbers use placeholders, $Z^*_{zz}=0.5$ and $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{ZO}}=0.1$ eV.

What is missing

  • Out-of-plane dipoles are dropped. The ionic vertices contain $Z^*_{zz}$, but before E.463 the “divergent local term $\sim\delta(z-z')$” is discarded and only $x,y$ are kept, so ZO-type modes (hBN ZO, MoS₂ A₂″) get no self-energy. §B.10 shows the contact term and its shift at Γ, but goes no further.
  • Heights are lost. With each dipole at its atom (the correction in §B.10's footnote), every atom keeps its height $z_\kappa$, but EPP §8 and §B.10 use the planar form, valid for $Q_\parallel|z_\kappa|\ll1$: no multi-plane sheets (MoS₂, InSe) at short wavelength, no multilayers or heterostructures.
  • Strictly 2D, electrostatic, freestanding. A $\delta(z)$ bubble, $c\to\infty$ (E.460), no substrate. The primer adds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}+r_0Q_\parallel$ (B10.8), a literature form, and the finite-$c$ sheet line (B10.27), in vacuum or a uniform medium; neither is in EPP.
  • No electrodynamic 2D vertex. EPP p. 86 calls the step to 2D “trivial”, but its electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex (E.415–E.416) is never evaluated with the sheet propagator, whose kernel $v^{\mathrm{2D}}=2\pi e^2/Q_\parallel$ comes from the $q_z$ integral. §B.10 gives the electrostatic 2D Fröhlich coupling, the radiative widths and the onset of the guided 2D polariton, not the finite-$c$ vertex.

Why it matters

  • LO dispersion and environment. The LO slope scales with $Z^{*2}$, falls with environmental screening and grows linearly with layer number (Sohier et al. 2017). For illustrative freestanding hBN it is 0.245 eV Å (§B.10), cut to 0.054 eV Å by a hypothetical non-dispersive $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}=4.5$, a scaling illustration only, since at 170 meV hBN sits in its own Reststrahlen band. EELS on Cu foil reports a reduced slope (Li et al. 2024). A metal is not a dielectric $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$: in the perfect-conductor image model (derived in the note, $Q_\parallel d\ll1$) the LO shift becomes quadratic in $Q_\parallel$, so the comparison needs a metallic-substrate kernel (first step 4).
  • Out-of-plane dipoles. Omitting the 2D dipolar correction, which includes them, overestimates the 300 K hole mobility of (C₂F₂)ₙ by 99% (Deng et al. 2021, preprint numbers), a figure that does not isolate the out-of-plane part. Royo and Stengel (2021) showed that out-of-plane charges make ZO branches disperse linearly; the placeholders give −0.014 eV Å, on top of the contact shift at Γ that §B.10 describes.
  • Radiative widths. In-plane polar modes of freestanding hBN have $\hbar\gamma^{\mathrm{rad}}=42$ μeV (full width) at Γ (§B.10, finite $c$); ZO modes are dark at normal incidence. Bright 2D excitons follow the same angular laws with meV-scale widths (Wang et al. 2018).
  • For your code. An untruncated supercell restores a spurious LO–TO splitting (Sohier et al. 2017), the artifact that also spoils untruncated 2D BSE.

How it could enter EPP

Full sheet propagator. With $\nabla=(\ii Q_x,\ii Q_y,\partial_z)$ in the mixed representation of §B.10, and $\zeta=\sqrt{Q_\parallel^2-\varepsilon\omega^2/c^2}$, $\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta\gt0$:

Derived in the note from E.375

$$\begin{aligned}D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ_\parallel,\omega;z,z')&=\frac{4\pi c}{\varepsilon\,\omega^2}\Big[\frac{\varepsilon\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\nabla_\alpha\nabla'_\beta\Big]\\ &\quad\times\frac{\ee^{-\zeta|z-z'|}}{2\zeta}\end{aligned}$$

In-plane at $z=z'=0$ it is (B10.27); its $zz$ block holds $-\frac{4\pi c}{\varepsilon\omega^2}\delta(z-z')$, EPP's divergent local term; $c\to\infty$ gives E.460. Generalizing E.458 to all components and heights, with $J^\kappa_{\alpha\nu}$ the part of the sheet's dynamic vertex (B10.17) carried by atom $\kappa$:

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'}(\bQ_\parallel,\omega)&=-\sum_{\kappa\kappa'}\sum_{\alpha\beta\in\{x,y,z\}}J^{\kappa*}_{\alpha\nu}\\ &\quad\times D^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ_\parallel,\omega;z_\kappa,z_{\kappa'})\,J^{\kappa'}_{\beta\nu'}\end{aligned}$$

with $D^{\rme}$ from the $z$-resolved Dyson equation of EPP p. 100, (B10.18). Without a mirror plane (Janus, buckled) LO-like and ZO-like modes couple through the matrix Dyson equation E.398. For a planar, mirror-symmetric sheet the electrostatic limit recovers E.472 in-plane and gives

Derived in the note

$$\omega^2_{\mathrm{ZO}}(\bQ_\parallel)=\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm{ZO}}-\frac{2\pi Q_\parallel}{\OS}\,|j_{z\nu}|^2$$

where $\bar\omega_{\mathrm{ZO}}$ contains the contact shift and $Z^*_{zz}$ is the open-circuit 2D charge. At finite $c$ the ZO width grows as $x^2/\sqrt{1-x^2}$, with $x=cQ_\parallel/(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\omega)$ and $\hbar\gamma^{\mathrm{rad}}_{0,z}\approx1.4$ μeV for the placeholder.

Environment, vertex, multilayers (proposals). Replace $D^0$ by the stack's dyadic Green's function, with separate s and p reflection; its static limit is the image kernel. Over a perfect metal (derived, illustrative; $d=3$ Å, $Q_\parallel=0.05$ Å⁻¹) the LO shift drops from 8.0 to 2.8 meV and the ZO shift grows from −0.72 to −1.25 meV. E.416 with the sheet propagator gives a 2D Fröhlich vertex whose in-plane, electrostatic term is Sohier's and §B.10's, and a layer-resolved $W$ (as in the quantum electrostatic heterostructure, QEH, model) should reproduce the slope proportional to the number of layers.

Pitfalls

  • Double counting. Cutoff DFPT already holds the $Q_\parallel$-linear dipole terms and the ZO contact shift: subtract its 2D nonanalytic part before adding $\Pi^{\ph}$. In a BSE $\Pi^{\rme}$, drop the $\bG_\parallel=0$ exchange for all $(G_z,G_z')$, since that block is the photon line.
  • Which $Z^*_{zz}$. Open- and short-circuit charges differ by $\varepsilon_{zz}$: the vacuum kernel needs the open-circuit one, a $W$ with the layer's own $z$ local fields the short-circuit one.
  • Sums and conventions. E.310 needs all $3N_\kappa$ modes in the mode sum; enforce $\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^*_\kappa=0$. Gaussian $r_0=2\pi\chi^{\mathrm{2D}}$ (B10.7), or $L(\varepsilon^{\SC}_{xx}-1)/2$ from a supercell of height $L$; take $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{env}}$ of polar substrates at the phonon frequency.

First steps

  1. Derive and check (days). The full sheet propagator and its limits: E.460 → E.472, the traceless bare kernel, (B10.27) and §B.10's 0.245 eV Å and 42 μeV; then the ZO branch.
  2. Freestanding benchmark (1–2 weeks). Cutoff DFPT for $Z^*_{xx}$, the open-circuit $Z^*_{zz}$, $\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ and force constants; reproduce Royo–Stengel's BN and SnS₂ ZO slopes before Janus MoSSe; cross-check $r_0$ with your RPA code.
  3. Your GW code's $W$ (2–4 weeks). The $\bG_\parallel=0$ block of the truncated $W$, projected on atomic heights and paired with the short-circuit charge; test MoS₂ (E′, A₂″).
  4. Environment (weeks). The stack's Green's function with $\varepsilon(\omega)$ of SiO₂ or hBN; compare with Li et al. only through a metallic-substrate kernel.
  5. Later. InSe (quadrupoles, topic 5), bilayer hBN, then the full finite-$c$ sheet Dyson equation, which gives topic 1 the $z$-resolved photon line for out-of-plane excitons. The layered-substrate kernel of step 4 is the one interfacial pairing needs (topic 6), and the one doped sheets on polar substrates need (topic 7).

3Anisotropy and hBN

Anisotropic crystals and polaritons beyond the zone centre

EPP's equations are tensors, but what it evaluates is isotropic. For hBN, the standard encapsulant, closing the gap gives the hyperbolic bands and the frequency-dependent environment that your $W$ sees.

Impact on 2D optics medium Difficulty low

  • The equations are tensors. The propagator is a $3\times3$ matrix, the ionic self-energy carries full $\mathbf Z^*$ tensors and the electronic screening enters as $\bq\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\rme}\cdot\bq$ (B9.7), so the directional LO–TO term exists for $c\to\infty$ ((B9.12), §B.9).
  • The evaluation is isotropic. EPP's dielectric model (§4.2.1, E.144–E.165) assumes diagonal $\mathbf Z^*$ and adds the cubic Lorentz field for all symmetries; in hBN that field is a tensor, and in the first-principles route it is already inside $\mathbf Z^*$ (§B.3). EPP §7.1 evaluates only $\bq=0$ without electrons. The primer adds finite $\bq$ for cubic crystals (§B.7) and, in Part A, the electrostatic oblique modes of bulk hBN (§A.4).
  • Nothing at finite $c$. No L–T mixing, Fresnel dispersion or hyperbolic regime, no slab, no damping beyond $\ii\eta$, no low-symmetry crystal. §B.9 solves hBN's uniaxial line with a constant $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ (its figure of four sectors) but has no $\Pi^{\ion}$ inside the tensor.

4Electron self-energies

Electron self-energies with photon and phonon exchange

EPP dresses photons and phonons but never the electron. A $GW$ gap is a clamped-nuclei gap, and the lattice part EPP could add to your $W$ is one pole per polar mode in its head.

Impact on 2D optics medium Difficulty medium

What is missing

  • No electron Dyson equation. EPP solves three bosonic Dyson equations: for the total field $D$, the electron-dressed field $D^{\rme}$ and the phonons $F$, whose self-energy is $\Pi^{\ph}=-J^*D^{\rme}J$ (E.398–E.399; (B7.5)). Electrons appear only in bubbles, with no $\Sigma$ or dressed $G$ (§X.2).
  • No Fan–Migdal, no Debye–Waller. Nothing matches Giustino's electron–phonon self-energy (G.151–G.161): no zero-point renormalization (ZPR), lifetimes or spectral functions (§A.7, §A.8). EPP's electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex appears only in the phonon self-energy (§B.8).
  • Linear coupling, zero-temperature bosons. The coupling is linear in the displacements on a frozen potential, so there is no Debye–Waller term. EPP's zero-temperature rule for boson lines, $n_{\mathrm B}=0$ (E.246–E.250), fails for $\Sigma$, whose numerators carry phonon occupations.

Why it matters

  • Magnitudes. Diamond's direct-gap ZPR is 628 meV with $GW$-corrected couplings, more than 40% above DFT (Antonius et al. 2014). ZPRs often exceed 0.3 eV and reach 0.7 eV in light-element materials, and in infrared-active crystals the nonadiabatic Fröhlich term gives the majority for many materials (Miglio et al. 2020).
  • Fröhlich scale. $-\alpha_{\mathrm F}\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ is about −2.6 meV for the conduction-band minimum of GaAs and −41 meV for that of GaN (model $\alpha_{\mathrm F}=0.071$ and 0.44; §A.8); for the gap of GaN, Nery and Allen (2016) find a Fröhlich correction of −0.05 of −0.15 eV; it is an analytic small-$q$ correction to an adiabatic grid result, not the same quantity as $-\alpha_{\mathrm F}\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$. Lattice screening in $W$ can lower $GW$ gaps by more than 15% in highly polar compounds (Botti and Marques 2013), although Lambrecht et al. (2017) object that the polar term was not confined in $q$.
  • Light cone. In GaAs the light cone, $q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega_{\mathrm{LO}})\approx6\times10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹ against the polaron wavevector $q_{\mathrm{pol}}=0.025$ Å⁻¹, holds about $1.5\times10^{-3}$ of the Fröhlich-shift integrand.
  • For your code. Without the ZPR, $GW$ agreement at the 0.1–0.5 eV level can be fortuitous, for instance in hBN. Temperature-dependent BSE needs electron–phonon energies and widths. In 2D the adiabatic Fröhlich integral diverges logarithmically while the nonadiabatic one converges (derived in the note).

How it could enter EPP

Electron Dyson equation (proposal). E.237 gives a paramagnetic self-energy from two $-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA$ vertices and an $A^2$ tadpole, $G=G^{\mathrm{KS}}+G^{\mathrm{KS}}(\Sigma^{\mathrm{para}}+\Sigma^{\mathrm{dia}}-V^{\mathrm{xc}})G$. The temporal-gauge kernel differs from Coulomb off shell; on shell, with complete band sums and a local Hamiltonian (nonlocal pseudopotentials and scissors break it), para plus dia is gauge invariant up to a band-independent constant (derived in the note). 5Short-range coupling

Short-range coupling and higher multipoles

EPP keeps only the polar channel. In a nonpolar crystal its electron–phonon coupling is zero, and in 2D, where short-range coupling is leading order, EPP §8 alone cannot give carrier or exciton scattering.

Impact on 2D optics high Difficulty high

What is missing

  • No deformation-potential vertex. EPP derives a rigid-ion vertex $g^0$ early on, (B1.4) in the primer, then drops it. In its Lagrangian (E.134) the electrons feel a frozen crystal potential, and the ions reach the field only through the Born-dipole current $\frac1c\dot\bp_{\ell\kappa}\cdot\bA(\btau_{\ell\kappa})$ with $\bp_{\ell\kappa}=e\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\bu_{\ell\kappa}$, the field taken at the atom (as §B.2 corrects; the draft takes it at the cell origin); no term contains both the electron coordinate and the displacement (§B.2). In Si, diamond or graphene ($Z^*=0$) the coupling vanishes; non-polar optical modes (MoS₂ A₁′) and the acoustic deformation potential are absent.
  • Only the dipole moment. The ionic vertex (B5.22) is the zeroth moment of the current a moving atom carries. Optical modes get the full Fröhlich term; acoustic modes keep only the internal-strain dipole, without the clamped-ion (quadrupole) response. In GaAs that term alone is about 1.2 C/m² along [111] in the central-force model of §B.1, and 0.6–0.9 C/m² for a Kleinman (internal-strain) parameter of 0.5–0.75 (unverified range), against a measured 0.19 C/m² (illustrative).
  • Force constants, local fields. EPP does not say what to subtract from DFPT's dipolar and multipolar tails, and its $\Pi^{\rme}$ has neither local fields nor $f^{\mathrm{xc}}$.

Why it matters

  • Transport. The 300 K mobility of n-type monolayer MoS₂, about 410 cm²/Vs, is set by intra- and intervalley deformation potentials and the Fröhlich interaction (Kaasbjerg et al. 2012). Above the L-valley threshold, hot electrons in GaAs also relax by intervalley scattering, computed ab initio by Sjakste, Vast and Tyuterev (2007).
  • 2D power counting (derived in the note). The sheet dipole vertex $g\sim2\pi e^2Z^*u/[\OS(1+r_0Q_\parallel)]$ is $O(Q_\parallel^0)$, like the optical deformation potential, while piezoelectric and quadrupole terms are $O(Q_\parallel)$, like the acoustic one: in 2D short-range coupling is leading order. In MoS₂ ($r_0\approx40$ Å, illustrative), at the thermal-carrier and phonon-emission wavevectors, 0.06–0.12 Å⁻¹, $1/(1+r_0Q_\parallel)\approx0.17$–$0.29$.
  • For your code. Exciton linewidths in W-based TMDs involve dark K–K′ and K–Λ excitons, hence phonons deep in the zone (Selig et al. 2016, not re-verified), as does phonon-assisted absorption. Reconciling DFPT's local-field screening with EPP's is the $\varepsilon_{\mathrm M}$ versus $\bar v$ split of your BSE code.

What is missing

  • No fermion propagator. Electrons appear only as the RPA bubble (E.282–E.283; §B.5): no $G$, no $\Sigma$, no pair amplitude, no gap equation.
  • Only the polar channel. Ions couple through $\frac1c\dot\bp_{\ell\kappa}\cdot\bA$ (E.134–E.135) on a frozen potential and a harmonic lattice; deformation-potential coupling, which dominates $\lambda_{\mathrm{ep}}$ in conventional superconductors and CDW metals, is absent (§B.8).
  • Only hardening (derived in the note). In RPA $W^{\rme}(\bq,0)\gt0$, so EPP's lattice Dyson equation (E.452–E.454; (B9.8)–(B9.10)) can never drive $\omega^2$ to zero. Cochran softening (the polar TO frequency driven to zero) needs the $\bG\neq0$ dipole sum, which the Dyson route drops.

Why it matters

  • Dilute polar superconductors. SrTiO₃ superconducts down to $5.5\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³, where $E_{\mathrm F}\approx1.1$ meV (Lin et al. 2013), far below its polar phonons: Migdal's condition and the scale separation behind $\mu^*$ (G.204) both fail. LO phonons (Gor'kov 2016), plasmons (Ruhman and Lee 2016) and soft TO modes have all been proposed; the plasmon–phonon hybrids of topic 7 contain the first two.
  • Interfacial coupling. Monolayer FeSe on SrTiO₃ opens gaps near 70 K (bulk FeSe about 8 K), with replica bands about 100 meV below the main bands (Lee et al. 2014). Faeth et al. (2021) find $\lambda_{\mathrm{ep}}=0.19\pm0.02$; in model RPA the FeSe electrons screen the coupling almost completely (Zhou and Millis 2017).
  • Light cone. Pairing samples $2k_{\mathrm F}\sim0.2$–$2$ Å⁻¹, so the Fermi-surface fraction inside the light cone is $2.5\times10^{-11}$–$2.5\times10^{-7}$ in 3D (derived in the note).
  • For your code. The Coulomb part of pairing (G.209) is a Fermi-surface matrix element of your full $W_{\bG\bG'}(\bq,\ii\omega)$, with $W^{\ph}$ added to the head only. Excitonic insulators are BSE problems with an anomalous self-energy.

How it could enter EPP

Pairing kernel from $D$ (proposal). In Nambu form EPP's current vertex enters as the unit matrix $\hat\tau_0$ and its charge and diamagnetic vertices as the Pauli matrix $\hat\tau_3$; in Coulomb-gauge form (exact for $c\to\infty$) the pairing interaction is $W=W^{\rme}+W^{\ph}$, with an attractive lattice part at the Matsubara frequency $\ii\omega$:

Derived in the note

$$W^{\ph}(\bq,\ii\omega)=-\frac{4\pi e^2}{q^2}\,\frac{\big(\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p}\big)^2/\varepsilon_\infty^2}{\omega^2+\bar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2}$$

for one isotropic mode, with $\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}=\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}+(\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p})^2/\varepsilon_\infty$; with carriers $\varepsilon_\infty$ becomes $\varepsilon^{\rme}(\bq,\ii\omega)$, pointwise in $\ii\omega$. The linearized gap equation G.209 then holds with

Proposal

$$\begin{aligned}&\lambda_{n\bk,m\bk'}(\ii\omega)-N_{\mathrm F}V_{n\bk,m\bk'}\\ &\quad\to-N_{\mathrm F}\big\langle W^{\rme}+W^{\ph}\big\rangle_{n\bk,m\bk'}(\ii\omega)\end{aligned}$$

where $N_{\mathrm F}V$ is the screened repulsion of G.209, whose Fermi-surface average is $\mu_{\mathrm C}$ (§A.9), and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{n\bk,m\bk'}$ a matrix element between the paired states, using the full $W(\bq,\ii\omega)$ without splitting. This is the dielectric formulation of pairing that Takada (1980) applied to n-SrTiO₃; EPP would add first-principles $\mathbf Z^*$, $\varepsilon^{\rme}(\bq,\ii\omega)$ and anisotropy, but still lacks the short-range $g$ and vertex corrections for $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/E_{\mathrm F}\gtrsim1$. A layered substrate Green's function turns the FeSe/SrTiO₃ coupling into a Fuchs–Kliewer (substrate surface-phonon) pole of $W$.

Meissner kernel, soft modes, condensates. With Nambu propagators $\Pi^{\mathrm T}(\bq\to0,0)\propto n_{\mathrm s}/m^*$, so E.279 must replace E.281, with Nambu's Ward identity in the longitudinal sector. Reinstating $g^0$ (E.107; (B1.4)) for soft modes (proposal) admits only two consistent partitions, screened-adiabatic or fully bare. And E.281 and E.311 set $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,0)=0$ at every $\bq$: this enforces the uniform no-go theorem (Andolina et al. 2019) but deletes the electrons' static orbital response, which a finite-$q$ photon condensate needs ($\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}\gt1/4\pi$ in 3D; Andolina et al. 2020).

Pitfalls

  • Double counting. No empirical $\mu^*$ on top of $W^{\rme}$, and no separate $v$ besides $D^{0\mathrm L}$.
  • Normal self-energy. Bare bands with the full normal part, or $GW$ bands with only $W^{\ph}$ and the anomalous part of $W^{\rme}$.
  • Gauge. $\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$ is exact longitudinally at all $q$ but transversely only as $q\to0$; truncated band sums fake a Drude weight.
  • Matsubara. $T_{\mathrm c}\approx0.1$–$0.4$ K with Matsubara energies up to 0.1–0.5 eV needs $5\times10^2$–$10^4$ positive frequencies: use a sparse basis.

First steps

  1. Derive (1–2 weeks). Nambu vertices; reduction to G.208–G.210 with $|g^{\mathrm{lr}}|^2$ (B8.17); Morel–Anderson (the $\mu^*$ pseudopotential) only for $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\gg\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$; the Meissner kernel with the Ward identity.
  2. Benchmark (2–3 weeks). Reproduce Takada (1980) and Gastiasoro et al. (2019) from EPP's single propagator, then add $\mathbf Z^*$ and $\varepsilon^{\rme}(\bq,\ii\omega)$ from your RPA code, or the hybrid $W$ of topic 7; for SrTiO₃ include the soft TO₁ mode (about 1–2 meV) and LO modes near 21, 59 and 98 meV.
  3. Interface (1–2 months). From Zhou and Millis, $W(\bQ_\parallel,\ii\omega;0,0)$ from §B.10 plus a layered substrate, compared with Faeth et al.
  4. Soft modes (about 1 week). Cochran softening for SrTiO₃ or BaTiO₃ from the $\bG\neq0$ dipole sum, a consistency check.
  5. Polaritons (months). MoSe₂ in a cavity with the $\Pi^{\mathrm{e,BSE}}$ of topic 1: no $q=0$ instability with $\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$.

7Plasmon–phonon hybrids

Free carriers: plasmons, coupled modes and dynamic screening

EPP's phonon self-energy already contains the plasmon of a doped crystal (E.453), but the manuscript sets $\omega=0$ in the screening (E.454). Kept dynamic, LO phonons and plasmons become hybrids. Lihm and Park (2024) compute them from first principles with one extra row in the dynamical matrix, and their self-energy is the carrier part of EPP's in the electrostatic limit.

Impact on 2D optics medium Difficulty low

GaAs numbers use the primer's set: $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}=33.3$ meV, $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}=36.2$ meV, $\varepsilon_\infty=10.9$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}=12.9$, $m^*=0.067\,m_{\mathrm e}$.

What is missing

  • The equations allow it. The phonon self-energy $\Pi^{\ph}=-J^*D^{\rme}J$ (E.398–E.399) uses the photon line dressed by the RPA bubble (E.388). With carriers the bubble gains a Drude term and $D^{\rme}$ a plasmon pole, so the pole condition E.453 contains the coupled modes (§B.7, §B.9).
  • The manuscript stops at E.454. It calls 3D screening “practically static” at phonon energies: true with a gap or with a plasmon far above the phonons, false for $q\lesssim\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}$ when the plasmon lies near or below them. The 2D result (E.471–E.472) is static too, and §7.3 keeps only diagonal self-energies, though one plasmon couples to every polar mode with a current along $\hat\bq$.
  • The primer has models, not a method. §B.9 derives the Drude modes $\mathrm L_\pm$ (B9.18) and the adiabatic result (B9.19), §B.8 the vertex screened at the phonon frequency, §B.10 a Drude sheet: one isotropic parabolic band at $T=0$, without carrier scattering.
  • No electron side. Without an electron Dyson equation (topic 4) the hybrids give no self-energy, satellite or pairing kernel.

Why it matters

  • Static screening fails. In n-GaAs the screened plasma frequency $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}$ crosses $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ near $7\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³; at $10^{18}$ cm⁻³ the modes sit at 47.8 and 30.3 meV. Static Thomas–Fermi screening instead pulls the LO mode to $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ below $k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}=0.021$ Å⁻¹, though the carriers cannot follow below $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}=0.010$ Å⁻¹.
  • First principles meet Raman. Lihm and Park reproduce the GaAs data of Mooradian and McWhorter (1967) without free parameters; in the same figure their static calculation loses the LO–TO splitting above $2\times10^{15}$ cm⁻³. In anatase TiO₂ one to three polar modes hybridize, depending on $\hat\bq$.
  • Electrons. Statically screened phonons underestimate $-\mathrm{Im}\,\Sigma$; adding a bare plasmon overestimates it at low density, where the plasmon-like hybrid couples with only $(\varepsilon_\infty/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s})^{3/2}$ of the bare plasmon's strength, 0.78 in GaAs (derived in the note).
  • For your code. A gated sheet's $\sqrt{Q_\parallel}$ plasmon crosses the LO branch: in the primer's MoS₂-like sheet at $10^{12}$ cm⁻² the modes split by 4.3 meV, against a 0.2 meV undoped shift (§B.10). Your dynamically screened $W$ of a doped sheet contains these hybrids.

How it could enter EPP

Lihm and Park's self-energy is the carrier part of EPP's. Split the electrons into the bound electrons of the undoped crystal and the carriers, with interacting response $\chi_{\mathrm c}$. The RPA chain (B9.6) and the self-energy (B9.8) regroup exactly:

Derived in the note from (B9.6) and (B9.8)

$$\begin{gathered}W^{\rme}=W^{\rme}_\infty+W^{\rme}_\infty\,\chi_{\mathrm c}\,W^{\rme}_\infty\\ W^{\rme}_\infty(\bq)=\frac{4\pi e^2}{\bq\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\bq}\\ \frac2\hbar\sqrt{\bar\omega_{\bq\nu}\bar\omega_{\bq\nu'}}\;\Pi^{\ph}_{\mathrm c,\nu\nu'}\\ =\frac{W^{\rme}_\infty\,\chi_{\mathrm c}\,W^{\rme}_\infty}{e^2\OV}\,(\bq\cdot\bar\bj_\nu)^*(\bq\cdot\bar\bj_{\nu'})\end{gathered}$$

The first term turns Part B's bare lattice into the undoped crystal, whose modes $\bar\omega_{\bq\nu}$ (mode currents $\bar\bj_\nu$) are the DFPT phonons. The second is their Eq. (1), with $U_\bq=W^{\rme}_\infty/\OV$ and $\delta\chi=\OV\chi_{\mathrm c}$; their propagator, in units of 1/frequency², is $\hbar F/(2\sqrt{\bar\omega\bar\omega'})$. As $W^{\rme}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}W^{\rme}_\infty=W^{\rme}_\infty\chi^0_{\mathrm c}W^{\rme}$, it keeps §B.8's count: one vertex screened by the bound electrons only, one fully screened.

A plasmon pole and one more row. With $W^{\rme}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}\simeq\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/[(\omega+\ii\gamma^{\mathrm{pl}}_\bq)^2-(\omega^{\mathrm{pl}}_\bq)^2]$, the strength fixed by the f-sum rule and the frequency by the static response (Lihm and Park fit both, and the width, to the RPA response), the squared hybrid frequencies are the eigenvalues of

Derived in the note; Lihm and Park, Eqs. (5)–(6)

$$\begin{gathered}\mathsf C(\bq)=\begin{pmatrix}(\omega^{\mathrm{pl}}_\bq)^2&\bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\bar\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p,1}&\cdots\\ \bar\omega_{\mathrm p}\bar\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p,1}&\bar\omega^2_{\bq1}&\cdots\\ \vdots&&\ddots\end{pmatrix}\\ \big(\bar\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p,\nu}\big)^2=\frac{4\pi\,|\hat\bq\cdot\bar\bj_\nu|^2}{\OV\,\hat\bq\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\hat\bq}\end{gathered}$$

Each wing is a product of two screened plasma frequencies, of the carriers and of one polar mode. For one LO mode the eigenvalues give (B9.18) at $q\to0$, the self-energy at $\omega=0$ gives (B9.19), and the secular equation is $\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}(\bq,\omega)=0$ for carriers and ions together. A matrix of twice the size adds half widths, $\omega_{\bq s}-\ii\gamma_{\bq s}$. Electrons see hybrid $s$ with a share $\bar\omega_{\mathrm p,s}$ of the plasma frequencies:

Derived in the note

$$\begin{gathered}\frac{W(\bq,\omega)}{W^{\rme}_\infty(\bq)}-1=\sum_s\frac{\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq s}}\\ \sum_s\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p,s}=\bar\omega^2_{\mathrm p}+\sum_\nu\big(\bar\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p,\nu}\big)^2\end{gathered}$$

Their Eq. (10) is then the Fan–Migdal form (A7.9) with hybrids for phonons. Beyond the paper (proposals). At finite $c$ the same $D$ gives transverse branches that start at $\omega_{\mathrm L\pm}$; infrared probes $q\lesssim q_{\mathrm{light}}(\omega)\sim10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹, far below the $2\times10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹ of their anatase figure, and there a uniaxial crystal mixes L and T (topic 3). In 2D the method takes a $\sqrt{Q_\parallel}$ pole and the sheet's $W^{\mathrm{e,2D}}_\infty$, with substrates from topic 2. With a dynamic $D^{\rme}$, $D=D^{\rme}+D^{\ph}$ gives the electron self-energy (topic 4) without adding a bare plasmon to static phonons, and the pairing kernel of dilute polar superconductors (topic 6).

Pitfalls

  • Dipole field twice. Dress undoped DFPT phonons with $W^{\rme}_\infty\chi_{\mathrm c}W^{\rme}_\infty$ only, not with the full doped $W^{\rme}$; from Part B's bare lattice, use one $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ in $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$ and $W^{\rme}_\infty$.
  • Carriers twice. Pair $\chi_{\mathrm c}$ with vertices screened by $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ only (Berges et al. 2023; Marini 2023), and never add a dynamic carrier self-energy to force constants from static DFPT with doping.
  • Limits and poles. Compare $q$ with $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}$ and $k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$. Fit the pole to the f-sum rule and the static response; inside the particle–hole continuum use the full Lindhard function or several poles.
  • Real carriers. Below about $3\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³ GaAs carriers are non-degenerate at 300 K (Debye screening); the strength needs their average inverse-mass tensor. Debye screening with $\varepsilon_\infty=10.9$ reaches $8\times10^{-4}$ Å⁻¹ at half the density Lihm and Park quote: settle this before benchmarking.
