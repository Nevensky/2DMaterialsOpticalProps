## 1. Overview (plain language)

This range turns the electron–phonon–photon **Lagrangian** (134) into a **quantum Hamiltonian** in which photons are explicit particles. It does so in three moves.

**(i) Check the field equations (Sec. 4.3).** The author treats the vector potential $\mathbf A(\mathbf r)$ as a field and writes the field part of (134) as a Lagrangian density. The Euler–Lagrange equations then give the Ampère–Maxwell law, with two sources: the electronic current $\mathbf j_{\rm e}$ and the ionic polarization current $\dot{\mathbf P}$. Gauss's law $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=4\pi\rho_{\rm e}$ follows from continuity. The picture is that *phonons act as a polarizable background ($\mathbf P$) and electrons act as free charges*.

**(ii) Legendre transform (Sec. 4.4).** The canonical momenta are minimally coupled: $\mathbf p_{\rm e}=m\mathbf v+\frac ec\mathbf A$ for electrons, $\pi=m_a\dot Q+\frac1cZ^*\mathbf A$ for ions, and $\mathbf p_A=-\mathbf E/4\pi c$ for the field. The Hamiltonian is "kinetic + lattice + $\frac1{8\pi}\int(E^2+B^2)$". Squaring the minimal-coupling brackets gives the interaction $V$, Eq. (186). It has four terms: paramagnetic and diamagnetic couplings to the electrons, and the analogous paramagnetic and diamagnetic couplings to the ionic dipoles.

**(iii) Quantize the field and find the Coulomb interaction (Secs. 4.4.1–4.4.3).** The gauge is the temporal gauge $\Phi=0$, so all three Cartesian components of $\mathbf A$ are dynamical, and $[A_\mu,p_{A\nu}]=i\hbar\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$. In Fourier space the author rotates to a triad: two transverse polarizations and one longitudinal, $\mathbf e_3=\hat{\mathbf q}$. The **two transverse polarizations are harmonic oscillators with quanta $\hbar cq$: photons.** The longitudinal polarization has **no restoring term**. Its "Hamiltonian" $2\pi c^2\sum\tilde p_{3}\tilde p_{3}$ is not an oscillator, and it is not diagonal in the ladder operators. Rewritten in real space and combined with Gauss's law, it becomes **exactly the instantaneous Coulomb energy** $\frac12\iint(\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion})(\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion})/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, with $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$. Electron–electron, electron–ion (dipole) and ion–ion (dipole–dipole) Coulomb interactions all come from this one term. The term is then moved from the "free" Hamiltonian into the interaction $V'$, Eq. (237). The longitudinal photons cannot simply be thrown away, though: the canonical commutator closes only when both transverse and longitudinal modes are kept (completeness of the triad).

**Why it matters for the rest of the manuscript.** Giustino's Hamiltonian has only the instantaneous Coulomb interaction $v$. Here $v$ is recovered as the longitudinal sector of a full electromagnetic theory, and transverse, retarded photons now sit beside it. Later sections build the photon propagator $D=D^{0,l}+D^{0,t}$, RPA photon self-energies and the electrodynamic phonon self-energy and Fröhlich vertex on this Hamiltonian.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **(p. 35, end of Sec. 4.2) Transverse optical modes and LST.** When $\epsilon_\mu(\omega)\to\infty$, $\mathbf E=0$, so $\nabla\times\mathbf E=0$ holds trivially. But $\mathbf D\neq0$, and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=0$ requires these to be **transverse electromagnetic modes**. The author warns that $\epsilon_\infty$ was added "ad hoc" in Eq. (158); in the later diagrammatic expansion all such ad hoc screening is to be replaced by consistently included electron and phonon screening. Static limit: LST relation (165). Conclusion: Lagrangian (134) gives the correct ionic equations of motion.
2. **(p. 35) Lagrangian in field form.** (134) is rewritten with the field part $L_A$ (free field, $\frac1c\int\mathbf j_{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf A$ and $\frac1c\int\dot{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf A$), using the electron current density (166) and the polarization density of point dipoles (167). $L_A=\int d\mathbf r\,\mathcal L_A$, Eqs. (168)–(169). Total $L$, Eq. (170).
3. **(p. 36) Functional derivatives.** For a local functional $F[\phi]=\int f(\phi)$, $\delta F/\delta\phi(\mathbf r)=\partial f/\partial\phi(\mathbf r)$, Eq. (171). So the field Euler–Lagrange equations (172) reduce to the density form (173).

## 3. Key equations

### Conventions used in this range

- **Units: Gaussian.** $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\partial_t\mathbf A$, $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ (gauge $\Phi=0$, Eqs. (132)–(133)). Field energy is $\frac1{8\pi}\int(E^2+B^2)$, $\mathbf D=\mathbf E+4\pi\mathbf P$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=4\pi\rho$, and the Coulomb kernel is $V(\mathbf r)=1/|\mathbf r|$ with charges carrying $e$. $\hbar$ is explicit. Conversion to SI: $e^2\to e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0$; the mode amplitude $\sqrt{2\pi\hbar c^2/\omega\Omega}\to\sqrt{\hbar/2\epsilon_0\omega\Omega}$.
- **Gauge: temporal (Weyl) gauge $\Phi=0$.** All three components of $\mathbf A$ are canonical coordinates, and the longitudinal part $\mathbf A_l$ is *not* zero (unlike the Coulomb gauge).
- **Charge sign.** In Sec. 4, $e$ is the (signed) electron charge: the Lorentz force is $e\mathbf E+\frac ec\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ (Sec. 4.1). Sec. 3 instead wrote the electron charge as $-e$.
- **Born charges.** $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$ has the dimension of charge (EPP convention), and $p_{ia\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}$ (135).
- **Fourier convention (photons).** $\mathbf A(\mathbf r)=\Omega^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q}\mathbf A_{\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}$, $\mathbf A_{\mathbf q}=\Omega^{-1/2}\int d\mathbf r\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\mathbf A(\mathbf r)$, and $\sum_{\mathbf q}\to\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^3}\int d\mathbf q$. Here $\Omega$ is the total normalization (BvK crystal) volume, $\Omega=N\Omega_{\rm uc}$, and $\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}=\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$. **The photon $\mathbf q$ runs over all of reciprocal space**, not just the BZ, whereas phonon and electron sums are restricted to $\mathbf q,\mathbf k\in$ BZ with $1/\sqrt N$ normalization (Eqs. (242)–(243)). The continuous transform in (226)–(229) uses $e^{-i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}$; the sign is immaterial for the even kernels involved.
- **Index conventions.** Greek $\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta$ in Cartesian sums run over $x,y,z$. In the polarization basis, $\alpha=1,2$ (transverse) and $\alpha=3$ (longitudinal). Phonon branches are $\alpha=1..3s$; in (219) and later, photon polarizations are $\sigma=1,2$. $a,b$ label atoms in the cell and $i,j$ label cells.

Longitudinal modes are the zeros $\epsilon_\mu(\omega)=0$, with $\mathbf E=-4\pi\mathbf P$ (164). Transverse modes are the poles $\epsilon_\mu\to\infty$, where $\mathbf E=0$ and $\mathbf D\ne0$ with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=0$ (p. 35).

$$\frac{\epsilon_\mu(0)}{\epsilon_\infty}=\frac{\tilde\omega^2_{{\rm LO}\mu}}{\tilde\omega^2_{{\rm TO}\mu}}\tag{165}$$
- Obtained from (159) at $\omega\to0$.
- Check: $\epsilon(0)=\epsilon_\infty+\omega_p^2/\tilde\omega_{\rm TO}^2$ and $\tilde\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\tilde\omega_{\rm TO}^2=\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$.

### Sec. 4.3: field form of the Lagrangian (p. 35)

**Lagrangian rewritten with the field operator** (unnumbered, p. 35):
$$L=\sum_e\Big[\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}-V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)\Big]+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}^2}{2}-\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\underbrace{\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}\Big\{\frac1{c^2}\dot{\mathbf A}^2(\mathbf r)-[\nabla\times\mathbf A(\mathbf r)]^2\Big\}+\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\dot{\mathbf P}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)}_{L_A}$$
- This is (134) with $E^2-B^2=\dot A^2/c^2-(\nabla\times A)^2$.
- The point-particle couplings $\frac ec\sum_e\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)$ and $\frac1c\sum_{ia}\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf R^0_i)$ are rewritten with the densities below.
- $m_e$, $\mathbf v_e$, $\mathbf r_e$: electron mass, velocity, position. $V_{\rm cr}$: frozen local crystal (pseudo)potential (136). $m_a$: mass of atom $a$. $Q_{ia\mu}$: displacement of atom $a$ in cell $i$. $k$: force-constant ("elastic") matrix.

$$\mathbf j_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)=e\sum_e\mathbf v_e\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_e)\tag{166}$$
- Electronic (charge) current density of point electrons; it is the full *kinetic* current, built from velocities.

$$\mathbf P(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}\mathbf p_{ia}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{167}$$
- Ionic polarization density: point dipoles $\mathbf p_{ia}=\hat Z^*_a\mathbf Q_{ia}$ placed at the **cell** position $\mathbf R^0_i$, not at the atomic site $\mathbf R^0_i+\boldsymbol\tau_a$. This is a dipole approximation; see Source issues.

$$L_A=\int d\mathbf r\,\mathcal L_A\big(\{A_\mu,\dot A_\mu,\partial_\mu A_\nu\}\big)\tag{168}$$
$$\mathcal L_A(\mathbf r)=\frac1{8\pi c^2}\dot{\mathbf A}^2(\mathbf r)-\frac1{8\pi}[\nabla\times\mathbf A(\mathbf r)]^2+\frac1c\mathbf j_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac1c\dot{\mathbf P}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)\tag{169}$$
- The Lagrangian density of the field and its two sources.
- Remark (not in the text): integrating by parts in time, $\frac1c\int\dot{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf A=-\frac1c\int\mathbf P\cdot\dot{\mathbf A}+\frac{d}{dt}(\dots)=\int\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf E+\frac{d}{dt}(\dots)$. So the ionic coupling is the familiar dipole energy $\sum\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$ in "velocity form". This is why (143), $m_a\ddot Q+kQ=Z^*E$, comes out.

$$L=\sum_e\Big[\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}-V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)\Big]+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q^2_{ia\mu}}{2}-\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+L_A\tag{170}$$

### Field Euler–Lagrange equations (p. 36)

$$\frac{\delta F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(\mathbf r)}=\int d\mathbf r'\frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi(\mathbf r')}\underbrace{\frac{\delta\phi(\mathbf r')}{\delta\phi(\mathbf r)}}_{\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}=\frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi(\mathbf r)}\tag{171}$$
- For a local functional $F[\phi]=\int d\mathbf r f(\phi)$, the functional derivative equals the partial derivative of the density.

$$\frac{\delta L}{\delta A_\mu(\mathbf r)}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\delta L}{\delta\dot A_\mu(\mathbf r)}-\sum_\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_\nu}\frac{\delta L}{\delta[\partial A_\mu(\mathbf r)/\partial x_\nu]}=0\tag{172}$$
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial A_\mu(\mathbf r)}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial\dot A_\mu(\mathbf r)}-\sum_\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_\nu}\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_\mu(\mathbf r)/\partial x_\nu]}=0\tag{173}$$

Intermediate derivatives (unnumbered, pp. 36–37):
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial A_\mu}=\frac1cj_{e\mu}+\frac1c\dot P_\mu,\qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial\dot A_\mu}=\frac1{4\pi c^2}\dot A_\mu=-\frac1{4\pi c}E_\mu,$$
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_x/\partial y]}=\frac{B_z}{4\pi},\quad \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_y/\partial x]}=-\frac{B_z}{4\pi},\quad \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_y/\partial z]}=\frac{B_x}{4\pi},\quad \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_z/\partial y]}=-\frac{B_x}{4\pi},\quad \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_z/\partial x]}=\frac{B_y}{4\pi},\quad \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_x/\partial z]}=-\frac{B_y}{4\pi},$$
$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial\dot A_\mu}=-\frac1{4\pi c}\dot E_\mu,\qquad \sum_\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_\nu}\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial[\partial A_\mu/\partial x_\nu]}=\frac1{4\pi}[\nabla\times\mathbf B]_\mu .$$

### Maxwell equations (p. 37–38)

$$\frac1cj_{e\mu}+\frac1c\dot P_\mu+\frac1{4\pi c}\dot E_\mu-\frac1{4\pi}[\nabla\times\mathbf B]_\mu=0\tag{174}$$
$$\nabla\times\mathbf B=\frac1c\dot{\mathbf E}+\frac{4\pi}{c}\dot{\mathbf P}+\frac{4\pi}{c}\mathbf j_{\rm e}\tag{175}$$
- Ampère–Maxwell law with two sources: free electronic current and ionic polarization current $\dot{\mathbf P}$.

$$\mathbf D=\mathbf E+4\pi\mathbf P\tag{176}$$
$$\nabla\times\mathbf B=\frac1c\dot{\mathbf D}+\frac{4\pi}{c}\mathbf j_{\rm e}\tag{177}$$
- The standard macroscopic Ampère law. Here $\mathbf P$ is only the *ionic* polarization; electronic polarization is carried by $\mathbf j_{\rm e}$.

$$\nabla\cdot[\nabla\times\mathbf B]=\frac1c\frac{d}{dt}\nabla\cdot\mathbf D+\frac{4\pi}{c}\nabla\cdot\mathbf j_{\rm e}\tag{178}$$
With $\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf B)=0$ and continuity $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j_{\rm e}=-\dot\rho_{\rm e}$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\nabla\cdot\mathbf D-4\pi\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{\rm e}=0\tag{179}$$
$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=4\pi\rho_{\rm e}\tag{180}$$
- Gauss's law, obtained by time integration with zero integration constant.
- In the temporal gauge, Gauss's law is **not** an Euler–Lagrange equation of motion. It is a constraint on initial data that the dynamics preserves (Eq. 179). See Concepts §4.2.
- Faraday's law and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ are not derived because they are identities of the potentials ($\mathbf E=-\dot{\mathbf A}/c$, $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$).

### Sec. 4.4: Legendre transform (pp. 38–39)

$$H=\sum_e\mathbf p_e\cdot\mathbf v_e+\sum_{ia}\pi_{ia}\cdot\dot{\mathbf Q}_{ia}+\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf p_A\cdot\dot{\mathbf A}-L\tag{181}$$

$$p_{e\mu}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial v_{e\mu}}=m_ev_{e\mu}+\frac ecA_\mu\tag{182}$$
- The electron canonical momentum, with $A_\mu$ evaluated at $\mathbf r_e$.

$$\pi_{ia\mu}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}}=m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}+\frac1c\sum_\alpha Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{183}$$
- The ionic canonical momentum. It is minimally coupled through the Born charge, so the ion acts as an effective charge $Z^*$ for its displacement.

$$p_{A_\mu}=\frac{\delta L}{\delta\dot A_\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial\dot A_\mu}=-\frac1{4\pi c}E_\mu\tag{184}$$
- The field momentum is (minus) the electric field. In the $\Phi=0$ gauge this is the *total* $\mathbf E$, transverse plus longitudinal.

Intermediate (p. 38): with everything inserted, the $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A$ and $\frac1cZ^*A\dot Q$ terms cancel pairwise, and $\int\mathbf p_A\cdot\dot{\mathbf A}=\frac1{4\pi}\int E^2$. Therefore:
$$H=\sum_e\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}+\sum_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q^2_{ia\mu}}{2}+\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\frac1{8\pi}\int d\mathbf r\,[\mathbf E^2+\mathbf B^2]\tag{185}$$
- The energy is the sum of kinetic, potential and field energies. No explicit coupling appears: it is hidden in the momenta. No explicit Coulomb term appears either: it is hidden in $\int E^2$ through Gauss's law (Sec. 4.4.3).

**In canonical variables** (unnumbered, p. 39):
$$H=\sum_e\frac1{2m_e}\Big[\mathbf p_e-\frac ec\mathbf A\Big]^2+\sum_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac1{2m_a}\Big[\pi_{ia\mu}-\frac1c\sum_\alpha Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)\Big]^2+\frac12\sum kQQ+\frac1{8\pi}\int d\mathbf r\,[\mathbf E^2+\mathbf B^2]$$

**Split $H=H_0'+V$:**
$$H_0'=\sum_e\Big[\frac{\mathbf p_e^2}{2m_e}+V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)\Big]+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{\pi^2_{ia\mu}}{2m_a}+\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\frac1{8\pi}\int d\mathbf r\,[\mathbf E^2+\mathbf B^2]$$
$$V=-\frac{e}{2m_ec}\sum_e[\mathbf p_e\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)+\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)\cdot\mathbf p_e]+\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\sum_e\mathbf A^2(\mathbf r_e)-\frac1c\sum_{ia\alpha\mu}\frac1{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\pi_{ia\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)+\frac1{c^2}\sum_{ia\mu\alpha\beta}\frac1{2m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)A_\beta(\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{186}$$
- Four couplings:
  - (1) Electron paramagnetic ($\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$), symmetrized because $\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)$ does not commute with $\mathbf p_e$; in the $\Phi=0$ gauge $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A\ne0$.
  - (2) Electron diamagnetic ($A^2$).
  - (3) Ion "paramagnetic" ($Z^*\pi\cdot\mathbf A$): the ionic current couples to $\mathbf A$.
  - (4) Ion "diamagnetic" ($Z^*Z^*AA$).
- $\pi$ (phonons) and $\mathbf A$ (photons) commute, so the ionic square needs no symmetrization.

### $H_0'$ in quasiparticle language (p. 40)

$$H_0'=\sum_n\sum_{\mathbf k\in{\rm BZ}}E_{n\mathbf k}c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in{\rm BZ}}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)\big(a^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+1/2\big)+\underbrace{\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}[\mathbf E^2+\mathbf B^2]}_{H^{0\prime}_{\rm ph}}\tag{187}$$
- Electrons are in the Kohn–Sham basis $\{\phi_{n\mathbf k},E_{n\mathbf k}\}$; phonons come from Hamiltonian (5) with $Q,\pi$ as in (83)–(84) (reproduced as (242)–(243)).
- DFT inputs already contain part of the e–e interaction ($E_{n\mathbf k}$) and the short-range, adiabatically screened ion–electron–ion interaction ($\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$).
- The prime signals that $H_0'$ is not free: $H^{0\prime}_{\rm ph}$ contains transverse photons *and* longitudinal e–e, e–i, i–i Coulomb interactions.

### Sec. 4.4.1: quantizing the field (pp. 40–45)

$$H^{0\prime}_{\rm ph}=\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}[\mathbf E^2+\mathbf B^2]=\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}\{16\pi^2c^2\mathbf p_A^2+[\nabla\times\mathbf A]^2\}\tag{188}$$
$$[A_\mu(\mathbf r),p_{A\nu}(\mathbf r')]=i\hbar\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')\,\delta_{\mu\nu}\tag{189}$$

## 4. Concepts & physical intuition

### 4.1 One Lagrangian, all of electrodynamics
- (134)/(169) contain two kinds of matter–field coupling, both of the form "current $\cdot\,\mathbf A$". The electron current is $\mathbf j_{\rm e}$ and the ionic current is $\dot{\mathbf P}$, the time derivative of the Born-charge dipoles.
- Varying $\mathbf A$ gives Ampère–Maxwell (175). Gauss's law (180) follows from continuity.
- So the same Lagrangian contains the **electrostatic** physics that Giustino uses (Coulomb, dipole fields, Fröhlich) *and* the **radiative** physics (transverse photons, retardation).
- Lecture line: "In Giustino's world, ions talk to electrons through a potential; here they talk through the electromagnetic field, which has two kinds of degrees of freedom."

### 4.2 Temporal gauge and Gauss's law as a constraint
- With $\Phi=0$, the variables are $\mathbf A$ (3 components) and $\mathbf p_A=-\mathbf E/4\pi c$. Varying $\mathbf A$ gives only the Ampère law. Gauss's law is not an independent equation of motion: its time derivative vanishes by Ampère + continuity (Eq. 179). It must be imposed as an initial condition, and the author sets the integration constant to zero.
- In the quantum theory, the operator $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E-4\pi\rho$ generates the residual, time-independent gauge transformations $\mathbf A\to\mathbf A+\nabla\chi(\mathbf r)$. Physical states are annihilated by it. Using $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi\rho$ inside $H$ (step 233→235) is therefore legitimate on the physical subspace.
- Coulomb gauge ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0$), for comparison: $\mathbf A$ is purely transverse, $\Phi$ is the instantaneous Coulomb potential, $[A_\mu,E_\nu]\propto\delta^\perp_{\mu\nu}$, and the Coulomb term appears directly in $H$.
- Both gauges end with the **same physics**: transverse photons + instantaneous Coulomb. EPP reaches it via the temporal gauge, which keeps $\mathbf A_l$ and longitudinal "photons" as bookkeeping devices.

### 4.3 Transverse vs longitudinal: why only two oscillators
In Fourier space each mode $\mathbf q$ has a 3D vector amplitude. Rotate to $(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\hat{\mathbf q})$:
- $\mathbf B_{\mathbf q}=i\mathbf q\times\mathbf A_{\mathbf q}$ sees only $\mathbf A_t$. The magnetic energy acts as a spring, $q^2|\tilde A_{1,2}|^2/8\pi$, for transverse modes only (Eq. 204).
- The electric energy $2\pi c^2|\tilde p|^2$ is a "kinetic" term for all three.
- **Transverse:** kinetic + spring gives a harmonic oscillator with $\omega=cq$. Quantized, this is a photon, $\hbar cq$ (214).
- **Longitudinal:** kinetic only, $H=2\pi c^2\tilde p_3^2$, like a free particle: $\dot{\tilde p}_3=0$ and $\tilde A_3$ grows linearly in time. So $\mathbf E_l$ is static unless charges move, and it is fixed by the charges through Gauss's law. It has no dynamics of its own and no propagation at $c$. That is why its energy becomes the **instantaneous** Coulomb energy (235).
- In perturbation theory (Sec. 4.7, Eq. (262)), the longitudinal "photons" have $\omega_{\mathbf q3}=0^+$ and $D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}=0$ (263). The Coulomb physics is then "hidden" in $H^0_{\rm Lph}$ (235). In Sec. 6.0.4 it re-emerges as an effective longitudinal propagator $D^{0,l}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-4\pi c\,q_\mu q_\nu/(q^2\omega^2)$ (374).
- Note the structural echo: the kernel in (228) is $\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu V$, and Sec. 6, Eq. (373), writes $D^{0,l}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{4\pi}{q^2}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}$. This is the same object, converted from a density–density to a current–current language through continuity ($\omega\rho=\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j$).

### 4.4 Why the longitudinal photons cannot be dropped (completeness)
- In the temporal gauge the canonical commutator is local, $i\hbar\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$ (189).
- Keeping only $\alpha=1,2$ gives $\sum_{\alpha=1,2}e_{\mu\alpha}e_{\nu\alpha}=\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$, the transverse projector. In real space this is $\delta^\perp_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r)=\frac23\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(\mathbf r)-\frac1{4\pi}\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}-3\hat r_\mu\hat r_\nu}{r^3}$, which has a **nonlocal dipolar tail**.
- The longitudinal part $\delta^\parallel_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r)=\frac13\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(\mathbf r)+\frac1{4\pi}\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}-3\hat r_\mu\hat r_\nu}{r^3}$ cancels that tail exactly.
- This is the content of "longitudinal photons play an important role in the quantization of transverse photons" (p. 45). The same $1/r^3$ tensor is the dipole–dipole interaction, which is why $\delta^\parallel$ reappears as the Coulomb kernel in (227).

### 4.5 Minimal coupling for ions
- (183): the ionic canonical momentum is $\pi=m_a\dot Q+\frac1c\hat Z^{*T}\mathbf A$, so the displaced ion behaves like a particle of charge $Z^*$ in the field.
- Expanding $[\pi-\frac1cZ^*\mathbf A]^2/2m_a$ gives an ionic paramagnetic vertex ($Z^*\pi\cdot\mathbf A/m_a$, i.e. ionic current $\times\,\mathbf A$) and an ionic diamagnetic term ($Z^*Z^*AA/2m_ac^2$). These mirror the electronic $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$ and $A^2$ terms.
- Later (Sec. 5.2) these give the paramagnetic and diamagnetic *ionic* RPA photon self-energies. The diamagnetic term guarantees gauge invariance (the f-sum rule for ions), just as $A^2$ does for electrons.

### 4.6 The Coulomb Hamiltonian (235): what each cross term means
- **e–e:** $\frac12\iint\rho_{\rm e}\rho_{\rm e}/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, the bare electron–electron interaction. It becomes $W^{\rm e}$ after RPA dressing. It double counts with the Hartree+xc already inside $E_{n\mathbf k}$, which the author acknowledges on p. 40.
- **e–ion:** $\iint\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf r')/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ with $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$. Integrating by parts gives $\sum_{ia}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\,\mathbf p_{ia}\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)/|\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i|^3$: the electron energy in the field of the ionic dipoles. After phonon quantization (242) this is the **bare (unscreened) Fröhlich vertex**, $\propto i\,\mathbf q\cdot\hat Z^*\mathbf e/q^2$. It is the $\epsilon^\infty\to1$ version of EPP Eq. (131) and of Verdi–Giustino. Electronic RPA screening later turns $1/q^2$ into $1/\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q$.
- **ion–ion:** $\frac12\iint\rho_{\rm ion}\rho_{\rm ion}/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, the dipole–dipole interaction. At long wavelength it equals $\frac1{8\pi}\int\mathbf E_l^2$ with $\mathbf E_l=-4\pi\mathbf P_\parallel$, i.e. an energy density $2\pi P_\parallel^2$.
  - For a diatomic crystal with relative displacement $u\parallel\mathbf q$ and $P=Z^*u/\Omega_{\rm uc}$, this adds a spring constant $4\pi Z^{*2}/\Omega_{\rm uc}$ per cell.
  - It therefore shifts only the LO mode: $\Delta\omega^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}M)=\omega_p^2$, unscreened, the same $\omega_p$ as in (160). With electronic screening this becomes $\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$. This is the "LO shift" of p. 34.
  - EPP Sec. 7.1 ("Lowest self-energy correction and LO-TO splitting") obtains it diagrammatically from this term.
  - TO modes ($\mathbf P\perp\mathbf q$) have $\rho_{\rm ion}=0$ and feel no Coulomb field. They couple only to transverse photons, which gives polaritons.

### 4.8 Limiting cases
- **$c\to\infty$:** $\hbar cq\to\infty$, so transverse photons decouple from low-energy matter (vertices $\propto1/c$). Only $H^0_{\rm Lph}$ survives, and the theory reduces to Giustino's Coulomb-only Hamiltonian (Eq. 62 there) for valence electrons and Born-charge dipoles.
- **$Z^*\to0$:** ions decouple from both fields. Only the short-range force constants (from DFT) remain, and there is no polar coupling.
- **Static ions ($\dot{\mathbf P}=0$):** $\mathbf P$ is a fixed background, $\rho_{\rm ion}$ a fixed charge, and (180) the usual electrostatics.

### 4.9 Dipole approximation for ions
- The ions enter only through point dipoles at cell positions $\mathbf R^0_i$, Eqs. (167), (241), with the field evaluated at $\mathbf R^0_i$.
- This is the leading order of a multipole expansion. It is valid for $q\ll1/a$, which covers all photon wavevectors.
- For the Coulomb term at large $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ (local fields), the dropped phases $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_a}$ and higher multipoles (quadrupoles) would matter.
- The short-range (deformation-potential) e–ph coupling of Sec. 2 is **not** in $V'$; electrons see only the frozen $V_{\rm cr}$.

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of the EPP manuscript

1. **Giustino's Eq. (1)** is $H=\sum\varepsilon c^+c+\sum\hbar\omega(a^+a+\frac12)+N_p^{-1/2}\sum g\,c^+c(a+a^+)+$ Debye–Waller.
   - EPP's $H_0$ (236) reproduces the first two lines exactly: KS bands $E_{n\mathbf k}$ and DFT phonons $\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$. It **adds free transverse photons** $\sum\hbar cq(b^+b+\frac12)$.
   - EPP's interaction $V'$ (237) has **no explicit $g$**. The polar e–ph coupling is generated by the fields:
     - (a) electrostatically, via the $\rho_{\rm e}\rho_{\rm ion}$ cross term of the Coulomb Hamiltonian (235), which gives the bare Fröhlich vertex;
     - (b) electrodynamically, via photon exchange between the electron vertex $-\frac1c\mathbf j_{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf A$ and the ion vertex $-\frac1c\sum Z^*\pi\cdot\mathbf A/m_a$.
   - The full $g$ appears only after the photon propagator is dressed (Secs. 5–7).
   - The short-range (deformation-potential) part of $g$ (EPP Sec. 2, Giustino's DFPT $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}$) is not in $V'$.
2. **Giustino Sec. IV.A, Eqs. (62)–(67)** has $H=T_{\rm e}+T_{\rm n}+U_{\rm ee}+U_{\rm nn}+U_{\rm en}$, all via the instantaneous $v=e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, with no vector potential.
   - EPP derives exactly this Coulomb structure, (235), as the **longitudinal sector of the electromagnetic field**, $\frac1{8\pi}\int\mathbf E_l^2$ plus Gauss's law, and adds the transverse sector.
   - Differences:
     - (i) "nuclei" enter only through the induced dipole density $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$, with $\mathbf P$ built from Born charges. The static lattice charge is in $V_{\rm cr}$ and is not expanded in displacements as in Giustino Eq. (107).
     - (ii) EPP's (235) keeps self-interaction terms. Giustino's (64) subtracts $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$ and (65) excludes $\kappa p=\kappa'p'$.

3. **Screened Coulomb interaction $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$** (Giustino Sec. IV.B.2–3):
   - The e–e part of (235) is the bare $v$. Dressing it with the electronic RPA polarization gives $W^{\rm e}$.

   - EPP generalizes $v\to D=D^{0,l}+D^{0,t}$ (Sec. 4.7, Sec. 6.0.4, Eq. 375), i.e. the screened interaction acquires transverse, retarded components.
   - For the user's RPA/BSE work: the "$v$" in $W=v+v\chi v$ is precisely $H^0_{\rm Lph}$. Transverse photons are the corrections beyond it, which matter only for $q\lesssim\omega/c$: polaritons and radiative effects.

   - EPP Sec. 3 (Eq. 131) inserted $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ "ad hoc". The p. 35 remark ("all ad hoc interventions should be avoided because all electron/phonon screening processes will be consistently included") announces that $\epsilon^\infty$ will instead be produced by the electronic RPA photon self-energy.
   - The electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex (Sec. 7.2) then reduces to Verdi–Giustino for $c\to\infty$.

## 8. Source issues

1. **$\rho_{\rm e}$ is used both as a number density and as a charge density.**
   - In (179)–(180), $\rho_{\rm e}$ is the *charge* density, by continuity with $\mathbf j_{\rm e}=e\sum\mathbf v\delta$ (166).
   - In (234), (239), $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ is a *number* density, yet it is used in Gauss's law (234) and in the Coulomb energy (235), which require charge density.
   - In (237) the $A^2$ term $\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int\rho_{\rm e}A^2$ uses the number density correctly, while the last (Coulomb) term of the same equation needs the charge density.
   - **Corrected (confident):** in (234)–(235) and the last term of (237), $\rho_{\rm e}\to e\Psi^+\Psi$; keep $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ in the $A^2$ term.
2. **Two different "electronic currents" share the symbol $\mathbf j_{\rm e}$.** (166) is the kinetic current $e\sum\mathbf v_e\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_e)$ (gauge invariant, includes the diamagnetic part $-\frac{e^2}{m_ec}\Psi^+\Psi\mathbf A$). (238) is the paramagnetic current only. In (237), $\mathbf j_{\rm e}$ must be the paramagnetic one, (238), for consistency with (186). Worth stating explicitly on the page.
3. **Gauss's law (180) by time integration.** The integration constant (a static charge distribution) is set to zero without comment. In the temporal gauge Gauss's law is a constraint, not an equation of motion. Quantum mechanically it holds on physical states, not as an operator identity. Its use in (234) to rewrite $H^0_{\rm Lph}$ therefore implicitly restricts to the physical subspace. This is a conceptual gap, not an algebra error.
4. **Polarization-vector convention (200)–(201).** "$\bar{\mathbf q}_0=\mathbf q/\|\mathbf q\|$ and $\overline{-\mathbf q}_0=\mathbf q/\|\mathbf q\|$" assigns the same unit vector to $\pm\mathbf q$. Then $\mathbf e_3(\mathbf q)$ cannot equal $\hat{\mathbf q}$ for all $\mathbf q$; it is $\pm\hat{\mathbf q}$ by half-space.
   - This is legitimate as a basis choice, but non-standard: textbooks use $\mathbf e_3(-\mathbf q)=-\mathbf e_3(\mathbf q)$.
   - Later (p. 46) $e_{3\mu}(\mathbf q)=q_\mu/q$ is used. Only the product $e_{3\mu}(\mathbf q)e_{3\nu}(-\mathbf q)=q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$ enters (227), so the results are unaffected.
5. **Handedness.** (197)–(198) define $\mathbf e_1\times\mathbf e_2=-\mathbf e_3$, a left-handed triad, consistent with Fig. 6. Harmless, but readers may expect right-handed.
6. **"Unitary" transformation (196).** It is real orthogonal. Fine, but the page should say "orthogonal (rotation)".
7. **Index order in (222).** The left side and the overbrace are printed as $e_{\alpha\mu}(\mathbf q)p_{A\mu\mathbf q}$ and $e_{\alpha\mu}(\mathbf q)e_{\mu\beta}(\mathbf q)$; the intended form is $e_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ (component $\mu$ of $\mathbf e_\alpha$). Similarly (223) prints $e_{3\mu}$, which means $e_{\mu3}=\mu$-component of $\mathbf e_3$. Notation only.
8. **Phonon/photon labels in the text below (219).** It says "phonons $a^+_{\mathbf q\sigma}$ and $a_{\mathbf q\sigma}$ … photons $b^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}$ and $b_{\mathbf q\alpha}$", but the equation uses $\alpha$ for phonons and $\sigma$ for photons. The labels are swapped in the sentence.
9. **Longitudinal ladder operators.** (208)–(209) are applied to $\alpha=3$ with frequency scale $cq$, although $H^0_{\rm Lph}$ has no $\tilde A_3$ term to fix it. The $a_{\mathbf q3}$ are therefore not normal-mode operators, and the choice is arbitrary. The text partly acknowledges this ("they do not diagonalise $H^0_{\rm Lph}$"); Sec. 4.7 later sets $\omega_{\mathbf q3}=0^+$ (262). Worth a footnote on the page.
10. **Dipoles at the cell origin.** (134), (167), (183), (186), (241) place the dipole of atom $a$ and evaluate $\mathbf A$ at $\mathbf R^0_i$, whereas $V_{\rm cr}$ (136) uses atomic sites $\mathbf R^0_{ia}$. This is a long-wavelength (dipole) approximation that drops the basis phases $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_a}$ appearing in Verdi–Giustino. It is not stated explicitly.
11. **Self-interaction / normal ordering in (235).** The e–e term contains the electron self-interaction (Giustino's Eq. (64) subtracts it with $-\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$), and the i–i term contains the infinite self-energy of each point dipole. Normal ordering and exclusion of self-terms are needed but not mentioned.
12. **Double counting** (acknowledged on p. 40 but unresolved here).
    - $E_{n\mathbf k}$ (KS) already contain Hartree+xc, and (235) re-adds the bare e–e Coulomb.
    - DFT $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$ may contain the dipole–dipole non-analytic term, which (235) re-adds via $\rho_{\rm ion}\rho_{\rm ion}$.
    - A consistent scheme requires short-range force constants and a subtraction (or a $G_0W_0$-like convention) for the electronic part.
13. **Canonical vs kinetic ionic momentum.** p. 40 states that $\pi_{ia\mu}$ "remains identical" to Eq. (84), where $P=m_a\dot Q$. With (183), $\pi=m_a\dot Q+\frac1cZ^*A$, so the identification holds for the canonical operator's mode expansion (Schrödinger picture), not for $m_a\dot Q$. Potentially confusing; the physical ionic velocity is $(\pi-Z^*A/c)/m_a$.
14. **Longitudinal $\mathbf A$ in $V'$.** The $\mathbf A$ in (237) includes $\alpha=3$ (Eq. 244), so the $\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$ vertex also couples to longitudinal "photons" while $H^0_{\rm Lph}$ already holds the Coulomb energy. Consistency rests on $D^{0l}=0$ (Sec. 4.7, Eq. 263) and on the connection formulas of Sec. 6. Not an error within this range, but readers should know this is where the subtlety sits.
15. **Commutator prefactors.** In the checks after (209) and after (217), $\sqrt{q'/q}$ is silently set to 1. Correct because of $\delta_{\mathbf q',-\mathbf q}$, but unstated.
16. **Charge-sign convention.** Sec. 4 uses $e$ as the signed electron charge (Lorentz force $+e\mathbf E$), while Sec. 3 wrote the electron charge as $-e$ (e.g. $\Delta V=-e\sum\phi$). Readers combining (131) with (237) must track this.
17. **Fourier sign.** (190) uses $e^{+i\mathbf q\mathbf r}$, while (226)–(229) use $e^{-i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}$. Inconsequential for these even kernels, but inconsistent.
18. **Unnumbered key equations.** The field-form Lagrangian (p. 35), the expanded $H$ (p. 38), the minimal-coupling $H$ and the $H_0'$ split (p. 39) carry no numbers. Here they are cited by page.
