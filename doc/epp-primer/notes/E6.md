## 1. Overview (plain language)

By p. 47 the author has built a Hamiltonian for valence electrons, harmonic ions and the full electromagnetic field in the temporal gauge ($\Phi=0$, Gaussian units). He has also shown that the longitudinal part of the field "Hamiltonian" is really the **instantaneous Coulomb interaction** between electronic and ionic (polarization) charges, Eq. (235). This range does three things.

**(i) It fixes the bookkeeping (Sec. 4.4.4).** The Coulomb term is moved from the "free" Hamiltonian into the interaction. The truly free part $H_0$ is then: Kohn–Sham-like band electrons, $3s$ harmonic phonon branches, and **two transverse photon polarizations**. The interaction $V'$ has five pieces: electronic paramagnetic $-\frac1c\int\mathbf j_e\cdot\mathbf A$, electronic diamagnetic $\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int\rho_e A^2$, their two **ionic analogues** (Born-charge currents $Z^*\pi/m_a$ coupling to $\mathbf A$, and a $Z^*Z^*AA/m_a$ term), and the Coulomb term. The bare Coulomb term already contains the bare Fröhlich coupling.

**(ii) It builds the free propagators (Secs. 4.5–4.7).** The free phonon propagator is $F_\sigma=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}$. The free transverse photon propagator is $D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}=-4\pi c\,(\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/q^2)/(\omega^2-c^2q^2+i\eta)$. The *longitudinal* photon propagator built from the interaction-picture field vanishes, because $\mathbf A_l$ has no dynamics in $H_0$. Its physics is "hidden" in the Coulomb term. A pragmatic rule for temperature is stated: set $n_B=0$ in free boson propagators, but keep the Fermi functions $f(T)$ in electronic bubbles.

**(iii) It sets up the photon Dyson equation (Sec. 5).** For now the Coulomb term is dropped, so only transverse photons propagate. In RPA, $D^t=D^{0t}+D^{0t}\Pi^{\rm RPA}D^t$, with $\Pi^{\rm RPA}=\Pi^{\rm e}+\Pi^{\rm ion}$. Each part is a **diamagnetic** (contact, $A^2$) term plus a **paramagnetic** (current–current) term. The ions are written to look exactly like electrons, so one formalism treats both. The ionic diamagnetic term is derived explicitly. The electronic self-energy in a crystal is then written in the gauge-invariant form $\Pi^{\rm para}(\mathbf q,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(\mathbf q,0)$: a Lindhard-like current–current bubble with Bloch current vertices $j_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf G)$. Its strictly-2D version is $l\,\Pi_{\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0}$.

For an RPA/BSE practitioner, the photon propagator $D$ plays the role that $W$ plays for Coulomb physics, and $\Pi$ is the current–current analogue of $\chi^0$. Later sections use this machinery to rebuild $\epsilon^\infty$, the LO–TO splitting and the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **Total Hamiltonian (p. 48).** Start from $H=H_0'+V$, Eqs. (186)/(219). Move $H^0_{\rm Lph}$ (the Coulomb term, Eq. 235) out of $H_0'$ and into the interaction: $H=H_0+V'$ with $H_0=H_0'-H^0_{\rm Lph}$ (Eq. 236) and $V'=V+H^0_{\rm Lph}$ (Eq. 237). From here on, Greek indices in sums are Cartesian.
2. **Operators (pp. 48–49).** Electronic current (238), density (239), field operator in the Bloch basis (240). Ionic charge $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ with the Born-charge polarization (241). Phonon displacement and momentum (242)–(243). Photon field and conjugate momentum with three polarizations (244)–(245). The author announces that only bulk 3D is treated, "the transition to q2D is straightforward".
3. **Temperature (p. 50).** Matsubara propagators, after $i\omega_n\to\omega+i\eta$ (246), become retarded ones: phonon (247), current–current (248). Free retarded boson propagators are $T$-independent; the electron bubble depends on $T$ only via $f$. Compare with the real-time time-ordered functions at finite $T$, (249)–(250). Rule: in the time-ordered ($T=0$) perturbation theory, put $n_{\mathbf q\sigma}=0$ in boson lines and keep $f(T)$ in bubbles, "with minor interventions around the term $i\eta$" (i.e. make it retarded).
4. **Free phonon GF (p. 51).** Definition (251), evaluation (252) with Bose occupation (253), set $n=0$ (254), Fourier transform (255), result (256).
5. **Free photon GF (pp. 52–53).** Definition (257). Split $\mathbf A=\mathbf A_t+\mathbf A_l$ (258); cross terms vanish (259). Transverse part: interaction-picture evolution with $\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}=qc$, $n=0$, Fourier transform (260), the transverse projector $\sum_{\alpha=1,2}e_{\mu\alpha}e_{\nu\alpha}=\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$, giving (261).
6. **Longitudinal photon GF (pp. 54–55).** $\mathbf A_l$ does not appear in $H_0$, so it is static in the interaction picture: $\omega_{\mathbf q3}=0^+$ (262). The time-ordered propagator is proportional to $\omega_{\mathbf q3}$ and vanishes (263). The retarded propagator (264)–(267) also vanishes in the limit (268), since $[A_l,A_l]=0$. The longitudinal physics is not lost: $\mathbf E^l=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}^l$ obeys Poisson's equation (269) and lives in the Coulomb term (235). (It reappears as $D^{0l}=-4\pi c\,q_\mu q_\nu/(q^2\omega^2)$ in Sec. 6, Eq. (374), p. 83.)
7. **Purely transverse problem (p. 56).** Drop the Coulomb term: interaction (270). Define an ionic "density tensor" (271) and an ionic current density (272), so that the ionic terms look like the electronic ones (273). Promote $\pi_{ia\mu}$ to a continuous field (274).
8. **Photon propagator and its expansion (p. 57).** Exact time-ordered propagator in the interacting ground state (275). Longitudinal pairings drop out ($D^{0l}=0$), so only $A^t$ propagates. Interaction-picture expansion over connected diagrams (276). In RPA this sums to the Dyson equation (277) ("following Lecture 9").
9. **Self-energy decomposition (p. 58).** $\Pi^{\rm RPA}=\Pi^{\rm e}+\Pi^{\rm ion}$ (278). Each part is diamagnetic plus paramagnetic: electronic (279), ionic (280). The ionic paramagnetic term has the same form as the electronic one; the ionic diamagnetic term is derived explicitly.
10. **Ionic diamagnetic term (pp. 58–59).** First-order term from the $A\rho^{\rm ion}A$ vertex. Contract four photon operators into two $D^{0t}$ (combinatorial factor 2). Fourier-transform the time convolution, insert $\delta(\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2)$, read off $\Pi^{\rm dia,ion}_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$.
11. **Electronic $\Pi$ in a crystal (pp. 59–60).** Gauge-invariant form (281): paramagnetic minus its static value, which replaces the diamagnetic term (proved in "Lecture 9" for q2D). Paramagnetic matrix (282), current vertices (283)–(284). Transition to q2D (285) and the strictly-2D limit $\Pi^{\rm 2D}=l\,\Pi_{\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0}$.
12. **Ionic paramagnetic term begins (p. 60).** Insert the ionic current (272) into the definition: (286). Write the $\pi\pi$ correlator in phonon operators: (287). Continued on p. 61 (next range): the result is a sum over phonon branches of $Z^*Z^*\hbar\omega_\sigma/(2\sqrt{m_am_b})$ times phonon-propagator poles. The Bose factors cancel in the principal part, as they must for a harmonic system.

## 3. Key equations

### Conventions used in this range
- **Units: Gaussian.** Coulomb $e^2/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, $V(q)=4\pi/q^2$ (Eq. 229), $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}$ (temporal gauge $\Phi=0$), $H_{\rm field}=\frac1{8\pi}\int(E^2+B^2)$, minimal coupling $\mathbf p-\frac ec\mathbf A$. $\hbar$ is explicit throughout.
- **Charge $e$** is the (signed) particle charge in $\mathbf p-\frac ec\mathbf A$ (Eq. 186). Its sign is not stated; for electrons $e=-|e|$. Only $e^2$ appears in $\Pi$.
- **Born charges** $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$ carry the dimension of charge (EPP $Z^*=|e|\times$ the dimensionless Born charge of Giustino/Verdi–Giustino). The first index is the field/dipole direction, the second the displacement direction.
- **Normalization.** $N$ is the number of unit cells (phonon expansion, $1/\sqrt N$). $\Omega$ is the **normalization (BvK crystal) volume** for the photon field: $\mathbf A(\mathbf r)=\Omega^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q}\mathbf A_{\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}$ (Eq. 190), and $\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q}\to\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}$ (p. 53). The same $\Omega$ multiplies the electronic bubble (282). $\Omega_\infty$ in (283) denotes integration over the whole crystal. The unit-cell volume is not named in this range; I call it $\Omega_0$ when needed.
- **Time Fourier transform:** $F(\omega)=\int dt\,e^{i\omega t}F(t)$ (Eqs. 255, 260), i.e. $e^{-i\omega t}$ time dependence. Matsubara continuation: $i\omega_n\to\omega+i\eta$ (246).
- **Space Fourier transform of propagators:** $D(\mathbf r-\mathbf r';\omega)=\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}D(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (p. 53). Crystal matrices: $j(\mathbf G)=\int d\mathbf r\,e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}j(\mathbf r)$ (283).
- **Propagator signs.** Phonon: $F=-\frac i\hbar\langle T QQ\rangle$ (251), as in Mahan. Photon: $D=+\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle TAA\rangle$ (257), **opposite sign and an extra $1/c$**. With this choice $D$ is the response of $\mathbf A$ to an external current ($\mathbf A^{\rm ext}=D^0\mathbf j^{\rm ext}$), and $\Pi$ is the response of the induced current to $\mathbf A$ ($\mathbf j^{\rm ind}=\Pi\mathbf A$). The paper states this on p. 83 (Sec. 6.0.4). I checked it against the Kubo formula with $H'=-\frac1c\int\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$: it is consistent, and $\Pi^{\rm dia}=-ne^2/mc$ is then the London/Drude $\mathbf j=-\frac{ne^2}{mc}\mathbf A$.
- **Dimensions** (my check): $[A^2]=$ energy/length, $[D(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';\omega)]=$ time/length², $[D(\mathbf q,\omega)]=$ time·length, $[\Pi(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';\omega)]=1/(\text{time}\cdot\text{length}^4)$, $[\Pi(\mathbf q,\omega)]=1/(\text{time}\cdot\text{length})$, so $D\Pi$ is dimensionless, and $4\pi c\,\Pi(\mathbf q,\omega)$ has units of frequency². All of (279)–(282) pass this check.
- **Index conventions.** Electrons: band $n$, wave vector $\mathbf k\in$ BZ. Phonons: cell $i$, atom $a=1..s$, Cartesian $\mu$, branch $\alpha$ or $\sigma=1..3s$. Photons: $\mathbf q$, polarization $\alpha=1,2$ (transverse), $3$ (longitudinal). Photon operators are $b_{\mathbf q\alpha}$ in (236), (244)–(245), but $a_{\mathbf q\alpha}$ in (258) and pp. 52–55 (notation clash with phonons; see Source issues).

### 3.1 Sec. 4.4.3 (end) – 4.4.4: Hamiltonian

**(235) Longitudinal part of the "free" photon Hamiltonian = instantaneous Coulomb interaction**
$$H^0_{\rm Lph}=\frac12\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\,\frac{[\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)+\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf r)][\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r')+\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf r')]}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\tag{235}$$
- Obtained (pp. 45–47) from $H^0_{\rm Lph}=2\pi c^2\sum_{\mathbf q}\tilde p_{A3\mathbf q}\tilde p_{A3-\mathbf q}$ (215), using $p_A=-E/4\pi c$, $e_{3\mu}=q_\mu/q$, integration by parts and Gauss's law $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi(\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion})$ (234).
- $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ (as written; see Source issues: it must be $e\Psi^+\Psi$ here). $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$, with $\mathbf P$ from (241).
- Expanding gives three terms: $\frac12\rho_eV\rho_e$ ($e$–$e$), $\rho_eV\rho_{\rm ion}$ ($e$–ion = **bare polar/Fröhlich coupling** of electrons to ionic dipoles), and $\frac12\rho_{\rm ion}V\rho_{\rm ion}$ (dipole–dipole between ions; the source of LO–TO splitting).

**(236) Fully free Hamiltonian**
$$H_0=\sum_n\sum_{\mathbf k\in{\rm BZ}}E_{n\mathbf k}c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in{\rm BZ}}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)\left(a^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+\tfrac12\right)+\sum_{\mathbf q\sigma=1,2}\hbar qc\left\{b^+_{\mathbf q\sigma}b_{\mathbf q\sigma}+\tfrac12\right\}\tag{236}$$
- $H_0=H_0'-H^0_{\rm Lph}$, where $H_0'$ is Eq. (219).
- $E_{n\mathbf k}$: band energies, taken as DFT Kohn–Sham energies (p. 40: "part of the electron–electron interaction is already included in $E_{n\mathbf k}$"). $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$: harmonic phonon frequencies, which (p. 40) already contain the "short-range" part of the ion–electron–ion interaction (adiabatic DFT screening). $c,a,b$: electron, phonon and photon annihilation operators. Only the **two transverse** photon polarizations ($\sigma=1,2$) are free oscillators with $\hbar\omega=\hbar qc$.
- Assumptions: harmonic phonons, independent quasiparticle electrons, no longitudinal photon oscillator.

**(237) Full interaction $V'=V+H^0_{\rm Lph}$**
$$\begin{aligned}V'=&-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac{e^2}{2m_{\rm e}c^2}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\mathbf A^2(\mathbf r)\\&-\frac1c\sum_{ia\alpha\mu}\frac1{m_{\rm a}}Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}\pi_{ia\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)+\frac1{2c^2}\sum_{ia\mu\alpha\beta}\frac1{m_{\rm a}}Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}Z^*_{{\rm a}\beta\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)A_\beta(\mathbf R^0_i)\\&+\frac12\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\,\frac{[\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)+\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf r)][\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r')+\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf r')]}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\end{aligned}\tag{237}$$
Term by term:
1. **Electronic paramagnetic** $-\frac1c\int\mathbf j_e\cdot\mathbf A$. It comes from $-\frac{e}{2m_ec}\sum_e(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A+\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf p)$ in (186). I checked the second-quantized form: integrating by parts gives exactly (238).
2. **Electronic diamagnetic** $\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int\rho_eA^2$ ($\rho_e$ = number density here).
3. **Ionic paramagnetic**: the Born-charge dipole current $Z^*\pi/m_a\approx Z^*\dot Q=\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}$ couples to $\mathbf A$ at the equilibrium site (dipole approximation).
4. **Ionic diamagnetic**: from expanding $[\pi-\frac1c Z^*A]^2/2m_a$ (Eq. 186). The sum over $\mu$ (displacement direction) makes it $(Z^*Z^{*T})_{\alpha\beta}/m_a$.
5. **Coulomb** $e$–$e$, $e$–ion, ion–ion (235).
- Italic remark on p. 48: the longitudinal Coulomb part is taken out of $H_0'$ and added to the interaction, $H_0'\to H_0$, $V\to V'$.

**(238)–(241) Electronic and ionic densities and currents**
$$\mathbf j_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)=\frac{e\hbar}{2im_{\rm e}}\left\{\Psi^+(\mathbf r)\nabla\Psi(\mathbf r)-[\nabla\Psi^+(\mathbf r)]\Psi(\mathbf r)\right\}\tag{238}$$
$$\rho_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)=\Psi^+(\mathbf r)\Psi(\mathbf r)\tag{239}$$
$$\Psi(\mathbf r)=\sum_n\sum_{\mathbf k\in{\rm BZ}}c_{n\mathbf k}\phi_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)\tag{240}$$
$$\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,\qquad P_\mu(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia\nu}Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{241}$$
- $\phi_{n\mathbf k}$: Bloch (KS) orbitals. $\mathbf P$: ionic dipole-moment density operator (point dipoles $Z^*Q$ at the lattice sites). The paper places all atoms of cell $i$ at $\mathbf R^0_i$ (see Source issues).

**(242)–(243) Phonon displacement and momentum** (standard; $N$ cells, $m_{\rm a}$ ionic mass, $e_{{\rm a}\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ eigenvectors)
$$Q_{ia\mu}=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in{\rm BZ}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_{\rm a}\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}\,e_{{\rm a}\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right)e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_i}\tag{242}$$
$$\pi_{ia\mu}=-\frac{i}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in{\rm BZ}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\rm a}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}{2}}\,e_{{\rm a}\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}-a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right)e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_i}\tag{243}$$
- These are identical to Giustino's Eq. (20)-type expansion, with $N_p\to N$ and physical masses (no reference mass $M_0$).

**(244)–(245) Electromagnetic field and conjugate momentum** (three polarizations)
$$\mathbf A(\mathbf r)=\sum_{\mathbf q\alpha=1,2,3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi c\hbar}{q\Omega}}\,\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf q)\left\{b_{\mathbf q\alpha}+b^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right\}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\tag{244}$$
$$\mathbf p_{\rm A}(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_{\mathbf q\alpha=1,2,3}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar q}{8\pi\Omega c}}\,\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf q)\left\{b_{\mathbf q\alpha}-b^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right\}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\tag{245}$$
- $\alpha=1,2$: transverse photons. $\alpha=3$: longitudinal, $\mathbf e_3=\hat{\mathbf q}$ (Eqs. 197–200). $\mathbf p_A=-\mathbf E/4\pi c$. The three polarizations are needed for the canonical commutator $[A_\mu(\mathbf r),p_{A\nu}(\mathbf r')]=i\hbar\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$ (p. 45). Note that the $\alpha=3$ amplitude $\sqrt{2\pi c\hbar/q\Omega}$ copies the transverse oscillator normalization, although $\alpha=3$ is not an oscillator in $H_0$.

### 3.2 Sec. 4.5: Temperature formalism

**(246) Analytic continuation**
$$i\omega_n\to\omega+i\eta\tag{246}$$

**(247) Free phonon propagator: Matsubara → retarded**
$$F_\sigma(\mathbf q,i\omega_n)=-\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{\omega_n^2+\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)}\;\longrightarrow\;F^r_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{(\omega+i\eta)^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)}\tag{247}$$
- "Bosonic propagators do not depend on $T$": true for **free** (harmonic) bosons, because the commutator of free fields is a c-number.

**(248) Current–current correlator: Matsubara → retarded**
$$\Pi_\mu(i\omega_n,\mathbf q)=\frac2\Omega\sum_{\mathbf k}\frac{f(\xi_{\mathbf k})-f(\xi_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q})}{i\omega_n+\xi_{\mathbf k}-\xi_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}\left|j^\mu_{\mathbf k,\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\right|^2\;\to\;\Pi^r_\mu(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac2\Omega\sum_{\mathbf k}\frac{f(E_{\mathbf k})-f(E_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q})}{\hbar\omega+E_{\mathbf k}-E_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+i\eta}\left|j^\mu_{\mathbf k,\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\right|^2\tag{248}$$
- $\xi=E-\mu$, $\mu$ the chemical potential. The factor 2 is spin. This is a generic correlator: its overall sign and $1/c$ differ from the physical $\Pi$ of (282) (see Source issues).

**(249)–(250) Real-time time-ordered functions at finite $T$**
$$F^T_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=2\frac{\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{\hbar}\left[\frac{1+n_{\mathbf q\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}-\frac{n_{\mathbf q\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)-i\eta}\right]\tag{249}$$
$$\Pi^T_\mu(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac2\Omega\sum_{\mathbf k}\frac{f(E_{\mathbf k})-f(E_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q})}{\hbar\omega+E_{\mathbf k}-E_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+i\eta\,{\rm sgn}(E_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-E_{\mathbf k})}\left|j^\mu_{\mathbf k,\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\right|^2\tag{250}$$
- (249) equals $\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\hbar}\left[\mathcal P\frac1{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}-i\pi(1+2n)\delta(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)\right]$: the $T$-dependence sits only in the imaginary part (my rewriting).
- **The rule (p. 50):** compare (247) with (249) and (248) with (250). To include $T\neq0$ correctly in the $T=0$ time-ordered formalism, set $n_{\mathbf q\sigma}=0$ in the boson propagator but keep $f=f(T)$ in density–density and current–current bubbles, with "minor interventions" on $i\eta$ (i.e. use the retarded prescription). This is a heuristic shortcut; see Concepts (d) and Source issues.

### 3.3 Sec. 4.6: Free phonon Green's function

**(251) Definition** ("see in Mahan")
$$F_\sigma(\mathbf q;t-t')=-\frac i\hbar\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right|T\{Q_{\mathbf q\sigma}(t)Q_{-\mathbf q\sigma}(t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right\rangle,\qquad Q_{\mathbf q\sigma}(t)=a_{\mathbf q\sigma}(t)+a^+_{-\mathbf q\sigma}(t)\tag{251}$$
- $Q_{\mathbf q\sigma}$ is dimensionless (no $\sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}$ factor), so $F$ has units of 1/energy and $\hbar F$ has units of 1/frequency, as Giustino's $D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}$ (Eq. 132).

**(252)–(253)**
$$F_\sigma(\mathbf q;t-t')=-\frac i\hbar\Big\{e^{-i\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)(t-t')}\left[\theta(t-t')(1+n_{\mathbf q\sigma})+\theta(t'-t)n_{\mathbf q\sigma}\right]+e^{i\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)(t-t')}\left[\theta(t-t')n_{\mathbf q\sigma}+\theta(t'-t)(1+n_{\mathbf q\sigma})\right]\Big\}\tag{252}$$
$$n_{\mathbf q\alpha}=\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right|a^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}a_{\mathbf q\alpha}\left|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right\rangle=\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)/k_BT}-1}\tag{253}$$

**(254)–(256)** With $n_{\mathbf q}=0$:
$$F_\sigma(\mathbf q;t-t')=-\frac i\hbar\left\{e^{-i\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)(t-t')}\theta(t-t')+e^{i\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)(t-t')}\theta(t'-t)\right\}\tag{254}$$
$$F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega t}F_\sigma(\mathbf q;t-t')\tag{255}$$
$$\boxed{F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}}\tag{256}$$
- I verified (254)→(256): the bracket gives $\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_\sigma+i\eta}-\frac1{\omega+\omega_\sigma-i\eta}$. This is Giustino's $D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}$ (Eqs. 132/134) times $1/\hbar$.

### 3.4 Sec. 4.7: Free photon Green's function

**(257) Definition**
$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t-t')=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right|T\{A_\mu(\mathbf r,t)A_\nu(\mathbf r',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right\rangle\tag{257}$$
- $|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\rangle$: photon vacuum. The $+i/\hbar c$ sign makes $D$ the $\mathbf A$-response to a current (see Conventions).

**(258)–(259) Transverse/longitudinal split**
$$\mathbf A(\mathbf r,t)=\underbrace{\sum_{\mathbf q\alpha=1,2}\sqrt{\tfrac{2\pi c\hbar}{q\Omega}}\,\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf q)\{a_{\mathbf q\alpha}(t)+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}(t)\}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}}_{\mathbf A_t(\mathbf r,t)}+\underbrace{\sum_{\mathbf q}\sqrt{\tfrac{2\pi c\hbar}{q\Omega}}\,\mathbf e_3(\mathbf q)\{a_{\mathbf q3}(t)+a^+_{-\mathbf q3}(t)\}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}}_{\mathbf A_l(\mathbf r,t)}\tag{258}$$
$$D^0_{\mu\nu}=\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{A^t_\mu A^t_\nu\}\rangle}_{D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}}+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{A^l_\mu A^l_\nu\}\rangle}_{D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}}\tag{259}$$
- Cross terms vanish because transverse and longitudinal operators are independent.

**Transverse, intermediate steps (pp. 52–53, unnumbered).**
$$D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t-t')=\frac{2\pi i}{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac1q e_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e_{\nu\alpha}(-\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\left\{e^{-i\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}(t-t')}\theta(t-t')+e^{i\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}(t-t')}\theta(t'-t)\right\},\quad\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}=qc$$
(after the "$T\neq0$ trick" $n_{\mathbf q\alpha}=0$; the prefactor is $\frac{i}{\hbar c}\cdot\frac{2\pi c\hbar}{\Omega}=\frac{2\pi i}{\Omega}$).

**(260) Frequency domain** (corrected; the printed version has a stray extra factor $2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}$, see Source issues)
$$D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega t}D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t)=-\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac1q e_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e_{\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\alpha}+i\eta}\tag{260}$$
- Uses $e_{\nu\alpha}(-\mathbf q)=e_{\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ (Eq. 201).

**Transverse projector** (from Eqs. 197–200; completeness of $\{\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3\}$):
$$\sum_{\alpha=1,2}e_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e_{\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\mu\nu}-e_{\mu3}(\mathbf q)e_{\nu3}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2}$$

**(261) Free transverse photon propagator**
$$\boxed{D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-4\pi c\left[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2}\right]\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q}+i\eta},\qquad\omega_{\mathbf q}=qc}\tag{261}$$
- The intermediate real-space forms are $D^{0t}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r';\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}[\ldots]\frac1{\omega^2-\omega_q^2+i\eta}=-4\pi c\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}[\ldots]\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega_q^2+i\eta}$.
- Meaning: this is the Green's function of $(\omega^2/c^2-q^2)\mathbf A_t=-\frac{4\pi}{c}\mathbf j_t$, i.e. $\mathbf A_t=D^{0t}\mathbf j_t$, with the Feynman $+i\eta$ (time-ordered, $T=0$). Static limit: $D^{0t}(\mathbf q,0)=4\pi P_T/(cq^2)$, which is Biot–Savart, $\mathbf A=4\pi\mathbf j/(cq^2)$.

**(262) Longitudinal photons have no dynamics in $H_0$**
$$i\hbar\dot a_{\mathbf q3}(t)=[a_{\mathbf q3}(t),H_0]=0\Rightarrow a_{\mathbf q3}(t)=a_{\mathbf q3}e^{-i0^+t},\qquad\omega_{\mathbf q3}=0^+\tag{262}$$

**Longitudinal propagator (unnumbered, p. 54), corrected as for (260):**
$$D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';\omega)=-\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^3}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\frac{\omega_{\mathbf q3}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q3}+i\eta},\qquad D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-4\pi\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^3}\frac{\omega_{\mathbf q3}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q3}+i\eta}$$

**(263) Longitudinal propagator in the $\Phi=0$ gauge vanishes**
$$\boxed{D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=0}\tag{263}$$
- Caveat stated by the author: inserting the time-independent $A_l$ directly into (259) gives a term $\propto\delta(\omega)$. So (263) holds everywhere except at $\omega=0$.

**(264)–(268) Retarded check**
$$D^{0l,r}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\theta(t-t')\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right|\left[A^l_\mu(\mathbf r,t),A^l_\nu(\mathbf r',t')\right]_-\left|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right\rangle\tag{264}$$

$$\lim_{\omega_{\mathbf q3}\to0^+}D^{0l,r}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=0\tag{268}$$
- I verified (266)→(267). Equivalently: $[A^l,A^l]=0$ when $A_l$ is time-independent, so the retarded function is identically zero.

**(269) Where the longitudinal physics went**

- $\mathbf A^l$ is static only in the interaction picture. The full (Heisenberg) $\mathbf A^l$ evolves and is tied to the charge by Gauss's law. That is exactly what (235) encodes. ### 3.5 Sec. 5: Perturbative expansion of the transverse photon propagator

**(270) Transverse-only interaction** ((237) without the Coulomb term)
$$V=-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac{e^2}{2m_{\rm e}c^2}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\mathbf A^2(\mathbf r)-\frac1c\sum_{ia\alpha\mu}\frac1{m_{\rm a}}Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}\pi_{ia\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)+\frac1{2c^2}\sum_{ia\mu\alpha\beta}\frac1{m_{\rm a}}Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}Z^*_{{\rm a}\beta\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)A_\beta(\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{270}$$

**(271) Ionic density tensor**
$$\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)=\frac{m_0}{e^2}\sum_{ia\mu}\frac1{m_{\rm a}}Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}Z^*_{{\rm a}\beta\mu}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{271}$$
- $m_0$: "some standard nucleon mass", an arbitrary reference mass that cancels in every physical quantity. The prefactor makes $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}$ a number density weighted by $(m_0/m_a)(Z^*Z^*/e^2)$, so that the ionic $A^2$ term looks like the electronic one with $m_e\to m_0$.

**(272) Ionic current density**
$$j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia\mu}\frac1{m_{\rm a}}Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}\pi_{{\rm a}\mu}(\mathbf r)\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{272}$$
- This is the paramagnetic part of the polarization current $\dot{\mathbf P}$. The full current is $Z^*\dot Q=Z^*(\pi-\frac1cZ^*A)/m_a$; the $-\frac1c Z^*Z^*A/m_a$ remainder is the ionic diamagnetic current, exactly as $\mathbf p\to\mathbf p-\frac ec\mathbf A$ for electrons.

**(273) Interaction in electron-like form**
$$V=-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf A+\frac{e^2}{2m_{\rm e}c^2}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho^{\rm e}\mathbf A^2-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm ion}\cdot\mathbf A+\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d\mathbf r\,A_\alpha(\mathbf r)\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)A_\beta(\mathbf r)\tag{273}$$
- I checked that (271)–(273) reproduce (270).

**(274) Ionic momentum as a continuous field**
$$\pi_{{\rm a}\mu}(\mathbf r)=-\frac{i}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in{\rm BZ}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\rm a}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}{2}}\,e_{{\rm a}\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}-a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right)e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\tag{274}$$
- (243) with $\mathbf R^0_i\to\mathbf r$. Inside (272) the $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$ makes the two forms equivalent.
- "The ion-photon interaction in (273) is identical to the electron-photon interaction, so we can use the identical tricks."

**(275) Full time-ordered photon propagator**
$$D_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t-t')=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\left\langle\Phi_0^{\text{el-phot-phon}}\right|T\{A_\mu(\mathbf r,t)A_\nu(\mathbf r',t')\}\left|\Phi_0^{\text{el-phot-phon}}\right\rangle\tag{275}$$
- $|\Phi_0^{\text{el-phot-phon}}\rangle$: interacting ground state of $H_0+V$ (with (273)). $A=A_t+A_l$. $A_l$–$A_t$ pairings vanish and $A_l$–$A_l$ gives $D^{0l}=0$, so $A_l$ "remains neutral" and effectively only $A^t$ propagates.

**(276) Interaction-picture expansion**
$$D^t_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t-t')=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n!}\left(-\frac i\hbar\right)^n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_n\left\langle\Phi_0^{\text{el+phot+phon}}\right|T\{A^t_\mu(\mathbf r,t)A^t_\nu(\mathbf r',t')V^t_I(t_1)\cdots V^t_I(t_n)\}\left|\Phi_0^{\text{el+phot+phon}}\right\rangle_C\tag{276}$$
- $A^t_\mu(\mathbf r,t)=e^{iH_0t/\hbar}A^t_\mu(\mathbf r)e^{-iH_0t/\hbar}$. $|\Phi_0^{\text{el+phot+phon}}\rangle=|\Phi^{\rm el}_0\rangle\times|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\rangle\times|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle$ is the free ground state of (236). $C$: connected diagrams only. The sum should start at $n=0$ to include $D^{0t}$ (see Source issues).

**(277) RPA Dyson equation for the transverse photon propagator**
$$\boxed{D^t_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)+\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d^3\mathbf r_1\int d^3\mathbf r_2\,D^{0t}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm RPA}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\omega)\,D^t_{\beta\nu}(\mathbf r_2,\mathbf r',\omega)}\tag{277}$$
with
$$D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right|T\{A^t_\mu(\mathbf r,t)A^t_\nu(\mathbf r',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right\rangle.$$
- Exact structure of a Dyson equation. "RPA" means $\Pi$ is approximated by the lowest-order irreducible diagrams: one bubble (paramagnetic) plus one contact (diamagnetic) per species. The derivation is deferred to "Lecture 9".

**(278) Electronic + ionic self-energy**
$$\Pi^{\rm RPA}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\Pi^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)\tag{278}$$

**(279) Electronic photon self-energy**
$$\Pi^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{mc}\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')\delta_{\alpha\beta}}_{\Pi^{\rm e,dia}}+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm el}_0\right|T\{j^{\rm e}_\alpha(\mathbf r,t)j^{\rm e}_\beta(\mathbf r',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm el}_0\right\rangle}_{\Pi^{\rm e,para}}\tag{279}$$
- $\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)$ should be read as $\langle\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle=n(\mathbf r)$; $m=m_{\rm e}$. Homogeneous limit: $\Pi^{\rm e,dia}=-ne^2/mc$, i.e. $4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm e,dia}=-\omega_p^2$ with $\omega_p^2=4\pi ne^2/m$.

**(280) Ionic photon self-energy**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}}+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right|T\{j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r,t)j^{\rm ion}_\beta(\mathbf r',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right\rangle}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,para}}\tag{280}$$
- Author's remark: strictly $\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle$, which equals $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}$ because (271) contains no phonon operators.
- $j^{\rm ion}$ is **linear** in phonon operators. So $\Pi^{\rm ion,para}$ is a single free phonon propagator between two Born-charge vertices, not a particle–hole bubble. The photon–phonon problem is quadratic and exactly solvable (Hopfield polaritons), so "RPA" is exact for the ionic part within the harmonic approximation.

**First-order ionic term (p. 58–59, unnumbered).**
$$D^{(1),t,\rm ion}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t-t')=-\frac i\hbar\frac{i}{\hbar c}\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1\int d^3\mathbf r_1\left\langle T\{A^t_\mu(\mathbf r,t)A^t_\nu(\mathbf r',t')A^t_\alpha(\mathbf r_1,t_1)\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r_1)A^t_\beta(\mathbf r_1,t_1)\}\right\rangle$$
$$=-2\frac{e^2}{2m_0c}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1\int d^3\mathbf r_1\,D^{0t}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1;t-t_1)\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r_1)D^{0t}_{\beta\nu}(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r';t_1-t')$$
- Factor 2: two equivalent contractions of the $A^2$ vertex. I checked the prefactor: each contraction is $\langle TAA\rangle=\frac{\hbar c}{i}D^0$, so $(-\frac i\hbar)(\frac i{\hbar c})\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\cdot2\cdot(\frac{\hbar c}{i})^2=-2\frac{e^2}{2m_0c}$. Correct.
- In frequency space, and with an extra $\int d^3\mathbf r_2\,\delta(\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2)$:
$$D^{(1),t,\rm ion}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d^3\mathbf r_1\int d^3\mathbf r_2\,D^{0t}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)\left(-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\right)\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r_1)\delta(\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2)D^{0t}_{\beta\nu}(\mathbf r_2,\mathbf r',\omega)$$

**Ionic diamagnetic self-energy (p. 59)**
$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm dia,ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')=-\frac1c\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}Z^*_{{\rm a}\beta\mu}}{m_{\rm a}}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}$$
- The second form (mine) shows that $m_0$ cancels. Macroscopic ($\mathbf q\to0$, cell average) value: $\Pi^{\rm dia,ion}_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{c\,\Omega_0}\sum_a(Z^*_aZ^{*T}_a)_{\alpha\beta}/m_a$. This defines an **ionic plasma frequency** $\Omega^2_{p,\rm ion}=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_0}\sum_a Z^{*2}_a/m_a$ (isotropic $Z^*$), the exact analogue of $\omega_p^2=4\pi ne^2/m$.

### 3.6 Sec. 5.1: Electronic RPA photon self-energy in $(\mathbf q,\omega)$

**(281) Gauge-invariant form**
$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}_{\alpha\beta\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)}\tag{281}$$
- "Proven in Lecture 9 (although that was for q2D crystals)". The diamagnetic term is replaced by minus the static paramagnetic term. This is the gauge-invariance / f-sum-rule identity $\Pi^{\rm dia}+\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega=0)=0$: a static, uniform (or longitudinal) vector potential is a pure gauge and drives no current in a normal (non-superconducting) system. Consequence: $\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}(\mathbf q,0)=0$.

**(282) Electronic paramagnetic photon self-energy (crystal, local fields)**
$$\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{2}{c\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf k\in{\rm BZ}}\sum_{nm}\frac{f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+i\eta}\,j^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf G)\left[j^\beta_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf G')\right]^*\tag{282}$$
- Factor 2: spin. $1/\Omega$: crystal (normalization) volume, since $\sum_{\mathbf k}$ runs over the $N$ BZ points. $f_{n\mathbf k}$: Fermi–Dirac at temperature $T$ (consistent with the rule of Sec. 4.5). $+i\eta$: retarded. The overall "$-$" follows from the $+\frac{i}{\hbar c}$ definition (I checked it with the Kubo formula).
- Structure: identical to the Adler–Wiser $\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}$ used in RPA/GW, with density vertices $\langle n\mathbf k|e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|m\mathbf k+\mathbf q\rangle$ replaced by current vertices.

**(283)–(284) Current vertices**
$$j^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf G)=\int_{\Omega_\infty}d\mathbf r\,e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}\,j^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)\tag{283}$$
$$j^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)=\frac{e\hbar}{2im}\left\{\phi^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)\partial_\alpha\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)-[\partial_\alpha\phi^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)]\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)\right\}\tag{284}$$
- $\mathbf k=(k_x,k_y,k_z)$ in the first BZ. Optical limit: $j^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k}(\mathbf G=0)=\frac{e}{m}\langle n\mathbf k|\hat p_\alpha|m\mathbf k\rangle$, the usual momentum matrix elements (my remark).

**(285) Transition to q2D and the strictly 2D limit**
$$\mathbf q\to\mathbf Q=(Q_x,Q_y),\qquad\mathbf k\to\mathbf K=(K_x,K_y),\qquad\sum_{\mathbf k\in1.{\rm BZ}}\to\sum_{\mathbf K\in1.{\rm SBZ}}\tag{285}$$
$$\Pi^{\rm RPA,e,2D}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf Q,\omega)=l\,\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}_{\alpha\beta\,\mathbf G=0\,\mathbf G'=0}(\mathbf Q,\omega)$$
- SBZ: surface (2D) Brillouin zone. $l$: vertical superlattice period (the interlayer distance of the supercell). In terms of the 2D conductivity, $\Pi^{\rm 2D}=i\omega\sigma^{\rm 2D}/c$ (my relation, from $\mathbf E=\frac{i\omega}{c}\mathbf A$). Physically meaningful for in-plane $\alpha,\beta$.

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) What "free" means here, and why the Coulomb term is moved.** In the temporal gauge the field has three polarizations per $\mathbf q$. The two transverse ones have a restoring term $q^2\tilde A^2/8\pi$ (Eq. 204) and are genuine oscillators (photons, $\hbar\omega=\hbar cq$). The longitudinal one has only a kinetic term $2\pi c^2\tilde p_{A3}^2$, with $\tilde p_{A3}\propto E_l$ fixed by Gauss's law. It is not an oscillator: it is the Coulomb energy of the charges, Eq. (235). Keeping it in $H_0$ would make $H_0$ non-diagonal ("photon scattering processes occur", p. 45). So the clean split is: free = bands + phonons + transverse photons; interaction = all couplings to $\mathbf A$ plus Coulomb.

**(b) Five couplings, one pattern.** Every charged species couples to $\mathbf A$ twice:
- a **paramagnetic** term $-\frac1c\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$, linear in $A$ (absorbs or emits one photon);
- a **diamagnetic** term $\propto A^2/m$ (two photons at one point; a contact or "seagull" vertex).
For electrons $\mathbf j=\mathbf j_e$ and the weight is $e^2/m_e$. For ions the "charge" is the Born tensor $Z^*$, the current is $Z^*\pi/m_a$ (the polarization current $\dot{\mathbf P}$), and the weight is $Z^*Z^*/m_a$. Equations (271)–(273) make this symmetry manifest. That is why one self-energy formalism serves both: $\Pi^{\rm e}$ and $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ each have "dia + para" (Eqs. 279–280).

**(c) The bare Fröhlich coupling is already in (237).** The cross term $\int\!\!\int\rho_e(\mathbf r)\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf r')/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ with $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ is the Coulomb energy of an electron in the dipole field of the displaced ions. In $\mathbf q$-space, $\rho_{\rm ion}(\mathbf q)=-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf P(\mathbf q)$ with $\mathbf P\propto\sum_a Z^*_a\mathbf e_{a\sigma}/\sqrt{m_a}\,(a+a^+)$. My derivation for a cubic diatomic crystal (LO mode, $\mathbf G=0$, electronic form factor 1, per $\sqrt N$, signs and phases dropped):
$$|g^{\rm bare}_{LO}(q)|=\frac{4\pi |e|Z^*}{\Omega_0\,q}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\mu\,\omega_{LO}}},\qquad |g^{\rm bare}|^2=\frac{4\pi e^2}{\Omega_0q^2}\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{LO}}\Omega^2_{p,\rm ion},$$
with $1/\mu=1/m_1+1/m_2$ and $\Omega^2_{p,\rm ion}=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\mu\Omega_0)$. The textbook Fröhlich result is $|g|^2=\frac{4\pi e^2}{\Omega_0q^2}\frac{\hbar\omega_{LO}}2\left(\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon_0}\right)$. With Lyddane–Sachs–Teller and $\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2=\Omega_{p,\rm ion}^2/\epsilon_\infty$, it equals $|g^{\rm bare}|^2/\epsilon_\infty^2$. So **Verdi–Giustino = (EPP bare Coulomb vertex)/$\epsilon_\infty$**. The missing $1/\epsilon_\infty$ is electronic screening. In EPP it is not put in by hand: it must come out of the electronic RPA self-energy $\Pi^{\rm e}$ built in Sec. 5.1. (Later sections do this.)

**(d) Temperature: the "$n_B=0$" rule (Sec. 4.5).**
- Physical (retarded) linear-response functions are what we want. For **free harmonic** bosons, the retarded propagator (247) does not depend on $T$.
- The real-time time-ordered propagator at finite $T$ (249) does depend on $T$, through $(1+2n)$ in its imaginary part. It is "not physical" in the sense that it is not a response function.
- So the author uses $T=0$ boson lines but keeps $f(T)$ in the electron bubble and uses the retarded $i\eta$. For the photon Dyson series in RPA this gives the right retarded result.
- It is **not** a general theorem. Where a boson line runs *inside* a fermion self-energy, thermal boson populations matter. An example is Giustino's Fan–Migdal self-energy (Eq. 157 with Eq. 158), where $n_{\mathbf q\nu}$ produces the Varshni temperature dependence of band gaps (Eq. 168). The rule is safe for the quantities computed in Secs. 5–7 (photon self-energies; the harmonic ionic response is $T$-independent anyway), but should not be carried over to electron self-energies.

**(e) Free propagators.**
- **Phonon:** $F=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}$. Poles at $\omega=\pm(\omega_\sigma-i0)$, the time-ordered (Feynman) prescription. Spectral weight $\propto{\rm sgn}(\omega)\delta(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)$ in the retarded version.
- **Transverse photon:** the same structure with $\omega_\sigma\to cq$, multiplied by the transverse projector and $-4\pi c$ (equivalently $-\frac{2\pi}{q}\cdot2\omega_q$). The light cone $\omega=cq$ is the photon's "dispersion".
- **Longitudinal photon:** zero in this construction, because in the interaction picture $\mathbf A_l$ is frozen. Coulomb gauge vs temporal gauge: in Coulomb gauge one has transverse photons plus the instantaneous $4\pi/q^2$; in the temporal gauge the longitudinal propagator is $-4\pi c\,q_\mu q_\nu/(q^2\omega^2)$ (Eq. 374, p. 83). Contracted with longitudinal currents and using continuity ($\omega\rho=\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j$), that is exactly $4\pi/q^2$ between charges. The manuscript keeps the Coulomb form (235) and sets $D^{0l}=0$, which avoids double counting. In Sec. 5 it drops both, which leaves a purely transverse theory.

**(f) What $D$ and $\Pi$ are, in optics language.**
- $D$ propagates the total vector potential: $\mathbf A=D\,\mathbf j^{\rm ext}$.
- $\Pi$ is the proper current response: $\mathbf j^{\rm ind}=\Pi\mathbf A$. With $\mathbf E=\frac{i\omega}c\mathbf A$ (for $e^{-i\omega t}$) and $\mathbf j=\sigma\mathbf E$: $\Pi=i\omega\sigma/c$, and
$$\epsilon(\mathbf q,\omega)=1+\frac{4\pi i\sigma}{\omega}=1+\frac{4\pi c\,\Pi(\mathbf q,\omega)}{\omega^2}\quad\text{(my relation, isotropic, macroscopic)}.$$
- Drude check: $\Pi=-ne^2/mc\Rightarrow\epsilon=1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2$.
- The transverse Dyson equation (277) for a homogeneous isotropic medium gives
$$D^t(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{-4\pi c}{\omega^2-c^2q^2+4\pi c\,\Pi_T(\mathbf q,\omega)}=\frac{-4\pi c}{\omega^2\epsilon_T(\mathbf q,\omega)-c^2q^2}.$$
Its poles $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon(\omega)$ are the **polaritons**: plasmon-polaritons for a Drude metal, exciton-polaritons for a resonant insulator, phonon-polaritons when $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ is included. This is the photon analogue of $W=v/\epsilon$.

**(g) Diamagnetic vs paramagnetic and the f-sum rule (Eq. 281).**
- At high frequency every charge responds as a free particle: $\Pi\to\Pi^{\rm dia}=-ne^2/mc$, so $\epsilon\to1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2$.
- At $\omega=0$, gauge invariance requires zero current, so $\Pi^{\rm para}(0)=-\Pi^{\rm dia}$.
- In an exact calculation these are equivalent. In practice (truncated band sums, nonlocal pseudopotentials whose velocity operator is not $\mathbf p/m$) $\Pi^{\rm para}(0)\neq-\Pi^{\rm dia}$. A naive "dia + para" then leaves a spurious $-\omega^2_{p,\rm missing}/\omega^2$ (Drude-like) term even in an insulator. Writing $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ enforces the sum rule by construction.
- Two-level illustration (my derivation from (281)–(282), one transition of energy $\Delta$, and with $E\equiv\hbar(\omega+i\eta)$):
$$4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)=-W\frac{\Delta^2}{E^2-\Delta^2},\quad 4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm para}(0)=W,\quad 4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm RPA}(\omega)=W\frac{E^2}{\Delta^2-E^2},\quad\epsilon(\omega)=1+\frac{W\hbar^2}{\Delta^2-E^2},$$
where $W=16\pi|j_{vc}|^2/(\Omega\Delta)$ summed over $\mathbf k$ (units of frequency²). This is the Lorentz oscillator. The TRK sum rule says $\sum_iW_i=\omega_p^2$ for a complete basis.
- The same structure appears for ions: $\Pi^{\rm ion}=\Pi^{\rm dia,ion}+\Pi^{\rm ion,para}$ vanishes at $\omega=0$ and tends to $-\Omega^2_{p,\rm ion}/4\pi c$ at $\omega\gg\omega_{TO}$.
- It is also the same mathematics as Giustino's $\Pi^{\rm NA}=\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$ (Eqs. 140, 145), though there the reason is different: avoiding double counting of the adiabatic part already in DFPT.

**(h) Why "RPA" here is natural.** $\Pi^{\rm e,para}$ is the bare (Kohn–Sham) current–current bubble, with no vertex corrections (no excitons). It is the transverse/current analogue of $\chi^0$. For the user's BSE work: replacing $\Pi^{\rm e,para}$ by the BSE current–current response would add excitonic effects to the photon self-energy and give exciton-polaritons. $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ needs no such caveat: it is exact for harmonic phonons coupled linearly to $\mathbf A$.

**(i) When does any of this matter? (orders of magnitude, [not in paper])**
- **Retardation is confined to tiny $q$.** For GaAs, $\hbar\omega_{TO}=33.3$ meV gives a photon wave number $q=\omega_{TO}/c=1.7\times10^{-4}\,{\rm nm}^{-1}$, or $6\times10^{-4}\,{\rm nm}^{-1}$ in the medium ($\sqrt{\epsilon_0}\approx3.6$). The BZ edge is $2\pi/a\approx11\,{\rm nm}^{-1}$. Polariton physics lives in ~$10^{-4}$ of the BZ radius, which is why electrostatic DFPT (Giustino's framework) works almost everywhere. Near $\Gamma$ (optics, IR spectroscopy, polaritons, nanophotonics) the transverse field matters.
- **Transverse (magnetic) interactions are $O(v^2/c^2)$.** The static transverse propagator is $4\pi/(cq^2)$ against Coulomb $4\pi/q^2$; with $j\sim\rho v$ the ratio is $(v/c)^2\sim10^{-5}$ for $v\sim10^6$ m/s. As $c\to\infty$ only the Coulomb (longitudinal) channel survives.
- **Electron plasma vs ionic plasma frequency.** Al: $\hbar\omega_p\approx15.8$ eV ($n=1.81\times10^{23}$ cm$^{-3}$). GaAs ionic: $\hbar\Omega_{p,\rm ion}=\hbar\sqrt{4\pi Z^{*2}/(\mu\Omega_0)}\approx44.6$ meV (my estimate with $Z^*=2.07|e|$, $\mu=36.1$ amu, $\Omega_0=a^3/4=45.2$ Å$^3$). This compares with $\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2)}=46.9$ meV from $\epsilon_\infty=10.9$, $\hbar\omega_{TO}=33.3$, $\hbar\omega_{LO}=36.2$ meV. The ratio $\sim(m_e/\mu)^{1/2}\times(Z^*/\text{valence})$ explains why ionic responses sit at meV and electronic ones at eV.
- **hBN (in-plane):** $\hbar\omega_{TO}\approx170$ meV, $\hbar\omega_{LO}\approx200$ meV, $\epsilon_\infty\approx4.9$, giving $\hbar\Omega_{p,\rm ion}\approx230$ meV. Hyperbolic phonon-polaritons in hBN are a direct application of $\Pi^{\rm e}+\Pi^{\rm ion}$ in a layered crystal.

**(j) Double-counting checkpoints the reader should keep in mind.**
- KS energies already contain the Hartree–xc mean field. The bare Coulomb term in $V'$ is then the GW-style residual interaction; the Hartree tadpole must not be added again. This is not stated in the range.
- DFT phonon frequencies $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$ must be the short-range (analytic, "TO-like") ones (p. 40). The long-range dipole–dipole part is in $\rho_{\rm ion}V\rho_{\rm ion}$, and the transverse part comes via $\Pi^{\rm ion}$.
- $Z^*$ is itself an electronically screened quantity (zero-field Born charge). The macroscopic screening $\epsilon_\infty$ must come only from $\Pi^{\rm e}$.

**(k) The 2D formula.** In a supercell with interlayer period $l$, $\Pi_{\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0}$ is the current response averaged over the supercell volume. Multiplying by $l$ gives the response per unit area, $\Pi^{\rm 2D}=i\omega\sigma^{2D}/c$. Taking only $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$ neglects the out-of-plane structure of the induced current (strictly-2D sheet) and in-plane local fields. It is the transverse/current counterpart of the 2D $\chi^0$/$\epsilon^{2D}$ used in 2D RPA (e.g. $\epsilon^{2D}(\mathbf Q,\omega)=1+2\pi i\sigma^{2D}Q/\omega$ in the electrostatic limit).

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of the EPP manuscript

**Hamiltonian (Giustino Eq. 1 vs EPP Eqs. 236–237).**
- *Reproduces:* the free part of Giustino's Eq. (1), bands $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}c^+c$ and phonons $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(a^+a+\frac12)$, is exactly the first two terms of (236). The phonon expansions (242)–(243) are Giustino's Eq. (20)-type normal-mode quantization, with physical masses and $1/\sqrt N$.
- *Generalizes:*
  - Giustino has no field and no explicit $e$–$e$ term. All electromagnetism is integrated out into $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$, $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ and the (statically screened) $g$.
  - EPP adds a third free subsystem (transverse photons, $\hbar cq$) and makes every interaction explicit: electron and ion currents coupled to $\mathbf A$ ($\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$, $A^2$) plus bare Coulomb.
  - The electron–phonon interaction is no longer a direct $g\,c^+c(a+a^+)$ term. It arises (i) from the $\rho_e V\rho_{\rm ion}$ Coulomb cross term (longitudinal, the bare Fröhlich coupling), and (ii) from both species sharing $\mathbf A$ (transverse, retarded, photon-mediated).
- *Departs:*
  - EPP's electron–ion coupling in (237) is only the **long-range dipolar (Born-charge)** part. The short-range deformation-potential part of Giustino's DFPT $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ (Eq. 38) has no counterpart in this Hamiltonian; it would need the pseudopotential coupling of EPP Sec. 2.
  - EPP has no Debye–Waller ($g^{\rm DW}$) term. The ionic $A^2$ term is a photon–photon contact vertex, not a two-phonon one.

**Fröhlich vertex (Verdi & Giustino PRL 115, 176401 (2015); Giustino Eq. 180).**
- The Coulomb cross term in (235)/(237) yields the **unscreened** polar vertex; see Concepts (c): $g^{\rm VG}=g^{\rm bare}/\epsilon_\infty$ in the cubic diatomic case (my derivation).
- EPP Sec. 3, Eq. (131), had inserted $\epsilon^\infty$ "ad hoc" to reproduce Verdi–Giustino (see E3 notes). Here $\epsilon^\infty$ is absent by design and must be generated by $\Pi^{\rm e}$, which is why Sec. 5.1 matters.
- Later (Sec. 7.2) the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex replaces $\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q$ by the full dynamical, transverse-plus-longitudinal response and recovers Verdi–Giustino in the electrostatic limit.

**Screened interaction, Dyson equations, Hedin–Baym (Giustino Sec. IV, Eqs. 88–105, Table I).**
- Giustino: $W=v+vPW$ with $P=P_e+P_n$. Hence $W=W_e+W_{\rm ph}$, $W_{\rm ph}=W_eDW_e$ (Eqs. 104–105).
- EPP (277)–(278): $D^t=D^{0t}+D^{0t}\Pi D^t$ with $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm e}+\Pi^{\rm ion}$. This is the same algebra for the transverse photon: $D^{0t}\leftrightarrow v$, $\Pi\leftrightarrow P$, $D^t\leftrightarrow W$. By the same steps $D^t=D^t_e+D^t_e\Pi^{\rm ion}D^t$, with $D^t_e=D^{0t}+D^{0t}\Pi^{\rm e}D^t_e$, the transverse analogue of $W=W_e+W_eP_nW$ (my observation; not written in the paper).
- $\Pi^{\rm ion,para}$ is a phonon propagator between Born-charge current vertices, just as $W_{\rm ph}$ is a phonon propagator between screened-potential vertices $\epsilon_e^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$ (Giustino Eq. 110).
- Differences:
  - Giustino's vertices are density (scalar-potential) vertices $Z_\kappa\nabla\delta$; EPP's are current vertices $Z^*\pi/m_a$, connected to the density ones by continuity. Sec. 6's "connection formulas" make this precise: $\Pi_{LL}=-(\omega^2/cq^2)e^2\chi$, so $1+4\pi c\Pi_{LL}/\omega^2=1-v\chi$ (my check).
  - Giustino uses point nuclear charges $Z_\kappa$ (bare), while EPP uses the electronically pre-screened Born charges $Z^*$.
- Giustino's $W_e$ is RPA when $P_e=-i\hbar GG$ (no vertex). EPP's $\Pi^{\rm e,para}$ (282) is the corresponding bare current bubble, the Adler–Wiser structure with current vertices.

**Phonon propagator and self-energy (Giustino Sec. V.A, Eqs. 130–146).**
- EPP (256) $=\frac1\hbar\times$ Giustino Eq. (132)/(134), $D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu}=2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}/(\omega^2-\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}^2)$, with the same Mahan-type definition (Giustino Eq. 133).
- The subtraction in EPP (281), $\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$, has the same form as Giustino's nonadiabatic phonon self-energy $\Pi^{\rm NA}\propto[W_e(\omega)-W_e(0)]$ (Eq. 140) and the bracket in Eq. (145). The reasons differ: gauge invariance/f-sum in EPP, and avoiding double counting of the adiabatic (DFPT) part in Giustino. In both cases the static part is already accounted for elsewhere.
- In this range EPP uses **bare harmonic phonons** with short-range DFT frequencies in $\Pi^{\rm ion}$. The renormalization of these phonons by the field ("electrodynamic phonon self-energy", LO–TO splitting, polaritons) comes later (Sec. 7.1, 7.3).

**Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic.**
- $\Pi^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ in (282) is fully dynamical. When it screens ionic dynamics at $\omega\sim\omega_{\rm ph}\ll E_g$, $4\pi c\Pi^{\rm e}\approx(\epsilon_\infty-1)\omega^2$, the adiabatic $\epsilon_\infty$ of Giustino Eq. (180).
- In metals and doped 2D systems the $\omega$-dependence at phonon frequencies is what drives Giustino's nonadiabatic effects (Sec. VII: graphene $E_{2g}$, Kohn anomalies, plasmon–phonon coupling). EPP's framework contains these automatically through (282) with finite-$T$ Fermi functions.

**DFPT.** In Giustino, DFPT gives the statically screened perturbation $\partial v^{\rm KS}=(\epsilon^{Hxc})^{-1}\partial v^{\rm en}$ (Sec. III.B). In EPP the corresponding screening of the long-range ionic field arises diagrammatically from the bare Coulomb term plus the RPA electronic $\Pi^{\rm e}$, dynamically and including the transverse field. DFT/DFPT still enters through $E_{n\mathbf k}$, $\phi_{n\mathbf k}$, short-range $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$, $e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ and $Z^*$.

**Temperature.** Giustino handles finite $T$ via Matsubara plus continuation, giving retarded self-energies (his Eq. 158 adds $n_{\mathbf q\nu}$ to the Fan–Migdal numerators). EPP's Sec. 4.5 rule ($n=0$ in boson lines) is consistent with Giustino for photon/phonon self-energies built from electron bubbles (his Eq. 145 contains only $f$'s). ## 8. Source issues

1. **Eq. (260), p. 53: stray factor.** The printed RHS reads $\ldots e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\,2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}\,\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\alpha}+i\eta}$ (confirmed on a 200-dpi render). Only one factor $2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}$ belongs there. The time integral gives $\frac{i}{\omega-\omega_q+i\eta}-\frac{i}{\omega+\omega_q-i\eta}=\frac{2i\omega_q}{\omega^2-\omega_q^2+i\eta}$, and the next equation, $-\frac{4\pi c}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf q}\ldots$, follows only with a single factor ($\frac1q\cdot2qc=2c$). **Corrected (confident):** $D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';\omega)=-\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac1q e_{\mu\alpha}e_{\nu\alpha}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\alpha}+i\eta}$. The middle expression also writes $D^0_{\mu\nu}$ for $D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}$.
2. **Unnumbered $D^{0l}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';\omega)$, p. 54:** the same duplicated $2\omega_{\mathbf q\alpha}$, and the subscript should be $\mathbf q3$ rather than $\mathbf q\alpha$. The following line, $-\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\sum\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^3}\ldots\frac{\omega_{\mathbf q3}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q3}+i\eta}$, is correct with a single factor.
3. **$\rho_{\rm e}$: number density or charge density?** Eq. (239) defines $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ (a number density). That is correct in the diamagnetic term $\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int\rho_eA^2$ and in (279). But in the Coulomb terms (235)/(237), and in Gauss's law (234), $\rho_{\rm e}$ must be the **charge** density $e\Psi^+\Psi$ to be added to $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ (which carries charge through $Z^*$). **Corrected (confident):** in (234), (235), (237, last line), replace $\rho_{\rm e}\to e\Psi^+\Psi$.
4. **Eq. (276): lower limit of the sum.** $\sum_{n=1}^\infty$ omits the zeroth-order term, which is $D^{0t}$ itself and appears as the first term of the Dyson equation (277). It should be $\sum_{n=0}^\infty$ (or "$D^{0t}+\ldots$"). Also, inside the bracket the interaction is written inconsistently as $V_I(t_1)\ldots V^t_I(t_n)$.
5. **Photon operators $b$ vs $a$.** (219), (236), (244)–(245) use $b_{\mathbf q\sigma}$ for photons (the author explicitly introduces $b$ to avoid confusion with phonons). But (258) and pp. 52–55 revert to $a_{\mathbf q\alpha}$, $a_{\mathbf q3}$, the phonon symbol. Page builders should use $b$ throughout.
6. **Index reuse.** In (287) $\alpha,\beta$ are phonon branch indices, while in the same equations $\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu$ are Cartesian. The text at p. 48 says Greek summation indices are Cartesian. On p. 61 the author switches branches to $\sigma$. Similarly $\alpha$ labels photon polarizations in (244) but Cartesian components in (237).
7. **Atomic positions.** $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$ in (241), (271), (272), (286), and $A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)$ in (237)/(270), place every atom of cell $i$ at the cell origin. Strictly this should be $\mathbf R^0_i+\boldsymbol\tau_a$ (basis vector). It is harmless at long wavelength (dipole approximation within a cell), but it drops the phase $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_a}$. This is the same omission Giustino flags for Verdi–Giustino's Eq. (4) (RMP footnote 7), and it matters for $\mathbf G\neq0$ local fields.
8. **Eq. (279): operator vs expectation value; $m$ vs $m_{\rm e}$.** The diamagnetic term should contain $\langle\Phi^{\rm el}_0|\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)|\Phi^{\rm el}_0\rangle$. The author makes this remark for the ionic case (p. 58) but not here, where it matters because $\rho^e$ is an operator. The mass is written $m$ in (279) and (284), but $m_{\rm e}$ in (237)/(238)/(270).
9. **Sign conventions of (248)/(250) vs (282).** (248) and (250) carry $+\frac2\Omega$, but the physical self-energy (282) carries $-\frac{2}{c\Omega}$. The difference is the $+\frac{i}{\hbar c}$ definition of $\Pi$ in (279), which gives an overall minus sign (I verified it with the Kubo formula; it yields $\Pi^{\rm para}(\mathbf q\to0,0)=+ne^2/mc$ for free electrons, cancelling $\Pi^{\rm dia}$). (248)–(250) should be read as generic correlators, not as $\Pi$. The Matsubara form in Sec. 4.5 also lacks $\hbar$: $i\omega_n\to i\hbar\omega_n$ in the denominator.
10. **Eq. (255): Fourier variable.** $\int dt\,e^{i\omega t}F_\sigma(\mathbf q;t-t')$ should be $\int d(t-t')\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\ldots$ (or set $t'=0$). Same in (260).
11. **Thermal vs ground-state averages (pp. 51, 53).** $n_{\mathbf q\alpha}=\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|a^+a|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle$ is called the occupation in the "noninteracting phonons ground state which is also at temperature $T$". A pure ground state has $n=0$; a thermal average needs a density matrix. Since $n$ is set to zero afterwards, this has no effect on results.
12. **The $n_B=0$ prescription (Sec. 4.5) is heuristic.** It is justified for linear-response (retarded) functions of harmonic bosons and for one-loop photon self-energies. It is not a general replacement for Matsubara theory: it would remove the thermal phonon population from electron self-energies (cf. Giustino Eqs. 158, 166–168). Also, the "minor interventions around $i\eta$" (switching ${\rm sgn}$-dependent $i\eta$ to retarded) are not spelled out. Flag in the page text.
13. **Eq. (263) vs Eq. (374).** In this range the longitudinal photon propagator is declared zero. On p. 83 it is $D^{0l}=-4\pi c\,q_\mu q_\nu/(q^2\omega^2)$ (the standard temporal-gauge result). They are not contradictory: (263) is the propagator of the interaction-picture $A_l$ (no dynamics in $H_0$), while (374) is the physical longitudinal propagator, recovered from the Coulomb term via connection formulas. But the wording "$D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}=0$" in a box invites misreading. Also, the longitudinal amplitude $\sqrt{2\pi c\hbar/q\Omega}$ in (244)/(258) copies the transverse-oscillator normalization even though $\alpha=3$ has no restoring force; the $\alpha=3$ "oscillator" is a formal device.
14. **Eq. (281) is quoted, not derived ("Lecture 9").** Subtlety: $\Pi^{\rm dia}+\Pi^{\rm para}(\mathbf q,0)=0$ holds exactly for the longitudinal channel at all $\mathbf q$ (pure gauge), and for the transverse channel only as $\mathbf q\to0$ in a normal system. At finite $q$ the static transverse response is the orbital (Landau/Van Vleck) magnetic susceptibility, $O(q^2)$, which (281) sets to zero. Negligible for optics; worth one sentence. With nonlocal pseudopotentials, the current vertex (284) should use the velocity operator $\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,\mathbf r]$; (281) partly compensates for this inconsistency.
15. **Normalization volumes.** $\Omega$ in (244)/(282) is the BvK crystal volume, and $\Omega_\infty$ in (283) is "all space" (the crystal). Neither is defined in the range; the unit-cell volume never appears. The prefactor $\frac{2}{c\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf k\in{\rm BZ}}$ with vertices integrated over the whole crystal is consistent only if the $\phi_{n\mathbf k}$ are normalized to 1 over the crystal. State this explicitly on the page.
16. **2D formula (285).** "$l$ is vertical superlattice" should read "$l$ is the vertical superlattice period". Restricting to $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$ is the strictly-2D (zero-thickness) limit and drops in-plane local fields. For $\alpha$ or $\beta=z$ the 2D sheet formula is not meaningful.
17. **Minor typography.** (240) lacks the argument $(\mathbf r)$ on $\phi_{n\mathbf k}$. $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\mathbf P$ should be $-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$, and (269) $\nabla\mathbf E^l$ should be $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E^l$. "$n_{\mathbf q}=0$" should be $n_{\mathbf q\sigma}=0$. The ket labels $\Phi_0^{\text{el-phot-phon}}$ (interacting, 275) and $\Phi_0^{\text{el+phot+phon}}$ (free, 276) differ by one character, which is easy to confuse. In (237)–(273) the subscripts $\mathbf j_{\rm e},\rho_{\rm e}$ turn into superscripts $\mathbf j^{\rm e},\rho^{\rm e}$. Typos: "buble", "parturbative", "corelator", "Finaly", "actiong".
18. **Conceptual (not an error, worth a note).** $Z^*$ already contains the electronic (adiabatic) response to ionic displacement. Electrons also appear explicitly through $\mathbf j_e$ and $\rho_e$. The manuscript avoids double counting the macroscopic part by removing the ad hoc $\epsilon^\infty$ (Sec. 3 → Sec. 5). Whether short-range electronic contributions are counted both in $Z^*$ and in $\Pi^{\rm e}$ is not discussed. Likewise the Hartree tadpole and the KS mean field (p. 40 remark) are not addressed in this range.
