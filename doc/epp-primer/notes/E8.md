## 1. Overview

By the end of Sec. 5 the manuscript has two separate pictures of how a crystal responds electromagnetically. In the **longitudinal** picture, charges $\rho$ interact through the instantaneous Coulomb propagator $v=1/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, and the crystal responds through density–density correlators $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$. This is the world of $W$, GW, BSE and DFPT. In the **transverse** picture, currents $\mathbf j$ couple to the photon field through $D^{0,t}$, and the crystal responds through the current–current correlator (photon self-energy) $\Pi_{jj}$. Sec. 6 opens (p. 68) by putting both into one interaction Hamiltonian (312). The resulting perturbation series for the field propagator is a **4×4 matrix**: a scalar (Coulomb) row and column plus a 3×3 vector block. It contains four kinds of bubbles ($\Pi_{\rho\rho},\Pi_{\rho j},\Pi_{j\rho},\Pi_{jj}$) for electrons and for ions, and two kinds of lines ($v$ and $D^{0,t}$).

Pages 73–86 show that this zoo collapses to **one** object. The key input is **charge conservation**: $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=i\omega\rho$. Every induced charge is the divergence of an induced current, so every correlator containing a $\rho$ can be written as derivatives of $\Pi_{jj}$. These are the **connection formulas** (341), (344), (349), (350); for example, $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj,\mu\nu}$. The author checks them explicitly for the RPA ionic correlator. The check works only because $\Pi_{jj}$ includes the diamagnetic term (Sec. 5's "$\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$").

The connection formulas let every scalar vertex be rewritten as a current vertex. The Coulomb line $v$ then becomes a **longitudinal vector-potential propagator** $D^{0,l}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu v$, which in $\mathbf q$-space is $-\frac{4\pi c}{q^2}\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{\omega^2}$. Added to the transverse photon propagator, it gives $D^0=D^{0,l}+D^{0,t}$. This is exactly the **vacuum Maxwell propagator in the temporal gauge** ($\Phi=0$), Eq. (375). The whole series resums into a single 3×3 RPA Dyson equation:

$$D=D^0+D^0\,(\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj})\,D .$$

$D$ is the fully screened, retarded electromagnetic interaction in a polarizable crystal of electrons and phonons. It is "W with photons". In the electrostatic limit ($c\to\infty$) its longitudinal part reduces to $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$. Its transverse part describes polaritons. Sec. 7 builds on this equation to derive the electrodynamic phonon self-energy, the LO–TO splitting and the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **[context, p. 68] One interaction for everything.** $V'$ (312) contains electron and ion couplings to $\mathbf A$ ($-\frac1c\int\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$ and the diamagnetic $A^2$ terms) and the full Coulomb term between $\rho^{\rm e}+\rho^{\rm ion}$. Here $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ (313) is the bound charge of the Born-charge point dipoles (314). The lowest-order propagator is $D=D^0+D^{1,\rm ion}+D^{1,\rm e}+\dots$ (316). $D^0={\rm diag}(v,\hat D^{0,t})$ is a 4×4 matrix (317). $D^{1,i}$ contains $v\Pi_{\rho\rho}v$, $v\Pi_{\rho j}\hat D^{0,t}$, $\hat D^{0,t}\Pi_{j\rho}v$, $\hat D^{0,t}\Pi_{jj}\hat D^{0,t}$ (319). These are shown in Fig. 7.
2. **[context, pp. 71–72] Induced charges and currents.** Cut the diagrams of Fig. 7 open and attach an external charge $\rho^{\rm ext}$ or field $\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$. This gives $\rho^{\rm ind,l}=\Pi_{\rho\rho}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}$ (330), $\rho^{\rm ind}=\Pi_{\rho j}\otimes\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$ (331), $\mathbf j^{\rm ind,l}=\Pi_{j\rho}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}$ (332) and $\mathbf j^{\rm ind}=\Pi_{jj}\otimes\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$ (333). Written in $\omega$-space (336)–(339). Continuity of the longitudinal part is (340).
3. **(p. 73) First two identities.** Continuity applied to (336)/(338) for arbitrary $\rho^{\rm ext}$ gives $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac i\omega\partial_\mu\Pi_{j\rho,\mu}$ (341). Applied to (337)/(339) for arbitrary $\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$ it gives $\Pi_{\rho j,\nu}=-\frac i\omega\partial_\mu\Pi_{jj,\mu\nu}$ (344).
4. **(pp. 73–74) Definitions and prefactors.** $\Pi_{j\rho}=-\frac i\hbar\langle T\{j\rho\}\rangle$ (345) and $\Pi_{\rho j}=+\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{\rho j\}\rangle$ (346). The digression explains why. The right-hand operator is paired with the perturbation that creates it: $\rho$ with $\int\rho\,\phi^{\rm ext}$ (factor $-i/\hbar$), and $\mathbf j$ with $-\frac1c\int\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$ (factor $i/\hbar c$). Fig. 8 counts the $1/c$ factors in two higher-order diagrams.
5. **(pp. 74–75) Reciprocity of time-ordered correlators.** Send $\omega\to-\omega$, $t\to-t$, and use bosonic time ordering and time-translation invariance. This gives $\Pi_{\rho j,\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)=-\frac1c\Pi_{j\rho,\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega)$ (347) and $\Pi_{jj,\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega)=\Pi_{jj,\nu\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)$ (348).
6. **(p. 75) Connection formulas.** Insert (347)–(348) into (344). This gives $\Pi_{j\rho,\mu}=-i\frac c\omega\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj,\mu\nu}$ (349). Insert (349) into (341). This gives the central result $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj,\mu\nu}$ (350). All four correlators are now functionals of $\Pi_{jj}$.
7. **(pp. 75–77) Explicit check for ions.** Take (351), the ionic version of (350). Compute $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ directly from $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ (353). Insert the Born-charge dipoles (314) and the phonon displacement operator (83), Fourier-transform the phonon $\langle T\{(a+a^\dagger)(a+a^\dagger)\}\rangle$, and do the lattice sums with (354). The result is exactly $-\frac c{\omega^2}\partial\partial'$ acting on the RPA ionic $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ (352). The author then "inductively" concludes that all connection formulas hold (see Source issues).
8. **(p. 78) Lowest-order 4×4 propagator via connection formulas.** Use (344), (349) and (350) to write the four sectors $D_{11}$ (scalar–scalar), $D_{1\nu}$, $D_{\mu1}$ and $D_{\mu\nu}$ with only $\Pi_{jj}$ (355)–(358) (Fig. 9).
9. **(p. 79) Integrate by parts** (Gauss's theorem, surface terms dropped), so that the derivatives act on the propagators $v$ instead of on $\Pi_{jj}$.
10. **(pp. 79–80) Convert every sector to a 3×3 tensor.** Act with $-\frac c{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu$ on $D_{11}$ (359), with $i\frac c\omega\partial_\mu$ on $D_{1\nu}$, and with $\frac i\omega\partial'_\nu$ on $D_{\mu1}$; leave $D_{\mu\nu}$ unchanged. This gives $D^{ll},D^{lt},D^{tl},D^{tt}$ (360)–(363).
11. **(p. 80) Define the longitudinal photon propagator** $D^{0,l}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac c{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu v$ (364). The author calls it "ad hoc", because the quantized longitudinal photon propagator vanishes in the $\Phi=0$ gauge (263).
12. **(p. 81) All four sectors now have the same form**, $D^{0,a}\Pi_{jj}D^{0,b}$ with $a,b\in\{l,t\}$ (365)–(368) (Fig. 10(a)–(d)). The rule is $\rho^i\to j^i_\mu$, $v\to D^{0,l}_{\mu\nu}$. Summing the sectors gives $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D^0+\dots$ (369), with $D^0=D^{0,l}+D^{0,t}$ (370) and $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$ (371) (Fig. 10(e)).
13. **(pp. 82–83) Meaning of $D^0$.** With $D^{0,t}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (372) and the Fourier transform of (364), (373)–(374), the sum is (375). This is the free electromagnetic propagator in vacuum, obtained from Maxwell's equations. Hence $\mathbf A^{\rm ext}=D^0\mathbf j^{\rm ext}$, $\Pi_{jj}\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$ is the first induced current, and $D^0\Pi_{jj}\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$ is the first induced field (376). So $D$ is the propagator of the total field $\mathbf A^l+\mathbf A^t$ in a polarizable crystal.
14. **(pp. 83–85) Higher orders.** The same transformation (377) applies to every diagram (Fig. 11(a)→(b)). Compatible diagrams sum into $D^0$ lines (Fig. 11(c)) and into total bubbles $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ (Fig. 11(d)). The result is the geometric series (378), i.e. the **Dyson equation (379)**.
15. **(pp. 85–86) Explicit $(\mathbf q,\omega)$ form (Sec. 6.1).** $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D$ (380), with $D^0$ (381) and $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$ (382). The electronic part (383)–(384) and the ionic part (385) are given in their gauge-invariant (para minus static para) forms. The author says the 2D version follows from $\Omega\to S$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}\to S_{\rm uc}$, $\mathbf q\to\mathbf Q$, $\mathbf k\to\mathbf K$ (see Source issues for why this is incomplete).

## 3. Key equations

### 3.0 Conventions used in this range

- **Units: Gaussian**, with $\hbar$ explicit. $v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=1/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ and $v(\mathbf q)=4\pi/q^2$. $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E^l=4\pi\rho$ (325). The temporal gauge $\Phi=0$ is used throughout: $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}$ (324). Charge densities $\rho$ and currents $\mathbf j$ include the charge $e$, or the Born charge $Z^*$ for ions. $v$ therefore carries no $e^2$.
- **Time Fourier transform:** $f(\omega)=\int dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}f(t-t')$ (p. 76). Time dependence is $e^{-i\omega t}$. Hence $\mathbf E=\frac{i\omega}{c}\mathbf A$ (334) and the continuity equation reads $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=i\omega\rho$ (335).
- **Space Fourier transform in a crystal** (294) **[context]:** $f(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}f_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\mathbf r'}$. Homogeneous case (373): $f(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')=\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}f(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}$. Therefore $\partial_\mu\to i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu$, $\partial'_\nu\to-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')_\nu$, and $\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\to(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')_\nu$.
- **Normalization:** $N$ unit cells, crystal volume $\Omega=N\Omega_{\rm uc}$. $\sum_{\mathbf q\in\rm BZ}\to\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{\rm BZ}d\mathbf q$ (354). $\sum_ie^{i\mathbf q\mathbf R^0_i}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q\mathbf G}$. $\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\to\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q}$. In 2D, $S$ (sheet area) and $S_{\rm uc}$ replace $\Omega,\Omega_{\rm uc}$.
- **Correlator definitions and prefactors.** All are **time-ordered** at $T=0$ ($n_{\mathbf q}=0$, Sec. 4.5), with Feynman $+i\eta$:
  - $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac i\hbar\langle T\{\rho\rho\}\rangle$ (320)
  - $\Pi_{j\rho}=-\frac i\hbar\langle T\{j\rho\}\rangle$ (345)
  - $\Pi_{\rho j}=+\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{\rho j\}\rangle$ (346)
  - $\Pi_{jj}=+\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{jj\}\rangle$ plus the diamagnetic term (321)–(322)
  - $\hat D^{0,t}=+\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{AA\}\rangle$ (318)

  With these signs, $\mathbf A=D\,\mathbf j^{\rm ext}$, $\mathbf j^{\rm ind}=\Pi_{jj}\mathbf A$ and $\rho^{\rm ind}=\Pi_{\rho\rho}\phi$, where $\phi=W\rho^{\rm ext}$ is a potential (not a potential energy).
- **"Bare" vs "screened".** $\Pi_{\rho\rho},\Pi_{\rho j},\Pi_{j\rho},\Pi_{jj}$ are bare, i.e. irreducible bubbles. In Hedin's language $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ is the irreducible polarizability $P$. $W$ is the screened longitudinal interaction. $\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$ is the screened (total) external field.
- **Index conventions.** $i\in\{\rm e,ion\}$ labels the species (not a site, except in lattice sums $\sum_i$). $\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta\in\{x,y,z\}$ are Cartesian. $\sigma=1\dots3s$ labels phonon branches. $\mathrm a,\mathrm b$ label atoms in the cell. The index "1" in $D_{11},D_{1\nu},D_{\mu1}$ is the **scalar (Coulomb) component** of the 4×4 matrix, not $x$. Primes on derivatives ($\partial'_\nu$) mean derivatives with respect to the second argument $\mathbf r'$. $\partial_{1\alpha}$ and $\partial_{2\beta}$ act on $\mathbf r_1$ and $\mathbf r_2$.
- **Born charges** $Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\nu}$ carry the dimension of charge, i.e. $|e|$ times Giustino's dimensionless $Z^*$. The ionic current vertex is $j_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathrm a\alpha}Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}e_{\mathrm a\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)/\sqrt{m_\mathrm a}$ (295).
- **Dimensions** (E6 check, still valid): $[D(\mathbf q,\omega)]=$ time·length and $[\Pi_{jj}(\mathbf q,\omega)]=1/$(time·length). So $D\Pi$ is dimensionless, and $4\pi c\,\Pi_{jj}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ has units of frequency². $[\Pi_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')]=1/{\rm length}^5$ (Gaussian), and both sides of (350) agree.

### 3.1 [context] Setting from pp. 68–72

**(312) Total interaction (transverse + longitudinal)**
$$V'=-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\mathbf A^2(\mathbf r)-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm ion}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d\mathbf r\,A_\alpha(\mathbf r)\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)A_\beta(\mathbf r)+\frac12\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\frac{[\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r)+\rho^{\rm ion}(\mathbf r)][\rho^{\rm e}(\mathbf r')+\rho^{\rm ion}(\mathbf r')]}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\qquad(312)$$
$$\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P\quad(313),\qquad P_\mu(\mathbf r)=\sum_{i\nu}Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\nu}Q_{i\mathrm a\nu}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\quad(314)$$
- (314) is a lattice of point dipoles, all placed at the cell origin $\mathbf R^0_i$. A sum over $\mathrm a$ is implied. $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}$ (315) = (271) is the ionic density tensor of the diamagnetic term.

**(316)–(319) Lowest-order 4×4 propagator**
$$D=D^0+D^{1,\rm ion}+D^{1,\rm e}+\dots\quad(316),\qquad D^0=\begin{bmatrix}v&0\\0&\hat D^{0,t}\end{bmatrix}\quad(317),\qquad D^{1,i}=\begin{bmatrix}v\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}v&v\Pi^i_{\rho j}\hat D^{0,t}\\\hat D^{0,t}\Pi^i_{j\rho}v&\hat D^{0,t}\Pi^i_{jj}\hat D^{0,t}\end{bmatrix}\quad(319)$$
- Mixed electron–ion correlators vanish at this order. $\Pi_{\rho j}$ is a row and $\Pi_{j\rho}$ a column 3-vector. The longitudinal photon pairing $\langle A_lA_l\rangle$ is zero (263), so only $\hat D^{0,t}$ appears in the vector block.

**(326)–(333) Induced charges and currents** ($\otimes$ = convolution in space and time; $i=$ e, ion)
$$\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}=\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}\ (330),\quad\rho^{i,\rm ind}=\sum_\mu\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}\otimes A^{\rm ext,sc}_\mu\ (331),\quad j^{i,{\rm ind},l}_\mu=\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}\ (332),\quad j^{i,\rm ind}_\mu=\sum_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}\otimes A^{\rm ext,sc}_\nu\ (333)$$
- (326)–(329) are the same expressions at lowest order, with $v$ in place of $W$ and $\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$ in place of $\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$. "$l$" marks the part induced by the longitudinal external field. The unlabelled ones are induced by the total field.

**(334)–(335) Frequency-domain field and continuity**
$$\mathbf E^{{\rm ext},\sigma,\rm sc}(\omega)=i\frac{\omega}{c}\mathbf A^{{\rm ext},\sigma,\rm sc}(\omega),\ \ \sigma=l,t\quad(334);\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=i\omega\rho\quad(335)$$

**(336)–(339)** are (330)–(333) written out with spatial integrals, e.g.
$$\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}(\mathbf r,\omega)=\int d\mathbf r_1d\mathbf r_2\,\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)W(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\omega)\rho^{\rm ext}(\mathbf r_2,\omega)\qquad(336)$$
$$j^{i,\rm ind}_\mu(\mathbf r,\omega)=\sum_\nu\int d\mathbf r_1\,\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)A^{\rm ext,sc}_\nu(\mathbf r_1,\omega)\qquad(339)$$
$$\sum_\mu\partial_\mu j^{i,{\rm ind},l}_\mu(\mathbf r,\omega)=i\omega\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}(\mathbf r,\omega)\qquad(340)$$

### 3.2 Sec. 6.0.1 (end): connection formulas, Eqs. (341)–(350)

**Unnumbered (p. 73, top).** (336) and (338) inserted into (340):
$$\int d\mathbf r_1d\mathbf r_2\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)W(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\omega)\rho^{\rm ext}(\mathbf r_2,\omega)=i\omega\int d\mathbf r_1d\mathbf r_2\,\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)W(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\omega)\rho^{\rm ext}(\mathbf r_2,\omega)$$

**(341) Density–density from current–density (boxed)**
$$\boxed{\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega);\qquad i={\rm e,ion}}\qquad(341)$$
- Meaning: the charge induced by a scalar perturbation is the divergence of the current induced by the same perturbation, divided by $i\omega$. The identity follows because $\rho^{\rm ext}$ is arbitrary and $W$ is invertible.
- $\mathbf q$-space form (mine): $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=\frac{1}{\omega}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu\Pi_{j\rho\mu}$, i.e. $\rho=\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j/\omega$.

**(342) Continuity for the total induced charge and current**
$$\sum_\mu\partial_\mu j^{i,\rm ind}_\mu(\mathbf r,\omega)=i\omega\rho^{i,\rm ind}(\mathbf r,\omega)\qquad(342)$$

**(343) Inserted into (337) and (339)**
$$\sum_{\mu\nu}\int d\mathbf r_1\,\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)A^{\rm ext,sc}_\nu(\mathbf r_1,\omega)=i\omega\sum_\nu\int d\mathbf r_1\,\Pi^i_{\rho j\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)A^{\rm ext,sc}_\nu(\mathbf r_1,\omega)\qquad(343)$$

**(344) Charge–current from current–current (boxed)**
$$\boxed{\Pi^i_{\rho j\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega);\qquad i={\rm e,ion}}\qquad(344)$$
- Same logic for an arbitrary vector potential. $\mathbf q$-space form (mine): $\Pi_{\rho j\nu}=\frac1\omega(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}$.

**(345)–(346) Definitions of the mixed correlators**
$$\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega t}\left\langle T\{j^i_\mu(\mathbf r,t)\rho^i(\mathbf r',0)\}\right\rangle\qquad(345)$$
$$\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega t}\left\langle T\{\rho^i(\mathbf r,t)j^i_\mu(\mathbf r',0)\}\right\rangle\qquad(346)$$
- **Small digression (pp. 73–74).** In first-order perturbation theory (Kubo), the right-hand operator of the correlator is the one created by the perturbation. $\Pi_{j\rho}$ pairs with $\int d\mathbf r\,\rho\,\phi^{\rm ext}$, giving $(-i/\hbar)$. $\Pi_{\rho j}$ pairs with $-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$, giving $(-i/\hbar)(-1/c)=i/\hbar c$. Every $\mathbf j$ vertex in a diagram therefore carries one factor $-1/c$. Fig. 8 checks the count for two higher-order diagrams. In the top one (part of $W$), the two $1/c$ factors sit in $D^{0,t}$ and $\Pi_{\rho j}$. In the bottom one (part of $\hat D^t$), three $1/c$ factors are needed (two $D^{0,t}$, one $\Pi_{\rho j}$) but only two current vertices appear. The third comes from the $\frac{i}{\hbar c}$ in the definition of $\hat D^t=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{A_\mu A_\nu\}\rangle$ itself.

**Unnumbered (p. 74): $\omega\to-\omega$ in (345)**
$$\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega)=-\frac i\hbar\int dt\,e^{-i\omega t}\langle T\{j^i_\mu(\mathbf r,t)\rho^i(\mathbf r',0)\}\rangle\overset{t\to-t}{=}-\frac i\hbar\int dt\,e^{i\omega t}\langle T\{\rho^i(\mathbf r',0)j^i_\mu(\mathbf r,-t)\}\rangle=-c\,\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)$$
- The last step uses the symmetry of $T$ for bosonic (even) operators and time-translation invariance: $\langle T\{\rho(0)j(-t)\}\rangle=\langle T\{\rho(t)j(0)\}\rangle$.

**(347) Reciprocity, charge–current (boxed)**
$$\boxed{\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)=-\frac1c\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega);\qquad i={\rm e,ion}}\qquad(347)$$

**(348) Reciprocity, current–current**
$$\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega)=\Pi^i_{jj\nu\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)\qquad(348)$$
- Same manipulation applied to $\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int dt\,e^{-i\omega t}\langle T\{j_\mu(\mathbf r,t)j_\nu(\mathbf r',0)\}\rangle$. These relations are specific to time-ordered functions; for retarded functions the analogous Onsager relation involves complex conjugation.

**(349) Connection formula: current–density from current–current (boxed)**
$$\boxed{\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-i\frac{c}{\omega}\sum_\nu\partial'_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega);\qquad i={\rm e,ion}}\qquad(349)$$
- Derivation (I verified it). From (347), $\Pi_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-c\,\Pi_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,-\omega)$. Apply (344) with $\mathbf r\leftrightarrow\mathbf r'$ and $\omega\to-\omega$: $\Pi_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,-\omega)=\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\nu\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj\nu\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,-\omega)$. Then (348) turns $\Pi_{jj\nu\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,-\omega)$ into $\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$.
- $\mathbf q$-space (mine): $\Pi_{j\rho\mu}=-\frac c\omega\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')_\nu$. Physical check: a scalar potential $\phi$ is equivalent, in the temporal gauge, to $\mathbf A_l=\frac{ic}{\omega}\nabla\phi\to-\frac{c}{\omega}\mathbf q\phi$. So $\mathbf j=\Pi_{jj}\mathbf A_l=-\frac c\omega\Pi_{jj}\mathbf q\,\phi$. Consistent.

**(350) Connection formula: density–density from current–current (boxed, the key result)**
$$\boxed{\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega);\qquad i={\rm e,ion}}\qquad(350)$$
- (349) inserted into (341): $(-\frac i\omega)(-\frac{ic}\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}$.
- **Reciprocal space** (mine, from (294)):
$$\Pi^i_{\rho\rho,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu\,\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)\,(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')_\nu$$
For a homogeneous isotropic medium, $\Pi_{\rho\rho}(q,\omega)=-\frac{cq^2}{\omega^2}\Pi_L(q,\omega)$, where $\Pi_L$ is the longitudinal projection of $\Pi_{jj}$. The **longitudinal dielectric function** is then $\epsilon_L=1-v\Pi_{\rho\rho}=1+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi_L$. This is the same formula as the "transverse" $\epsilon=1+4\pi c\Pi/\omega^2$ ($\Pi=i\omega\sigma/c$, $\epsilon=1+4\pi i\sigma/\omega$).
- Assumptions: the correlators obey continuity. In practice this means $\Pi_{jj}$ must be the **gauge-invariant** (para + dia) correlator, and the current operator must be consistent with the Hamiltonian (see Sec. 4 and Source issues).

For insulators $\Pi^{\rm e}\propto\omega^2$ at small $\omega$, so $4\pi c\Pi^{\rm e}/\omega^2\to\epsilon_\infty-1$.

**(384) Electronic current vertices** (the $\mathbf G=0$ component of (283)–(284))
$$j^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=\frac{e\hbar}{2im_e}\int_\Omega d\mathbf r\,e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\left\{\phi^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)\partial_\mu\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)-[\partial_\mu\phi^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)]\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)\right\}\qquad(384)$$
- Optical limit: $j^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k}=\frac{e}{m_e}\langle n\mathbf k|\hat p_\mu|m\mathbf k\rangle$.

**(385) Ionic contribution**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{j_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}\qquad(385)$$
$$j_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathrm a\alpha}\frac{Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}}{\sqrt{m_\mathrm a}}e_{\mathrm a\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q),\qquad j^*_{\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathrm b\beta}\frac{Z^*_{\mathrm b\nu\beta}}{\sqrt{m_\mathrm b}}e^*_{\mathrm b\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$
- $\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)$ and $e_{\mathrm a\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ are the "bare" phonons of Sec. 1. Their long-range dipolar part is not included: it is generated by dressing with $D$ (Sec. 7.1, LO–TO).

**2D replacements (p. 86):** $\Omega\to S$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}\to S_{\rm uc}$, $\mathbf q\to\mathbf Q$, $\mathbf k\to\mathbf K$, "etc., but formally all expressions are identical". Compare (300) and (305) **[context]**: the 2D self-energies carry $\delta(z)\delta(z')$. See Sec. 3.8 (S4) and Source issues for the correct sheet $D^0$.

### 3.8 Supplementary derived forms (mine, flagged; useful for the page and the visualizations)

**S1. Isotropic homogeneous medium.** Write $\Pi_{\mu\nu}=\Pi_L\hat q_\mu\hat q_\nu+\Pi_T(\delta_{\mu\nu}-\hat q_\mu\hat q_\nu)$, with $D^0_L=-4\pi c/\omega^2$ and $D^0_T=-4\pi c/(\omega^2-c^2q^2+i\eta)$. Then (380) decouples:
$$D_L(q,\omega)=\frac{D^0_L}{1-D^0_L\Pi_L}=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2\,\epsilon_L(q,\omega)},\qquad D_T(q,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2\epsilon_T(q,\omega)-c^2q^2},\qquad\epsilon_{L,T}=1+\frac{4\pi c\,\Pi_{L,T}}{\omega^2}$$
Poles: $\epsilon_L=0$ gives plasmons and LO phonons; $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon_T$ gives polaritons. Here $D^{lt}=D^{tl}=0$ to all orders, because $P_LP_T=0$.

**S2. Electrostatic limit and $W$.** By (359), in $\mathbf q$-space, $D^{ll}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}q_\mu q_\nu D_{11}$. In S1, $D_{11}=-\frac{\omega^2}{cq^2}D_L=\frac{4\pi}{q^2\epsilon_L}=W$. As $c\to\infty$, $D^{0,l}\propto c$, $\Pi\propto1/c$ and $D^{0,t}\propto1/c$, so $D^{0,t}\Pi\to0$ and only the $ll$ sector survives. $D_{11}$ then obeys $W=v+v(\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho})W$. This gives $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}W^{\rm e}+\dots$, Giustino's $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$ (Eqs. 104–105).

**S3. Fröhlich form of the phonon part of $W$.** At $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$ with $W^{\rm e}=4\pi/(\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q)$:
$$W^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}W^{\rm e}=\sum_\sigma\left(\frac{4\pi}{\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q}\right)^2\frac{|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma(\mathbf q)|^2}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_\sigma}F^0_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=\sum_\sigma\Omega\,|g^{\mathcal L}_\sigma(\mathbf q)|^2F^0_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)$$
Here $|g^{\mathcal L}|^2$ is the square of Giustino Eq. (180) without the Bloch overlap, in Gaussian units ($e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\to1$, $Z^*$ including $e$). $F^0_\sigma=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}$ is (298). The factor $\Omega=N\Omega_{\rm uc}$ comes from continuum vs crystal normalization. I am confident about the structure; check the overall normalization against Sec. 7's conventions before quoting it.

**S4. Sheet (2D) propagator at $z=z'=0$.** For a 2D crystal at $z=0$, Fourier-transform (375) back in $q_z$: $D^0(\mathbf Q,\omega;z,z')=\int\frac{dq_z}{2\pi}e^{iq_z(z-z')}D^0(\mathbf Q,q_z,\omega)$. For in-plane components at $z=z'=0$:
$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf Q,\omega;0,0)=\frac{2\pi c}{\omega^2\beta}\left(\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\mu\nu}-Q_\mu Q_\nu\right),\qquad\beta=\sqrt{Q^2-\omega^2/c^2}$$
Take ${\rm Re}\,\beta>0$. Inside the light cone, $\beta=-i\sqrt{\omega^2/c^2-Q^2}$ (outgoing waves). Projections:
$$D^0_{L,\rm 2D}=-\frac{2\pi c\,\beta}{\omega^2},\qquad D^0_{T,\rm 2D}=\frac{2\pi}{c\,\beta}$$
I verified these by numerical $q_z$ integration (script above). The electrostatic part alone is $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}Q^2v_{\rm 2D}$, with $v_{\rm 2D}=2\pi/Q$. The retarded sheet "Coulomb" interaction is therefore $v_{\rm eff}(Q,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{cQ^2}D^0_{L,\rm 2D}=\frac{2\pi}{Q}\sqrt{1-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2Q^2}}$. For a sheet, the in-plane longitudinal current is not purely longitudinal in 3D, so $l$–$t$ mixing is built into 2D geometry. The sheet Dyson equation, with $\Pi^{\rm 2D}$ from (285)/(300)/(305), is $D=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rm 2D}D$ evaluated at $z=z'=0$.

**S5. Two-level electronic model of (383)** (one pair $v\to c$, gap $\Delta$, $|j_{vc}|$; mine):
$$\Pi^{\rm e}_L(\omega)=-\frac{4|j|^2}{c\Omega}\frac{(\hbar\omega)^2}{\Delta[(\hbar\omega)^2-\Delta^2]},\qquad\Pi^{\rm para,e}_L(\omega)=-\frac{4|j|^2}{c\Omega}\frac{\Delta}{(\hbar\omega)^2-\Delta^2}$$
$$\epsilon(\omega)=1+\frac{16\pi\hbar^2|j|^2}{\Omega\Delta}\frac{1}{\Delta^2-(\hbar\omega)^2}\ \ (\text{gauge-invariant}),\qquad\epsilon^{\rm para\,only}(\omega)=1-\frac{16\pi\hbar^2|j|^2\Delta}{\Omega(\hbar\omega)^2[(\hbar\omega)^2-\Delta^2]}\ \ (\text{spurious }1/\omega^2)$$

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Why charge and current correlators are not independent.** A crystal has one response. The density response is the longitudinal part of the current response, because $\rho$ and $\mathbf j$ are tied by $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$. In $\mathbf q$-space the four connection formulas read:
$$\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,\mathbf q\cdot\Pi_{jj}\cdot\mathbf q,\qquad\Pi_{\rho j}=\frac1\omega\,\mathbf q\cdot\Pi_{jj},\qquad\Pi_{j\rho}=-\frac c\omega\,\Pi_{jj}\cdot\mathbf q .$$
In QED these are the **Ward identities**, $q_\mu\Pi^{\mu\nu}=0$ for the 4-current. The 4×4 matrix of $(\rho,\mathbf j)$ correlators has only a 3×3 block of independent information. Sec. 6 uses this to eliminate the scalar row and column.

**(b) Gauge invariance needs the diamagnetic term.** The ionic proof works only because $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}\propto\omega^2/(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)$, and the $\omega^2$ comes from the diamagnetic subtraction $-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ (Eq. 311). With the paramagnetic part alone, the "density response" obtained via (350) is
$$-\frac{c}{\omega^2}q^2\Pi^{\rm para}_L=\frac{q^2|j|^2}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\left[\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}-\frac{1}{\omega^2}\right].$$
It contains a spurious $-1/\omega^2$ term. In $\epsilon_L=1-v\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ this becomes $+\Omega_p^2/\omega^2$: a Drude-like term of the wrong sign, exactly minus the missing diamagnetic response $-\Omega_p^2/\omega^2$. An insulator would appear singular at $\omega\to0$. Microscopically, $\langle T\{\dot P\dot P\}\rangle(\omega)=\omega^2\langle T\{PP\}\rangle(\omega)+$ (an equal-time contact term from differentiating the $T$-product), and the diamagnetic term cancels the contact term exactly. For the user's work: this is the familiar trouble with **velocity-gauge** optical spectra (TDDFT, BSE, RPA with $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$), which diverge as $1/\omega^2$ unless the diamagnetic or sum-rule term is included. (350) is also why **length-gauge** and **velocity-gauge** results agree once both are done consistently: $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ (density vertices $\langle c|e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r}|v\rangle\approx-i\mathbf q\cdot\langle c|\mathbf r|v\rangle$) and $\Pi_{jj}$ (momentum vertices) are related by $\langle c|\mathbf p|v\rangle=im\omega_{cv}\langle c|\mathbf r|v\rangle$. That is the origin of the factor $\hbar\omega/(E_n-E_m)$ in (383).

**(c) Prefactors $-i/\hbar$ vs $i/\hbar c$.** A scalar vertex couples as $+\int\rho\phi$ and a current vertex as $-\frac1c\int\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$. Every $\mathbf j$ vertex therefore brings a $-1/c$. The bookkeeping of Fig. 8 is the diagrammatic statement that $D^{0,t}$, $\Pi_{\rho j}$ and $\Pi_{jj}$ each carry exactly one $1/c$.

**(d) Reciprocity, (347)–(348).** Exchanging the two operators of a time-ordered correlator (and their positions) corresponds to $\omega\to-\omega$. This is the frequency-domain version of the $T$-product symmetry. It is what lets one derivative be moved from the left argument to the right one: from (344), derivative on $\mathbf r$, to (349), derivative on $\mathbf r'$.

**(e) The 4×4 → 3×3 reduction is a change to the temporal gauge.** The operators (359) are exactly what turns a Coulomb-gauge-like description (scalar potential $\phi$ sourced by $\rho$) into a temporal-gauge description (vector potential $\mathbf A_l$ sourced by $\mathbf j$). They use $\mathbf A_l=\frac{ic}{\omega}\nabla\phi$ and $\rho=-\frac i\omega\nabla\cdot\mathbf j$. The Coulomb line becomes
$$D^{0,l}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial\partial' v\;\xrightarrow{\ \mathbf q\ }\;-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\hat q\hat q,$$
i.e. Coulomb's law written for $\mathbf A$. The author calls (364) "ad hoc", because the quantized longitudinal photon has no dynamics in $H_0$ and its propagator vanishes (263). But $D^{0,l}$ is not a new particle. It is the instantaneous Coulomb interaction re-expressed as a response of $\mathbf A_l$, and Sec. 6.0.4 confirms this by showing that $D^{0,l}+D^{0,t}$ is the vacuum Maxwell propagator. In the temporal gauge, a static charge produces $\mathbf A_l$ that grows linearly in time ($\mathbf E=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}$ is constant). This is the meaning of the $1/\omega^2$ double pole.

**(f) What $D$ is.** $D$ is "W with photons": the full screened electromagnetic interaction between currents in a polarizable crystal of electrons and phonons.
- **Longitudinal sector:** contains $W$. In an isotropic medium $D_{11}=W=v/\epsilon_L$ exactly (S1, S2). Poles at $\epsilon_L=0$ give plasmons and LO phonons.
- **Transverse sector:** dressed photons. Poles at $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon_T(\omega)$ give exciton- and phonon-polaritons.
- **Mixed sectors $lt$, $tl$:** vanish in isotropic homogeneous media, because $\hat q\cdot\Pi\cdot\hat t=0$. They are nonzero in anisotropic crystals off the principal axes ($\propto(\Pi_\parallel-\Pi_\perp)\sin\theta\cos\theta$, e.g. hyperbolic phonon polaritons in hBN), when local fields are present, and intrinsically in 2D sheets (S4).

**(g) The electrostatic limit (Giustino's world).** $D^{0,l}\propto c$ while $D^{0,t}\to\frac{4\pi}{cq^2}P_T\propto1/c$ (Biot–Savart), and every $\Pi\propto1/c$. So $D^{0,l}\Pi$ is $c$-independent while $D^{0,t}\Pi\propto1/c^2$. Taking $c\to\infty$ keeps only the $ll$ sector, and $D^{ll}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}qq\,W$ with $W=v+v\Pi_{\rho\rho}W$. This is the RPA $W$ of GW and BSE when $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm e}$, and $W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$ when ions are included.

**(h) Orders of magnitude: where retardation matters.** The light wave vector is $q_{\rm light}=\hbar\omega/\hbar c$, with $\hbar c=1973.27$ eV·Å:
- $\hbar\omega=50$ meV (optical phonons): $q_{\rm light}=2.5\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$, i.e. $\lambda\approx25\ \mu$m.
- $\hbar\omega=200$ meV (hBN TO): $1.0\times10^{-4}$ Å$^{-1}$.
- $\hbar\omega=2$ eV (excitons in TMDs): $1.0\times10^{-3}$ Å$^{-1}$.

The BZ extends over $\sim1$–$2$ Å$^{-1}$. So $|D^{0,t}_T/D^{0,l}_L|=1/|1-c^2q^2/\omega^2|\approx(q_{\rm light}/q)^2$ is negligible over all but a $10^{-5}$–$10^{-3}$ fraction of the BZ near $\Gamma$. That is why electrostatic DFPT (Giustino) is excellent almost everywhere. But optics probes exactly $q\approx q_{\rm light}$, and the famous non-analyticity of LO–TO at $\Gamma$ lives at $q\to0$. **The order of the limits $q\to0$ and $c\to\infty$ matters.** For $q\lesssim\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\omega_{\rm TO}/c$ the TO phonon is a polariton. The LO phonon is purely longitudinal and stays at $\omega_{\rm LO}$ for all $q$ (S1).

**(i) Polar crystal numbers** (for the visualizations).
- **GaAs:** $\hbar\omega_{\rm TO}=33.2$ meV (268 cm$^{-1}$), $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}=36.2$ meV (292 cm$^{-1}$), $\epsilon_\infty=10.9$. LST gives $\epsilon_0=12.96$. From (385), $\Omega_p^2=4\pi|j_{\rm TO}|^2/\Omega_{\rm uc}=\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2)$, so $\hbar\Omega_p=47.6$ meV. With $\Omega_{\rm uc}=45.2$ Å$^3$ and $\mu=36.1$ amu this implies $Z^*\approx2.2$ (literature ≈2.1–2.2).
- **hBN** (bulk, in-plane): $\hbar\omega_{\rm TO}\approx169.5$ meV (1367 cm$^{-1}$), $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}\approx199.6$ meV (1610 cm$^{-1}$), $\epsilon_\infty\approx4.9$. Out-of-plane: $\approx96.7$ meV (780 cm$^{-1}$) and $\approx102.9$ meV (830 cm$^{-1}$), $\epsilon_{\infty,z}\approx2.95$.
- **NaCl:** 20.3 / 32.7 meV, $\epsilon_\infty=2.34$.

These are standard literature values; they are not quoted in the manuscript.

**(j) The ionic $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ is a point-dipole grating.** Because $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ is $\mathbf G$-independent, $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}\propto[(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf j][(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\cdot\mathbf j]^*$ does not decay with $\mathbf G$. Its $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$ element contains $\mathbf q\cdot Z^*\cdot\mathbf e/\sqrt m$, the Fröhlich numerator. The author warns (p. 62) that the point-dipole model gives unphysical Bragg (umklapp) scattering, which "should be avoided whenever possible". In practice keep $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$, or regularize with a form factor, as Verdi–Giustino do with $e^{-|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|^2/4\alpha}$.

**(k) 2D (the user's systems).** The replacements $\Omega\to S$ etc. are only half the story. The sheet sees the free propagator at $z=z'=0$ (S4). Two consequences follow. (i) For a polar monolayer the longitudinal optical mode obeys
$$\omega^2=\omega_{\rm TO}^2+\frac{2\pi Z^{*2}}{\mu S_{\rm uc}}\frac{\beta}{1+r_0\beta},\qquad r_0=2\pi\alpha_{\rm 2D},\quad\beta\to Q\ \text{(electrostatic)},$$
with $Z^*$ in EPP charge units. For a dimensionless Born charge, write $e^2Z^{*2}$ with $e^2=14.40$ eV·Å.
so **there is no LO–TO splitting at $Q=0$ in 2D** (Sohier, Gibertini, Calandra, Mauri, Marzari, Nano Lett. 17, 3758 (2017)). Here $\alpha_{\rm 2D}$ is the electronic sheet polarizability, $\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}\approx\omega^2\alpha_{\rm 2D}/c$. For hBN ($Z^*\approx2.7$, $\mu=6.10$ amu, $S_{\rm uc}=5.43$ Å$^2$), $(\hbar\omega)^2-(\hbar\omega_{\rm TO})^2=0.0832\,Q[{\rm Å}^{-1}]/(1+r_0Q)$ eV$^2$. That is 1475 cm$^{-1}$ at $Q=0.1$ Å$^{-1}$ for $r_0=7.6$ Å, and 1553 cm$^{-1}$ for $r_0=0$. (ii) Retardation for the sheet plasmon of doped graphene ($\omega^2=2D_w\beta$, $D_w=e^2E_F/\hbar^2$) matters only for $Q<Q_c=e^2E_F/(\hbar c)^2=3.7\times10^{-6}E_F[{\rm eV}]$ Å$^{-1}$. Below that the plasmon merges with the light line: exactly $\omega^2=2D_w(\sqrt{Q^2+Q_c^2}-Q_c)$. Inside the light cone a polar sheet phonon radiates. Its radiative width at $Q=0$ is $\hbar\Gamma=2\pi\alpha\hbar^2Z^{*2}/(\mu S_{\rm uc})\approx0.04$ meV for hBN (dimensionless $Z^*$, $\alpha=e^2/\hbar c$; equivalently $\Gamma=2\pi|j|^2/(cS_{\rm uc})$ from $1-D^0_{T,\rm 2D}\Pi^{\rm ion}_T=0$ with $\beta=-i\omega/c$) (≈0.3 cm$^{-1}$; my estimate from S4).

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of the EPP manuscript

**Reproduces / generalizes standard e–ph theory:**
- **$W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$ (Giustino Eqs. 104–105, Sec. IV.B).** In the electrostatic limit, the $ll$ sector of the Dyson equation (379) with $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ maps via (350) and (364) to $W=v+v(\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho})W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}W^{\rm e}+\dots$ This is Giustino's $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$, where Giustino's dressed nuclear-density correlator $D$ corresponds to the bare EPP $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ resummed with $W^{\rm e}$. EPP generalizes this to the full electromagnetic $D$, with transverse photons, retardation, and $l$–$t$ mixing.
- **Nuclear density correlator (Giustino Eq. 108, with 103 and 109).** Giustino writes $D(12)=\sum Z_\kappa\nabla\delta(\mathbf r_1-\boldsymbol\tau)\,D_{\tau\tau}\,Z_{\kappa'}\nabla\delta(\mathbf r_2-\boldsymbol\tau')$. EPP's (353) and the unnumbered p. 76 equation, $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}=-\frac i\hbar\partial\partial'\sum Z^*Z^*\langle T\{QQ\}\rangle\delta\delta$, have **the same form**. Differences: Born charges $Z^*$ replace bare nuclear charges $Z_\kappa$, which absorbs the electronic dipolar screening at long wavelength. The phonons are the bare (short-range) ones of Sec. 1. All dipoles are placed at the cell origin.
- **Phonon self-energy (Giustino Eqs. 116–121).** Giustino's $\Pi(\omega)$ is a mixed second derivative $Z\partial\,Z'\partial'$ of $W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)$ (Eq. 121). EPP's connection formula $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial\partial'\Pi_{jj}$ and its gauge-invariant $\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$ forms ((311), (383), (385)) play the same structural roles. The "dynamic minus static" subtraction appears on both sides: Giustino's acoustic sum rule (Eq. 120) vs EPP's gauge-invariance condition $\Pi_{jj}(\mathbf q,0)=0$. Both are "a uniform static displacement/gauge generates no force/current" sum rules. **Naming clash for the page:** Giustino's $\Pi$ is the **phonon** self-energy; EPP's $\Pi_{jj}$ is the **photon** self-energy, i.e. the current–current polarizability. EPP's phonon self-energy appears only in Sec. 7 (Eqs. 386–393), built from $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ dressed by $D^{\rm e}$ (388).
- **Hedin–Baym equations (Giustino Sec. IV.D, Table I).** $W=v+vP_{\rm e}W$ becomes $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D$. Here $P\leftrightarrow\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial\partial'\Pi_{jj}$ and $v\leftrightarrow D^{0,l}$, and $D^{0,t}$ is new. The EPP Dyson equation is the photon-propagator analogue of the Hedin–Baym $W$ line, with phonons included inside $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ rather than as a separate $D$.
- **Fröhlich vertex (Verdi & Giustino, PRL 115, 176401 (2015); Giustino Eq. 180, Sec. VI.A.3).** The ionic current vertex $j_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathrm a\alpha}Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}e_{\mathrm a\alpha\sigma}/\sqrt{m_\mathrm a}$ (295) contracted with $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ is **exactly the Verdi–Giustino numerator** $\sum_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot Z^*_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$. The $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$ element of $W^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}W^{\rm e}$ equals $\Omega|g^{\mathcal L}|^2F^0_\sigma$ (S3). So this range already contains the electrostatic Fröhlich interaction. Sec. 7.2 extracts the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex from the full $D$. The manuscript cites Verdi–Giustino at Eq. (131) (p. 24) and again in Sec. 7, but not on pp. 73–86.
- **DFPT (Giustino Sec. III.B).** DFPT is the static, electrostatic version: $\omega\to0$ in $\Pi^{\rm e}$ (with RPA+xc), $c\to\infty$, and only the $ll$ sector. In EPP the static screening comes from $\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}(\omega\to0)$, which is finite through (350), even though $\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}(\mathbf q,0)=0$. The LO–TO non-analytic term of DFPT corresponds to the pole of $D_L=-4\pi c/(\omega^2\epsilon_L)$; Sec. 7.1 derives it.
- **Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic (Giustino Sec. V.A, Eqs. 125, 135, 141; Sec. VII).** EPP keeps the full $\omega$-dependence of $\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (383). When phonons are later dressed by $D^{\rm e}$ (Sec. 7, Eq. 388), the self-energy is automatically nonadiabatic. Giustino's $\Pi^{\rm NA}=g^{\rm b}[P_{\rm e}(\omega)-P_{\rm e}(0)]g$ (Eq. 141) has the same "dynamic minus static" structure as (383). "Retardation" in Giustino means frequency-dependent electronic screening. In EPP it additionally means a finite speed of light.
- **Giustino Eq. (1) Hamiltonian.** It has no photon field. Its linear vertex $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,c^\dagger c(a+a^\dagger)$ is replaced in EPP (312) by electron–photon ($\mathbf j^{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf A$, $\rho^{\rm e}A^2$), ion–photon ($\mathbf j^{\rm ion}\cdot\mathbf A$, $\rho^{\rm ion}A^2$) and Coulomb ($\rho^{\rm e}+\rho^{\rm ion}$) couplings. The e–ph interaction is mediated by the field. In Giustino the quadratic Debye–Waller term is needed for translational invariance. In EPP the quadratic diamagnetic $A^2$ terms are needed for gauge invariance; this range shows explicitly that they are what makes the connection formulas hold.

**Departs from standard theory:**
- The longitudinal field is treated in the temporal gauge as a vector-potential propagator $D^{0,l}\propto1/\omega^2$, not as a scalar Coulomb line. This is unusual in the e–ph literature, but equivalent for conserved currents.
- Ionic responses use point Born-charge dipoles at cell origins ($\mathbf G$-independent $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$). DFPT uses the full microscopic $\partial V/\partial\tau$.
- Everything is RPA (no $f_{xc}$, no excitonic vertex), at $T=0$, time-ordered.

**Links inside the EPP manuscript:**
- Sec. 4.7: $D^{0,t}$ (261) = (372). $D^{0,l}=0$ in the quantized theory (263) is why (364) is called "ad hoc".
- Sec. 5: $\Pi^{\rm e}$ (279), (281)–(285) feed into (383)–(384). $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ (280), (290), (297)–(311) feed into (352)/(385). The 2D forms are (285), (300) and (305).
- Sec. 6 opening (pp. 68–72): (312)–(340) and Fig. 7.
- Sec. 1 (83) and (31)–(32): the phonon displacement operator used in the proof.
- Sec. 7 (pp. 86 ff.): takes (379)/(380) as its starting point. $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ is dressed by $D^{\rm e}$ (388), which is (379) with only $\Pi^{\rm e}$. The author notes the analogy $\chi_A=\chi^0_A+\chi^0_AW_B\chi^0_A+\dots$ The dynamical ionic current vertices are $J_{\mu\sigma}=\sqrt{\hbar/(2c\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma)}\,\omega\,j_{\mu\sigma}$ (392). Then come LO–TO (7.1), the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex (7.2) and the electrostatic limit (7.3).
- "Lecture 9" (external, not in the PDF) is cited for the vacuum propagator (Eq. 72 there), the $\Pi_{jj}$ perturbative derivation, and the q2D gauge-invariance proof.

## 8. Source issues

1. **Small digression, p. 74, wrong correlator name.** "However $\Pi_{j\rho}$ is paired with the interaction $-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j\mathbf A^{ext}$ from the right which then gives the factor $\frac{i}{\hbar c}$." This should read **$\Pi_{\rho j}$**. The first sentence already treats $\Pi_{j\rho}$, which is paired with $\int\rho\,\phi^{\rm ext}$. Confident.
2. **Same digression: "$\int d\mathbf r\rho V\phi^{ext}$".** The "$V$" is stray. The intended perturbation is $\int d\mathbf r\,\rho\,\phi^{\rm ext}$, with $\phi^{\rm ext}=v\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}$.
3. **"Proof" of Sec. 6.0.2 is a single-case verification, not an induction.** The author verifies (351) for the RPA ionic correlator only and then says "we inductively conclude that all other connection formulas must also be correct". The electronic case and (341), (344), (349) are not independently checked. They do hold in general, as consequences of continuity (Ward identity), **provided** (i) $\Pi_{jj}$ is the full gauge-invariant (para + dia) correlator and (ii) the current operator is consistent with the Hamiltonian. Condition (ii) fails for nonlocal pseudopotentials with the bare current (384), unless $\mathbf j$ is augmented by $\frac{ie}{\hbar}[V_{\rm NL},\mathbf r]$. Worth a footnote on the page.
4. **(341)/(344) "equal only if".** Strictly this needs arbitrary $\rho^{\rm ext}$ (and invertible $W$) or arbitrary $\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$. That is fine physically, but the argument derives an operator identity from an integrated one, and the reader should know why.
5. **Index "1" for the scalar component.** $D_{11}$, $D_{1\nu}$, $D_{\mu1}$ use "1" for the Coulomb (scalar) slot, while $\mu,\nu=x,y,z$. "1" could be confused with $x$. The conventional label is 0 (time-like). Notation only.
6. **(353) and p. 76: $\langle\Phi^{\rm ion}_0|$ vs $\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|$** used elsewhere (e.g. (280), (322)). Same state.
7. **(314) [context] summation label.** It is written $\sum_{i\nu}$, but $Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\nu}Q_{i\mathrm a\nu}$ needs $\sum_{i\mathrm a\nu}$. The dipole of atom $\mathrm a$ is also placed at $\mathbf R^0_i$ instead of $\mathbf R^0_i+\boldsymbol\tau_\mathrm a$. This is the point-dipole simplification acknowledged on p. 62, but it means $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}$ lacks the structure factors $e^{-i\mathbf G\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\mathrm a}$. Harmless at $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$.
8. **(364) is not really "ad hoc".** It follows from classical electrostatics in the temporal gauge: $\mathbf A_l=\frac{ic}{\omega}\nabla\phi$ plus continuity, as shown in Sec. 4(e). Its $1/\omega^2$ double pole has no $i\eta$ prescription, a known temporal-gauge infrared ambiguity. (375) assigns $i\eta$ only to the light-cone pole. Not an error; a gap in the motivation.
9. **Fig. 10(c) label typo.** "$j^{ie}$" should be "$j^{\rm e}$".
10. **"Let's now act; on 1. longitudinal sector"** (p. 79): stray semicolon.
11. **(369)–(371) notation.** (369) uses $\Pi_{jj\alpha\beta}$, but (371) defines $\Pi_{\mu\nu}$ (no $jj$). (379)/(382) revert to $\Pi_{jj}$. Same object.
12. **Sec. 6.0.5 uses capital "$V$"** for the longitudinal propagator, elsewhere $v$.
13. **(380) neglects local fields.** In a crystal, $\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$ is a matrix in $\mathbf G,\mathbf G'$ (282), and $D^0$ is diagonal in $\mathbf G$ with argument $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$. The correct form is $D_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}=D^0(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}+\sum_{\mathbf G''}D^0(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\Pi_{\mathbf G\mathbf G''}D_{\mathbf G''\mathbf G'}$. (380)–(385) are its $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$, no-local-field approximation (or the homogeneous-medium form). Even $\Pi^{\rm ion}$, which is $\mathbf G$-independent, is nonzero for $\mathbf G\ne\mathbf G'$. Not stated in the text.
14. **2D transition "trivial".** The replacements $\Omega\to S$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}\to S_{\rm uc}$, $\mathbf q\to\mathbf Q$, $\mathbf k\to\mathbf K$ are not sufficient. The 2D self-energies (300), (305) carry $\delta(z)\delta(z')$, so the Dyson equation must be taken at $z=z'=0$ with the sheet propagator $D^0(\mathbf Q,\omega;0,0)=\frac{2\pi c}{\omega^2\beta}(\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\mu\nu}-Q_\mu Q_\nu)$, $\beta=\sqrt{Q^2-\omega^2/c^2}$ (in-plane components). That replaces $\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\frac{\cdots}{q^2-\omega^2/c^2}$; see S4. The $zz$ component needs separate care: its $q_z$ integral contains a contact term. Confident for the in-plane components, which I verified numerically.
15. **(384) integration domain** "$\Omega$" vs "$\Omega_\infty$" in (283). Notation only. The Bloch functions are normalized in the crystal volume.
16. **(375) "identical to the propagator of the free EM field".** True in the temporal gauge. Photon propagators are gauge-dependent, and only their action on conserved currents is gauge-invariant. That is exactly what the connection formulas guarantee. Worth stating.
17. **Time-ordered vs retarded.** The text interprets $D^0\mathbf j^{\rm ext}$ as the physical (causal) field (p. 83). With the time-ordered definitions used throughout, this holds for $\omega>0$ at $T=0$. Retarded functions are obtained by $\omega\to\omega+i\eta$ in all denominators (for $\omega<0$ the time-ordered and retarded functions differ in the sign of ${\rm Im}$). Conceptual caveat, not an error.
18. **"inductively conclude" (p. 85).** Going from the geometric series (378) to the Dyson equation (379) is standard. "Inductively" here only means summing the series.
