## 1. Overview

This part of the review is the "applications" half of the theory. All the machinery has already been built:

- the Hedin–Baym equations;
- the Fan–Migdal (FM) and Debye–Waller (DW) self-energies;
- the DFPT matrix elements $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$;
- Wannier interpolation.

Giustino now shows how that machinery becomes measurable quantities, in four families.

1. **Band structures at finite temperature (Sec. IX.A).** Atoms vibrate even at $T=0$, so every band energy is shifted. This is the *zero-point renormalization* (ZPR). The shift grows with temperature as the phonon populations $n_{\mathbf q\nu}(T)$ grow, which is the Varshni effect: gaps usually shrink on heating. In diamond the direct gap is reduced by more than 0.5 eV at $T=0$ (628 meV with $GW$ matrix elements, Fig. 13).
   - The standard tool is the Allen–Heine formula. Its practical form uses a rigid-ion sum rule, so that the DW term is written with first-order DFPT matrix elements only.
   - The alternative is "brute force": average the Kohn–Sham eigenvalues over displaced supercells.
   - In polar materials the long-range Fröhlich coupling makes the *adiabatic* formula diverge. One must keep the phonon frequency in the denominators.
2. **Optics (Sec. IX.B).** In indirect-gap semiconductors such as silicon, light can be absorbed below the direct gap only if a phonon supplies the missing crystal momentum. This process is described by second-order perturbation theory, Eq. (200) (Hall–Bardeen–Blatt). It contains one electron–photon vertex ($\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v$, classical field, dipole approximation) and one electron–phonon vertex ($g$).
3. **Transport (Sec. X).** The imaginary part of the FM self-energy gives carrier lifetimes, of order fs to ps. Inserting the corresponding golden-rule rates into the linearized Boltzmann equation gives the phonon-limited mobility, for example of $n$-Si (Fig. 16).
4. **Superconductivity (Sec. XI).** The same $|g|^2$ build the Eliashberg function $\alpha^2F(\omega)$. Its inverse moment gives $\lambda$, which enters the McMillan–Allen–Dynes $T_c$ formula. The full anisotropic Migdal–Eliashberg equations give the two gaps of MgB$_2$. SCDFT avoids the empirical $\mu^*$.

The section closes with two critiques:

- **DFT underestimates $g$.** The DFT screening is too strong, and $GW$ or hybrid functionals increase $\lambda$ by 40–300% in some systems.
- **Open problems** (Sec. XIII): nonadiabatic and *retardation* effects, anharmonicity, quadratic couplings, bare versus screened vertices in the phonon self-energy, and spin.

Appendices A and B fix the BvK and ladder-operator conventions used throughout.

For the EPP story, this range shows where every application relies on two simplifications: an **instantaneous, longitudinal (electrostatic) Coulomb interaction** and a **classical external photon field**. EPP relaxes both.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **DW in metals (p. 41).** The DW self-energy is the expectation value of a static local potential $V_{\rm DW}$, Eq. (190). It is constant for a homogeneous electron gas, and in semiconductors it varies smoothly with $\mathbf k$ within a band ($\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf p$ argument). This is why it is traditionally dropped in metals. It can still differ strongly between *different* bands, which matters for band gaps.
2. **Mass enhancement (p. 41).** From Eq. (189) [context], $\mathbf V_{n\mathbf k}=\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}/(1+\lambda_{n\mathbf k})$, so $m_{\rm ep}=m^*(1+\lambda_{n\mathbf k})$. Grimvall's entropy formula, Eq. (191), together with $\Sigma^{\rm FM}\simeq-\lambda\hbar\omega$ at low energy, gives the low-$T$ heat capacity $C_e\propto(1+\lambda)T$, Eq. (192). At high $T$ the renormalization disappears.
3. **Allen–Heine (IX.A.1).** The temperature-dependent band structure follows from the FM+DW self-energies, Eqs. (166)/(167) [context]. The DW term needs second derivatives of $V^{\rm KS}$, which are costly.
   - Translational invariance gives two sum rules, Eqs. (193)–(194).
   - The **rigid-ion approximation** (RIA) drops off-site second derivatives.
   - Together these express DW through *first-order* matrix elements at $\mathbf q=0$, Eqs. (195)–(198).
4. **Applications of Allen–Heine.**
   - Silicon and BN with BSE excitons (Marini 2008).
   - Diamond (Giustino–Louie–Cohen 2010), followed by: the nonadiabatic Green's-function approach (Cannuccia–Marini); a test of the RIA (error ~10 meV, below 4 meV in Poncé et al.); and $GW$ matrix elements (Antonius 2014, +~200 meV, Fig. 13).
   - GaN (Kawai 2014, AH plus BSE); Si, diamond, BN and AlN (Poncé 2015); LiNbO$_3$ and black phosphorus; first full finite-$T$ band structures (Antonius 2015, Poncé 2015).
5. **Polar divergence.** In polar crystals the adiabatic Eq. (167) diverges for band extrema as the broadening $\eta\to0$ on dense grids. The integrand behaves as $q^{-4}$ while $d\mathbf q=4\pi q^2dq$. There are several remedies:
   - principal-value integration with finite $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$, then $\omega\to0$ (Fan 1951);
   - analytic integration near $\mathbf q=0$ (Nery–Allen 2016);
   - Brillouin–Wigner Eq. (166) with the physical linewidth $\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}$ instead of $\eta$;
   - spectral functions.

   Fröhlich physics in the ZPR:
   - Botti–Marques (2013) compute $W$ *including lattice screening* with an LST model dielectric, giving ZPR above 1 eV in LiF, LiCl, NaCl and MgO.
   - Nery–Allen give a Fröhlich ZPR of 45 meV out of 150 meV total in GaN.
6. **Nonperturbative adiabatic approach (IX.A.2).** Taylor-expand $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$ to second order in displacements and average over the harmonic nuclear wavefunction, Eq. (199). The canonical average gives Bose–Einstein $n_{\mathbf q\nu}(T)$.
   - With perturbative derivatives this becomes Allen–Heine.
   - With frozen-phonon supercells there is no RIA.
   - Related tools: Brooks' theorem; Monte Carlo sampling; the "optimum configuration" of Zacharias–Giustino; nondiagonal supercells.
   - Beyond-harmonic effects (LiH, ice, etc.) and links to path-integral MD.
7. **Phonon-assisted absorption (IX.B).** The second-order golden rule gives Eq. (200), which was first applied *ab initio* to Si (Noffsinger 2012, Fig. 14).
   - **Limitation:** clamped-nuclei energies in the delta functions, so the onset does not depend on $T$.
   - **Fix:** put the AH renormalization only in the delta functions and in the first denominator (real transitions). Alternatively, use the Williams–Lax semiclassical average of $\epsilon_2$ over nuclear configurations, Eq. (201) (Zacharias–Patrick–Giustino 2015).
8. **Linewidths and lifetimes (X.A).** Eq. (170) [context] is applied to:
   - the broadening of absorption edges (diamond, Si);
   - PL linewidths in perovskites, where the bare golden rule overestimates and a $Z_{n\mathbf k}$ renormalization is needed;
   - hot-carrier thermalization in GaAs (Fig. 15) and Si;
   - real-time dynamics (NEGF).
9. **Mobility (X.B).** The semiclassical current $\mathbf J$ and the linearized Boltzmann equation are Eqs. (202)–(203). The energy-relaxation-time approximation (RTA) drops the in-scattering term, so that the right-hand side becomes $-(f-f^0)/\tau$. Applied to $n$-Si (Li 2015, Fig. 16), with a list of other materials.
10. **Superconductors (XI).**
    - (A) The McMillan–Allen–Dynes formula, Eq. (204), with $\alpha^2F$, $\lambda$ and $\omega_{\log}$ from Eqs. (205)–(207), and $\mu^*$ from Morel–Anderson.
    - (B) Anisotropic Migdal–Eliashberg, Eqs. (208)–(210), on the Matsubara axis. MgB$_2$ shows two gaps (Fig. 17).
    - (C) SCDFT gap equation, Eq. (211), with a real-space order parameter (Fig. 18).
11. **Beyond LDA (XII).** Eq. (212) shows that $g$ is the bare vertex screened by $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$. DFT over-screens, so $g$ is too small.
    - Evidence from LDA+U, $GW$, hybrids and DMFT, in many systems.
    - A scaling argument: AH ZPR $\propto\epsilon_\infty^{-2}$, while the phonon frequencies carry only an $\epsilon_\infty^{-1/2}$ screening term.
12. **Conclusions (XIII).** Open problems:
    - non-adiabatic and nonlocal $g$;
    - retardation in doped oxides ("uncharted territory");
    - anharmonicity;
    - quadratic couplings (DW) beyond Fröhlich-type Hamiltonians;
    - bare versus screened vertex in the phonon self-energy;
    - spin–phonon coupling and the generalization of Hedin–Baym to noncollinear spin and SOC.
13. **Appendix A.** BvK grid and the sum rules (A1).
14. **Appendix B.** Complex normal coordinates (B1)–(B3). Real normal coordinates via the partition of $\mathbf q$ into sets $\mathcal A,\mathcal B,\mathcal C$ (B4). Harmonic oscillators (B5)–(B10). Circular (complex) ladder operators (B11)–(B14). This gives $z_{\mathbf q\nu}=l_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})$, Eq. (B15), and therefore Eq. (20).

## 3. Key equations

### 3.0 Conventions (valid for this whole range)

- **Units.** SI: $\epsilon_0$ appears explicitly in Eq. (200), and the Coulomb interaction is $e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$. $\hbar$ is explicit, $\omega$ is an angular frequency, and $\varepsilon$, $E$ are energies. Self-energies $\Sigma_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)$ are energies written as functions of $\omega$, hence the $\hbar^{-1}$ in Eqs. (189) and (191). (EPP uses Gaussian units, e.g. $v_Q=2\pi/Q$ in 2D. Convert with $e^2\to e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0$.)
- **Lattice and normalization.**
  - The BvK supercell has $N_p=N_1N_2N_3$ cells of volume $\Omega$, and $\Omega_{\rm BZ}=(2\pi)^3/\Omega$.
  - All $\mathbf k,\mathbf q$ integrals are *BZ averages*, $\int d\mathbf q/\Omega_{\rm BZ}\leftrightarrow N_p^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf q}$. In Sec. XI, $\sum_{\mathbf k'}\equiv\Omega_{\rm BZ}^{-1}\int d\mathbf k'$.
  - "uc" means an integral over one unit cell.
  - $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc}$ (Eq. (38) [context]). It has units of energy and does not depend on $N_p$.
- **Phonons.**
  - $l_{\mathbf q\nu}=[\hbar/(2M_0\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2}$ (Eq. (21)), where $M_0$ is an arbitrary reference mass.
  - $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}=l_{\mathbf q\nu}\sum_{\kappa\alpha}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\,\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}$.
  - $n_{\mathbf q\nu}=[e^{\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}/k_BT}-1]^{-1}$ (Bose–Einstein).
  - $f_{n\mathbf k}$ are Fermi–Dirac occupations, and $f^0$ is the equilibrium value.
- **Fourier sign.** $\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\propto\sum_{\mathbf q}e^{+i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}(\cdots)$ (Eqs. (20), (B3)), and $z_{\mathbf q\nu}\propto\sum_pe^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}(\cdots)$ (B1).
- **Density of states.** $N_{\rm F}$ is the DOS at $\varepsilon_{\rm F}$ **per spin** and per unit cell. This is implied by the $\tfrac23\pi^2$ in Eq. (192), compared with the textbook $\tfrac13\pi^2 g(\varepsilon_F)$ for the total DOS.
- **Dimensions.** $\alpha^2F(\omega)$ and $\lambda$ are dimensionless.

### 3.1 End of Sec. VIII: DW in metals, mass enhancement, heat capacity

**Eq. (189) [context, p. 40]: mass enhancement parameter.**
$$\lambda_{n\mathbf k}=Z_{n\mathbf k}^{-1}-1=-\hbar^{-1}\,\partial\,{\rm Re}\Sigma_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)/\partial\omega\big|_{\omega=E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar}\qquad(189)$$
- $Z_{n\mathbf k}$ is the quasiparticle strength of Eq. (174).
- With $\Gamma=0$ in Eq. (164), the renormalized velocity is $\mathbf V_{n\mathbf k}=Z_{n\mathbf k}\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}=\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}/(1+\lambda_{n\mathbf k})$, where $\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}=\hbar^{-1}\nabla_{\mathbf k}\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$.
- For a parabolic band $\varepsilon=\hbar\mathbf k_F\cdot\hbar(\mathbf k-\mathbf k_F)/m^*$, this gives $m_{\rm ep}=m^*(1+\lambda_{n\mathbf k})$. DW is ignored here.

**Eq. (190): DW self-energy as a static local potential.**
$$\Sigma^{\rm DW}_{nn\mathbf k}=\langle u_{n\mathbf k}|V_{\rm DW}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc},\qquad V_{\rm DW}(\mathbf r)=\Omega_{\rm BZ}^{-1}\sum_\nu\int d\mathbf q\,(n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1/2)\,\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}\Delta_{-\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r)\qquad(190)$$
- This follows from Eqs. (160)+(40) [context], with the factor $\tfrac12$ of Eq. (40) combined with the $(2n+1)$ of Eq. (161) to give $(n+\tfrac12)$.
- $V_{\rm DW}$ is lattice periodic and has no $\mathbf k$ dependence, so the only $\mathbf k$ dependence of $\Sigma^{\rm DW}$ is through $u_{n\mathbf k}$.
- For the homogeneous electron gas, $|u_{n\mathbf k}|^2=1/\Omega$, so $\Sigma^{\rm DW}$ is a constant. The early DW calculations (Antončík 1955; Walter et al. 1970) simply modified the ionic pseudopotentials, i.e., a Debye–Waller-smeared form factor.

**Eq. (191): Grimvall's electronic entropy.**
$$S_{\rm e}=\frac{N_{\rm F}k_B\hbar^3}{(k_BT)^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{\omega\,[\omega-\hbar^{-1}{\rm Re}\Sigma^{\rm FM}_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega,T)]}{\cosh^2(\hbar\omega/2k_BT)}\,d\omega\qquad(191)$$
- The derivation writes the thermodynamic potential of the coupled system in terms of propagators and self-energies, and neglects terms of order $(m_e/M_0)^{1/2}$ and $e$–$e$ interactions (Grimvall 1969).
- The weight $\cosh^{-2}(\hbar\omega/2k_BT)$ restricts the integral to $\hbar\omega\lesssim2k_BT$.

**Eq. (192): low-temperature heat capacity.**
$$C_{\rm e}=T\frac{\partial S_{\rm e}}{\partial T}=\frac23\pi^2k_B^2N_{\rm F}(1+\lambda_{n\mathbf k})T\qquad(192)$$
- This uses $\Sigma^{\rm FM}\simeq-\lambda_{n\mathbf k}\hbar\omega$ (linear in $\omega$, from Eq. (189)) below the Debye temperature.
- Check: $\int_0^\infty x^2\cosh^{-2}x\,dx=\pi^2/12$. Then (191) gives $S_e=\tfrac23\pi^2N_Fk_B^2T(1+\lambda)=C_e$, consistent with (192).
- With $\lambda=0$ one recovers the free-electron gas. At high $T$ the full $\Sigma^{\rm FM}(\omega,T)$ must be used, and the EPI renormalization of $C_e$ disappears.

### 3.2 Sec. IX.A.1: Allen–Heine theory in practice

**Eq. (193): first translational sum rule.**
$$\sum_{\kappa p}\langle\psi_{n\mathbf k}|\partial_{\kappa\alpha p}V^{\rm KS}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm sc}=0\qquad(193)$$
- $\partial_{\kappa\alpha p}V^{\rm KS}\equiv\partial V^{\rm KS}/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}$, and "sc" denotes an integral over the BvK supercell.
- A rigid translation of the whole crystal cannot shift an eigenvalue (Hellmann–Feynman). Equivalently, $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ of the three translational modes at $\mathbf q=0$ vanish. This is an alternative statement of the acoustic sum rule.

**Eq. (194): second translational sum rule.**
$$\sum_{\kappa'p'}\langle\psi_{n\mathbf k}|\partial^2_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}V^{\rm KS}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm sc}=-2{\rm Re}\sum_{\kappa'p'}\sum_{m\mathbf q}{}^{'}\frac{\langle\psi_{n\mathbf k}|\partial_{\kappa\alpha p}V^{\rm KS}|\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\rangle_{\rm sc}\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\partial_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}V^{\rm KS}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm sc}}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}\qquad(194)$$
- The prime excludes states with $\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$.
- **Origin.** Second-order perturbation theory, plus the requirement that an *additional* rigid displacement of an already displaced crystal does not change the eigenvalue.
- **Rigid-ion approximation.** Take $V^{\rm KS}=\sum$ of atom-centred potentials. Then on the left only $\kappa'p'=\kappa p$ survives, which gives an explicit expression for $\langle\psi|\partial^2_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa\alpha'p}V^{\rm KS}|\psi\rangle$ in terms of first derivatives.

**Eq. (195): DW self-energy in the RIA.**
$$\Sigma^{\rm DW}_{nn\mathbf k}=-\sum_{\nu m}{}^{'}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\frac{g^{2,{\rm DW}}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k}}(2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)\qquad(195)$$
- The denominator contains $\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k}$ at the **same $\mathbf k$**, not $\mathbf k+\mathbf q$. In the RIA, the DW term couples only states at the same $\mathbf k$, through $\mathbf q=0$ matrix elements. It has the same "energy-denominator" form as the FM term of Eq. (167), but with the opposite sign.
- This was used in practice by Giustino, Louie and Cohen (2010).

**Eqs. (196)–(198): effective DW matrix elements from standard DFPT $g$.**
$$g^{2,{\rm DW}}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha\\ \kappa'\alpha'}}\frac{t^\nu_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\alpha'}(\mathbf q)}{2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\,h^*_{mn,\kappa\alpha}(\mathbf k)\,h_{mn,\kappa'\alpha'}(\mathbf k)\qquad(196)$$
$$t^\nu_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\alpha'}(\mathbf q)=\frac{e_{\kappa\alpha\nu}(\mathbf q)e^*_{\kappa\alpha'\nu}(\mathbf q)}{M_\kappa}+\frac{e_{\kappa'\alpha\nu}(\mathbf q)e^*_{\kappa'\alpha'\nu}(\mathbf q)}{M_{\kappa'}}\qquad(197)$$
$$h_{mn,\kappa\alpha}(\mathbf k)=\sum_\nu(M_\kappa\omega_{0\nu})^{1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha\nu}(0)\,g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,0)\qquad(198)$$
- **Meaning of $h$.** $h_{mn,\kappa\alpha}(\mathbf k)$ is (up to normalization) $\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k}|\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q=0}V^{\rm KS}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$. It is obtained by inverting the mode transformation at $\Gamma$, assuming real $e(0)$.
- **Normalization check.** The true Cartesian matrix element is $(2/\hbar)^{1/2}\times h$. The $\hbar$ and the 2 are absorbed into the $1/(2\omega)$ of (196), so (195)–(198) are dimensionally consistent, with $g^{2,\rm DW}$ in units of energy$^2$.
- **Translational modes.** For the three translational modes at $|\mathbf q|=0$, set $g^{\rm DW}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=0$.
- **Derivation.** Uses Eqs. (20), (33)–(35), (38) and the canonical average over adiabatic nuclear states.
- **Cost.** Only first-order DFPT, plus inexpensive matrix multiplications.
- **Error of the RIA.** About 10 meV in diamond (Antonius 2014), and below 4 meV in all cases tested by Poncé, Antonius, Gillet et al. (2014).
- **Convergence.** Sums over unoccupied states are slow. Gonze, Boulanger and Côté (2011) replace most of them with a Sternheimer-like DFPT solution.

**[context] The adiabatic Allen–Heine formula, Eq. (167)** (transcribed in G4), is the target of all of IX.A:
$$E_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\Big[\sum_m\frac{|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}+g^{\rm DW}_{nn\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)\Big](2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)$$
Its nonadiabatic Brillouin–Wigner version, Eq. (166), keeps $\pm\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ and $i\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}$ in the denominators.

**Polar divergence (text, p. 43; no equation number).**
- Near an extremum, intraband terms behave as $|g|^2\propto q^{-2}$ (Fröhlich, Eq. (180)) and $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\approx-\hbar^2q^2/2m^*$.
- So the adiabatic integrand goes as $q^{-4}$, while $d\mathbf q=4\pi q^2dq$. Then $\int dq/q^2$ diverges.
- Keeping $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$ in the denominator gives a convergent result. It is the weak-coupling Fröhlich shift $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$ for the band edge (see Viz 2).

### 3.3 Sec. IX.A.2: nonperturbative adiabatic (frozen-phonon) approach

**Eq. (199): thermal or quantum average of a KS eigenvalue.**
$$\langle\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\{n_{\mathbf q\nu}\}}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}(n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1/2)\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}}{\partial n_{\mathbf q\nu}}\qquad(199)$$
- **Formal definition.** $\partial/\partial n_{\mathbf q\nu}\equiv\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}\Delta_{-\mathbf q\nu}$, with $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}$ as in Eqs. (33)–(35).
- **How it arises.** Second-order Taylor expansion in $\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}$, plus Eq. (20), averaged over the harmonic nuclear eigenstate $\{n_{\mathbf q\nu}\}$ built from (B10) with ladder operators. The first-order terms average to zero, and $\langle\Delta\tau\Delta\tau\rangle\propto l^2(2n+1)$.
- **Finite $T$.** The canonical average replaces $n_{\mathbf q\nu}$ with $n_{\mathbf q\nu}(T)$.
- **Status.** Eq. (199) is the conceptual starting point of Allen and Cardona (1981). With $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}\Delta_{-\mathbf q\nu}\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$ evaluated by second-order perturbation theory, one recovers Allen–Heine.
- **Brooks' theorem** (Allen and Hui 1980; King-Smith et al. 1989): $\partial\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}/\partial n_{\mathbf q\nu}\leftrightarrow\hbar\,\partial\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}/\partial f_{n\mathbf k}$. The gap renormalization can be computed from the change of phonon frequencies when electrons are promoted.
- **Practice.** Second derivatives of $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$ along supercell eigenmodes $e_{\kappa\alpha\nu}(\mathbf q)$ by finite differences. This needs no RIA, but large supercells. Ways to cut the cost:
  - importance-sampling Monte Carlo (Patrick–Giustino 2013);
  - constrained Monte Carlo (Monserrat 2016b);
  - a single "optimum" configuration (Zacharias–Giustino 2016), exact for large supercells;
  - nondiagonal supercells (Lloyd-Williams–Monserrat 2015): $N_p$ $\mathbf q$-points with supercells of only $N_p^{1/3}$ cells.

### 3.4 Sec. IX.B: phonon-assisted optical absorption

**Eq. (200): Hall–Bardeen–Blatt indirect absorption coefficient.**
$$\alpha(\omega)=\frac{\pi e^2}{\epsilon_0c\Omega}\frac{1}{\omega\,n_{\rm r}(\omega)}\int\frac{d\mathbf k\,d\mathbf q}{\Omega^2_{\rm BZ}}\sum_{mn\nu}\sum_{s=\pm1}(f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\left|\mathbf e\cdot\sum_p\left[\frac{\mathbf v_{np}(\mathbf k)\,g_{pm\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)}{\varepsilon_{p\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega}+\frac{g_{np\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,\mathbf v_{pm}(\mathbf k+\mathbf q)}{\varepsilon_{p\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+s\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\right]\right|^2(n_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tfrac12+\tfrac s2)\,\delta(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega+s\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(200)$$

Symbols:
- $\alpha(\omega)$ is the absorption coefficient, in m$^{-1}$ (SI).
- $\mathbf e$ is the photon polarization.
- $\mathbf v_{mn}$ are matrix elements of the electron velocity operator.
- $n_{\rm r}(\omega)$ is the real part of the refractive index.
- $s=+1$ is phonon **emission**, with weight $n+1$ and onset at $\hbar\omega=E_g+\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$.
- $s=-1$ is phonon **absorption**, with weight $n$ and onset at $\hbar\omega=E_g-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$.
- The initial state is $n\mathbf k$ and the final state is $m\mathbf k+\mathbf q$.

The two paths:
- **First term:** photon first. A vertical transition to the virtual state $p\mathbf k$, then phonon scattering. The denominator is $\varepsilon_{p\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega$.
- **Second term:** phonon first, to $p\,\mathbf k+\mathbf q$, then a vertical photon transition. The denominator is $\varepsilon_{p\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+s\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$.

Assumptions:
- Electric-dipole approximation (photon momentum neglected), valid up to a few eV.
- Classical field.
- Independent particles: no excitons and no local fields.
- Clamped-nuclei energies.
- The occupation factor $(f_n-f_m)(n+\tfrac12+\tfrac s2)$ differs from the exact net rate by terms $\propto f_m(1-f_n)$, which are negligible in semiconductors.
- Prefactor check: from $\alpha=\hbar\omega W/I$, with $I=\tfrac12n_r\epsilon_0c\omega^2A_0^2$, one gets $\pi e^2/(\epsilon_0cn_r\omega V)\sum|\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v|^2\delta$ with $V=N_p\Omega$. Spin is implicit in the band sum.

**Eq. (201): Williams–Lax / Zacharias–Patrick–Giustino temperature-dependent $\epsilon_2$.**
$$\epsilon_2(\omega;T)=\frac1Z\sum_{\{n_{\mathbf q\nu}\}}e^{-E_{\{n_{\mathbf q\nu}\}}/k_BT}\,\langle\epsilon_2(\omega)\rangle_{\{n_{\mathbf q\nu}\}}\qquad(201)$$
- $\epsilon_2(\omega)$ is the imaginary part of the dielectric function at clamped nuclei, evaluated at displaced nuclear configurations. The average $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\{n\}}$ is over the nuclear wavefunction with quantum numbers $\{n_{\mathbf q\nu}\}$, as in Eq. (199).
- $E_{\{n\}}$ is the energy of that nuclear state, and $Z$ is the canonical partition function.
- **Approximations.** Initial states are quantum mechanical, and final states are a quasiclassical continuum.
- **What it gives.** An *adiabatic* approximation to Eq. (200) that automatically includes the AH temperature dependence of the band structure. It was applied to Si line shapes at various $T$, and later to diamond and GaAs.

### 3.5 Sec. X: lifetimes and mobility

**[context] Eq. (170): golden-rule scattering rate**, used throughout Sec. X (see G4):
$$\frac{1}{\tau_{n\mathbf k}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{m\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2\Big[(1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})+(f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\Big]$$

**Semiclassical current (text, p. 46).**
$$\mathbf J=-2e\,\Omega_{\rm BZ}^{-1}\sum_n\int d\mathbf k\,\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}f_{n\mathbf k}\quad\text{(as printed)}$$
The factor 2 is spin. A $1/\Omega$ is missing: see Source issues.

**Eq. (202): linearized Boltzmann transport equation (as printed).**
$$\frac{\partial f^0_{n\mathbf k}}{\partial\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}}\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}\cdot(-e)\mathbf E=-\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\Gamma_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,\big[(f_{n\mathbf k}-f^0_{n\mathbf k})-(f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\big]\qquad(202)$$

**Eq. (203): scattering kernel.**
$$\Gamma_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\sum_{s=\pm1}\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\,(n_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tfrac12-\tfrac s2)\,\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+s\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\qquad(203)$$
- $s=+1$ is absorption (weight $n$) and $s=-1$ is emission (weight $n+1$).
- $\Gamma$ is the *equilibrium* transition rate $n\mathbf k\to m\mathbf k+\mathbf q$, Pauli factors included. It is symmetric by detailed balance.
- The sum over $m$ is implicit in Eq. (202).
- The left-hand side is the collisionless (drift) term. The right-hand side is out-scattering minus in-scattering.
- The equation is solved self-consistently for all $f_{n\mathbf k}$, which gives $\mathbf J$ and the conductivity.
- **Energy RTA.** Drop the in-scattering term $(f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})$, so that the right-hand side is $-(f_{n\mathbf k}-f^0_{n\mathbf k})/\tau_{n\mathbf k}$ with $\tau$ from Eq. (170).
- With $\Gamma$ as printed, the RTA statement is consistent only after dividing by $f^0(1-f^0)$; see Source issues for the corrected form.
- **Useful identity.** Using it, I verified that $\sum_{m\nu\mathbf q}\Gamma_{mn\nu}=f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{n\mathbf k})/\tau_{n\mathbf k}$ exactly:
$$f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})(n+1)=f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{n\mathbf k})(1-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n)\quad\text{(emission)}$$
$$f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\,n=f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{n\mathbf k})(f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n)\quad\text{(absorption)}$$

### 3.6 Sec. XI: superconductors

**Eq. (204): McMillan–Allen–Dynes formula.**
$$k_BT_c=\frac{\hbar\omega_{\log}}{1.2}\exp\left[-\frac{1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^*(1+0.62\lambda)}\right]\qquad(204)$$
- McMillan obtained the functional form from an approximate solution of the Eliashberg gap equations.
- Assumptions: an isotropic, single-gap superconductor, with $\mu^*$ usually an adjustable parameter of 0.1–0.2. This makes $T_c$ uncertain, especially at moderate coupling.
- $T_c\to0$ when $\lambda\le\mu^*/(1-0.62\mu^*)$.

**Eq. (205): isotropic Eliashberg function** (isotropic version of Eq. (187)).
$$\alpha^2F(\omega)=\frac1{N_{\rm F}}\int\frac{d\mathbf k\,d\mathbf q}{\Omega^2_{\rm BZ}}\sum_{mn\nu}|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{\rm F})\delta(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{\rm F})\delta(\hbar\omega-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(205)$$
This is a double Fermi-surface average of $|g|^2$, resolved by phonon energy. It is dimensionless.

**Eq. (206): coupling strength.**
$$\lambda=2\int_0^\infty\frac{\alpha^2F(\omega)}{\omega}d\omega\qquad(206)$$

**Eq. (207): logarithmic average phonon frequency.**
$$\omega_{\log}=\exp\left[\frac2\lambda\int_0^\infty d\omega\,\frac{\alpha^2F(\omega)}{\omega}\log\omega\right]\qquad(207)$$
For discrete peaks of strength $\lambda_i$, $\omega_{\log}=\prod_i\omega_i^{\lambda_i/\lambda}$.

**Coulomb pseudopotential (text, p. 47).**
- $1/\mu^*=1/\mu+\log(\omega_p/\omega_{\rm ph})$, i.e., $\mu^*=\mu/[1+\mu\ln(\omega_p/\omega_{\rm ph})]$ (Morel–Anderson).
- $\hbar\omega_p$ is the characteristic plasma energy and $\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}$ the largest phonon energy.
- $\mu=N_{\rm F}\langle\langle V_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}\rangle\rangle_{\rm FS}$, with $V_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}=\langle\mathbf k'n',-\mathbf k'n'|W|\mathbf kn,-\mathbf kn\rangle$, where $W$ is the screened Coulomb interaction of Sec. IV.B.2 (Lee, Chang and Cohen 1995; Lee and Chang 1996).
- **$\lambda$ versus $\lambda_{n\mathbf k}$.** $\lambda$ is a Fermi-surface average, while $\lambda_{n\mathbf k}$ of Eq. (189) is the velocity renormalization of one band. They are related, but not interchangeable.

**Eqs. (208)–(209): anisotropic Migdal–Eliashberg equations** (imaginary axis, Nambu–Gor'kov).
$$Z_{n\mathbf k}(i\omega_j)=1+\frac{\pi k_BT}{N_{\rm F}}\sum_{n'\mathbf k'j'}\frac{\omega_{j'}/\omega_j}{\sqrt{\hbar^2\omega^2_{j'}+\Delta^2_{n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega_{j'})}}\,\lambda_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega_j-i\omega_{j'})\,\delta(\varepsilon_{n'\mathbf k'}-\varepsilon_{\rm F})\qquad(208)$$
$$Z_{n\mathbf k}(i\omega_j)\Delta_{n\mathbf k}(i\omega_j)=\frac{\pi k_BT}{N_{\rm F}}\sum_{n'\mathbf k'j'}\frac{\Delta_{n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega_{j'})}{\sqrt{\hbar^2\omega^2_{j'}+\Delta^2_{n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega_{j'})}}\big[\lambda_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega_j-i\omega_{j'})-N_{\rm F}V_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}\big]\,\delta(\varepsilon_{n'\mathbf k'}-\varepsilon_{\rm F})\qquad(209)$$
- Fermion Matsubara frequencies: $i\omega_j=i(2j+1)\pi k_BT/\hbar$.
- $Z_{n\mathbf k}(i\omega_j)$ is the mass renormalization function (analogous to $Z_{n\mathbf k}$ of Eq. (189)), and $\Delta_{n\mathbf k}(i\omega_j)$ is the gap function.
- They are solved self-consistently at each $T$. $T_c$ is the highest $T$ with a nontrivial $\Delta\neq0$. Real-axis gaps follow by analytic continuation (Marsiglio–Schossmann–Carbotte 1988), and from them the thermodynamics.

**Eq. (210): anisotropic coupling.**
$$\lambda_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega)=\frac{N_{\rm F}}{\hbar}\sum_\nu\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\omega^2}|g_{nn'\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2,\qquad\mathbf q=\mathbf k'-\mathbf k\qquad(210)$$
- **Structure.** $2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}/(\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\omega^2)$ is minus the bare phonon propagator on the bosonic Matsubara axis. So $\lambda(i\omega)$ is literally "$|g|^2\times D^{\rm ph}$": the phonon-mediated part of the effective electron–electron interaction, i.e., $W_{\rm ph}$ projected on Cooper pairs.
- Its static, Fermi-surface-averaged value reproduces Eq. (206).
- Index order: see Source issues.

**Eq. (211): SCDFT gap equation** (Lüders et al. 2005; Marques et al. 2005).
$$\Delta_{n\mathbf k}=-\mathcal Z_{n\mathbf k}\Delta_{n\mathbf k}-\sum_{n'\mathbf k'}\frac{\mathcal K_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}\Delta_{n'\mathbf k'}}{2E_{n'\mathbf k'}}\tanh\left(\frac{E_{n'\mathbf k'}}{2k_BT}\right),\qquad E^2_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon^2_{n\mathbf k}+|\Delta_{n\mathbf k}|^2\qquad(211)$$
- $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$ is measured from $\varepsilon_F$ (implicit).
- $\Delta_{n\mathbf k}=\langle u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)|\Delta(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')|u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r')\rangle$ is the pairing field in the KS basis.
- $\mathcal K=\mathcal K^{\rm ep}+\mathcal K^{\rm ee}$. $\mathcal K^{\rm ep}$ and $\mathcal Z$ are built from $g_{mn\nu}$, the DFT bands and the phonons. $\mathcal K^{\rm ee}$ is built from the screened Coulomb $V_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}$, so **no $\mu^*$** is needed.
- It is BCS-like. There is no explicit frequency dependence, but retardation enters through the kernels.
- **Theorem.** A Hohenberg–Kohn theorem for three densities (normal density, pair density, nuclear density). The KS system is a non-interacting, superconducting Bogoliubov–de Gennes system.
- **Order parameter:** $\chi(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=\langle\hat\psi_\uparrow(\mathbf r)\hat\psi_\downarrow(\mathbf r')\rangle$, with $\chi_{n\mathbf k}=(\Delta_{n\mathbf k}/2|E_{n\mathbf k}|)\tanh[E_{n\mathbf k}/(2k_BT)]$ (Fig. 18).

### 3.7 Sec. XII: matrix elements beyond LDA

**Eq. (212): screened electron–phonon vertex.**
$$g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\int d\mathbf r'\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)\,\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm en}(\mathbf r')|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc}\qquad(212)$$
- It combines Eqs. (38), (142) and (143).
- $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm en}$ is the bare electron–nucleus perturbation, screened by the **many-body, frequency-dependent electronic** dielectric matrix $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$.
- **DFT.** $\epsilon_{\rm e}\to\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$ (RPA+$xc$, Eq. (54)), static. Because of the band-gap problem, DFT overestimates screening, so $g$ is underestimated.

**Scaling argument (text, p. 51).**
- The AH renormalization, Eq. (167), carries electronic screening as $\epsilon_\infty^{-2}$ (two screened vertices).
- The adiabatic phonon frequencies (Eqs. (126)–(127)) carry a screening term that scales as $\epsilon_\infty^{-1/2}$ ($\omega\propto\sqrt{C}$, with the screened part of $C\propto\epsilon_\infty^{-1}$, as in $\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2\propto1/\epsilon_\infty$).
- Linearized, a 20% error in $\epsilon_\infty$ gives about 40% error in the ZPR and about 10% in the phonon frequencies.

### 3.8 Appendix A: BvK

- **Lattice.** $\mathbf R_p=\sum_in_i\mathbf a_i$ with $0\le n_i\le N_i-1$, $N_p=N_1N_2N_3$, and $\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij}$.
- **$\mathbf q$ grid.** $\mathbf q=\sum_j(m_j/N_j)\mathbf b_j$, with the same number of $\mathbf q$ as cells.

**Eq. (A1): BvK sum rules.**
$$\sum_{\mathbf q}\exp(i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p)=N_p\delta_{p0},\qquad\sum_p\exp(i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p)=N_p\delta_{\mathbf q0}\qquad(A1)$$
- $\mathbf q\to\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ and $\mathbf R_p\to\mathbf R_p+\mathbf T$ (supercell vector) change nothing.
- One may therefore use a Wigner–Seitz BZ and a Wigner–Seitz supercell, which is useful for symmetry and for truncating force constants, Eq. (13).

### 3.9 Appendix B: ladder operators in extended systems

**Eq. (B1): complex normal coordinate.**
$$z_{\mathbf q\nu}=N_p^{-1/2}\sum_{\kappa\alpha p}e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}(M_\kappa/M_0)^{1/2}e^*_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\,\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\qquad(B1)$$
There are $2\times3MN_p$ real variables for $3MN_p$ degrees of freedom, so the description is redundant.

**Eq. (B2): reality condition.**
$$z_{-\mathbf q\nu}=z^*_{\mathbf q\nu}\qquad(B2)$$

**Eq. (B3): inverse.**
$$\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=N_p^{-1/2}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)z_{\mathbf q\nu}\qquad(B3)$$
It is real by Eqs. (18) and (B2).

**Partition of the $\mathbf q$ grid.**
- $\mathcal A$: $-\mathbf q+\mathbf G=\mathbf q$ (Γ and the centres of the BZ faces).
- $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal C=-\mathcal B$ (mod $\mathbf G$).
- Write $z_{\mathbf q\nu}=x_{\mathbf q\nu}+iy_{\mathbf q\nu}$.

**Eq. (B4): real normal coordinates.**
$$\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=N_p^{-1/2}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}\Big[\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A,\nu}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)x_{\mathbf q\nu}+2{\rm Re}\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal B,\nu}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)(x_{\mathbf q\nu}+iy_{\mathbf q\nu})\Big]\qquad(B4)$$
The $\mathcal A$ term should carry $e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}$ as well ($=\pm1$ for $\mathbf q\in\mathcal A$). It is omitted in print; see Source issues. There are exactly $3MN_p$ independent real coordinates: $x$ for $\mathcal A$, and $(x,y)$ for $\mathcal B$.

**Eq. (B5): independent oscillators.**
$$\hat H_{\rm p}=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal B,\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\big(-\partial^2/\partial\tilde x^2_{\mathbf q\nu}-\partial^2/\partial\tilde y^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tilde x^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tilde y^2_{\mathbf q\nu}\big)+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A,\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\big(-\partial^2/\partial\tilde x^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tilde x^2_{\mathbf q\nu}\big)\qquad(B5)$$
This uses Eqs. (12)–(18), (A1) and (B4).

**Eqs. (B6)–(B7): scaled coordinates.**
$$\tilde x_{\mathbf q\nu}=x_{\mathbf q\nu}/2l_{\mathbf q\nu}\ \ (\mathbf q\in\mathcal A)\ \ \text{[as printed; should be }x/\sqrt2\,l\text{, see Source issues]}\qquad(B6)$$
$$\tilde x_{\mathbf q\nu}=x_{\mathbf q\nu}/l_{\mathbf q\nu},\quad\tilde y_{\mathbf q\nu}=y_{\mathbf q\nu}/l_{\mathbf q\nu}\ \ (\mathbf q\in\mathcal B)\qquad(B7)$$
The three $\omega=0$ modes at $\mathbf q=0$ have their potential terms removed.

**Eq. (B8): real ladder operators.**
$$\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}=(\tilde x_{\mathbf q\nu}+\partial/\partial\tilde x_{\mathbf q\nu})/\sqrt2\qquad(B8)$$
$\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,y}$ is defined in the same way.

**Eq. (B9): Hamiltonian in real ladder operators.**
$$\hat H_{\rm p}=\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal B,\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu,x}\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}+\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu,y}\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,y}+1)+\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A,\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu,x}\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}+1/2)\qquad(B9)$$

**Eq. (B10): ground state.**
$$\chi_0(\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})=A\,e^{-\frac12\left(\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A,\nu}\tilde x^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\sum_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal B,\nu}(\tilde x^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tilde y^2_{\mathbf q\nu})\right)}\qquad(B10)$$

**Eqs. (B11)–(B12): circular quanta.** The $\mathcal B$ oscillators are isotropic 2D oscillators, so their degenerate states can be recombined into states of definite angular momentum.
$$\hat a^+_{\mathbf q\nu}=(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}+i\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,y})/\sqrt2,\qquad\hat a^-_{\mathbf q\nu}=(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}-i\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,y})/\sqrt2\qquad(B11,\,B12)$$
Formally $\hat a^-_{\mathbf q\nu}=\hat a^+_{-\mathbf q\nu}$, so $\hat a^-$ is associated with phonons propagating along $-\mathbf q$.

**Eqs. (B13)–(B14): compact definitions.**
$$\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}=\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}\ (\mathbf q\in\mathcal A),\qquad\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}=(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}+i\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,y})/\sqrt2\ (\mathbf q\in\mathcal B,\mathcal C)\qquad(B13,\,B14)$$
- With these, Eq. (B5) becomes Eq. (22): $\hat H_{\rm p}=\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu}\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\tfrac12)$.
- Eigenstates are $\prod_{\mathbf q\nu}(n_{\mathbf q\nu}!)^{-1/2}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu})^{n_{\mathbf q\nu}}\chi_0$, so phonon numbers can be assigned independently to each $\mathbf q$ and $\nu$.

**Eq. (B15): basic identity.**
$$z_{\mathbf q\nu}=l_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(B15)$$
Combined with (B3), this gives Eq. (20).

**Verification.**
- For $\mathbf q\in\mathcal B$: $\hat a_{\mathbf q}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q}=\tilde x+i\tilde y=z/l$. ✓
- For $\mathbf q\in\mathcal A$: $\hat a+\hat a^\dagger=\sqrt2\,\tilde x$, which requires $\tilde x=x/(\sqrt2\,l)$, not $x/2l$.

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**Zero-point renormalization and the Varshni effect.**
- Even at $T=0$ every phonon mode carries $\tfrac12\hbar\omega$, so the electrons see a "blurred" lattice. This is the origin of the $+\tfrac12$ in $(2n+1)$ and $(n+\tfrac12)$.
- Gap versus $T$ is flat at low $T$, while $n_{\mathbf q\nu}\approx0$. It becomes linear for $k_BT\gtrsim\hbar\omega$, where $2n+1\approx2k_BT/\hbar\omega$.
- The size of the ZPR scales roughly as $1/M$ (through $l^2$), which gives the isotope effect, and with the stiffness of the bands.
- **Diamond**, light atoms with a large ZPR, direct gap:
  - more than 0.5 eV, from empirical pseudopotentials (Zollner, Cardona, Gopalan 1992);
  - about 0.4 eV in DFPT-LDA (the value at $T=0$ is about 7.33 eV in Fig. 13);
  - 628 meV with $GW$ (Antonius 2014).
  
  $GW$ increases the coupling (+~200 meV, i.e., about 40%).
- **Other numbers:**
  - Ionic crystals: ZPR above 1 eV for LiF, LiCl, NaCl and MgO (Botti–Marques).
  - GaN: Fröhlich part 45 meV out of 150 meV total (Nery–Allen).
  - Sulfur hydrides: AH ZPR of the bands shifts $T_c$ by up to 20 K (Sano 2016).

**Why the DW term needs care.**
- At second order in displacement, FM (two first-order vertices) and DW (one second-order vertex) are of the *same order* and often of comparable size. In diamond they are of comparable magnitude.
- DW is $\mathbf k$-smooth within a band but varies strongly *between* bands, so it matters for gaps. In metals near $E_F$ it is a near-constant shift and is usually dropped. Giustino questions this for multi-sheet Fermi surfaces (Sec. XIII).
- Translational invariance (the acoustic sum rule at second order) plus the RIA lets one write DW as an FM-like sum with $\mathbf q=0$ vertices. This is the Allen–Heine trick: a "Fan term with the opposite sign", which ensures that a uniform translation gives zero shift.

**Adiabatic versus non-adiabatic.**
- The adiabatic AH formula drops $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ next to the electronic energy differences. It works when the gaps between coupled states are much larger than $\hbar\omega$, and fails for intraband scattering near an extremum ($\Delta\varepsilon\sim\hbar^2q^2/2m^*\to0$).
- For non-polar coupling ($|g|^2\to$ const or $\propto q^2$) the singularity is integrable.
- For **Fröhlich** coupling ($|g|^2\propto q^{-2}$) it is not: the integral $\int d^3q\,q^{-2}/q^2$ diverges.
- **The polaron length scale.** Keeping $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$ regularizes the integral at $q_p=\sqrt{2m^*\omega_{\rm LO}/\hbar}$, and the result is the familiar weak-coupling polaron shift $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$. For GaAs, $q_p\approx0.025$ Å$^{-1}$; for GaN, $q_p\approx0.07$ Å$^{-1}$.
- The q-grid must resolve $q\lesssim q_p$, i.e., $N_q\gtrsim q_{\rm BZ}/q_p\sim40$ per direction for GaAs. Hence the convergence troubles of Poncé et al.

**Frozen-phonon versus perturbation theory.**
- Frozen-phonon supercell methods need no RIA and can include anharmonicity, but they are costly.
- Brooks' theorem says that electrons and phonons "see" the same coupling: changing an electron occupation shifts a phonon frequency by as much as adding a phonon shifts an electron level.

**Indirect absorption.**
- In Si the conduction-band minimum lies near X, so a photon (with negligible momentum) cannot connect $\Gamma_{25'}$ to $\Delta_1$ directly. A phonon with $\mathbf q\approx\mathbf k_{\rm CBM}$ must be emitted or absorbed.
- Each phonon branch gives two thresholds, at $E_g\pm\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}$. For two parabolic bands with constant matrix elements, each branch contributes $\propto(\hbar\omega-E_g\mp\hbar\omega_{\rm ph})^2$ (Macfarlane–Roberts shape). In a $\sqrt\alpha$ plot these become straight segments.
- The absorption branch ($\propto n$) freezes out at low $T$.
- Typical magnitudes in Si (Fig. 14): $\alpha$ is about $10^{-1}$–$10^{1}$ cm$^{-1}$ just above 1.1 eV, about $10^3$ cm$^{-1}$ at 1.5 eV and about $10^4$ cm$^{-1}$ at 2.5 eV. These are many orders of magnitude below direct absorption (about $10^6$ cm$^{-1}$ above 3.4 eV). This is why Si solar cells are thick.
- The two denominators describe virtual (energy non-conserving) intermediate states. The AH renormalization should shift only the *real* energies (delta function and first denominator), not the virtual ones (Patrick–Giustino; Zacharias–Giustino).

**Lifetimes and the GaAs valley picture (Fig. 15).**
- Below the threshold for LO emission ($\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}\approx36$ meV) only absorption is possible, and $\tau\sim$ ps.
- Above it, Fröhlich emission gives $\tau$ of a few hundred fs.
- At $E_L\approx0.25$–0.3 eV (in the figure, $E_L=0.25$ eV) the L valley opens with its large DOS, and intervalley scattering makes $\tau$ drop to about 10 fs. At $E_X\approx0.45$ eV the X valley adds more phase space.
- By 1.5 eV, $\tau\approx3$–4 fs.
- Accurate *effective masses* (GW) matter because the rates scale with the DOS.

**Mobility.** In the energy-RTA the in-scattering term is dropped. For Si the exact BTE, the energy-RTA and the momentum-RTA nearly coincide (Fig. 16). For polar, anisotropic Fröhlich scattering they can differ strongly. Theory overestimates the experimental mobility of $n$-Si, possibly because of DFT matrix elements (Sec. XII).

**Superconductivity.**
- Typical parameters:

  | Material | $\lambda$ | $\omega_{\log}$ | $T_c$ |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Al | ≈0.43 | ≈300 K | 1.2 K |
  | Pb | ≈1.55 | ≈56–67 K | 7.2 K |
  | MgB$_2$ (isotropic) | ≈0.7–0.75 | ≈700 K | McMillan–Allen–Dynes gives about 20–30 K, versus 39 K measured |
  | MgB$_2$ (anisotropic ME) | | | 50 K (Margine–Giustino) |
  | H$_3$S | ≈2 | ≈1300 K | about 200 K |

- MgB$_2$ shows **two gaps**: σ about 7–9 meV and π about 1.5–3 meV at low $T$ (Fig. 17). Coulomb repulsion enters through $\mu^*$, which explains part of the 50 K versus 39 K discrepancy.
- Li-decorated graphene; Ca-intercalated bilayer graphene, with $\mu^*$ from RPA $W$: $T_c=7$–8 K versus 4 K measured.
- $\mu^*$ is the Coulomb repulsion $\mu=N_F\langle W\rangle$ reduced by retardation: electrons pair at a time delay of about $1/\omega_{\rm ph}$, while the Coulomb repulsion acts on the much shorter time scale $1/\omega_p$. Hence the logarithm $\ln(\omega_p/\omega_{\rm ph})$, and $\mu\approx0.3$–0.5 becomes $\mu^*\approx0.1$–0.15.
- Hybrid functionals and $GW$ enhance $\lambda$:
  - Ba$_{1-x}$K$_x$BiO$_3$: about 3× from PBE to HSE, giving $T_c=31$ K versus 32 K measured;
  - β-ZrNCl: +50%, giving 18 K versus 16 K;
  - MgB$_2$: only slight;
  - C$_{60}$: +42% (PBE0), +48% ($GW$);
  - H$_3$S with PBE0: $T_c$ from 201–217 K to 253–270 K;
  - FeSe with DMFT: about 3× for selected modes.

**Beyond-LDA evidence (Sec. XII, numbers).**
- CaCuO$_2$ half-breathing mode: LSDA 65.3 meV, measured 80.1 meV, LSDA+U 80.9 meV.
- MnO: DFT underestimates the LO energies by up to 15 meV.
- Graphene/graphite $A_1'$ at K and $E_{2g}$ at Γ: $g$ is about 40% larger in $GW$ than in DFT, and the $GW$ slopes near K agree with inelastic X-ray scattering.
- Diamond ZPR: +40% with $GW$. Si is similar.
- LiF, MgO, TiO$_2$: $GW$ correction about 5% only.
- "Constant screening" (Faber 2015): vary $G$ but keep $W$ fixed, giving errors below 10% with respect to full $GW$ in diamond, graphene and C$_{60}$.

**Phonon normal-coordinate quantization (App. B).** The trick for travelling waves is to pair $\mathbf q$ with $-\mathbf q$, i.e., a standing-wave basis with real $x$ and $y$ (cos and sin). Each pair forms a 2D isotropic oscillator. Recombining into circular quanta $\hat a^\pm$ gives quanta with definite momentum $\pm\mathbf q$. This is the same construction as quantizing the EM field in a box, which is why the EPP photon operators look identical.

### Viz 2: the Fröhlich catastrophe of the adiabatic AH formula (and the light cone)

**Model.** CBM of a parabolic band ($m^*$) coupled to a dispersionless LO phonon. Fröhlich $|g|^2\propto1/q^2$, integrated over a sphere $q_{\min}<q<q_D$. With $q_p=\sqrt{2m^*\omega_{\rm LO}/\hbar}$ and the Fröhlich constant
$$\alpha=\Big(\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon_0}\Big)\sqrt{\frac{m^*}{m_e}}\sqrt{\frac{\rm Ry}{\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}}}\qquad({\rm Ry}=13.606\ {\rm eV})$$
the closed forms are:
- Non-adiabatic (RS, $T=0$, emission only):
$$\Delta E_{\rm NA}=-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}\frac2\pi\Big[\arctan\frac{q_D}{q_p}-\arctan\frac{q_{\min}}{q_p}\Big]$$
- Adiabatic:
$$\Delta E_{\rm A}=-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}\frac2\pi\,q_p\Big(\frac1{q_{\min}}-\frac1{q_D}\Big)$$
- Finite $T$: multiply the adiabatic result by $(2n+1)$. In the non-adiabatic result, add the absorption term with $+\hbar\omega$ replaced as in Eq. (166). For simplicity, one can show only $T=0$.

**Grid mapping.** $q_{\min}=q_D/N_q$, with $q_D=(6\pi^2/\Omega)^{1/3}$ and $N_q$ the number of q-points per direction (slider 4–1000, log). Plot $\Delta E$ versus $N_q$ (log x). The adiabatic curve diverges linearly in $N_q$; the non-adiabatic curve saturates at $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$.

**Second panel.** Integrand $dE/d\ln q$ versus $q$ (log axis $10^{-6}$ to 1 Å$^{-1}$).
- Adiabatic: $\propto q^{-1}$. Non-adiabatic: $\propto q/(q^2+q_p^2)$.
- Mark $q_p$, $q_D$ and the **photon light-cone wavevector** $q_\gamma=\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega_{\rm LO}/c$ (≈$6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$ for GaAs, since $\hbar\omega/\hbar c=0.036/1973$ Å$^{-1}=1.8\times10^{-5}$, times $\sqrt{10.9}$).

**Material presets.**

| Material | $m^*$ | $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$ (meV) | $\epsilon_\infty$ | $\epsilon_0$ | $\Omega$ (Å$^3$) | $\alpha$ | $-\alpha\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$ (meV) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GaAs | 0.067 | 36.2 | 10.9 | 12.9 | 45.2 | ≈0.068 | ≈2.5 |
| GaN | 0.20 | 92 | 5.35 | 9.7 | 45.7 | ≈0.45 | ≈42 |
| MgO | 0.35 | 89 | 2.95 | 9.8 | 18.7 | ≈1.7 | ≈150 (outside weak coupling) |

The GaN value is close to the 45 meV quoted by Nery–Allen.

**What to notice.**
- The adiabatic divergence comes entirely from $q\to0$.
- Convergence requires $q_{\min}\ll q_p$.
- The fraction of the converged Fröhlich ZPR that comes from inside the light cone, $(2/\pi)\arctan(q_\gamma/q_p)$, is about $10^{-3}$. Electrodynamic (EPP) corrections therefore hardly touch the ZPR, but they act exactly where the adiabatic integrand is singular.

## 7. Connections

### 7.1 The electron–phonon-only baseline that EPP generalizes

1. **Instantaneous, longitudinal Coulomb only.** Every quantity in this range is built from the electrostatic coupling:
   - the Allen–Heine renormalization;
   - the lifetimes (Eq. 170);
   - the BTE kernel (Eq. 203);
   - $\alpha^2F$, $\lambda$, and the Migdal–Eliashberg and SCDFT kernels;
   - the screened vertex (Eq. 212).

   That coupling is $g$ screened by $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$, plus $W$ for $\mu$ and $\mathcal K^{\rm ee}$. There is no vector potential, current vertex or transverse photon.
   - **"Retardation" in Giustino** (Secs. XI.C, XIII) means *dynamical* screening, the ω-dependence of $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$ in Eq. (212), or the phonon time-scale in $\mu^*$. It does not mean finite-$c$ electrodynamics.
   - **EPP replaces the instantaneous Coulomb $v$ and $W_{\rm e}$** by the full retarded propagator $D_{\mu\nu}$, longitudinal plus transverse, dressed by electronic and ionic RPA photon self-energies. It recovers this range's formulas in the electrostatic limit $c\to\infty$ (EPP Sec. 7.3: $\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}\propto-(c/\omega^2)q_\mu q_\nu W^{\rm e}$, Gaussian units; EPP Sec. 8.1 for 2D).
2. **The photon as a classical external field.** In Eq. (200) light enters only through $\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v$ (dipole approximation, $\mathbf q_{\rm photon}=0$), and the medium only through the macroscopic $n_r(\omega)$ put in by hand.
   - In EPP the photon is quantized. Absorption follows from the imaginary part of the *transverse* photon self-energy (the current–current correlator $\Pi_{jj}$), and $n_r(\omega)$ follows from the same self-energy.
   - Eq. (200) is the lowest phonon-dressed correction to the electronic $\Pi_{jj}$: one $\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v$ vertex and one $g$ vertex, plus phonon-induced self-energy and vertex diagrams.
   - Plain RPA with clamped-nuclei $G$ gives only direct transitions. Indirect absorption requires dressing $G$ by $\Sigma^{\rm ep}$, as Giustino notes in IX.B with reference to Eqs. (95) and (151).
   - Eq. (200) also lacks direct photon–ion coupling (one-phonon IR/reststrahlen absorption via Born charges). That is exactly the **ionic (paramagnetic) part of the RPA photon self-energy** in EPP (EPP Sec. 5.2).
3. **Polar (Fröhlich) coupling and LO–TO.**
   - The polar divergence of the adiabatic AH formula (p. 43) and the Fröhlich ZPR of Nery–Allen use the electrostatic long-range vertex of Eq. (180). This vertex contains $Z^*_\kappa$, $\epsilon^\infty$ and $\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q$ in the denominator.
   - EPP re-derives this vertex and generalizes it to an **electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex** (EPP Sec. 7.2), in which the instantaneous longitudinal interaction is replaced by the full $(\mathbf q,\omega)$ propagator.
   - Physically, the electrodynamic corrections are confined to $q\lesssim\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\omega/c\sim10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ Å$^{-1}$, where TO phonons hybridize with photons (polaritons). This is the same $q\to0$ region that makes the adiabatic integrand singular. Since $q_p\sim10^{-2}$ Å$^{-1}\gg\omega/c$, ZPR and mobilities are essentially electrostatic (Viz 2).
   - LO–TO splitting appears here only implicitly, through Eqs. (126)–(127) and the $\epsilon_\infty^{-1/2}$ scaling of phonon frequencies (Sec. XII). EPP derives LO–TO splitting as the lowest-order photon (Coulomb) self-energy correction of the phonon (EPP Sec. 7.1), and the LST relation from the ionic equations of motion (EPP Eq. (165)).
4. **Lattice screening of $W$ (Botti–Marques).** IX.A.1 discusses computing $W$ *including lattice screening* (Eqs. (85)–(86)) with an LST model, $\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega^2)/(\omega_{\rm TO}^2-\omega^2)$, instead of the split $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$ of Eq. (104). This is the closest point in Giustino to the EPP viewpoint: a single photon (Coulomb) propagator dressed by electronic *and* ionic polarization.
   - EPP makes it systematic: electronic plus ionic RPA photon self-energies, in both the longitudinal ($W$) and transverse (polariton) channels, with connection formulas between them.
   - Giustino notes that Botti–Marques ≈ Fan–Migdal retaining only the long-range polar part of $g$. EPP's electrostatic limit should reproduce the same equivalence.
5. **Screened Coulomb $W$, its nuclear part, and superconductivity.**
   - $\mu$ (Morel–Anderson) and $\mathcal K^{\rm ee}$ (SCDFT) use matrix elements of the *electronic* $W$ (RPA in Margine et al. 2016).
   - $\lambda_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}(i\omega)$ of Eq. (210) is $|g|^2$ times the bare phonon propagator, i.e., $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$ of Eq. (105) projected on the Fermi surface.
   - The pairing kernel $\lambda-\mu^*$ is therefore $-N_F\langle W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}\rangle$, with the electronic part renormalized by retardation.
   - In EPP language, both pieces are the longitudinal part of one photon propagator dressed by electronic and ionic self-energies (the "dielectric formulation of superconductivity", Kirzhnits–Maksimov–Khomskii). Transverse (current–current, magnetic) interactions add corrections of order $(v_F/c)^2$.
6. **Phonon self-energy: bare versus screened vertices.** Sec. XIII lists as open "whether the phonon self-energy should be calculated using bare or screened matrix elements".
   - EPP's induced phonon self-energy (EPP Fig. 14, Eqs. (411)–(412)) has the form $J^*D^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj}D^0J$: **one screened and one bare vertex**.
   - This is consistent with Giustino's Eq. (141)/(145) (numerator $g^{\rm b}g^*$, see G4) and gives a concrete electrodynamic answer.
7. **Adiabatic versus non-adiabatic.**
   - The AH adiabatic limit drops $\hbar\omega$ in the denominators.
   - The BTE uses the golden rule.
   - Migdal–Eliashberg is fully dynamical in $\lambda(i\omega)$.
   - EPP's self-energies $\Pi(\mathbf q,\omega)$ are dynamical by construction. Two *different* limits are recovered separately: static/adiabatic ($\omega\to0$) and electrostatic ($c\to\infty$).
8. **Dielectric screening of $g$ (Eq. 212) and the RPA.**
   - The DFT vertex is the bare vertex screened by $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$, the same object as in the user's RPA and $W$ calculations.
   - The $\epsilon_\infty^{-2}$ scaling of the AH ZPR and the "constant screening" approximation (keep $W$, vary $G$) connect directly to $GW$/BSE practice.
   - EPP's "screened electron–phonon vertex" (EPP Sec. 7.2.2) generalizes Eq. (212) from $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}v$ to the full $D^{\rm e}=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rm e}D^0$.
9. **Excitons.** Marini (2008) and Kawai et al. (2014) combined AH with the BSE. Eq. (200) is independent-particle. A full EPP treatment of phonon-assisted absorption would need vertex corrections beyond RPA, which is where the user's BSE expertise enters.
10. **Quadratic (DW) couplings.** DW is the second-order (two-phonon, one-vertex) term. In electrodynamics the analogous contact terms are the diamagnetic $A^2$ terms, e.g., the ionic diamagnetic self-energy in EPP Sec. 5.2.3. Treat this as an analogy only.
11. **Normal coordinates and quantization (App. B).** EPP Sec. 1 builds normal coordinates for lattice vibrations, and EPP Sec. 4.3–4.4 quantizes the EM field. Both use the same $\mathbf q/-\mathbf q$ pairing and ladder-operator construction as (B1)–(B15). Appendix A's BvK conventions ($N_p$, $\Omega$, $\Omega_{\rm BZ}$) are the dictionary between the two papers.

3. **Eqs. (202)–(203): normalization of the linearized BTE (confident).**
   - With $\Gamma$ as printed (the equilibrium rate, including $f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})$), the detailed-balance identities give $\sum\Gamma=f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{n\mathbf k})/\tau_{n\mathbf k}$.
   - Dropping the in-scattering term then gives $-(f-f^0)f^0(1-f^0)/\tau$, not the stated $-(f-f^0)/\tau$.
   - Consistent form, linearizing in $\phi$ with $f=f^0+f^0(1-f^0)\phi$:
   $$\frac{\partial f^0_{n\mathbf k}}{\partial\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}}\mathbf v_{n\mathbf k}\cdot(-e)\mathbf E=-\sum_{m\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\Gamma_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\Big[\frac{f_{n\mathbf k}-f^0_{n\mathbf k}}{f^0_{n\mathbf k}(1-f^0_{n\mathbf k})}-\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(1-f^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})}\Big]$$
   - Equivalently, keep Eq. (202) but use the kernel $(2\pi/\hbar)|g|^2[(1-f^0_m+n)\delta_{\rm em}+(f^0_m+n)\delta_{\rm abs}]$ for out-scattering and $(1+n-f^0_n)$, $(n+f^0_n)$ for in-scattering. That is the form used in modern codes.
   - Also, the band sum $\sum_m$ is implicit in (202).
4. **Eq. (210): band-index order (fairly confident; notational).**
   - With Eq. (38), $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\cdots|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$. The scattering $n\mathbf k\to n'\mathbf k'$ with $\mathbf q=\mathbf k'-\mathbf k$ therefore involves $|g_{n'n\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2$, not $|g_{nn'\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2$.
   - The two are equal only under time-reversal and band relabelling.
5. **Eq. (200): index ordering in the amplitude (notational).**
   - Read literally with Eq. (38), $g_{pm\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle p\,\mathbf k+\mathbf q|\Delta v|m\,\mathbf k\rangle$. That is inconsistent with an intermediate state $p\mathbf k$ and a final state $m\mathbf k+\mathbf q$.
   - The intended amplitude, up to an overall complex conjugate that is irrelevant under $|\cdot|^2$, is
   $$\mathbf e\cdot\sum_p\Big[\frac{g_{mp\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\mathbf v_{pn}(\mathbf k)}{\varepsilon_{p\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega}+\frac{\mathbf v_{mp}(\mathbf k+\mathbf q)g_{pn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)}{\varepsilon_{p\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+s\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\Big]$$
   - For phonon emission the vertex strictly involves $-\mathbf q$ phonons, i.e., $g^*$ at $-\mathbf q$. This does not matter for $|g|$ under time reversal.
   - The occupation factor $(f_n-f_m)(n+\tfrac12+\tfrac s2)$ is exact only up to terms $\propto f_m(1-f_n)$, which are fine for semiconductors.
