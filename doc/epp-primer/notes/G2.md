## 1. Overview (plain language)

Section II of the review wrote down, without justification, the "standard" electron–phonon Hamiltonian, Eq. (1): free electrons with energies $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$, free phonons with energies $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$, and a coupling term linear (and quadratic) in the phonon field with matrix elements $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ (and $g^{\rm DW}$). Section III shows where every ingredient of Eq. (1) comes from in a density-functional calculation.

**Part A (lattice vibrations).** The Born–Oppenheimer total energy $U(\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})$ of the clamped-nuclei electronic ground state is expanded to second order in the ionic displacements (harmonic approximation). The second derivatives are the interatomic force constants; their lattice Fourier transform, divided by the square root of the masses, is the dynamical matrix. Diagonalizing it gives the phonon frequencies $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ and the polarization vectors $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$. Ladder operators then turn the crystal into $3MN_p$ independent quantum harmonic oscillators, and the displacement operator is written in terms of $\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu}$ times a zero-point length $l_{\mathbf q\nu}$.

**Part B (coupling).** The electrons are described by the Kohn–Sham (KS) Hamiltonian. When the nuclei move, the KS potential changes. Expanding $V^{\rm KS}$ to first order in the displacements and inserting the phonon expansion gives the linear coupling Hamiltonian with $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$. Going to second order gives the Debye–Waller (DW) term. The key object, the *self-consistent* change of the KS potential, can be computed by finite differences in a supercell (frozen phonons) or, much more efficiently, by density-functional perturbation theory (DFPT). DFPT solves a Sternheimer equation for the linear change of the occupied orbitals (no empty states needed), builds the induced density and closes the loop through the Hartree and xc kernels. Rewriting this loop in symbolic form shows that the DFPT perturbation is just the bare electron–ion perturbation screened by a static dielectric matrix: $\partial v^{\rm KS}=(\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc})^{-1}\partial v^{\rm en}$. In the RPA ($f^{xc}=0$) this is the same $\epsilon^{-1}=(1-v\chi^0)^{-1}$ that appears in GW and BSE. Finally, the free-electron limit of these formulas recovers the historical matrix elements: Bloch (unscreened, constant potential), Nordheim (rigid ion) and Bardeen (Lindhard-screened ion).

Everything in this section is **static and electrostatic**. The Coulomb interaction is instantaneous, screening is evaluated at $\omega=0$ (adiabatic), and there is no transverse electromagnetic field. This is exactly the baseline that the EPP formalism generalizes.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **Setup (p. 7).** $M$ atoms per unit cell. A BvK supercell of $N_p$ cells with lattice vectors $\mathbf R_p$ and atomic positions $\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}=\mathbf R_p+\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$. A uniform grid of $N_p$ wave vectors $\mathbf q$. DFT gives the clamped-nuclei total energy $U(\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})$.
2. **Harmonic expansion** of $U$ to second order (Eq. 12). This defines the force constants $C$ (Eq. 13).
3. **Fourier transform** gives the dynamical matrix $D^{\rm dm}(\mathbf q)$ (Eq. 14). It is a Hermitian eigenproblem, so the frequencies are real and the eigenvectors orthonormal and complete (Eqs. 15–17), with time-reversal relations (Eq. 18).
4. **Quantum nuclear Hamiltonian** (Eq. 19) in the harmonic plus Born–Oppenheimer approximations.
5. **Ladder operators** (Appendix B; Eqs. 20–21). The displacements are expanded in $\hat a+\hat a^\dagger$ with zero-point amplitude $l_{\mathbf q\nu}$. This gives $\hat H_{\rm p}=\sum\hbar\omega(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+1/2)$ (Eq. 22). The three $\omega=0$ modes at $\mathbf q=0$ are excluded.
6. **KS Hamiltonian** (Eqs. 23–28): $V^{\rm KS}=V^{\rm en}+V^{\rm H}+V^{xc}$, with bare Coulomb electron–nucleus potential (Eq. 26) and instantaneous Hartree potential (Eq. 27). Bloch form and normalization (Eq. 29). Second quantization of the electronic part (Eq. 30).
7. **First-order expansion** of $V^{\rm KS}$ in the displacements (Eq. 31). Inserting Eq. (20) gives the normal-mode form (Eqs. 32–35), with lattice-periodic scattering potentials $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}$ and $\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}$.
8. **Second quantization of the perturbation** (Eq. 36) gives the linear coupling Hamiltonian (Eq. 37) and the matrix element (Eq. 38). It is set to zero for the translational modes at $\mathbf q=0$ (acoustic sum rule).
9. **Second order:** $\hat H^{(2)}_{\rm ep}$ and $g^{\rm DW}$ (Eqs. 39–40). It is needed for consistency with the second-order expansion of $U$. To first order in perturbation theory only $\mathbf q'=-\mathbf q$ contributes, giving a static energy shift $\propto(2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)$.
10. **How to get $\partial V^{\rm KS}$.** (a) Frozen phonon / supercell finite differences (Eq. 41). (b) DFPT: decompose $\partial v^{\rm KS}$ (Eq. 42). The bare ionic part is analytic in reciprocal space (Eq. 43); Hartree (Eq. 44) and xc (Eqs. 45–46) are linear in the induced density.
11. **Sternheimer equation** (Eq. 47). It is ill-conditioned or singular, so one projects onto the unoccupied manifold (Eq. 48) and adds $\alpha\hat P^{\rm occ}$. This gives the induced density (Eq. 49) and an SCF loop.
12. **Dielectric reformulation.** Expanding $\partial\tilde u$ in all KS states gives $\partial n=\chi^0\partial v^{\rm KS}$ (Eqs. 50–51). Symbolically $\partial v^{\rm KS}=\partial v^{\rm en}+(v^{\rm C}+f^{xc})\chi^0\partial v^{\rm KS}$ (Eq. 52), so $\partial v^{\rm KS}=(\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc})^{-1}\partial v^{\rm en}$ (Eqs. 53–54). Test-electron versus test-charge (RPA) dielectric matrix (Eqs. 55–56). DFPT is "RPA + $xc$" static screening.
13. **Adiabatic caveat (p. 12).** All perturbations are static: phonons are treated as quasistatic, and retardation (frequency dependence of the screening) is deferred to Sec. IV.
14. **Recovering history (pp. 12–13).** With free electrons and one atom per cell, the bare $g$ (Eq. 57) has umklapp terms. Without umklapp it reduces to the Bloch/Nordheim form (Eq. 58). With the Lindhard RPA screening (Eqs. 59–60) one gets Bardeen's screened matrix element (Eq. 61).
15. **Transition to Sec. IV (p. 13).** The DFT approach rests on KS potentials and Born–Oppenheimer phonons. A rigorous, self-consistent theory needs quantum field theory (Hedin–Baym).

## 3. Key equations (faithful transcriptions, with the paper's numbering)

### Conventions (state these on the page)

- **Units: SI.** The Coulomb potential energy is $e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 r)$, and $\hbar$ is explicit. All "potentials" $V$, $v$ are **potential energies of an electron** (units of energy), not electrostatic potentials. The electron charge is $-e$, so the electron–nucleus energy is negative, Eq. (26).
- **Lattice:** $\mathbf R_p=\sum_i n_i\mathbf a_i$, $0\le n_i\le N_i-1$, $N_p=N_1N_2N_3$ cells in the BvK supercell. $\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}=\mathbf R_p+\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$. $\mathbf T$ is a lattice vector **of the BvK supercell** (periodic images), not of the unit cell. $\mathbf q=\sum_j (m_j/N_j)\mathbf b_j$, with $N_p$ points; $\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij}$. $\Omega$ is the unit-cell volume.
- **Sum rules (A1):** $\sum_{\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}=N_p\delta_{p0}$ and $\sum_p e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}=N_p\delta_{\mathbf q\mathbf 0}$.
- **Indices:** $\kappa=1..M$ atoms, $\alpha=x,y,z$, $p=1..N_p$ cells, $\nu=1..3M$ branches, $n,m$ bands, $v$ occupied bands.
- **Phase convention:** forward lattice transforms carry $e^{+i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}$ (Eqs. 14, 20). Normal-mode phases follow Maradudin and Vosko (1968): $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(-\mathbf q)=e^*_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$.
- **Fourier transform of lattice-periodic functions** (stated below Eq. 43): $f(\mathbf G)=\Omega^{-1}\int_{\rm uc}d\mathbf r\,e^{-i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf r}f(\mathbf r)$. For aperiodic functions such as $V_\kappa$ and the Coulomb potential, the integral runs over **all space** with the same $\Omega^{-1}$ prefactor: $v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q)=\Omega^{-1}\int d\mathbf r\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r|)=e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega|\mathbf q|^2)$. Bare ion: $V_\kappa(\mathbf q)=-Z_\kappa e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega|\mathbf q|^2)$. With these conventions $V_\kappa(\mathbf q)$, $v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q)$ and $g$ all have units of **energy**.
- **Normalization:** $\psi_{n\mathbf k}$ is normalized to 1 in the BvK supercell (volume $N_p\Omega$). $u_{n\mathbf k}$ is normalized to 1 in the unit cell. Hence $\psi=N_p^{-1/2}ue^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}$ (Eq. 29). Brillouin-zone averages appear as $N_p^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf k}$.
- **Coupling Hamiltonian normalization:** in Eqs. (1) and (37) the factor $N_p^{-1/2}$ is **outside** $g$. In Eq. (39) the factor $N_p^{-1}$ is outside $g^{\rm DW}$. With Eq. (38), $g$ is independent of $N_p$ and has units of energy. See Source issues for Eqs. (2), (57), (58), (61).
- **Reference mass:** $M_0$ is arbitrary, typically the proton mass. It cancels in all physical quantities: $l_{\mathbf q\nu}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}=[\hbar/(2M_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2}$.

### Context: the target Hamiltonian, Eq. (1) (p. 3)

$$
\hat H=\sum_{n\mathbf k}\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}\hat c^\dagger_{n\mathbf k}\hat c_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu}\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+1/2)
+N_p^{-1/2}\sum_{\substack{\mathbf k,\mathbf q\\ mn\nu}}g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\hat c^\dagger_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\hat c_{n\mathbf k}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})
+\Big[N_p^{-1}\sum_{\substack{\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\mathbf q'\\ mn\nu\nu'}}g^{\rm DW}_{mn\nu\nu'}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\mathbf q')\hat c^\dagger_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q+\mathbf q'}\hat c_{n\mathbf k}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})(\hat a_{\mathbf q'\nu'}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q'\nu'})\Big] \quad (1)
$$

Section III derives each line: line 1 from Eqs. (30) and (22), line 2 from Eq. (37), lines 3–4 from Eq. (39).

### A. Lattice vibrations

**Eq. (12), harmonic expansion of the Born–Oppenheimer energy:**
$$
U=U_0+\frac12\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}\frac{\partial^2U}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\Delta\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'} \quad (12)
$$
- $U(\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})$ is the total potential energy of electrons (in their ground state) plus nuclei (classical, clamped at $\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}$) in the BvK supercell. Every DFT code outputs it.
- $U_0$ is the energy at the equilibrium positions $\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}$, and $\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}-\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}$. Derivatives are taken at equilibrium. The linear term vanishes because the forces are zero at equilibrium.
- *Approximation:* harmonic, i.e. truncation at second order.

**Eq. (13), interatomic force constants:**
$$
C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\partial^2U/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'} \quad (13)
$$
- Units: energy/length$^2$ (e.g. eV/Å$^2$). Translational invariance gives $C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=C_{\kappa\alpha 0,\kappa'\alpha',p'-p}$ and the acoustic sum rule $\sum_{\kappa'p'}C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=0$ (rigid translation costs no energy). The sum rule is not written in this section but is used implicitly when the $\mathbf q=0$, $\omega=0$ modes are invoked.
- In polar crystals $C$ contains a long-range dipole–dipole tail $\propto|\mathbf R_p|^{-3}$. This is the origin of the non-analytic LO–TO term. It is **not** discussed in this section; see Sec. VI.A.3 and Gonze and Lee (1997).

**Eq. (14), dynamical matrix:**
$$
D^{\rm dm}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\alpha'}(\mathbf q)=(M_\kappa M_{\kappa'})^{-1/2}\sum_p C_{\kappa\alpha 0,\kappa'\alpha'p}\exp(i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p) \quad (14)
$$
- $M_\kappa$ is the mass of ion $\kappa$. The superscript "dm" distinguishes it from the many-body phonon propagators $D(12)$ and $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$ of Sec. IV.B.
- It is a $3M\times3M$ Hermitian matrix: $D(\mathbf q)^\dagger=D(\mathbf q)$ follows from the symmetry of $C$ and translational invariance. Also $D(-\mathbf q)=D(\mathbf q)^*$ because $C$ is real.

**Eq. (15), eigenvalue problem:**
$$
\sum_{\kappa'\alpha'}D^{\rm dm}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\alpha'}(\mathbf q)\,e_{\kappa'\alpha',\nu}(\mathbf q)=\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}\,e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q) \quad (15)
$$
- $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ is the classical vibrational frequency of independent oscillator $(\mathbf q,\nu)$. Real eigenvalues follow from Hermiticity, and the dynamical stability condition is $\omega^2\ge0$.
- $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$ are **mass-weighted** eigenvectors ("normal modes", "polarization"). The actual displacement pattern of atom $\kappa$ is $\propto e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$.

**Eqs. (16)–(17), completeness and orthonormality:**
$$
\sum_\nu e^*_{\kappa'\alpha',\nu}(\mathbf q)e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\kappa\kappa'}\delta_{\alpha\alpha'} \quad (16)
\qquad
\sum_{\kappa\alpha}e^*_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu'}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\nu\nu'} \quad (17)
$$
- $\nu=1,\dots,3M$.

**Eq. (18), time-reversal relations:**
$$
\omega^2_{-\mathbf q\nu}=\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu},\qquad e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(-\mathbf q)=e^*_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q) \quad (18)
$$
- These follow from Eq. (14) plus a phase choice (Maradudin–Vosko). The phase choice is arbitrary but must be used consistently with Eq. (20).

**Eq. (19), quantum nuclear Hamiltonian (whole BvK supercell):**
$$
\hat H_{\rm p}=\frac12\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\Delta\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}-\sum_{\kappa\alpha p}\frac{\hbar^2}{2M_\kappa}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\tau^2_{\kappa\alpha p}} \quad (19)
$$
- $U_0$ is omitted. Two approximations enter: (i) harmonic; (ii) Born–Oppenheimer **adiabatic**, because $C$ is computed with the electrons in their ground state for each frozen nuclear configuration. Validity is discussed in Sec. V.A.1. Strictly, Born–Oppenheimer is unnecessary in multicomponent DFT (Kreibich and Gross 2001, 2008).

**Appendix B, complex normal coordinates** (for completeness; Eqs. B1–B3, B15):
$$
z_{\mathbf q\nu}=N_p^{-1/2}\sum_{\kappa\alpha p}e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}(M_\kappa/M_0)^{1/2}e^*_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\ \ ({\rm B1}),\qquad z_{-\mathbf q\nu}=z^*_{\mathbf q\nu}\ \ ({\rm B2}),
$$
$$
\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=N_p^{-1/2}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)z_{\mathbf q\nu}\ \ ({\rm B3}),\qquad z_{\mathbf q\nu}=l_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})\ \ ({\rm B15}).
$$
- The construction of the ladder operators is subtle. The $\mathbf q$ grid is split into set $\mathcal A$ (inversion-invariant points, $-\mathbf q=\mathbf q+\mathbf G$) and sets $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal C=-\mathcal B$. One uses real ladder operators for $\mathcal A$ (B13) and circular combinations $\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}=(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,x}+i\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu,y})/\sqrt2$ for $\mathcal B,\mathcal C$ (B14). The commutators are $[\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu},\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q'\nu'}]=\delta_{\nu\nu'}\delta_{\mathbf q\mathbf q'}$ and $[\hat a,\hat a]=[\hat a^\dagger,\hat a^\dagger]=0$ (Bose–Einstein statistics).

**Eq. (20), displacement operator in ladder operators:**
$$
\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=\left(\frac{M_0}{N_pM_\kappa}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\,l_{\mathbf q\nu}\,(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu}) \quad (20)
$$
- The right-hand side is Hermitian (real) thanks to Eq. (18). $\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu}$ creates a phonon of momentum $-\mathbf q$. Both terms change the crystal momentum by $+\mathbf q$ when acting on electrons, as in Eq. (37).

**Eq. (21), zero-point displacement amplitude:**
$$
l_{\mathbf q\nu}=[\hbar/(2M_0\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2} \quad (21)
$$
- It has dimensions of length and is similar in size to $\Delta\tau$ because $M_0\sim$ proton mass.
- Numerically $l^2\,[\text{Å}^2]=2.090/(M\,[\text{amu}]\times\hbar\omega\,[\text{meV}])$. Examples: proton with 50 meV gives $l\approx0.20$ Å; the actual Si atom ($M=28.09$) at 64 meV has $(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}l\approx0.034$ Å.
- It is undefined for the three $\omega=0$ modes at $\mathbf q=0$, which are skipped in all sums.

**Eq. (22), phonon Hamiltonian:**
$$
\hat H_{\rm p}=\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu}\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+1/2) \quad (22)
$$
- These are $3MN_p$ independent oscillators. The ground state is a product of Gaussians (B10). Excited states are $\prod_{\mathbf q\nu}(n_{\mathbf q\nu}!)^{-1/2}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu})^{n_{\mathbf q\nu}}\chi_0$.

### B.1 Kohn–Sham Hamiltonian

**Eq. (23):**
$$
\hat H^{\rm KS}=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\nabla^2+V^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r;\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\}) \quad (23)
$$
- Collinear spins. $\mathbf k$ labels both the wave vector and the spin. $\hat H^{\rm KS}\psi_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}\psi_{n\mathbf k}$.
- **There is no vector potential $\mathbf A$**: the electrons couple only to a scalar (longitudinal, instantaneous) potential.

**Eq. (24):**
$$
V^{\rm KS}=V^{\rm en}+V^{\rm H}+V^{xc} \quad (24)
$$

**Eq. (25), electron–nuclei potential energy:**
$$
V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r;\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})=\sum_{\kappa p,\mathbf T}V_\kappa(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}-\mathbf T) \quad (25)
$$

**Eq. (26), all-electron Coulomb case:**
$$
V_\kappa(\mathbf r)=-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Z_\kappa}{|\mathbf r|} \quad (26)
$$
- $Z_\kappa$ is the atomic number. For pseudopotentials, $V_\kappa$ has the same $-Z_\kappa^{\rm ion}e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r|)$ tail but is finite at $r=0$, and $Z_\kappa\to$ ionic charge (nuclear charge minus core electrons). Nonlocality from angular-momentum channels is short-ranged and ignored for notational simplicity.

**Eq. (27), Hartree potential:**
$$
V^{\rm H}(\mathbf r;\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{\mathbf T}\int_{\rm sc}\frac{n(\mathbf r';\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'-\mathbf T|}d\mathbf r' \quad (27)
$$
- The integral runs over the supercell (sc). This is the **instantaneous** Coulomb (electrostatic) interaction.

**Eq. (28), xc potential:**
$$
V^{xc}(\mathbf r;\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})=\delta E^{xc}[n]/\delta n\big|_{n(\mathbf r;\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})} \quad (28)
$$

**Eq. (29), Bloch form:**
$$
\psi_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)=N_p^{-1/2}u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r} \quad (29)
$$
- The density is $n(\mathbf r)=\sum_{v\mathbf k}|\psi_{v\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)|^2$ ($v$ = occupied states). It is solved self-consistently.

**Eq. (30), second-quantized electrons:**
$$
\hat H_{\rm e}=\sum_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}\langle\psi_{n\mathbf k}|\hat H^{\rm KS}|\psi_{n'\mathbf k'}\rangle\hat c^\dagger_{n\mathbf k}\hat c_{n'\mathbf k'}=\sum_{n\mathbf k}\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}\hat c^\dagger_{n\mathbf k}\hat c_{n\mathbf k} \quad (30)
$$
- *Drastic approximation:* the electrons are treated as sharp quasiparticles with KS energies. Sec. IV removes this.

### B.2 Coupling Hamiltonian to first and second order

**Eq. (31), first-order expansion of $V^{\rm KS}$:**
$$
V^{\rm KS}(\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})=V^{\rm KS}(\{\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}\})+\sum_{\kappa\alpha p}\frac{\partial V^{\rm KS}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p} \quad (31)
$$
- $\partial V^{\rm KS}$ is the **self-consistent** change: it includes the induced $\partial V^{\rm H}+\partial V^{xc}$, i.e. electronic screening.

**Eq. (32), normal-mode form:**
$$
V^{\rm KS}=V^{\rm KS}(\{\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}\})+N_p^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm KS}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu}) \quad (32)
$$

**Eqs. (33)–(35), scattering potentials:**
$$
\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm KS}=e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS} \quad (33)
$$
$$
\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}=l_{\mathbf q\nu}\sum_{\kappa\alpha}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\,\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS} \quad (34)
$$
$$
\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}=\sum_p e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_p)}\frac{\partial V^{\rm KS}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha}}\Big|_{\mathbf r-\mathbf R_p} \quad (35)
$$
- $\partial V^{\rm KS}/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha}|_{\mathbf r-\mathbf R_p}$ is the response to displacing atom $\kappa$ of the reference cell along $\alpha$, evaluated at $\mathbf r-\mathbf R_p$. By translation symmetry this equals $\partial V^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r)/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}$. Equivalently, $\sum_p e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}\partial V^{\rm KS}/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}$: a **Bloch-like perturbation** with crystal momentum $\mathbf q$.
- $\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}$ and $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}$ are lattice-periodic, so DFPT can work in a single unit cell for each $\mathbf q$.
- Check: inserting Eq. (20) into Eq. (31) and using Eq. (35) reproduces Eqs. (32)–(34) exactly.

**Eq. (36), second-quantized perturbation:**
$$
\hat H_{\rm ep}=\sum_{n\mathbf k,n'\mathbf k'}\langle\psi_{n\mathbf k}|V^{\rm KS}(\{\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\})-V^{\rm KS}(\{\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}\})|\psi_{n'\mathbf k'}\rangle\hat c^\dagger_{n\mathbf k}\hat c_{n'\mathbf k'} \quad (36)
$$
- The bra and ket integrals run over the supercell.

**Eq. (37), linear e–ph coupling Hamiltonian (energy of the whole BvK supercell):**
$$
\hat H_{\rm ep}=N_p^{-1/2}\sum_{\substack{\mathbf k,\mathbf q\\ mn\nu}}g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\hat c^\dagger_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\hat c_{n\mathbf k}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu}) \quad (37)
$$
- This uses Eqs. (29), (32)–(35) and (A1). Crystal momentum is conserved modulo $\mathbf G$: an electron $n\mathbf k$ scatters to $m\mathbf k+\mathbf q$ by absorbing a $(\mathbf q\nu)$ phonon or emitting a $(-\mathbf q\nu)$ phonon.

**Eq. (38), e–ph matrix element (THE central quantity):**
$$
g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc} \quad (38)
$$
- The integral runs over one unit cell. Units: energy. It equals $\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm KS}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm sc}$ because of the normalizations.
- It is gauge-dependent through the phases of $u$; $|g|$ is gauge-invariant for nondegenerate bands.
- For the three translational modes at $\mathbf q=0$, $g\equiv0$ (acoustic sum rule; Sec. IX.A.1).
- *Assumptions:* KS potential, adiabatic (static) screening, linear in displacements. Eqs. (22), (30) and (37) together are the starting point of most first-principles e–ph calculations.

**Eq. (39), second-order (Debye–Waller) coupling Hamiltonian:**
$$
\hat H^{(2)}_{\rm ep}=N_p^{-1}\sum_{\substack{\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\mathbf q'\\ mn\nu\nu'}}g^{\rm DW}_{mn\nu\nu'}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\mathbf q')\hat c^\dagger_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q+\mathbf q'}\hat c_{n\mathbf k}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})(\hat a_{\mathbf q'\nu'}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q'\nu'}) \quad (39)
$$
- *Rationale:* expanding $V^{\rm KS}$ only to first order is inconsistent with expanding $U$ to second order (Allen and Heine 1976; Allen 1978).

**Eq. (40):**
$$
g^{\rm DW}_{mn\nu\nu'}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\mathbf q')=\tfrac12\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q+\mathbf q'}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}\Delta_{\mathbf q'\nu'}v^{\rm KS}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc} \quad (40)
$$
- $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}$ is the same as in Eqs. (33)–(35), now applied twice (second derivatives of $V^{\rm KS}$). The factor $\tfrac12$ is the Taylor coefficient.

**DW energy shift (unnumbered, p. 10), first-order perturbation theory in $\hat H^{(2)}_{\rm ep}$:**
$$
\Delta\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}=N_p^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}g^{\rm DW}_{nn\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)(2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)
$$
- Only $\mathbf q'=-\mathbf q$, $\nu'=\nu$ survive, since $\langle(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})(\hat a_{-\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu})\rangle=2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1$.
- $n_{\mathbf q\nu}$ is the phonon occupation; in equilibrium $n=[e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}-1]^{-1}$, so $2n+1=\coth(\hbar\omega/2k_BT)$. The shift is nonzero at $T=0$ (zero-point renormalization).
- This is only the DW part. The Fan–Migdal part comes from $\hat H_{\rm ep}$ at second order (Secs. V.B, IX.A.1).

### B.3 DFPT

**Eq. (41), frozen-phonon finite difference:**
$$
\frac{\partial V^{\rm KS}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}\Big|_{\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}}\simeq[V^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r;\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}+b)-V^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r;\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})]/b \quad (41)
$$
- $b$ is a small displacement, of order the zero-point amplitude ($\sim0.1$ Å). Historically (Dacorogna, Cohen, Lam 1985…) all atoms were displaced along a chosen eigenmode ("frozen-phonon" method).
- Drawback: long-wavelength phonons require impractically large supercells.

**Eq. (42), DFPT decomposition:**
$$
\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}=\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm en}+\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm H}+\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{xc} \quad (42)
$$

**Eq. (43), bare ionic perturbation in reciprocal space:**
$$
\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm en}(\mathbf G)=-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\alpha V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa} \quad (43)
$$
- This is the gradient of the ion potential (factor $-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\alpha$, since $\partial/\partial\tau=-\nabla_{\mathbf r}$) with a structure factor.
- For Coulomb $V_\kappa$ at $\mathbf G=0$, $\mathbf q\to0$: $\propto q_\alpha/q^2\sim1/q$. This is the bare long-range dipole field of a displaced ion, the seed of polar (Fröhlich) coupling. Only the component of the displacement **along** $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ couples (longitudinal projection).

**Eq. (44), induced Hartree perturbation:**
$$
\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm H}(\mathbf G)=\Omega\,v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\,\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}n(\mathbf G),\qquad v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q)=\Omega^{-1}\!\int d\mathbf r\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r|}=\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0\Omega q^2} \quad (44)
$$

**Eq. (45), induced xc perturbation:**
$$
\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{xc}(\mathbf G)=\Omega\sum_{\mathbf G'}f^{xc}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G,\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\,\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}n(\mathbf G') \quad (45)
$$

**Eq. (46), xc kernel:**
$$
f^{xc}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=\frac{\delta^2E^{xc}[n]}{\delta n(\mathbf r)\delta n(\mathbf r')}\Big|_{n(\mathbf r;\{\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}\})} \quad (46)
$$
- In LDA the kernel is local, so Eq. (45) is best done in real space. Spin-unpolarized case. Generalizations exist (DFT+U, DMFT, noncollinear).

**Sternheimer equation, Eq. (47):**
$$
(\hat H^{\rm KS}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{v\mathbf k})\,\partial u_{v\mathbf k,\mathbf q}=-\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}\,u_{v\mathbf k},\qquad \hat H^{\rm KS}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=e^{-i(\mathbf k+\mathbf q)\cdot\mathbf r}\hat H^{\rm KS}e^{i(\mathbf k+\mathbf q)\cdot\mathbf r} \quad (47)
$$
- The first-order wave-function change is $\partial u_{v\mathbf k,\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}$, with $\partial u$ lattice-periodic. $v$ runs over occupied states.
- For $\mathbf q=0$ one adds the eigenvalue shift $\langle u_{v\mathbf k}|\partial_{\kappa\alpha,0}v|u_{v\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc}u_{v\mathbf k}$ to the right-hand side. This is cancelled by the projectors of Eq. (48), except in metals (de Gironcoli 1995; Baroni et al. 2001).
- Advantage: no unoccupied states are needed.

**Projected Sternheimer, Eq. (48):**
$$
(\hat H^{\rm KS}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{v\mathbf k})\,\partial\tilde u_{v\mathbf k,\mathbf q}=-(1-\hat P^{\rm occ}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\,\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}u_{v\mathbf k} \quad (48)
$$
- $\partial\tilde u_{v\mathbf k,\mathbf q}=(1-\hat P^{\rm occ}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\partial u_{v\mathbf k,\mathbf q}$ and $\hat P^{\rm occ}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=\sum_v|u_{v\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\rangle\langle u_{v\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|$. This works because $\hat P^{\rm occ}$ commutes with $\hat H^{\rm KS}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}$.
- Rationale: the density change depends only on the occupied-to-unoccupied component (Giannozzi et al. 1991). The bare Eq. (47) is ill-conditioned when $\varepsilon_{v\mathbf k}\approx\varepsilon_{v'\mathbf k+\mathbf q}$ and singular when they are exactly degenerate.
- Fix: add $\alpha\hat P^{\rm occ}_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}$ to the operator on the left. This does not change the solution because $\hat P^{\rm occ}\partial\tilde u=0$, and it makes the operator nonsingular when $\alpha$ exceeds the valence bandwidth.

**Induced density, Eq. (49):**
$$
\partial n_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)=2N_p^{-1}\sum_{v\mathbf k}u^*_{v\mathbf k}\,\partial\tilde u_{v\mathbf k,\mathbf q} \quad (49)
$$
- Spin degeneracy is folded into the $\mathbf k$ sum (factor 2 implicit). The explicit 2 comes from time-reversal symmetry. The physical density change is $e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\partial n_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)$.
- **SCF loop (p. 11):** start with $\partial v^{\rm KS}=\partial v^{\rm en}$ (Eq. 43); solve Eq. (48) for all $v,\mathbf k$; build $\partial n$ (Eq. 49); compute $\partial v^{\rm H}$, $\partial v^{xc}$ (Eqs. 44–45); add them to get a screened $\partial v^{\rm KS}$; repeat until converged.
- The same $\partial v^{\rm KS}$ gives the force constants of Eq. (13), so any DFPT phonon code already has everything needed for $g$. A variational formulation (Gonze) is equivalent.
- $g^{\rm DW}$ requires second-order Sternheimer equations. In practice all *ab initio* DFPT calculations use the Allen–Heine rigid-ion approximation, which expresses $g^{\rm DW}$ through products of $g$ (Sec. IX.A.1).

### B.4 Dielectric approach

**Eq. (50), linear response:**
$$
\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}n(\mathbf r)=\int_{\rm uc}d\mathbf r'\,\chi^0_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\,\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r') \quad (50)
$$

**Eq. (51), independent-particle polarizability (lattice-periodic part):**
$$
\chi^0_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=N_p^{-1}\sum_{mn\mathbf k}\frac{f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}\,u^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)u^*_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r')u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r') \quad (51)
$$
- $f$ are occupations, all bands are included, and spin factor 2 is implicit in the $\mathbf k$ sum. It is obtained by expanding $\partial\tilde u$ over all states, inserting into Eq. (48), projecting on conduction states, inserting into Eq. (49), and using time reversal.
- This is the static ($\omega=0$) Adler–Wiser $\chi^0$, the same object used in RPA, GW and BSE.

**Eq. (52), symbolic SCF equation:** with symbolic notation $\partial n=\chi^0\partial v^{\rm KS}$,
$$
\partial v^{\rm KS}=\partial v^{\rm en}+(v^{\rm C}+f^{xc})\chi^0\partial v^{\rm KS} \quad (52)
$$

**Eq. (53), screened perturbation:**
$$
\partial v^{\rm KS}=(\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc})^{-1}\partial v^{\rm en} \quad (53)
$$

**Eq. (54), test-electron dielectric matrix:**
$$
\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}=1-(v^{\rm C}+f^{xc})\chi^0 \quad (54)
$$
- The "H$xc$" superscript means Hartree plus xc screening. In many-body language this is the "test-electron" dielectric matrix: the perturbing particle is an electron, so it also feels the xc response.

**Eq. (55), test-charge (RPA) dielectric matrix:**
$$
\epsilon^{\rm H}=1-v^{\rm C}\chi_0 \quad (55)
$$
- Obtained by neglecting $f^{xc}$ (Pines and Bohm 1952). Note the notation $\chi_0$ here versus $\chi^0$ elsewhere.

**Eq. (56), reciprocal-space form:**
$$
\epsilon^{\rm H}_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}-\Omega^2\sum_{\mathbf G''}\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G''}(\mathbf q)\,v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\,\delta_{\mathbf G''\mathbf G'} \quad (56)
$$
- This equals $\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}-\Omega^2v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)$.
- The $\Omega^2$ goes with $\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)=\Omega^{-2}\int_{\rm uc}\!\int_{\rm uc}d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\,e^{-i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf r}\chi^0_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')e^{i\mathbf G'\cdot\mathbf r'}$. This is implied by the convention under Eq. (43) but not written.
- The off-diagonal elements are local-field effects.
- **Result:** Eqs. (38) and (53) together mean that the DFPT e–ph matrix element is the bare electron–nucleus vertex screened by $(\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc})^{-1}$, i.e. "RPA + $xc$".
- **Static only (p. 12):** $\partial v^{\rm en}$ is frequency-independent (adiabatic or quasistatic phonons). Retardation was recognized by Bardeen and Pines (1955), and first *ab initio* by Lazzeri and Mauri (2006). The formal framework is in Sec. IV.

### B.5 Connection with early formulations

Assumptions: unscreened bare $V_\kappa$, one atom at the origin, free-electron states $u_{n\mathbf k}=\Omega^{-1/2}e^{i\mathbf G_n\cdot\mathbf r}$ with reduced-zone labelling $n\leftrightarrow\mathbf G_n$, inserted into Eqs. (21), (34), (38) and (43).

**Eq. (57), bare matrix element with umklapp:**
$$
g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-i[\hbar/(2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2}\,V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G_m-\mathbf G_n)\,(\mathbf q+\mathbf G_m-\mathbf G_n)\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa,\nu}(\mathbf q) \quad (57)
$$
- See Source issues: with $g$ defined by Eq. (38), the $N_p$ inside the bracket should be absent.

**Eq. (58), no umklapp ($\mathbf G_m=\mathbf G_n$):**
$$
g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-i[\hbar/(2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2}\,\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa,\nu}(\mathbf q)\,V_\kappa(\mathbf q) \quad (58)
$$
- Bloch's Eq. (2) is obtained by $V_\kappa(\mathbf q)\to V_0$. Nordheim's rigid-ion model uses the true $V_\kappa(\mathbf q)$.
- For Coulomb ions, $V_\kappa(\mathbf q)=-Z_\kappa e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega q^2)$, so $g\propto q^{-1}\omega_{\mathbf q}^{-1/2}$. With acoustic $\omega=v_sq$ this gives $g\propto q^{-3/2}$: an unphysical divergence.

**Eq. (59), free-electron (Lindhard) static polarizability:**
$$
\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)=-\frac{m_ek_F}{\pi^2\hbar^2\Omega}F(|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|/2k_F)\,\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'} \quad (59)
$$
- Here $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}=\hbar^2(\mathbf k+\mathbf G_n)^2/2m_e-\varepsilon_F$, using the extended-zone scheme.
- $F$ is the Lindhard function defined below Eq. (4):
$$F(x)=\tfrac12+\tfrac{1-x^2}{4x}\ln\left|\tfrac{1+x}{1-x}\right|,$$
with $F(0)=1$, $F(1)=1/2$ (log singularity in $F'$ there), and $F\to1/(3x^2)$ for $x\gg1$.
- $m_ek_F/(\pi^2\hbar^2)=N(\varepsilon_F)$ is the density of states per volume including spin.

**Eq. (60), Lindhard dielectric matrix:** inserting Eq. (59) into Eq. (56), with no xc,
$$
\epsilon_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}\left[1+\frac{k^2_{\rm TF}}{|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|^2}F(|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|/2k_F)\right],\qquad k_{\rm TF}=\left[\frac{4m_ee^2k_F}{4\pi\epsilon_0\pi\hbar^2}\right]^{1/2} \quad (60)
$$
- The diagonal elements equal Eq. (4): $\epsilon(q)=1+(k_{\rm TF}/q)^2F(q/2k_F)$.
- In atomic units $k_{\rm TF}^2=4k_F/(\pi a_0)$. For jellium with density parameter $r_s$: $k_Fa_0=1.9192/r_s$, $(k_{\rm TF}a_0)^2=2.444/r_s$, $\varepsilon_F=50.11/r_s^2$ eV.

**Eq. (61), Bardeen's screened matrix element:**
$$
g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-i[\hbar/(2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2}\,\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa,\nu}(\mathbf q)\,V_\kappa(\mathbf q)/\epsilon(q) \quad (61)
$$
- One atom, no umklapp; it uses Eq. (53) with $\epsilon$ instead of $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$. This is Eq. (3) of Bardeen (1937).
- As $q\to0$, $V_\kappa(q)/\epsilon(q)\to-Z_\kappa e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega k_{\rm TF}^2)=-n/N(\varepsilon_F)=-\tfrac23\varepsilon_F$ for jellium with $n=Z/\Omega$. The divergence is removed, and $g\propto q^{1/2}$ for acoustic phonons.

**Context equations from Sec. II needed for the connection (pp. 3–5):**
- Bloch, Eq. (2): $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-i\left(\frac{\hbar}{2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}(\mathbf q)V_0$, with $V_0=\hbar^2/(16m_ea_0^2)\approx1.70$ eV.
- Bardeen, Eq. (3): $V_0\to V_\kappa(\mathbf q)/\epsilon(q)$. Lindhard, Eq. (4): $\epsilon(q)=1+(k_{\rm TF}/q)^2F(q/2k_F)$.
- Bardeen–Pines (retarded, dynamically screened vertex), Eq. (5): $V_0\to V_\kappa(\mathbf q)/\epsilon(q,\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})$.
- Fröhlich, Eq. (7) (isotropic polar crystals): $V_0\to-\left[\frac{e^2M_\kappa\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\epsilon_0\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^\infty}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^0}\right)\right]^{1/2}\frac{1}{|\mathbf q|^2}$, so $g\propto1/|\mathbf q|$. Note $\epsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity while $\epsilon^0$ is the static relative permittivity.

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Two small parameters, two expansions.** The theory expands the *energy* $U$ to second order (harmonic phonons) and the *potential* $V^{\rm KS}$ to first order (linear coupling $g$). Allen–Heine's point is that these orders are mismatched. Consistent second-order energetics needs $\partial^2V^{\rm KS}$, i.e. the DW term. The DW term is a static, second-order "tadpole-like" vertex. Its first-order expectation value gives a band shift $\propto\sum(2n+1)$ that survives at $T=0$. This, together with the Fan–Migdal term, gives the zero-point renormalization of band gaps (Sec. IX.A).

**(b) Mass weighting and what an eigenvector means.** $D^{\rm dm}$ is mass-weighted so that the eigenproblem is Hermitian. The physical displacement of atom $\kappa$ in mode $\nu$ is $e_{\kappa\nu}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$.
- Acoustic $\mathbf q\to0$: all atoms move together, $e_{\kappa}\propto\sqrt{M_\kappa}$.
- Optical: the centre of mass is fixed, $\sum_\kappa\sqrt{M_\kappa}e_\kappa=0$.
- Light atoms vibrate with larger amplitude and higher frequency.

**(c) Zero-point length.** $l_{\mathbf q\nu}=\sqrt{\hbar/2M_0\omega}$ is the rms displacement of the ground-state Gaussian (per unit $|e|$). Single-mode, single-oscillator scale: $\sqrt{\hbar/(2M\omega)}\sim0.03$ Å for Si optical phonons ($M=28$, $\hbar\omega\approx64$ meV), $\sim0.03$ Å for C in graphene ($M=12$, $\hbar\omega\approx196$ meV), $\sim0.1$–0.2 Å for H. This is why the finite-difference step $b\sim0.1$ Å is used (Eq. 41), and why the linear expansion is reasonable: $l\ll$ bond length ($\sim1.5$–2.5 Å).
- Einstein-mode thermal rms per Cartesian direction, from Eqs. (16), (20), (A1): $\langle\Delta\tau^2_{\kappa\alpha}\rangle=\frac{\hbar}{2M_\kappa\omega}\coth\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_BT}$. This gives the classical limit $k_BT/(M\omega^2)$ at high $T$ and the zero-point floor at $T=0$.

**(d) $g$ is "displacement × potential gradient".** From Eq. (34), $g\sim\sqrt{\hbar/2M\omega}\times\langle\partial V/\partial\tau\rangle$: a zero-point length times a deformation potential (eV/Å). Typical values are $\partial V/\partial\tau\sim$ few eV/Å and $l\sim0.03$ Å, giving $g\sim0.1$ eV. Diamond optical modes give $g\approx150$–450 meV (Fig. 6, p. 35, outside this range).
- $g$ has units of energy. The physical coupling strength in rates is $|g|^2$ times a density of final states. The $N_p^{-1/2}$ in Eq. (37) becomes a BZ integral $N_p^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf q}\to\Omega_{\rm BZ}^{-1}\int d\mathbf q$ in Fermi golden-rule expressions.

**(e) Bloch-wave perturbations.** A displacement pattern $\propto e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}$ produces a potential change $e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\times$(lattice-periodic). The response at wave vector $\mathbf q$ therefore decouples from all other wave vectors (linear response plus translational symmetry). DFPT exploits this: one unit-cell calculation per $\mathbf q$ instead of an $N_p$-cell supercell. The cost saving is roughly $N_p^2$, typically $>10^3$ (p. 10), because DFT costs scale as $N_{\rm el}^3$.

**(f) Why the bare ionic perturbation must be screened.** Eq. (43) with Coulomb ions gives $\partial v^{\rm en}\propto(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\propto1/|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|$.
- At $\mathbf G=0$ this diverges as $q\to0$. In metals the electrons screen it completely: Thomas–Fermi $\epsilon\sim k_{\rm TF}^2/q^2$, so the screened potential is finite, $-\tfrac23\varepsilon_F$ at $q\to0$ for jellium. For Na this is about $-2.2$ eV; for Al about $-7.8$ eV.
- In insulators $\epsilon\to\epsilon^\infty$ (finite), so the $1/q$ survives in polar materials. This is the Fröhlich coupling, Eq. (7), $g\propto1/q$.
- In non-polar insulators the dipolar terms cancel by symmetry (Born charges zero).
- Only the **longitudinal** projection $(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf e$ couples through the scalar potential (Bloch's "polarization factor": only longitudinal sound waves scatter free electrons, p. 4).

**(g) DFPT = static RPA(+xc) screening.** Eq. (53) is the key conceptual bridge to many-body theory. The self-consistent field in DFPT sums the geometric series $\epsilon^{-1}=1+v\chi^0+(v\chi^0)^2+\dots$ (plus $f^{xc}$). This is exactly what the SCF loop does iteratively.
- Test-charge $\epsilon^{\rm H}$ (RPA) is the one in $W=\epsilon^{-1}v$ in GW/BSE. Test-electron $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$ includes the xc hole of the scattered electron.
- Local fields (off-diagonal $\mathbf G\mathbf G'$) matter in real solids.
- For the user's community: the DFPT vertex screens the electron–ion potential, while $W$ screens the electron–electron potential, with the same static $\chi^0$. Note that $W$ here contains **only electronic** screening; the ionic (phonon) part of the screening appears in Sec. IV as $W^{\rm ph}$.

**(h) Ill-conditioning and projectors.** The Sternheimer operator $H_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{v\mathbf k}$ has near-zero eigenvalues for occupied states at $\mathbf k+\mathbf q$ with energies close to $\varepsilon_{v\mathbf k}$. Occupied-to-occupied rotations do not change the density, so they are projected out. Adding $\alpha\hat P^{\rm occ}$ shifts the occupied block away from zero. The physics needs only "virtual excitations" valence→conduction, which is also why $\chi^0$ in Eq. (51) has $f_n-f_m$ in the numerator.

**(i) Adiabatic and static.** Static screening means the electrons follow the nuclei instantaneously. This is justified when $\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}\ll$ gap (insulators), or more loosely $\ll\varepsilon_F$ (metals). It fails for doped semiconductors, graphene and metals with Kohn anomalies (Lazzeri–Mauri 2006: graphene $E_{2g}$).
- "Retardation" in this paper means **frequency dependence of the electronic screening** $\epsilon(q,\omega)$ (Bardeen–Pines, Eq. 5). It does *not* mean the finite speed of light. The latter is absent throughout Giustino's review.

**(j) History as a sequence of better potentials:**
- Bloch (1929): constant $V_0\approx1.70$ eV, no screening, $g\propto\sqrt q$.
- Nordheim (1931): rigid ion $V_\kappa(\mathbf q)$, diverges as $q^{-2}$.
- Mott–Jones (1936): Wigner–Seitz-truncated ion (crude screening).
- Bardeen (1937): Lindhard-screened ion, self-consistent linearized Hartree, a precursor of DFPT.
- Bardeen–Pines (1955): dynamically screened, exchange included.
- Fröhlich (1950): polar crystals, $1/q$.
- Pseudopotentials (Sham 1961), frozen phonon (Dacorogna, Cohen, Lam 1985), DFPT (1987–1992).

**Numbers quoted in the range:**
- $b\sim0.1$ Å (zero-point amplitude).
- DFPT saving $\propto N_p^2$, typically $>10^3$.
- $M_0$ = proton mass.
- 3 translational modes at $\mathbf q=0$; $3M$ branches; $3MN_p$ oscillators.

**Useful conversions for the page:**
- $\hbar\omega\,[\text{meV}]=64.654\sqrt{K\,[\text{eV/Å}^2]/M\,[\text{amu}]}$.
- $\hbar^2/(2\,\text{amu})=2.0901$ meV Å$^2$.
- $e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)=14.400$ eV Å; 1 Ha = 27.211 eV; $a_0=0.52918$ Å.

- The zero-point rms is about 0.03–0.05 Å, i.e. $\ll a$.

### V3. The DFPT self-consistency loop as a geometric series (Eqs. 49–54)

**What:** for a single Fourier component of jellium (no local fields), iterate the linear-response SCF with linear mixing and plot $\partial v_n/\partial v^{\rm en}$ versus iteration $n$, with the exact $1/\epsilon$ as a horizontal line. A second plot shows the contraction factor versus $q/k_F$ for the chosen $\beta$.

**Controls:** $q/k_F\in[0.05,3]$; $r_s$; mixing $\beta\in(0,1.5]$; RPA versus RPA+LDA-$f^{xc}$ toggle; optional Kerker mixing $\beta(q)=\beta_0q^2/(q^2+q_0^2)$.

**Formulas:**
- $v\chi^0(q)=-(k_{\rm TF}/q)^2F(q/2k_F)$.
- With xc: $(v+f^{xc})\chi^0=v\chi^0(1+f^{xc}q^2\epsilon_0/e^2)$. Simplest LDA exchange-only kernel in atomic units: $f^{xc}_x=-\pi/k_F^2$, so $(v+f_x)\chi^0=v\chi^0(1-q^2/(4k_F^2))$. Present it as a toy and label it so.
- Output: $\partial v^{\rm out}=\partial v^{\rm en}+(v+f)\chi^0\,\partial v^{\rm in}$. Update: $\partial v_{n+1}=(1-\beta)\partial v_n+\beta\,\partial v^{\rm out}_n$, starting from $\partial v_0=\partial v^{\rm en}$ (as in the paper).
- Fixed point $\partial v^*=\partial v^{\rm en}/\epsilon$, with $\epsilon=1-(v+f)\chi^0$.
- Error contraction per step: $r=1-\beta\epsilon$. Converges iff $0<\beta<2/\epsilon$; one-step convergence at $\beta=1/\epsilon$.

**Learner should notice:**
- The undamped loop ($\beta=1$) is the Neumann series $\sum(v\chi^0)^k$. It diverges when $\epsilon>2$, which always happens at small $q$ in metals since $\epsilon\sim k_{\rm TF}^2/q^2$ ("charge sloshing").
- Mixing or Kerker preconditioning fixes this. The converged result is exactly the RPA-screened perturbation, i.e. DFPT computes $\epsilon^{-1}\partial v^{\rm en}$ without ever building $\epsilon$.

## 7. Connections (baseline that EPP generalizes, and links within Giustino)

**What is electron–phonon-only / electrostatic in this range:**

1. **Instantaneous, longitudinal Coulomb interaction only.** $V_\kappa$ (Eq. 26), $V^{\rm H}$ (Eq. 27) and $v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q)=e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega q^2)$ (Eq. 44) are the static Coulomb kernel, i.e. the longitudinal (scalar-potential, Coulomb-gauge) part of the EM interaction with $c\to\infty$. The KS Hamiltonian (Eq. 23) has **no vector potential** $\mathbf A$, no $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$ coupling, and no transverse photons. Ionic motion enters only through the scalar potential of displaced charges. The ionic **current** (velocity), which sources transverse fields, is absent. EPP adds the transverse photon field and its coupling to both electronic and ionic currents, with retardation $\propto\omega/c$.
2. **Only longitudinal displacement components couple.** In Eq. (43) and Eqs. (57), (58), (61) the coupling carries $(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}$: only the projection of the polarization on the wave vector produces a Coulomb potential. In the electrodynamic theory the transverse projection couples to the transverse (photon) field. Near the light cone ($q\sim\omega_{\rm TO}\sqrt{\epsilon}/c$) this gives phonon-polaritons. The electrostatic limit recovered in EPP should reduce to Eq. (43) screened by $\epsilon$.
3. **Polar (Fröhlich) coupling.** In this range it appears only implicitly: the $\mathbf G=0$, $q\to0$ term of Eq. (43) with Coulomb $V_\kappa$ ($\propto1/q$), screened by $\epsilon^\infty$ instead of the metallic $k_{\rm TF}^2/q^2$. It is made explicit in Sec. II.A.3, Eq. (7) ($g\propto1/q$, with factor $1/\epsilon^\infty-1/\epsilon^0$), and Sec. VI.A.3, Eq. (180) (Verdi–Giustino / Sjakste et al. long-range vertex):
$$g^{\mathcal L}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=i\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_\kappa\left(\frac{\hbar}{2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf G\neq-\mathbf q}\frac{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}(\mathbf q)}{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)}\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa)}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm sc}.$$
This is the electrostatic Fröhlich vertex that EPP's "electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex" generalizes: the static $\epsilon^\infty$ becomes a retarded EM propagator, with a transverse part.
4. **LO–TO splitting, non-analytic terms, Born effective charges.** These are **not treated** in Eqs. (12)–(22). The force constants (Eq. 13) of a polar crystal contain the long-range dipole–dipole term, so $D^{\rm dm}(\mathbf q\to0)$ is non-analytic: it depends on the direction $\hat{\mathbf q}$, with $\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2\propto(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot Z^*\cdot\mathbf e)^2/(\Omega\epsilon_0\,\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q})$. Giustino refers to Gonze and Lee (1997) only in Sec. VI.A.3. In the electrostatic picture the LO mode is pushed up by the macroscopic longitudinal field; the TO mode is unaffected for all $q$. In the full electrodynamic picture (EPP), the TO mode couples to transverse photons. The electrostatic (LO–TO) picture is recovered for $q\gg\omega/c$.
5. **Dielectric screening.** Eqs. (50)–(56) contain only the **static electronic** dielectric matrix. Test-electron $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}=1-(v+f^{xc})\chi^0$; test-charge RPA $\epsilon^{\rm H}=1-v\chi^0$; local fields $\epsilon_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)$. In EPP language this corresponds to the *electronic* part of the longitudinal photon self-energy (polarization) at $\omega=0$ in the RPA. The *ionic* part (the lattice contribution to $\epsilon(\omega)$, Lorentz oscillators at $\omega_{\rm TO}$) is absent here. It appears in Giustino only through $W^{\rm ph}$ (Sec. IV.B.2–3).
6. **Screened Coulomb interaction $W$ and its phonon contribution.** Eq. (53) says $g$ is the bare vertex screened by the same $\epsilon^{-1}$ as $W=\epsilon^{-1}v$. Sec. IV.B.2 (p. 17) splits the total screened interaction as $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$, Eq. (104), with $W^{\rm ph}(12)=\int d(34)W^{\rm e}(13)D(34)W^{\rm e}(24)$, Eq. (105) (Hedin–Lundqvist 1969).
   - In the phonon basis, $W^{\rm ph}\sim\sum_\nu|g|^2D_\nu$ is the phonon-mediated (retarded) electron–electron interaction.
   - In polar materials $W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$ at $\omega\to0$ interpolates from $\epsilon^\infty$ to $\epsilon^0$ screening. This is relevant to exciton screening in polar 2D materials and BSE with phonon screening.
   - EPP generalizes this to a photon propagator whose self-energy has electronic and ionic parts, which also allows transverse components.
7. **Adiabatic versus nonadiabatic.** Eq. (19) (Born–Oppenheimer force constants) and the static screening in Eqs. (50)–(56) are adiabatic. The p. 12 paragraph flags retardation of screening (Bardeen–Pines, Eq. 5: $\epsilon(q,\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})$; Lazzeri–Mauri 2006). The fully dynamical treatment is in Sec. IV.C and Sec. V.A: the phonon self-energy $\Pi(\omega)$ is split into adiabatic $\Pi^A=\Pi(\omega=0)$ and nonadiabatic parts; DFPT force constants correspond to $\Pi^A$.
   - **Caution for the page:** "retardation" in Giustino means frequency-dependent electronic screening. In EPP, retardation also means the finite speed of light in the photon propagator. Distinguish the two explicitly.
8. **Phonon self-energy and dressed versus bare vertices.** DFPT $g$ (Eq. 38) is the *dressed* (screened) vertex. The bare vertex $g^b_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r)=\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)$ is Eq. (142), and $g=\epsilon_e^{-1}g^b$ is Eq. (144), Sec. V.A.3 (p. 24). The phonon self-energy in terms of bare and dressed vertices is shown in Fig. 1 and Eq. (141). The electrostatic phonon self-energy is $\Pi\sim g^b\,\chi\,g^b$, which is the term EPP's "electrodynamic phonon self-energy" generalizes.
9. **Hedin–Baym equations.** The transition paragraph on p. 13 motivates Sec. IV.D (Table I, p. 20): self-consistent Hedin–Baym equations for coupled electrons and phonons, using only the Coulomb interaction $v$. EPP's extension replaces $v$ with the full photon propagator (longitudinal + transverse).
10. **Second-order (DW) terms.** Eqs. (39)–(40) correspond to the $\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\nabla\delta\cdot\mathbf u$ term in the nuclear density expansion (Eq. 106, p. 17) and the DW self-energy (Sec. V.B.2). An electrodynamic analogue would include diamagnetic-like ($A^2$) terms, but that is outside Giustino.

## 8. Source issues

1. **Eqs. (57), (58), (61) (and Eq. (2) on p. 3): $N_p$ inside the square root is inconsistent with Eqs. (37)–(38).**
   - Eqs. (1) and (37) factor out $N_p^{-1/2}$, and $g$ is defined by Eq. (38) without $N_p$. Following the stated derivation (Eqs. 21, 34, 38, 43 with $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa=0$ and $u=\Omega^{-1/2}e^{i\mathbf G_n\cdot\mathbf r}$) gives $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-i[\hbar/(2M_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})]^{1/2}V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G_m-\mathbf G_n)(\mathbf q+\mathbf G_m-\mathbf G_n)\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa,\nu}(\mathbf q)$, with no $N_p$.
   - The printed forms would be correct only if $g$ absorbed the $N_p^{-1/2}$ of Eq. (37). Eq. (180) on p. 35 has the same feature.
   - Confident: for the page, use the Eq. (38) convention (no $N_p$), or state explicitly that $N_p^{-1/2}g$ is meant.
2. **Eq. (49) versus Eqs. (44), (45), (50): notation.** The induced density is written $\partial n_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)$ in Eq. (49) but $\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}n$ elsewhere. They are the same object (lattice-periodic part).
3. **Eq. (55):** $\chi_0$ (subscript) versus $\chi^0$ (superscript) in Eqs. (50)–(54), (56), (59). Same object.
4. **Eq. (56):** the sum over $\mathbf G''$ with $\delta_{\mathbf G''\mathbf G'}$ is redundant; it is simply $\epsilon^{\rm H}_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)=\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}-\Omega^2v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)$. The $\Omega^2$ requires $\chi^0_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q)\equiv\Omega^{-2}\iint_{\rm uc}e^{-i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf r}\chi^0_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')e^{i\mathbf G'\cdot\mathbf r'}$, which is not stated. Likewise the normalization of $f^{xc}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G,\mathbf q+\mathbf G')$ in Eq. (45) is not given. It must reduce to $v^{\rm C}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\delta_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}$ for a translation-invariant kernel. I verified that Eqs. (56), (59), (60) are mutually consistent with this convention.
5. **Fourier convention for $V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)$ in Eq. (43).** The stated convention, $f(\mathbf G)=\Omega^{-1}\int_{\rm uc}$, applies to periodic functions. For the aperiodic $V_\kappa$ the integral must be over all space (with $\Omega^{-1}$), as for $v^{\rm C}$ after Eq. (44). This is implicit, not stated.
6. **Eq. (60):** written as $\epsilon_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}$ without the superscript H, although it is $\epsilon^{\rm H}$ (RPA, $f^{xc}$ neglected). Eq. (61) then uses "$\epsilon$ instead of $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$", i.e. the diagonal Lindhard $\epsilon(q)$ from Eq. (4).
7. **Eq. (35) notation** $\partial V^{\rm KS}/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha}|_{\mathbf r-\mathbf R_p}$ is compact but ambiguous. It means the derivative with respect to displacing atom $\kappa$ of the reference cell ($p=0$), evaluated at $\mathbf r-\mathbf R_p$, which equals $\partial V^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r)/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}$.
8. **Notation for polarization vectors** varies: $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$ (Eqs. 15–20, 34), $\mathbf e_{\kappa,\nu}(\mathbf q)$ (Eqs. 57, 58, 61), $\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}(\mathbf q)$ (Eqs. 2, 180). Same object.
9. **Eq. (41)** mixes vector and component notation: $V^{\rm KS}(\mathbf r;\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}+b)$ means only the $\alpha$ component of atom $\kappa$ in cell $p$ is displaced by $b$.
10. **Text after Eq. (47):** "an additional term $\langle u_{v\mathbf k}|\partial_{\kappa\alpha,0}v|u_{v\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc}u_{v\mathbf k}$ on the right-hand side". This is the first-order eigenvalue shift. With Eq. (47) as written, the full $\mathbf q=0$ equation is $(H-\varepsilon_v)\partial u=-(\partial v-\partial\varepsilon_v)u$, so the added term has a **plus** sign. Not an error, but worth stating.
11. **Eq. (49) factors of 2.** One factor of 2 (spin) is "implicitly included in the sum over $\mathbf k$" and an explicit 2 comes from time reversal. A reader could double count, so the page should state this explicitly.
12. **Unit-system caution (context, Eq. 7, p. 5):** $\epsilon_0$ (vacuum permittivity) and $\epsilon^0$ (static relative permittivity) appear in the same formula. Distinguish them typographically on the page.
13. **Text on p. 13:** "the Thomas-Fermi screening length is given by $k_{\rm TF}=\ldots$". $k_{\rm TF}$ is a wave vector; the screening length is $1/k_{\rm TF}$. Minor wording.
14. **Eq. (51):** for $m=n$ and $q\to0$ in metals, $(f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{n\mathbf k+\mathbf q})/(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\to\partial f/\partial\varepsilon$ (Fermi-surface term). The formula as written needs this limit, plus smearing in practice. Not an error.
