## 1. Overview (plain language)

Section IV of the review built an exact, self-consistent field theory of coupled electrons and nuclei in the harmonic approximation: the **Hedin–Baym equations** (Table I). Their central objects are the electron Green's function $G$, the nuclear displacement–displacement correlation function $D$ (the "phonon propagator"), the electronic polarization $P_{\rm e}$, and the screened Coulomb interaction $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$. That framework is formally complete but cannot be solved for real materials. Section V shows how the standard working formulas of first-principles e–ph calculations follow from it once a short, explicit list of approximations is made.

The strategy is the same for phonons and for electrons. **(1)** Pick a "noninteracting" reference that DFT already delivers. For phonons this is the *adiabatic* (Born–Oppenheimer) system, whose self-energy $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(\omega=0)$ equals the DFT interatomic force constants. For electrons it is the *clamped-nuclei* system, which is what DFT or DFT+GW computes. **(2)** Put everything else into a Dyson equation with a self-energy that carries the e–ph physics.

- For phonons the self-energy is the **nonadiabatic** part $\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)=\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$. It shifts and broadens the phonon lines: the linewidth gives the phonon lifetime, and the real part gives nonadiabatic Kohn-anomaly corrections. It contains one bare and one screened e–ph vertex.
- For electrons the self-energy splits into the **Fan–Migdal** term ("$GW_{\rm ph}$", the dynamical polarization of the lattice), the static **Debye–Waller** term (from the smearing of the crystal potential by zero-point and thermal motion), and a small, never-computed correction to the clamped-nuclei GW self-energy.

With standard approximations ($\Gamma=1$, Kohn–Sham $G$, adiabatic $D$, static RPA+$xc$ screening of the vertex) these become the familiar formulas. They give temperature-dependent band structures (the Allen–Heine theory and the zero-point renormalization of the gap), carrier lifetimes (Fermi golden rule), ARPES kinks and polaron satellites (spectral function), and, beyond Dyson, the cumulant expansion.

Everything here is **electrostatic**: the only interaction is the instantaneous Coulomb $v$, screened by electrons into $W_{\rm e}$, and "retardation" means only the frequency dependence of $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)$. There are no transverse photons. This is the baseline that EPP generalizes.

## 2. Logical flow

**A. Phonons (pp. 20–25)**

1. **Formal solution for $D$** (Eq. 123). Inverting the nuclear equation of motion, Eq. (118), gives $\mathbf D(\omega)=[\mathbf M\omega^2-\Pi(\omega)]^{-1}$. Resonances satisfy $\Omega_\nu(\omega)=\omega$ (Eq. 124), where $\Omega_\nu^2$ are the eigenvalues of the mass-scaled self-energy. Because $\Pi$ is complex and frequency dependent, the roots can be complex and a mode can have several roots.
2. **Partition** $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)$ with $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(\omega=0)$ (Eq. 125). The adiabatic approximation means setting $\omega=0$ in Eq. (121), i.e. $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)\to\epsilon_{\rm e}(0)$.
3. **Show that $\Pi^{\rm A}$ = DFT force constants.** Rewrite Eq. (121) as Eq. (126), using the density-response identity Eq. (127) and the acoustic sum rule Eq. (120). This gives the Hellmann–Feynman / Born–Oppenheimer form, Eq. (128). The only difference from DFPT is that $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)$ is the exact many-body dielectric matrix, not RPA+$xc$.
4. **Properties of $\Pi^{\rm A}$.** It is real and symmetric, from $W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,-\omega)$, so its eigenvalues are real and phonons are infinitely long-lived. This fails in metals, where $\epsilon(\mathbf q,\omega)$ of the electron gas diverges for $\omega,\mathbf q\to0$. In practice the occupations are smeared (de Gironcoli 1995).
5. **Adiabatic propagator** (Eq. 129). In normal modes it is diagonal (Eqs. 130–132), with the mode transformation $S$ (Eq. 131). It is equivalent to the textbook ladder-operator form (Eqs. 133–134).
6. **Beyond adiabatic: Dyson equation** $\mathbf D=\mathbf D^{\rm A}+\mathbf D^{\rm A}\Pi^{\rm NA}\mathbf D$ (Eq. 135; Fig. 1(a)). In the mode basis, Eq. (136). $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ has four effects: (i) its real part shifts frequencies; (ii) its imaginary part broadens lines; (iii) off-diagonal elements couple modes; (iv) frequency dependence can create extra poles, e.g. coupled plasmon–phonon modes.
7. **First-order (diagonal) solution:** complex zeros $\tilde\Omega=\Omega-i\gamma$ (Eqs. 137–139). This gives the Allen/Grimvall formulas $\gamma\simeq-{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})$ and $\Omega\simeq\omega+{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})$, with phonon lifetime $\tau^{\rm ph}=(2\gamma)^{-1}$.
8. **What is $g$ in "$\Pi=|g|^2GG$"?** Expanding the Hamiltonian gives *bare* vertices $g^{\rm b}$ (bare Coulomb). DFT gives *screened* vertices. The lowest-order diagram (Fig. 1(b)) has two bare vertices. To dress one of them, all proper polarization insertions must be summed.
9. **Nonperturbative result.** Write $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ through $W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)$ (Eq. 140). Insert the Dyson equation for $W_{\rm e}$ (Eq. 94). In the mode basis this gives Eq. (141), with **one bare** vertex $g^{\rm b}$ (Eq. 142) and **one screened** vertex $g^{\rm cc}$ (Eqs. 143–144), and with the full irreducible $P_{\rm e}$ (Fig. 1(c), Keating 1968). The dressed vertex satisfies $g=g^{\rm b}+vGG\Gamma g$ (Fig. 1(d)).
10. **Practical formula.** Approximations (i)–(v): $\Gamma=1$, Kohn–Sham $G$, RPA+$xc$ screening, static screened vertex, spin degeneracy. Together they give Eq. (145), whose numerator is $g^{\rm b}g^*$. Most calculations replace $g^{\rm b}\to g$ and keep only the imaginary part. This gives the golden-rule phonon linewidth, Eq. (146).
11. **When it matters.** $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ is large only when occupied and empty states are separated by about $\hbar\omega$, i.e. in metals and small-gap or doped semiconductors. At finite temperature, $f\to$ Fermi–Dirac.

**B. Electrons (pp. 25–32)**

12. **Reference = clamped nuclei.** Setting $D=0$ in Hedin–Baym gives Hedin's equations at clamped nuclei, Eqs. (147)–(150): $G^{\rm cn}$, $V^{\rm cn}_{\rm tot}$, $\langle\hat n^{\rm cn}\rangle$, $\Sigma^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}=i\hbar G^{\rm cn}\Gamma^{\rm cn}W^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}$.
13. **Dyson equation** $G=G^{\rm cn}+G^{\rm cn}\Sigma^{\rm ep}G$ (Eq. 151) with $\Sigma^{\rm ep}=\Sigma^{\rm FM}+\Sigma^{\rm DW}+\Sigma^{\rm dGW}$ (Eqs. 152–155; Fig. 2). This is exact within the harmonic approximation; it is a regrouping of Table I.
14. **Fan–Migdal** $=i\hbar G\Gamma W_{\rm ph}$, the lattice analog of GW. Written with dressed couplings it has the form $g^2DG\Gamma$, with **two screened** vertices (Eq. 156).
15. **Practical Fan–Migdal** (Eq. 157). It uses approximations (i)–(v): $\Gamma=1$, KS $G$, $D\to D^{\rm A}$, RPA+$xc$ vertex, static vertex. Closing the contour in the upper half-plane gives the two-term formula. Its finite-temperature version (Eq. 158) is the retarded self-energy, with Bose and Fermi factors.
16. **Debye–Waller.** Expanding $\hat n$ to second order gives Eq. (159). This assumes uncorrelated electron and displacement operators (Gillis 1970). With $D\to D^{\rm A}$ and $V_{\rm tot}\to V^{\rm KS}$ (approximations vi–vii) one gets Eq. (160); at finite $T$, Eq. (161) multiplies it by $2n+1$.
17. **Quasiparticles.** $G^{-1}=G^{\rm cn,-1}-\Sigma^{\rm ep}$ (Eq. 162). With KS or GW poles, Eq. (163). Brillouin–Wigner quasiparticle equations follow (Eqs. 164–165); Rayleigh–Schrödinger is the limit $E\to\varepsilon$, $\Gamma\to0$.
18. **Band-structure renormalization:** Eq. (166) (BW, finite $T$). With RS and without phonon energies in the denominators this becomes the **adiabatic Allen–Heine formula** (Eq. 167), which gives the zero-point renormalization at $T=0$.
19. **Toy model.** A two-band parabolic semiconductor with a dispersionless phonon gives Eq. (168): $E_g(T)=E_g^{\rm cn}-|\Delta E_g^{\rm ZP}|[1+2n(\hbar\omega_0/k_BT)]$ (Varshni effect; Fig. 3(a)).
20. **Lifetimes.** $\tau=\hbar/(2|\Gamma|)$. In the golden-rule form, Eq. (169) at $T=0$ and Eq. (170) at finite $T$ (emission plus absorption). Debye–Waller does not contribute because it is real. Toy model: $\Gamma(T)=\Gamma^{\rm ZP}[1+2n]$ (Fig. 3(b)).
21. **Beyond perturbation theory: spectral function** (Eqs. 171–172). The quasiparticle approximation gives Lorentzians with weight $Z$ (Eqs. 173–174); the linewidth is renormalized by $Z$. The model of Fig. 4 shows the photoemission kink when $\varepsilon_F\gg\hbar\omega_0$, and the heavy band plus polaron satellite when $\varepsilon_F\sim\hbar\omega_0$.
22. **Model Hamiltonians** (Fröhlich, Holstein, SSH, …) allow nonperturbative polaron physics. **Cumulant expansion:** $G(t)\propto\exp[-i\varepsilon t/\hbar+C(t)]$ with $C$ obtained from $\Sigma^{\rm FM}$. It resums higher-order diagrams and reproduces the polaron satellites seen in ARPES of $n$-doped TiO$_2$ (Verdi, Caruso, Giustino 2017).

## 3. Key equations

### Conventions (state these on the page)

- **Units: SI**, with $\hbar$ explicit. $v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|)$ is the Coulomb **potential energy** between two charges $e$ [context, Eq. (64), p. 14]. $V_{\rm tot}$, $V^{\rm en}$, $W$ are potential energies of an electron. Densities are "in units of the electron charge": $\hat n_{\rm e}$ is the electron number density and $\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)=-\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p})$ [Eq. (67), p. 14]. The physical charge density is $-e\langle\hat n\rangle$ with $\hat n=\hat n_{\rm e}+\hat n_{\rm n}$.
- **Space-time labels** $1\equiv(\mathbf r_1t_1)$ (spin implied), $1^+=(\mathbf r_1,t_1+\eta)$; $\int d(34)\equiv\int d\mathbf r_3dt_3d\mathbf r_4dt_4$ [p. 15–16].
- **Time Fourier transform:** $f(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,f(t)e^{i\omega t}$ [p. 19]. At equilibrium every function depends only on time differences.
- **Green's functions are zero-temperature and time-ordered:** $G(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't')=-(i/\hbar)\langle0|\hat T\hat\psi(\mathbf xt)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x't')|0\rangle$ [Eq. (70)]; $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')=-(i/\hbar)\langle\hat T\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}(t)\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')\rangle$ [Eq. (109)]. Finite $T$ is obtained by Matsubara plus analytic continuation; the results are **retarded** self-energies (Eq. 158). Eq. (145), with $\hbar(\omega+i\eta)$, is already in retarded form.
- **BZ normalization:** $N_p^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf q}\to\Omega_{\rm BZ}^{-1}\int d\mathbf q$; $\int d\mathbf q\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}=\Omega_{\rm BZ}\delta_{p0}$; $\sum_pe^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}=\Omega_{\rm BZ}\delta(\mathbf q)$ [p. 14]. The integrals over $\mathbf k,\mathbf q$ in Eqs. (145)–(170) are therefore **BZ averages**, and the $N_p^{-1/2}$ of Eq. (1)/(37) has been absorbed. $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ is the Eq. (38) matrix element (units of energy, independent of $N_p$).
- **Mode quantities:** $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$, $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$ from the dynamical matrix (Eqs. 14–17); $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(-\mathbf q)=e^*_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$ (Maradudin–Vosko).
- **Occupations** (footnote 3, p. 25): $f_{n\mathbf k}=f[(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_F)/k_BT]$ with $f(x)=1/(e^x+1)$; $n_{\mathbf q\nu}=n(\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}/k_BT)$ with $n(x)=1/(e^x-1)$. Note $1+2n(x)=\coth(x/2)$.
- **Units of self-energies:** $\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$ has units of force constants (energy/length$^2$). In the mode basis, $\Pi_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}$ has units of **frequency** and $\hbar\Pi$ of energy (Eq. 141). $D_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}$ has units of 1/frequency. $\Sigma_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)$ is an **energy** written as a function of the angular frequency $\omega$, which is why $\Sigma(\tilde E/\hbar)$ appears in Eqs. (164)–(165).

### V.A.1 Phonons in the Born–Oppenheimer adiabatic approximation (pp. 21–22)

$$
\mathbf D(\omega)=[\mathbf M\omega^2-\Pi(\omega)]^{-1}\qquad(123)
$$
- $\mathbf M$ is the diagonal matrix of nuclear masses $M_\kappa$, and $\mathbf D$ the matrix of $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)$. This is the formal solution of Eq. (118).

$$
\Omega_\nu(\omega)-\omega=0,\qquad \nu=1,\dots,3M\qquad(124)
$$
- $\Omega^2_\nu(\omega)$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathbf M^{-1/2}\Pi(\omega)\mathbf M^{-1/2}$, parametric in $\omega$. Eq. (124) is the many-body generalization of the dynamical-matrix eigenproblem (Sec. III.A): $\Pi(\omega)$ replaces the force constants $C$. Because $\Pi$ is complex and $\omega$-dependent, roots can leave the real axis, and one "eigenmode" may have several roots.

$$
\Pi(\omega)=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega),\qquad \Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(\omega=0)\qquad(125)
$$
- This is the adiabatic/nonadiabatic split. Setting $\omega=0$ in Eq. (121) is the adiabatic approximation (Keating 1968), i.e. $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)\to\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)$ over the phonon frequency range. $\Pi^{\rm A}$ defines the "noninteracting" (adiabatic) phonons and $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ carries the EPI.
- Historical alternative (Grimvall 1981): use the *bare* force constants of nuclei without electrons as the reference. This is not useful in modern calculations, because those dispersions are very far from the interacting ones (cf. EPP's frozen-core $\omega^0$; see Connections).

$$
\Pi^{\rm A}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\sum_{\kappa''p''}(\delta_{\kappa'p',\kappa''p''}-\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'})\left[\int d\mathbf r\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\partial\tau_{\kappa''\alpha'p''}}\frac{\partial V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}+\frac{\partial^2U_{\rm nn}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa''\alpha'p''}}\right]\qquad(126)
$$
- $U_{\rm nn}$ is the nucleus–nucleus energy [Eq. (65)] and $V^{\rm en}$ the electron–nuclei potential [Eq. (25)]. All derivatives are taken at equilibrium.

$$
\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\partial\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}}=-Z_\kappa\int d\mathbf r'\left[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';0)-\delta(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\right]\nabla'_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\qquad(127)
$$
- This is the static electron-density response to displacing nucleus $\kappa p$, screened by the **exact** static dielectric matrix. It is derived like Eq. (100), with an external potential that moves the nucleus. (See Source issues for the overall sign.)

$$
\Pi^{\rm A}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\int d\mathbf r\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\frac{\partial V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}+\int d\mathbf r\,\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle\frac{\partial^2V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}+\frac{\partial^2U_{\rm nn}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\qquad(128)
$$
- This follows from (126) via the sum rule (120). It is precisely the Born–Oppenheimer/Hellmann–Feynman force-constant formula, cf. Eq. (10) of Baroni *et al.* (2001). It has three terms: the induced-density response, the "static" term with the unperturbed density, and the ion–ion term.
- Difference from DFPT's $C$ of Eq. (13): here the density response is governed by the exact many-body $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)$, and $\langle\hat n_{\rm e}\rangle$ is the exact density, so the force constants are *dressed* by all e–e and e–ph interactions. DFPT uses $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$ (RPA+$xc$) and the clamped-nuclei density. In practice $\Pi^{\rm A}\to$ the DFT force constants, which agree excellently with experiment.
- $\Pi^{\rm A}$ is **real and symmetric**: $W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,-\omega)$ follows from $W(12)=W(21)$. Hence real eigenvalues and infinitely long-lived harmonic phonons.

$$
\mathbf D^{\rm A}(\omega)=[\mathbf M\omega^2-\Pi^{\rm A}]^{-1}\qquad(129)
$$

$$
D^{\rm A}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}S^*_{\mathbf q\nu,\kappa\alpha p}S_{\mathbf q\nu,\kappa'\alpha'p'}\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}}\qquad(130)
$$

$$
S_{\mathbf q\nu,\kappa\alpha p}=e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}(2M_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})^{-1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\qquad(131)
$$
- These follow from identifying $\Pi^{\rm A}$ with $C$ of Eq. (13), inverting Eq. (129) with Eqs. (14)–(17), and using $D^{{\rm dm},*}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\alpha'}(\mathbf q)=D^{\rm dm}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\alpha'}(-\mathbf q)$. $S$ has units $(\text{mass}\times\text{frequency})^{-1/2}$. The $\hbar$ of the zero-point amplitude cancels against the $-i/\hbar$ of Eq. (109).

$$
D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu,\mathbf q'\nu'}(\omega)=\Omega_{\rm BZ}\,\delta(\mathbf q-\mathbf q')D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(\omega),\qquad D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(\omega)=\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}}\,\delta_{\nu\nu'}\qquad(132)
$$
- In the eigenmode representation (inverse transform of Eq. 131) the adiabatic propagator is diagonal: free bosons with poles at $\pm\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$.

$$
D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(tt')=-i\langle\hat T[\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\nu}(t)\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}(t')+\hat a_{-\mathbf q\nu}(t)\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu}(t')]\rangle\delta_{\nu\nu'}\qquad(133)
$$
- This is the ladder-operator form, obtained with Eqs. (20), (109), (131). Its evaluation with the harmonic Hamiltonian (22) gives:

$$
D^{\rm A}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-i\eta}\qquad(134)
$$
- Here $\eta\to0^+$. It is time-ordered: the pole at $+\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ lies just below the real axis and the pole at $-\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ just above. It is the textbook form (Schrieffer 1983; Schäfer and Wegener 2002), but it "does not carry general validity in a field-theoretic framework since it rests on the adiabatic approximation."

### V.A.2 Phonons beyond the adiabatic approximation (pp. 22–23)

$$
\mathbf D(\omega)=\mathbf D^{\rm A}(\omega)+\mathbf D^{\rm A}(\omega)\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)\mathbf D(\omega)\qquad(135)
$$
- This combines Eqs. (123) and (125). The nonadiabatic self-energy "dresses" the adiabatic phonons (Fig. 1(a)).

$$
D^{-1}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\left[\delta_{\nu\nu'}(\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu})-2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(\omega)\right]\qquad(136)
$$
- $\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}$ and $D^{-1}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}$ are obtained with the transform (131) and its inverse. The first term is $(D^{\rm A})^{-1}$; the equation reads $D^{-1}=(D^{\rm A})^{-1}-\Pi^{\rm NA}$.
- The mode-basis self-energy is not printed explicitly. Consistency with (130) and (136) implies $\Pi_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}=\sum S_{\mathbf q\nu,\kappa\alpha p}\,\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}\,S^*_{\mathbf q\nu',\kappa'\alpha'p'}$ per unit cell. In words, $2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\Pi_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}=\mathbf e^\dagger\mathbf M^{-1/2}\Pi\mathbf M^{-1/2}\mathbf e$, a frequency-squared correction. This is my reconstruction; the paper only says "using the transform of Eq. (131) and its inverse".
- **Four effects of $\Pi^{\rm NA}$:** (i) Re of the diagonal shifts the frequencies; (ii) Im broadens them; (iii) off-diagonal elements couple adiabatic modes; (iv) frequency dependence may give multiple poles (new spectral structures).

$$
\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}=\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\tilde\Omega_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(137)
$$
- These are the complex zeros $\tilde\Omega_{\mathbf q\nu}=\Omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-i\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu}$ of $D^{-1}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}$ for nondegenerate modes, keeping only the diagonal elements (first-order perturbation theory in practice).

$$
\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu}=-\frac{\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\Omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\Omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-i\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(138)
$$

$$
\Omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}=\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+\gamma^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\Omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-i\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(139)
$$
- These are the imaginary and real parts of (137), since $\tilde\Omega^2=\Omega^2-\gamma^2-2i\Omega\gamma$. Apart from the $\gamma^2$ term they coincide with Allen (1972b) and Grimvall (1981).
- **Usual simplification:** for $|\Omega-\omega|\ll\omega$ and $|\gamma|\ll\omega$,
$$\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu}\simeq-{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}),\qquad \Omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\simeq\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}+{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad\text{(unnumbered, p. 23)}$$
- **Phonon lifetime:** $\tau^{\rm ph}_{\mathbf q\nu}=(2\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu})^{-1}$ (unnumbered). In the time domain the phonon amplitude decays as $e^{-\gamma t}$, so the occupation (energy) decays as $e^{-2\gamma t}$.

### V.A.3 Phonon self-energy used in *ab initio* calculations (pp. 23–25)

$$
\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\,Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\left[W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)-W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)\right]Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'_{\alpha'}\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'})\qquad(140)
$$
- This combines (119) and (125). Only the frequency-dependent part of the electronically screened interaction survives, because the second line of (119) is static. The physics: the nuclei, through their point charges $Z_\kappa$, interact via the electron-screened Coulomb interaction. The *dynamical* part of the screening is what makes phonons decay and shift.

$$
\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu,\mathbf q'\nu'}(\omega)=\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\,g^{\rm b}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r)P_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)g^{\rm cc}_{\mathbf q'\nu'}(\mathbf r',\omega)-\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\,g^{\rm b}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r)P_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)g^*_{\mathbf q'\nu'}(\mathbf r',0)\qquad(141)
$$
- Obtained by inserting $W_{\rm e}=v+vP_{\rm e}W_{\rm e}$ (Eq. 94) into (140). This produces terms like $vP_{\rm e}W_{\rm e}$, and the gradients of $v$ and $W_{\rm e}$ become couplings. It is **nonperturbative** and relies only on the harmonic approximation. It agrees with the homogeneous electron gas result (Scalapino 1969) and with Keating (1968) (Fig. 1(c)). In symbolic notation $P_{\rm e}=GG\Gamma$, so $\Pi=g^{\rm b}GG\Gamma g$.
- **Key structure:** one vertex is **bare** ($g^{\rm b}$), the other **screened** ($g^{\rm cc}$). The full irreducible polarization $P_{\rm e}$ sits in between, and its adiabatic ($\omega=0$) value is subtracted.

$$
g^{\rm b}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r)=\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)\qquad(142)
$$
- This is the bare coupling function: the variation (as in Eqs. 33–35) of the nuclear potential $V^{\rm en}$ of Eq. (25), in practice the ionic pseudopotentials. In metals it is long ranged.

$$
g_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,\omega)=\int d\mathbf r'\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)g^{\rm b}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r')\qquad(143)
$$

$$
g^{\rm cc}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,\omega)=\int d\mathbf r'\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)g^{{\rm b},*}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r')\qquad(144)
$$
- These are the dressed couplings (Hedin and Lundqvist 1969). They are dynamically screened by the full electronic dielectric matrix, and they are short ranged in metals. Since $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ is real at $\omega=0$: $g^{\rm cc}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,0)=g^*_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,0)$. Diagrammatically, $g=\epsilon^{-1}g^{\rm b}$ with $\epsilon=1-vP$ and $P=GG\Gamma$, so $g=g^{\rm b}+vGG\Gamma g$ (Fig. 1(d)).

**Approximations (i)–(v)** that take (141) to (145):
(i) $\Gamma(123)=\delta(12)\delta(13)$, the GW-type vertex; (ii) $G\to$ Kohn–Sham $G$ at equilibrium positions; (iii) $\epsilon_{\rm e}\to$ RPA+$xc$ from DFT (Sec. III.B.2); (iv) **frequency dependence of the screened coupling neglected**, $g^{\rm cc}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,\omega)\simeq g^{\rm cc}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,0)=g^*_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,0)$, "ubiquitous in the literature but never mentioned explicitly"; (v) spin degeneracy and time-reversal symmetry.

$$
\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(\omega)=2\sum_{mn}\int\frac{d\mathbf k}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,g^{\rm b}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,g^*_{mn\nu'}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\left[\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-f_{n\mathbf k}}{\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar(\omega+i\eta)}-\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-f_{n\mathbf k}}{\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}}\right]\qquad(145)
$$
- This is the $\mathbf q=\mathbf q'$ component; components with $\mathbf q\neq\mathbf q'$ vanish by lattice periodicity. The sums run over all KS states; the factor 2 is spin. $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ is Eq. (38), the screened DFPT matrix element. $g^{\rm b}_{mn\nu}$ is the same with $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}$ replaced by the variation of the bare ionic (pseudo)potential.
- It agrees with Calandra, Profeta and Mauri (2010), who derived it from TDDFPT. Extension to finite $T$: replace $f$ by Fermi–Dirac (Baym 1961; Gillis 1970).
- The bracket is $\chi^0(\omega)-\chi^0(0)$ (see Concepts): only the *dynamical* part of the bare polarizability enters, because the static part is already in $\Pi^{\rm A}$ (DFT phonons). **This is the double-counting subtraction.**

$$
\frac{1}{\tau^{\rm ph}_{\mathbf q\nu}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\,2\sum_{mn}\int\frac{d\mathbf k}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2(f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\,\delta(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})\qquad(146)
$$
- This is the standard phonon linewidth (Allen 1972b; Grimvall 1981). It follows from (145) by taking Im with $1/(x-i0^+)\to i\pi\delta(x)$, using $\tau=(2\gamma)^{-1}$ and $\gamma=-{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}$, **and** replacing $g^{\rm b}\to g$. It can also be derived from the Fermi golden rule in an independent-particle picture (Albers *et al.* 1976). Physically, a phonon is absorbed by creating an electron–hole pair at energy $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$.
- The author notes that the replacement $|g|^2\to g^{\rm b}g^*$ "will be important to assess" in the future. See the caveat in Source issues: within RPA for the electron gas, approximation (iv) combined with a bare vertex is not innocuous.

### V.B.1 Electron self-energy: Fan–Migdal and Debye–Waller (pp. 26–28)

Clamped-nuclei problem (Hedin's equations with $D=0$, so $\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle\to n^0_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)$):

$$
\left[i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t_1}+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2(1)-V^{\rm cn}_{\rm tot}(1)\right]G^{\rm cn}(12)-\int d3\,\Sigma^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}(13)G^{\rm cn}(32)=\delta(12)\qquad(147)
$$

$$
V^{\rm cn}_{\rm tot}(1)=\int d2\,v(1,2)\langle\hat n^{\rm cn}(2)\rangle\qquad(148)
$$

$$
\langle\hat n^{\rm cn}(1)\rangle=-i\hbar\sum_{\sigma_1}G^{\rm cn}(11^+)+n^0_{\rm n}(\mathbf r_1)\qquad(149)
$$

$$
\Sigma^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}(12)=i\hbar\int d(34)\,G^{\rm cn}(13)\Gamma^{\rm cn}(324)W^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}(41^+)\qquad(150)
$$
- $\Gamma^{\rm cn}$ and $W^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}$ come from the Hedin–Baym equations at clamped nuclei. These are exactly Hedin's (1965) equations, i.e. the GW world (reviews: Hedin and Lundqvist 1969; Hybertsen and Louie 1986; Aryasetiawan and Gunnarsson 1998; Onida, Reining and Rubio 2002).

$$
G(12)=G^{\rm cn}(12)+\int d(34)\,G^{\rm cn}(13)\Sigma^{\rm ep}(34)G(42)\qquad(151)
$$

$$
\Sigma^{\rm ep}=\Sigma^{\rm FM}+\Sigma^{\rm DW}+\Sigma^{\rm dGW}\qquad(152)
$$

$$
\Sigma^{\rm FM}(12)=i\hbar\int d(34)\,G(13)\Gamma(324)W_{\rm ph}(41^+)\qquad(153)
$$

$$
\Sigma^{\rm DW}(12)=\int d3\,v(13)\left[\langle\hat n(3)\rangle-\langle\hat n^{\rm cn}(3)\rangle\right]\delta(12)\qquad(154)
$$

$$
\Sigma^{\rm dGW}(12)=\Sigma_{\rm e}(12)-\Sigma^{\rm cn}_{\rm e}(12)\qquad(155)
$$
- Eqs. (147)–(155) are an **exact** reformulation of Table I (harmonic approximation). Check: $G^{-1}=G^{{\rm cn},-1}-(V_{\rm tot}-V^{\rm cn}_{\rm tot})-(\Sigma_{\rm e}-\Sigma^{\rm cn}_{\rm e})-i\hbar G\Gamma W_{\rm ph}$.
- **Fan–Migdal (FM)** = $GW_{\rm ph}$. In the same way that GW's correlation part describes the dynamical *electronic* polarization caused by an added electron or hole, $GW_{\rm ph}$ describes the dynamical polarization of the *lattice*. With $\Gamma=\delta\delta$ it is called the Fan self-energy in the semiconductor community (Fan 1951; Allen and Heine 1976; Allen and Cardona 1981; Cardona 2001) and the Migdal self-energy in the metals and superconductivity community (Migdal 1958; Engelsberg and Schrieffer 1963; Scalapino 1969; Schrieffer 1983).
- **Debye–Waller (DW):** static. It is the difference between the self-consistent potential of the fully interacting system and that at clamped nuclei, a time-independent correction to the crystal potential from the "fuzziness" of the nuclear charge. It is the analog of the DW factor in x-ray and neutron diffraction (Antončík 1955; Walter *et al.* 1970; Cardona and Thewalt 2005). Hedin and Lundqvist (1969) omitted it; Allen and Heine (1976) and Allen (1978) included it.
- **dGW:** the change in the electronic GW self-energy because $G$, the density and $W_{\rm e}$ are modified by the EPI. It is a fraction of the $GW\Gamma$ correction at clamped nuclei. It has never been investigated.

$$
\Sigma^{\rm FM}(12)=i\sum_{\nu\nu'}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,d(34)\,e^{-i\omega(t_4-t_1^+)}\,G(13)\Gamma(324)\,g^{\rm cc}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r_4,\omega)\,D_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}(\omega)\,g_{\mathbf q\nu'}(\mathbf r_1,\omega)\qquad(156)
$$
- Obtained from (110), (130), (131), (144). Symbolically $\Sigma=g^2DG\Gamma$ (Fig. 2(c)). **Both vertices are screened** ($g$ and $g^{\rm cc}$), because $W_{\rm ph}=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\nabla V)D(\nabla V)\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$. Compare the phonon self-energy (141), which has one bare and one screened vertex.

**Approximations (i)–(v) for FM:** (i) $\Gamma(123)=\delta(13)\delta(23)$; (ii) $G\to$ KS $G$ at clamped nuclei; (iii) $D\to D^{\rm A}$ (Eq. 134); (iv) screened vertex from RPA+$xc$ DFT; (v) $g_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,\omega)\simeq g_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,0)$.

$$
\Sigma^{\rm FM}_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{m\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,g^*_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,g_{mn'\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\left[\frac{1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\omega-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}/\hbar-\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}+i\eta}+\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\omega-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}/\hbar+\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-i\eta}\right]\qquad(157)
$$
- These are the $\mathbf k=\mathbf k'$ matrix elements in the KS basis; $\mathbf k\neq\mathbf k'$ vanish by periodicity. $f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=1$ for occupied, 0 otherwise ($T=0$). Spin is omitted since FM is spin-diagonal. The formula is obtained by closing the $\omega$ contour in the upper half-plane (because of $t_1^+$). The infinitesimals of the electron and phonon propagators are crucial (Schrieffer 1983).
- First term: the intermediate state is an empty electron state plus one emitted phonon. Second term: a filled state (hole) plus a phonon.

$$
\left[\frac{1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\cdots+i\eta}+\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\cdots-i\eta}\right]\to\left[\frac{1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\cdots+i\eta}+\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\cdots+i\eta}\right]\qquad(158)
$$
- This is the finite-$T$ form (Matsubara, then continuation through the upper half-plane; Mahan 1993, Sec. 3.5). Now $f$ and $n$ are Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein. The sign change of the second $i\eta$ means this is the **retarded** self-energy, with all poles below the real axis (Abrikosov, Gor'kov and Dzyaloshinski 1975). "$1-f+n$" counts phonon emission (stimulated plus spontaneous) and "$f+n$" counts absorption.

$$
\Sigma^{\rm DW}(12)=\delta(12)\,\frac{i\hbar}{2}\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}\frac{\partial^2V_{\rm tot}(1)}{\partial\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau^0_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\,D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t_1^+,t_1)\qquad(159)
$$
- This expands $\hat n(3)$ to second order in the displacements using (79) and (109), with $\langle\Delta\hat\tau\Delta\hat\tau'\rangle=i\hbar D(t^+,t)$. It requires the extra approximation that electronic field operators and displacements are uncorrelated, $\langle\hat n\Delta\hat\tau\rangle=\langle\hat n\rangle\langle\Delta\hat\tau\rangle$ (Gillis 1970). This is satisfied automatically by adiabatic phonons. Diagram: Fig. 2(d), a phonon line that starts and ends at the same time. It involves the variation of the *screened* $V_{\rm tot}$, and agrees with the perturbative derivation of Marini, Poncé and Gonze (2015).

**Further approximations:** (vi) $D\to D^{\rm A}$; (vii) $V_{\rm tot}\to V^{\rm KS}$ at clamped nuclei. The local, frequency-independent $xc$ potential can be moved between $V_{\rm tot}$ and $\Sigma$ without changing the theory (Keating 1968).

$$
\Sigma^{\rm DW}_{nn'\mathbf k}=\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,g^{\rm DW}_{nn'\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)\qquad(160)
$$
- $g^{\rm DW}$ is from Eq. (40): $g^{\rm DW}_{mn\nu\nu'}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\mathbf q')=\tfrac12\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q+\mathbf q'}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}\Delta_{\mathbf q'\nu'}v^{\rm KS}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc}$. Only $\nu=\nu'$ survives (Kronecker delta in Eq. 132), and only $\mathbf q'=-\mathbf q$. The frequency integral is done with (134), closing in the lower half-plane. Valid at $T=0$; spin-diagonal.

$$
g^{\rm DW}_{nn'\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)\to g^{\rm DW}_{nn'\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)\,(2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)\qquad(161)
$$
- The finite-$T$ form is immediate: DW involves no electron $G$, so one only needs the canonical average of (133) at equal times, $\langle(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger)^2\rangle=2n+1$.
- DW is usually ignored for metals and superconductors but is well known in semiconductor band-gap theory. The justification for metals is that it is frequency independent and therefore a slowly varying function over each Fermi-surface sheet. A first-principles analysis "is currently lacking".

### V.B.3 Temperature dependence of band structures (pp. 28–30)

$$
G^{-1}_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)=G^{{\rm cn},-1}_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)-\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)\qquad(162)
$$

$$
G^{-1}_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)=(\hbar\omega-\tilde\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k})\delta_{nn'}-\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega),\qquad \tilde\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}\pm i\hbar\eta\qquad(163)
$$
- $G^{\rm cn}$ has simple poles at the KS or GW energies $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$ (Hedin and Lundqvist 1969). The upper sign is for occupied states and the lower for unoccupied (time-ordered). Spin is omitted.

$$
E_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+{\rm Re}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{n\mathbf k}(\tilde E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar)\qquad(164)
$$

$$
\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}={\rm Im}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{n\mathbf k}(\tilde E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar)\qquad(165)
$$
- These assume a small pole shift $\varepsilon\to\tilde E=E+i\Gamma$, nondegenerate states, and neglect of off-diagonal $\Sigma_{nn'}$ (otherwise diagonalize, or treat the off-diagonal terms perturbatively). They must be solved self-consistently: this is **Brillouin–Wigner (BW)** perturbation theory to second order in the coupling (Mahan 1993). **Rayleigh–Schrödinger (RS)** follows from $E\to\varepsilon$, $\Gamma\to0$ inside $\Sigma$.

$$
E_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\sum_m|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2\,{\rm Re}\left[\frac{1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu}}{E_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}+i\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}}+\frac{f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu}}{E_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}+i\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}}\right]+\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,g^{\rm DW}_{nn\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)(2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)\qquad(166)
$$
- This combines (157)+(158), (160)+(161) and (164). It is the temperature-dependent "band structure renormalization" from the EPI. It rests on approximations (i)–(vii) and on the harmonic approximation.

$$
E_{n\mathbf k}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_\nu\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\left[\sum_m\frac{|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}+g^{\rm DW}_{nn\nu\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,-\mathbf q)\right](2n_{\mathbf q\nu}+1)\qquad(167)
$$
- This is the **adiabatic Allen–Heine formula** (Allen and Heine 1976). It follows from (166) by two more approximations: RS instead of BW, and neglect of $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ in the denominators. Then the occupations cancel, since $(1-f+n)+(f+n)=1+2n$.
- **Zero-point renormalization:** $\Delta E^{\rm ZP}_{n\mathbf k}=E_{n\mathbf k}(T=0)-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$, the effect of zero-point motion on energies computed at clamped nuclei ($n_{\mathbf q\nu}=0$).
- The same result follows from second-order RS perturbation theory in Fock space starting from Eq. (1) (Kittel 1963; Chakraborty and Allen 1978). Historically Allen–Heine expanded energies in displacements within the adiabatic approximation and then took a Bose–Einstein canonical average. It is "reassuring" that the field theory reproduces this. First-principles uses: Marini (2008); Giustino, Louie and Cohen (2010); review in Sec. IX.A.1.

**Toy model (p. 29–30):** a semiconductor with parabolic, nondegenerate valence and conduction bands, extrema coupled to all states by one dispersionless phonon $\omega_0$, and $g^{\rm DW}\ll$ FM. The dominant denominators are $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\simeq\pm\hbar^2|\mathbf q|^2/2m^*_n$ (upper sign for valence, lower for conduction; intraband $m=n$). The valence maximum is pushed up and the conduction minimum down:

$$
E_g(T)=E_g^{\rm cn}-|\Delta E_g^{\rm ZP}|\left[1+2n(\hbar\omega_0/k_BT)\right]\qquad(168)
$$
- $E_g^{\rm cn}$ is the clamped-nuclei gap and $|\Delta E^{\rm ZP}_g|$ the zero-point correction. The minus sign comes from the curvatures of the band extrema. This is the **Varshni effect** (Varshni 1967): first measured by Becker and Fan (1949), which motivated Fan (1951). Exceptions: copper halides (Göbel *et al.* 1998) and lead-halide perovskites (D'Innocenzo *et al.* 2014) show an "inverse Varshni" blueshift; some chalcopyrites are nonmonotonic (Bhosale *et al.* 2012). The low-$T$ subtleties (Allen and Nery 2017) are not captured.
- Limits: at $T\to0$, $E_g=E_g^{\rm cn}-|\Delta E^{\rm ZP}_g|$ (flat, exponentially activated). For $k_BT\gg\hbar\omega_0$, $1+2n\simeq2k_BT/\hbar\omega_0+\hbar\omega_0/(6k_BT)$, so $E_g$ is linear in $T$. The high-$T$ asymptote, extrapolated to $T=0$, intercepts $E_g^{\rm cn}$ (Fig. 3(a)). This is how the ZPR is extracted from experiment.

### V.B.4 Carrier lifetimes (p. 30)

- After Fourier transforming $G_{nn'\mathbf k}(\omega)$ of Eq. (163), an electron or hole added at time $t$ in $|n\mathbf k\rangle$ has a persistence amplitude that decays as $\exp[\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}(t'-t)/\hbar]$. From (157) and (165), $\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}<0$ for an added electron and $>0$ for a hole (time-ordered convention). The **lifetime** is
$$\tau_{n\mathbf k}=\hbar/(2|\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}|)\qquad\text{(unnumbered)}.$$

$$
\frac{1}{\tau_{n\mathbf k}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{m\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2\left|(1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\right|\qquad(169)
$$
- This takes $\tilde E\to\varepsilon$ in (157) and the absolute value of the imaginary part, with ${\rm Im}\,1/(x\pm i0^+)=\mp\pi\delta(x)$ ($T=0$).

$$
\frac{1}{\tau_{n\mathbf k}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{m\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2\Big[(1-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})+(f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+n_{\mathbf q\nu})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}+\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})\Big]\qquad(170)
$$
- This is the finite-$T$ version from (158). The sign change in the third line comes from the continuation to the retarded self-energy. It coincides with the Fermi golden rule (Grimvall 1981). The two lines are **phonon emission** and **phonon absorption**. DW does not contribute, because its diagonal elements are real (Lautenschlager, Allen and Cardona 1986). First *ab initio* uses: Eiguren *et al.* (2002, 2003).
- **Toy model:** neglecting $\hbar\omega$ in the deltas gives $\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}(T)=\Gamma^{\rm ZP}_{n\mathbf k}[1+2n(\hbar\omega_0/k_BT)]$ (unnumbered). Hence $\tau\propto1/[1+2n]$ (Fig. 3(b)). Experimental trends: Lautenschlager *et al.* (1987).

### V.B.5 Kinks and satellites (pp. 31–32)

BW (164)–(165) is insufficient when electronic energy scales are comparable to phonon energies: metals (including superconductors), narrow-gap semiconductors and doped semiconductors. One then needs the full Dyson equation (163) and the spectral function.

$$
A(\mathbf k,\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_n{\rm Im}\,G^{\rm ret}_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)\qquad(171)
$$
- In the retarded $G$, all poles in the upper half-plane are replaced by their complex conjugates. $A$ is positive definite. It is a momentum-resolved many-body density of states, the quantity probed by ARPES (Damascelli, Hussain and Shen 2003).

$$
A(\mathbf k,\omega)=\sum_n\frac{-(1/\pi)\,{\rm Im}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)}{[\hbar\omega-\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}-{\rm Re}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)]^2+[{\rm Im}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)]^2}\qquad(172)
$$
- This uses (163); one must use the **retarded** self-energy, i.e. (158) for FM, while the static DW is unchanged. Units: 1/energy.

$$
A(\mathbf k,\omega)=\sum_n Z_{n\mathbf k}\frac{-(1/\pi)Z_{n\mathbf k}\,{\rm Im}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn\mathbf k}(E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar)}{[\hbar\omega-E_{n\mathbf k}]^2+[Z_{n\mathbf k}\,{\rm Im}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn\mathbf k}(E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar)]^2}\qquad(173)
$$

$$
Z_{n\mathbf k}=\left[1-\hbar^{-1}\,\partial{\rm Re}\,\Sigma^{\rm ep}_{nn\mathbf k}(\omega)/\partial\omega\big|_{\omega=E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar}\right]^{-1}\qquad(174)
$$
- The quasiparticle approximation linearizes (172) around each $E_{n\mathbf k}$. The result is a sum of Lorentzians of weight $Z_{n\mathbf k}$ and half-width $Z_{n\mathbf k}|{\rm Im}\,\Sigma(E_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar)|$. $Z$ is the "quasiparticle strength", the same as in GW (Hedin and Lundqvist 1969).
- Consequence (p. 31): in a rigorous field-theoretic approach, the broadening and lifetime of Eqs. (169)–(170) should be renormalized by $Z_{n\mathbf k}$ (broadening $\times Z$, lifetime $\times Z^{-1}$) and evaluated at $E_{n\mathbf k}$ instead of $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$. This also follows from Taylor-expanding $\Sigma$ along the imaginary axis with the Cauchy–Riemann conditions.

### V.B.6 Model Hamiltonians, polarons, cumulant (p. 32)

- **Model EPI Hamiltonians** are derived from Eq. (1) with *a priori* bands, frequencies and couplings: Fröhlich (1954), Holstein (1959), SSH (1979), Hubbard–Holstein, Peierls–Hubbard, $t$-$J$ Holstein, SSH–Holstein. They typically use tight binding, an Einstein phonon and coupling linear in the displacements. Nonperturbative tools: Lang–Firsov canonical transformations, path integrals, exact diagonalization, variational and QMC methods (Alexandrov 2008; Alexandrov and Devreese 2010). Topics: weak versus strong coupling polarons, large versus small polarons, band-like versus hopping transport.
- *Ab initio* limitation: self-energies are evaluated with bare propagators, as in (157), so diagrams beyond Migdal are omitted (Migdal 1958).
- **Cumulant expansion** (Hedin and Lundqvist 1969; Langreth 1970; Aryasetiawan, Hedin and Karlsson 1996): instead of a Dyson equation, the time evolution is written as
$$G_{nn\mathbf k}(t)=(i/\hbar)\exp\left[-i(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}/\hbar)t+C_{nn\mathbf k}(t)\right]\qquad\text{(unnumbered)}.$$
The cumulant $C$ comes from a low-order self-energy (e.g. FM, Eq. 157). The exponential automatically generates higher-order diagrams (Mahan 1993). Reviews: Zhou *et al.* (2015), Gumhalter *et al.* (2016). It is closely related to the momentum-average approximation for the Holstein polaron (Berciu 2006).
- Successes: e–e valence satellites (Kheifets 2003; Guzzo 2011–2014; Lischner 2013; Kas, Rehr and Reining 2014; Caruso and Giustino 2015). For EPI: elemental metals (Story *et al.* 2014); ARPES of $n$-doped TiO$_2$ (Verdi, Caruso and Giustino 2017), reproducing the polaron satellites of Moser *et al.* (2013). *Ab initio* polarons: "yet to begin", but Verdi *et al.* (2017) computed a first Fröhlich polaron spectral function.

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Two references, two Dyson equations.** The section does not perturb around bare particles. It uses the best DFT objects as the zeroth order: adiabatic DFPT phonons ($D^{\rm A}$) and clamped-nuclei KS/GW electrons ($G^{\rm cn}$). The self-energies then contain *only what DFT misses*: $\Pi^{\rm NA}=\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$ for phonons, and $\Sigma^{\rm FM}+\Sigma^{\rm DW}+\Sigma^{\rm dGW}$ for electrons. This avoids double counting by construction, and it is why the subtraction of the static term in (140)/(145) is essential.

**(b) Adiabatic phonons = static screening.** In Born–Oppenheimer the electrons follow the nuclei instantaneously, so the nucleus–nucleus interaction is screened by $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega=0)$. This is valid when $\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}$ (10–200 meV) is much smaller than the electronic excitation energies. Insulators with eV gaps are safe. Metals are not, strictly: the Fermi surface allows electron–hole excitations at arbitrarily low energy, and $\epsilon(\mathbf q\to0,\omega\to0)$ of the electron gas is singular (the $q\to0$ and $\omega\to0$ limits do not commute). In practice: smearing.

**(c) What $\chi^0(\omega)-\chi^0(0)$ does.** For an intraband channel in a metal, consider two regimes of $\omega$ relative to $qv_F$.
- *Adiabatic regime* ($\omega\ll qv_F$, phonon inside the particle–hole continuum): $\chi^0(q,\omega)\approx\chi^0(q,0)+i\,{\rm Im}\chi^0$. The real shift is about zero and the linewidth is finite (Landau damping): $\gamma\propto\omega/(qv_F)$.
- *Dynamical regime* ($\omega\gg qv_F$, small $q$, phonon above the continuum): $\chi^0(q,\omega)\to0$, so $\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}\to-|g|^2\chi^0(q,0)\Omega=+|g|^2N_F\Omega>0$. There is no damping, but the phonon **hardens**: the electrons cannot follow, so the static softening (Kohn anomaly) built into DFT is undone.

This is the physics of nonadiabatic Kohn anomalies, e.g. graphene $E_{2g}$ and MgB$_2$, in the calculations cited on p. 25 (Lazzeri and Mauri 2006; Caudal 2007; Piscanec 2007; Saitta 2008; Calandra, Profeta and Mauri 2010). Maksimov and Shulga (1996) gave a model analysis. $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ is large only if occupied–empty gaps are about $\hbar\omega$, i.e. in metals and small-gap or doped semiconductors.

**(d) Bare versus screened vertex.** Expanding the Hamiltonian gives the bare $g^{\rm b}$, built from the bare Coulomb nuclear potential. In metals it is long ranged ($\propto1/q$ for a point ion). DFT gives the screened $g$, which is short ranged. The phonon self-energy must contain **exactly one** of each, $\Pi=g^{\rm b}P_{\rm e}g$, because one screening chain is already summed in $g$ and repeating it would double count. The electron self-energy contains **two screened** ones, since $W_{\rm ph}=\epsilon^{-1}g^{\rm b}Dg^{\rm b}\epsilon^{-1}$. Simple counting: in the phonon self-energy the phonon line is external, and the polarization bubble with proper insertions screens only one end. In $W_{\rm ph}$ both ends of the internal phonon line attach to an electron line, and each is screened.
- In the electron gas with RPA, the exact relation is ${\rm Im}[g^{\rm b}P(\omega)g(\omega)]=|g^{\rm b}|^2{\rm Im}P/|\epsilon(\omega)|^2=|g(\omega)|^2{\rm Im}P$. So the common practice "$|g|^2$" (Eq. 146) gives the correct linewidth when $\epsilon(\omega_{\rm ph})\approx\epsilon(0)$. In contrast, $g^{\rm b}g^*(0)$ with a *static* screened vertex overestimates it by $\epsilon(q,0)$ (see Source issues). The real lesson: the frequency dependence of the screened vertex (approximation iv) and the bare/screened bookkeeping must be treated consistently.

**(e) Phonon lifetimes.** $\tau^{\rm ph}=1/(2\gamma)$. Eq. (146) is the e–ph-limited phonon linewidth, which can be compared with Raman or neutron linewidths. For metals it relates to the Eliashberg function through Allen's formula, $\gamma_{\mathbf q\nu}\propto N_F\,\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\lambda_{\mathbf q\nu}$. This is outside the range (Sec. XI), and the prefactor depends on whether $\gamma$ means the half or the full width. Typical linewidths are 0.1–10 meV (outside the text; order of magnitude only).

**(f) Coupled plasmon–phonon modes** (p. 23). When $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$ has dynamical structure near phonon frequencies, as for a plasmon in a degenerately doped polar semiconductor, $\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)$ can produce extra poles: coupled plasmon–LO-phonon modes (Varga 1965; Mooradian and Wright 1966, Raman on GaAs; Richter 1984). These are "the electronic analog of photon polaritons" (Yu and Cardona 2010). The author suggests computing them from (136). This is precisely the territory EPP addresses with full electrodynamics: phonon–plasmon–photon mixing.

**(g) Fan–Migdal as "$GW_{\rm ph}$".** An added electron polarizes the lattice (it displaces the ions around it), and that polarization acts back on the electron. Formally this is the same as GW with $W_{\rm e}$ replaced by the phonon-mediated $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$. It is a retarded, attractive interaction that acts at frequencies $\lesssim\omega_{\rm ph}$. The "lattice polarization" picture is literal in polar crystals (Fröhlich polaron).

**(h) Debye–Waller as "fuzzy crystal potential".** Each ion is smeared by its zero-point and thermal motion ($\langle u^2\rangle\propto(2n+1)\hbar/2M\omega$). The electron sees the thermally averaged potential, which gives a static energy shift with no lifetime. In the AH formula, FM (second order in $g$) and DW (first order in $g^{\rm DW}$) are of the same order in the displacement, $\langle u^2\rangle$. They cancel substantially, and the acoustic sum rule ties them together (rigid-ion approximation; Sec. IX.A.1).

**(i) Why gaps shrink.** In (167) the dominant intraband terms have denominators $\pm\hbar^2q^2/2m^*$. Level repulsion from states just above the conduction-band minimum (which lie higher) pushes the CBM down. States just below the valence-band maximum push the VBM up. Hence the gap closes, the ZPR is negative, and $|\Delta E_g|$ grows as $2n+1$. An "inverse Varshni" sign requires other channels to dominate, e.g. interband terms with reversed ordering, or lattice expansion in halide perovskites (outside the text).

**(j) Orders of magnitude (from the text, Fig. 3).** Representative parameters for "common semiconductors": $E^{\rm cn}_g=1$ eV, $\Delta E^{\rm ZP}_g=100$ meV, $\hbar\omega_0=100$ meV, $\Gamma^{\rm ZP}=50$ meV. With $\hbar=658.2$ meV fs, $\Gamma=50$ meV gives $\tau=\hbar/2\Gamma=6.6$ fs. Electron energy uncertainty at the 10–100 meV scale means fs lifetimes. Literature values outside the range, for orientation only: indirect-gap ZPR in Si is about $-60$ meV and in diamond a few hundred meV (Cardona and Thewalt 2005).

**(k) Brillouin–Wigner versus Rayleigh–Schrödinger versus Dyson.** RS evaluates $\Sigma$ at the bare energy (one-shot). BW solves $E=\varepsilon+{\rm Re}\Sigma(E)$ self-consistently and can have multiple roots (the S-shaped kink in Fig. 4(a)). The full Dyson/spectral function captures incoherent weight and satellites that no single-pole picture can (Fig. 4(b)). The cumulant goes further and reproduces replica satellites with the correct spacing.

**(l) Kinks.** For $\varepsilon_F\gg\hbar\omega_0$ (metal), within $\hbar\omega_0$ of $E_F$ the quasiparticle velocity is reduced by $1/(1+\lambda)$ (mass enhancement; $Z\approx1/(1+\lambda)$). Beyond $\hbar\omega_0$ the band returns to the bare dispersion but is broadened (phonon-emission channel open, ${\rm Im}\Sigma\neq0$). This is the "photoemission kink" (Valla *et al.* 1999). First-principles kinks: Eiguren *et al.* (2003); Giustino, Cohen and Louie (2008); Sec. VIII.

**(m) Polaron satellites.** For $\varepsilon_F\lesssim\hbar\omega_0$ (lightly doped semiconductor) one finds a heavier quasiparticle band and an incoherent satellite about $\hbar\omega_0$ below it, where an electron is removed together with phonon excitations. This is seen in ARPES of doped oxides (Moser 2013; Chen 2015; Cancellieri 2016; Wang 2016). BW cannot describe it; the spectral function carries qualitatively new information.

### V1. Nonadiabatic phonon renormalization and Landau damping in a doped 2D semiconductor (Eqs. 135–139, 145; Concepts c)

- **Physics:** one optical phonon $\omega_0$ coupled with a constant matrix element to a single parabolic 2D conduction band (intraband channel), with the adiabatic part already in $\omega_0$. Plot $\Omega(q)$, $\gamma(q)$ and the phonon spectral function.
- **Controls:** carrier density $n$ ($10^{12}$–$10^{14}$ cm$^{-2}$, log slider, default $10^{13}$); $m^*/m_{\rm e}$ (0.1–1, default 0.5); $\hbar\omega_0$ (10–200 meV, default 50); coupling $G\equiv|g|^2A_{\rm cell}N_0$ (0–15 meV, default 5); broadening $\eta$ (0.5–5 meV, default 1); toggle "first-order (Eq. 139 with $\gamma^2$ dropped)" vs "self-consistent (Eq. 137)".
- **Derived scales** (spin 2, valley 1; with valley degeneracy $g_v$ use $k_F=\sqrt{4\pi n/(2g_v)}$, which changes nothing else in reduced units): $k_F=\sqrt{2\pi n}$, $\varepsilon_F=(\hbar^2/2m_{\rm e})k_F^2/(m^*/m_{\rm e})$, $v_F=2\varepsilon_F/(\hbar k_F)$. Example: $n=10^{13}$ cm$^{-2}$ $=10^{-3}$ Å$^{-2}$ gives $k_F=0.0793$ Å$^{-1}$ and $\varepsilon_F=47.9$ meV for $m^*=0.5$.

- **2D Lindhard function** (normalized by $N_0=m^*/\pi\hbar^2$; retarded, $T=0$; I verified it against direct numerical integration):
$$\tilde\chi(Q,W)=-\left[1+\frac{s(a)+s(b)}{2Q^2}\right],\quad a=W-Q^2,\ b=-W-Q^2,\quad s(z)=z\sqrt{1-4Q^2/z^2}\ \text{(principal sqrt)}.$$
Static: $\tilde\chi(Q,0)=-1$ for $Q\le2$, and $-(1-\sqrt{1-4/Q^2})$ for $Q>2$. Checks: inside the continuum, for small $W$, ${\rm Im}\tilde\chi\approx-{\rm Re}W/(Q\sqrt{4-Q^2})$; for large $W$, $\tilde\chi\to2Q^2/W^2$ (f-sum rule).
- **Eq. (145) for this model** (with $g^{\rm b}g^*\to|g|^2$): the bracket times $2\int d\mathbf k/\Omega_{\rm BZ}$ equals $A_{\rm cell}[\chi^0(q,\omega)-\chi^0(q,0)]$, so
$$\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}(Q,\omega)=G\,[\tilde\chi(Q,W)-\tilde\chi(Q,0)].$$
- **Renormalized mode** (Eqs. 138–139, first order): $\Omega=\sqrt{\omega_0^2+2\omega_0{\rm Re}\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega_0)}$ and $\gamma=-(\omega_0/\Omega){\rm Im}\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega_0)$. Self-consistent option: iterate $\tilde\Omega\leftarrow\sqrt{\omega_0^2+2\omega_0\Pi^{\rm NA}(\tilde\Omega)}$ on the real-axis value $\Pi^{\rm NA}({\rm Re}\tilde\Omega)$, 10–20 iterations. Also show $\tau^{\rm ph}=1/(2\gamma)$ in ps.
- **Phonon spectral function heatmap** (from Eq. 136): $B(Q,\omega)=-\frac1\pi{\rm Im}\frac{2\omega_0}{\omega^2-\omega_0^2-2\omega_0\Pi^{\rm NA}(Q,\omega)}$ on a grid with $\hbar\omega\in[0,2\hbar\omega_0]$ and $Q\in[0,3]$. In energy units: $\hbar^2\omega^2-\hbar^2\omega_0^2-2\hbar\omega_0\,\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}$.
- **Overlay** the particle–hole continuum boundaries (2D, $T=0$): $\hbar\omega_\pm(q)=\varepsilon_F(Q^2\pm2Q)$, lower edge $\max(0,\cdot)$. Also overlay the adiabatic line $\hbar\omega_0$.
- **What to notice:**
  - (1) At small $Q$, where $\hbar\omega_0>\varepsilon_F(2Q+Q^2)$, the phonon lies above the continuum. It is undamped but **hardened** by about $G$: $\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}\to+G$, the nonadiabatic stiffening.
  - (2) Where the phonon enters the continuum, it acquires a Landau-damping width $\hbar\gamma\approx G\,\hbar\omega_0/(\varepsilon_FQ\sqrt{4-Q^2})$ and its frequency returns to the adiabatic value.
  - (3) For $Q>2$ the electrons decouple and $\Pi^{\rm NA}\to0$.
  - (4) Increasing $n$ (larger $\varepsilon_F$) shrinks the nonadiabatic window: the adiabatic approximation improves when $\varepsilon_F\gg\hbar\omega_0$, except very near $\Gamma$.
  - (5) Off-diagonal effects (iii) and multiple poles (iv) are not in this one-mode model; mention them.

### V4. Dyson (Fan–Migdal) versus cumulant for a localized level coupled to one phonon (p. 32, cumulant; Eqs. 157, 172–174)

- **Model:** one occupied level $\varepsilon_0$ (hole, as in ARPES) coupled with constant $g$ to a dispersionless phonon $\hbar\omega_0$. Define $S=|g|^2/(\hbar\omega_0)^2$ (Huang–Rhys factor). The independent-boson model, which the cumulant solves exactly (Langreth 1970).
- **Controls:** $S$ (0–4, default 1); $\hbar\omega_0$ (20–200 meV, default 100); $\eta$ (5–40 meV); number of replicas $N_{\max}$ (auto, about $S+6\sqrt{S}+5$).
- **Dyson-FM** (Eq. 157 with only the occupied term, retarded): $\Sigma(E)=\dfrac{|g|^2}{E-\varepsilon_0+\hbar\omega_0+i\eta}$, so $A_{\rm D}(E)=-\frac1\pi{\rm Im}[E+i\eta-\varepsilon_0-\Sigma(E)]^{-1}$. Analytic poles ($x=E-\varepsilon_0$): $x_\pm=\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{2}\left(-1\pm\sqrt{1+4S}\right)$, with weights $Z_\pm=\left[1+S(\hbar\omega_0)^2/(x_\pm+\hbar\omega_0)^2\right]^{-1}$ (they sum to 1).
- **Cumulant** (exact here): $A_{\rm C}(E)=\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-S}\dfrac{S^n}{n!}\,\dfrac{\eta/\pi}{(E-\varepsilon_0-S\hbar\omega_0+n\hbar\omega_0)^2+\eta^2}$.
- **Display:** both spectra on one axis ($E-\varepsilon_0$ from $-(S+6)\hbar\omega_0$ to $2\hbar\omega_0$), with the QP weights $Z_+$ vs $e^{-S}$ and the QP–satellite separations $\hbar\omega_0\sqrt{1+4S}$ vs $\hbar\omega_0$.
- **What to notice:** both give the same first-order QP shift $+S\hbar\omega_0$ (toward $E_F$) at small $S$. Dyson produces **one** satellite, too far away ($\hbar\omega_0\sqrt{1+4S}$) and with the wrong weight. The cumulant gives the Poisson (Franck–Condon) ladder of replicas spaced exactly by $\hbar\omega_0$, which is what ARPES on doped TiO$_2$ shows (Moser 2013; Verdi, Caruso and Giustino 2017). It is the same story as plasmon satellites in GW versus GW+C.

### V5 (optional). Bare versus screened vertex in a metal (Eqs. 141–146; Concepts d)

- **Model:** 3D jellium-like metal with point ions. Bare coupling $|g^{\rm b}(q)|\propto q\,|V_{\rm ion}(q)|\propto1/q$ with $V_{\rm ion}=-Ze^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega q^2)$. Screened coupling $g=g^{\rm b}/\epsilon(q)$ with Thomas–Fermi $\epsilon(q)=1+k_{\rm TF}^2/q^2$, so $|g|\propto q/(q^2+k_{\rm TF}^2)$.
- **Controls:** $k_{\rm TF}/k_F$ (0.5–2). Display $|g^{\rm b}|$, $|g|$ and $|g^{\rm b}g|$ vs $q/k_F$, normalized at $q=2k_F$, and the ratio $g^{\rm b}g/|g|^2=\epsilon(q)$.
- **What to notice:** the bare vertex is long ranged and diverges at small $q$, while the screened vertex vanishes linearly. The two choices in (145) vs (146) differ by the factor $\epsilon(q,0)$, which is huge at small $q$. Only a consistent treatment (exact RPA: ${\rm Im}[g^{\rm b}Pg(\omega)]=|g(\omega)|^2{\rm Im}P$) settles which is right. This bridges to the EPP phonon self-energy, which also has one bare ($D^0$) and one screened ($D^{\rm e}$) vertex.

## 7. Connections

### 7.1 What in this range is the electron–phonon-only (electrostatic) baseline that EPP generalizes

1. **Only the instantaneous, longitudinal Coulomb interaction.** All self-energies are built from $v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ and its electronic screening $W_{\rm e}=v+vP_{\rm e}W_{\rm e}$: Eqs. (119)–(121), (140)–(141), (150), (153), (156). There is no vector potential, no current vertex and no transverse photon. "Retardation" in Giustino means the frequency dependence of $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)$ only. EPP replaces $W_{\rm e}$ by the full electromagnetic propagator $D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (longitudinal + transverse, retarded with finite $c$), dressed by the electronic RPA photon self-energy (EPP Eq. 388). It recovers Giustino's world in the electrostatic limit $c\to\infty$ (EPP Sec. 7.3, Eqs. 449–454).

2. **Phonon Dyson equation and pole condition: identical structure.** Giustino Eq. (135) $\mathbf D=\mathbf D^{\rm A}+\mathbf D^{\rm A}\Pi^{\rm NA}\mathbf D$ corresponds to EPP Eqs. (397)–(398), $F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F$. Giustino Eq. (132) $D^{\rm A}=2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}/(\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu})$ corresponds to EPP Eq. (390), $F^0_\sigma=(2\omega_\sigma/\hbar)/(\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma+i\eta)$; EPP carries an extra $1/\hbar$, so its $\Omega^{\rm e}$ is an energy while Giustino's $\Pi_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}$ is a frequency. Giustino Eq. (137) $\tilde\Omega^2=\omega^2+2\omega\Pi^{\rm NA}(\tilde\Omega)$ corresponds to EPP's pole condition $\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+2\omega_\sigma\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\mathbf q,\omega)/\hbar$ (Eqs. 405, 407). Giustino's off-diagonal $\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu'}$ (effect iii, mode coupling) corresponds to EPP's 3s×3s matrix Dyson equation ("phonon mixing").

3. **Phonon self-energy: charge vertices × $W_{\rm e}$ → current vertices × $D^{\rm e}$.** Giustino Eq. (119)/(121): $\Pi\propto Z_\kappa\nabla\,W_{\rm e}\,Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'$. In reciprocal space this is $Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa'}q_\alpha q_{\alpha'}W_{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$, nuclear point charges coupled by the electron-screened Coulomb interaction. EPP Eq. (399): $\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}=-\sum J^*_{\alpha\sigma}D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}J_{\beta\sigma'}$, with dynamical ionic **current** vertices $J\propto\omega\,j_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ built from Born effective charges (EPP Eqs. 392, 295). In the limit $c\to\infty$, EPP Eq. (452), $\Omega^{\rm e}\to\hbar W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2/(2\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma)$, is the long-range dipolar part of Giustino's (121). Note the difference in the "charges": Giustino uses the bare $Z_\kappa$ with the full electronic $W_{\rm e}$ (all electrons screen), while EPP uses $Z^*$ (core and valence-band screening folded into the ionic vertex) together with the valence $D^{\rm e}$.

4. **Adiabatic versus nonadiabatic, and double counting.** Giustino Eq. (125) splits $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}$. Eqs. (140)/(145) subtract $W_{\rm e}(0)$ and $\chi^0(0)$ because the DFT phonons already contain the static screening. EPP discusses exactly this (EPP text near Eqs. 108–109): starting from frozen-core bare phonons $\omega^0$, "everyone can interact with everyone" without double counting. Starting from DFT phonons $\omega$, "the electronic bubble diagram in the phonon self-energy represents a kind of double-counting… if in chain of bubble diagrams we carefully exclude adiabatic effects and leave only dynamic effects, we avoid double counting". This is Giustino's $\Pi^{\rm NA}$. Giustino's remark on the historical "bare force constants" reference (Grimvall 1981) corresponds to EPP's $\omega^0$ reference.

5. **One bare vertex, one screened vertex.** Giustino Eq. (141), $\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}=g^{\rm b}P_{\rm e}g^{\rm cc}$ (Fig. 1(c)), corresponds to EPP Eqs. (411)–(416) and Fig. 14: $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}=\sum g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}\chi^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\,g^{{\rm dyn},0}_{nm\sigma'}$. The **bare electrodynamic vertex** $g^{{\rm dyn},0}=iJ^{e*}D^0J^{\rm ion}$ uses the bare photon propagator; the **screened electrodynamic vertex** $g^{\rm dyn}=iJ^{\rm ion*}D^{\rm e}J^{e}$ uses the electronically dressed propagator. Giustino's $g^{\rm b}=\Delta V^{\rm en}$ (Eq. 142) and $g=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b}$ (Eq. 143) are their electrostatic limits. EPP Eq. (418): $g^{{\rm dyn},0}\to$ the bare Fröhlich vertex (EPP Eq. 131 = Verdi and Giustino 2015, Eq. 4). EPP Eq. (448): $g^{\rm dyn}\to$ the Fröhlich vertex screened by $\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)\cdot\mathbf q$.

6. **Dynamical screening of the vertex.** Giustino's approximation (iv) for phonons and (v) for electrons, $g(\mathbf r,\omega)\simeq g(\mathbf r,0)$, "ubiquitous but never mentioned", is relaxed in EPP. There the screened Fröhlich vertex carries $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$, so in doped systems it can describe plasmon–phonon coupling (Giustino p. 23, coupled plasmon–phonon modes; "electronic analog of photon polaritons"). EPP Eq. (454) then takes $\omega\to0$ in $W^{\rm e}$ for 3D phonon energies, which is the static limit used by Giustino-style calculations. In 2D (EPP Sec. 8) this is the screened 2D Fröhlich/LO interaction of Sohier *et al.* (Nano Lett. 2017).

7. **LO–TO splitting, Born effective charges and non-analytic terms.** In Giustino's framework the LO–TO splitting sits inside $\Pi^{\rm A}$ (Eq. 128, the DFT force constants). It arises from the long-range part of $v$ screened by the static $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(0)$, and it gives the direction-dependent non-analytic term at $\mathbf q\to0$ (handled in Sec. VI.A.3 / Gonze–Lee, not in this range). In EPP it is the **lowest-order** self-energy $\Omega^0=-J^*D^0J$ with the bare photon propagator (EPP Eqs. 401, 406–410). At $\mathbf q=0$ with finite $c$, EPP finds a frequency-independent shift $\tilde\omega^2_\sigma=\omega^2_\sigma+(4\pi/\Omega_{\rm uc})|\mathbf j_\sigma|^2$ for every polar mode, the polariton picture (EPP Gaussian units). Giustino's instantaneous-Coulomb result is the opposite order of limits ($c\to\infty$ before $q\to0$), where only the longitudinal mode shifts. EPP remarks that what is usually called LO–TO splitting is better called an "LO shift" (EPP Eq. 162).

8. **$W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$ and the ionic photon self-energy.** Giustino's $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$ (Eqs. 104–105, 110) is the phonon contribution to the screened interaction. It is what $\Sigma^{\rm FM}$ uses (Eqs. 153, 156), and it is the lattice part of the dielectric response that BSE/GW users know as "phonon screening" ($\epsilon_\infty\to\epsilon_0$). In EPP it corresponds to the **ionic part of the RPA photon self-energy**, $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}\propto\sum_\sigma j_\sigma j^*_\sigma/(\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma)$ (EPP Eqs. 389–391; a Lorentz oscillator in the current–current channel). The total propagator $D=D^0+D^0(\Pi^{\rm e}+\Pi^{\rm ion})D$ contains both $W_{\rm e}$-like and $W_{\rm ph}$-like physics, plus transverse polaritons.

9. **Electron self-energy.** Giustino's Fan–Migdal "$GW_{\rm ph}$" with two screened vertices (Eq. 156) becomes, in EPP language, electron self-energy diagrams with exchange of the ion-dressed EM propagator. With the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex this gives polar-polaron physics (Giustino Sec. V.B.5–6: TiO$_2$ satellites, Fröhlich polarons). The transverse part adds retardation at $q\lesssim\omega/c$, a tiny phase-space region for electrons but conceptually where polaritons live.

10. **Hedin–Baym as the common parent.** Giustino's Table I (Hedin–Baym) is the electrostatic special case of a theory with photons. In EPP, the "connection formulas" (EPP Sec. 6) relate the bosonic propagators (photon $D$, phonon $F$) to induced charges and currents and to correlators. They play the role of Giustino's Eqs. (99)–(105) and (113), which relate $\delta\langle\hat n\rangle/\delta\varphi$, $\delta\langle\hat n\rangle/\delta J$ and $\delta\langle\Delta\tau\rangle/\delta F$ to $D$ and $W_{\rm ph}$.

11. **Units.** Giustino uses SI ($e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0$). EPP uses Gaussian units ($4\pi$, $c$ explicit: e.g. EPP $D^0_{\alpha\beta}(q=0,\omega)=-4\pi c\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}/\omega^2$; EPP Eq. 131 mixes in a $4\pi\epsilon_0$). The page should flag this when displaying the two side by side.

## 8. Source issues

1. **Eq. (127), overall sign (likely error; confident about the physics).** With $\hat n_{\rm n}=-\sum Z_\kappa\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p})$ (Eq. 67), $\partial\hat n_{\rm n}/\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=+Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p})$, as in Eq. (107). Eq. (100) gives a total-density response $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\,\delta n_{\rm n}$, so $\delta n_{\rm e}=(\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}-1)\delta n_{\rm n}=+Z_\kappa\int[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}-\delta]\nabla'\delta$. The printed $-Z_\kappa$ gives, for perfect screening ($\epsilon^{-1}\to0$), $\partial n_{\rm e}/\partial\tau=+Z\nabla\delta$, i.e. electrons *moving opposite to the nucleus*. That is unphysical: the screening cloud must follow, $\delta n_{\rm e}=-\delta n_{\rm n}$. Corrected form: $\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle/\partial\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}=+Z_\kappa\int d\mathbf r'[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}-\delta]\nabla'_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})$. A further subtlety: since $\epsilon^{-1}$ as defined in Eq. (80) acts on potentials, its action on densities is strictly through the transpose $v^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}v=(\epsilon^{-1})^{\rm T}$ (also in Eq. 100). This does not affect Eq. (128), which is written directly in terms of $\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}\rangle/\partial\tau$.

2. **Fig. 3 caption versus plotted curves (confident).** The caption states $\hbar\omega_0=100$ meV. The curves correspond to $\hbar\omega_0\approx50$ meV: $E_g(500\,{\rm K})\approx0.81$ eV (model 0.809 vs 0.878 for 100 meV), $\Gamma(500\,{\rm K})\approx95$ meV (model 95.6 vs 60.9), and the asymptote at 500 K $\approx0.83$ eV (model 0.828 vs 0.914).

3. **Fig. 3(b) lifetime scale.** The dashed curve starts at about 16.5 fs (axis reading), but $\tau=\hbar/(2\Gamma^{\rm ZP})=658.2/(2\cdot50)=6.6$ fs. The shape ($\propto1/\Gamma$, ratio about 1.9 between 0 and 500 K) is consistent; the absolute right-axis scale is not. I cannot identify the intended convention, so the page should compute $\tau=\hbar/2|\Gamma|$ and not copy the axis.

4. **Fig. 1(d) caption and text cite Eq. (144) for the dressed coupling $g$.** $g$ is defined in Eq. (143); Eq. (144) defines $g^{\rm cc}$. Minor reference slip.

5. **Fig. 4 caption notation.** The cutoff is called $\varepsilon_{\max}$ in one sentence and $\varepsilon_c$ in another (same quantity, 5 eV). It is not stated whether $N_F$ counts both spins. With $|g|^2=\hbar\omega_0/N_F$, the mass-enhancement parameter $\lambda=2N^\sigma_F|g|^2/\hbar\omega_0$ is 2 (per-spin $N_F$) or 1 (total $N_F$). The dimensionality of the parabolic band (3D presumably) is not stated either.

6. **Eq. (145): bare × static-screened vertex (caveat, not a typo; my analysis).** Within RPA for the homogeneous electron gas, the exact result from (140)/(141) is ${\rm Im}\,\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}=|g^{\rm b}|^2{\rm Im}P(\omega)/|\epsilon(\omega)|^2=|g(\omega)|^2{\rm Im}P(\omega)$. Applying approximation (iv) ($g^{\rm cc}(\omega)\to g^*(0)$) while keeping the bare vertex gives $|g^{\rm b}|^2{\rm Im}P(\omega)/\epsilon(0)$. That is too large by about $\epsilon(q,0)$, which is $\gg1$ in metals at small $q$. The cancellation between ${\rm Re}P\,{\rm Im}\epsilon^{-1}$ and ${\rm Im}P\,{\rm Re}\epsilon^{-1}$ is lost. So the "common practice" $|g|^2$ of Eq. (146) is, at least in this limit, the *more* consistent static approximation, and the suggestion that one should replace $|g|^2$ by $g^{\rm b}g^*$ requires keeping the frequency dependence of the screened vertex. The author does flag approximation (iv) as of uncertain validity. Later literature, e.g. Berges *et al.*, Phys. Rev. X 13, 041009 (2023), "To screen, or not to screen", discusses this issue; that reference is outside the source and should be cited with care.

7. **Eq. (165) and the lifetime statement (p. 30), sign conventions.** $\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}<0$ (electrons) and $>0$ (holes) holds for the **time-ordered** $T=0$ self-energy of Eq. (157). With the retarded self-energy of Eq. (158), ${\rm Im}\Sigma\le0$ for all states. Also, "decreases as $\exp[\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}(t'-t)/\hbar]$" is correct for electrons ($t'>t$). For holes the time ordering is reversed; a convention-free form is $\exp(-|\Gamma_{n\mathbf k}||t'-t|/\hbar)$. Use $|\Gamma|$ on the page.

8. **Cumulant formula (p. 32):** the prefactor $(i/\hbar)$ in $G(t)=(i/\hbar)\exp[\dots]$ corresponds to the hole branch ($t<0$) of the time-ordered $G$ (Eq. 70). For electrons ($t>0$) the prefactor is $-(i/\hbar)$. Not an error for the ARPES (hole) context, but it is unstated.

9. **Eq. (167):** the $m=n$, $\mathbf q\to0$ intraband terms (and degenerate states) have vanishing denominators. They are implicitly excluded or handled as principal values; this is not stated. In polar materials, the adiabatic formula combined with the Fröhlich $g\propto1/q$ diverges for band extrema, which is a known limitation not discussed here (see Sec. IX). Not an error, but important for anyone coding Eq. (167).

10. **Eq. (124):** "$\nu=1,\dots,3M$" implies the problem has been block-diagonalized in $\mathbf q$. In the real-space supercell form of Eq. (123) the matrix has dimension $3MN_p$. Minor.

11. **Eq. (130) versus a direct derivation from Eqs. (20), (109):** the derivation naturally gives $S_{\mathbf q\nu,\kappa\alpha p}S^*_{\mathbf q\nu,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$ (conjugate on the second index). The printed form has the conjugate on the first. The two are equivalent under $\mathbf q\to-\mathbf q$, using $S_{-\mathbf q}=S^*_{\mathbf q}$ and $\omega_{-\mathbf q}=\omega_{\mathbf q}$. No error.

12. **Mode-basis self-energy not defined explicitly** (Eqs. 136, 141, 145). Only "the transform of Eq. (131) and its inverse" is mentioned. The normalization consistent with (136)–(139) is $2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\Pi_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}=\mathbf e^\dagger_\nu\mathbf M^{-1/2}\Pi(\mathbf q)\mathbf M^{-1/2}\mathbf e_\nu$ (my reconstruction).

13. **Eq. (138)** evaluates $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ at the complex frequency $\Omega-i\gamma$. This requires the analytic continuation of the retarded $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ into the lower half-plane (second sheet). In practice it is evaluated on the real axis, as in the simplified forms. Not an error.

14. **Eq. (145) is written in retarded form** ($\omega+i\eta$ for all $\omega$), although the surrounding formalism is $T=0$ time-ordered. The two coincide for $\omega>0$, the case used for phonon linewidths. Minor.
