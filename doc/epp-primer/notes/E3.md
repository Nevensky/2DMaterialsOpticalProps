## 1. Overview (plain language)

This range is the hinge of the manuscript. Section 1 built phonons: normal modes, polarization vectors and the quantized displacement operator, Eq. (83). Sections 2–3 now couple those phonons to electrons in two ways, and Section 4 opens the step that justifies the manuscript's title: the coupling is rebuilt through the electromagnetic field itself.

**Sec. 2: short-range (deformation-potential) coupling from a pseudopotential.** Valence electrons move in a crystal potential made of rigid, local ionic pseudopotentials $v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_{ia})$. When the ions vibrate, the potential changes by $\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-\sum\mathbf Q\cdot\nabla v_a$ to first order. Fourier-transforming $v_a$, inserting the phonon expansion of $\mathbf Q$ and using Bloch's theorem gives the textbook electron–phonon Hamiltonian. Its matrix element $g^0_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$, Eq. (107), is a sum over reciprocal vectors $\mathbf G$ of "polarization · $(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)$ × form factor $V_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)$ × charge vertex $\rho$". This is the plane-wave form of the *bare* electron–ion vertex. The full model Hamiltonian, Eq. (109), adds the bare valence electron–electron Coulomb interaction. The author then discusses a key practical issue: perturbation theory around bare quantities never double counts, but it converges badly. Perturbation theory around DFT quantities ($E_{n\mathbf k},\phi_{n\mathbf k},\omega_\alpha$) converges well, but it double counts the adiabatic electronic screening of phonons, so only *dynamic* (nonadiabatic) corrections may be added.

**Sec. 3: long-range (polar, Fröhlich) coupling from Born charges.** In polar crystals a displaced ion creates a dipole $\mathbf p=\hat Z^*\mathbf Q$ (Born effective charges, Eq. 110). The author solves Poisson's equation for a point dipole in an anisotropic dielectric $\hat\epsilon^\infty$. The result is $\phi(\mathbf q)=-\frac{i}{\epsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q}{\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q}$ (Eq. 120). Summing over all dipoles gives an induced potential energy, Eq. (128), that is *formally identical* to the pseudopotential one, Eq. (102), once $V_a\to V_a^{\mathcal L}=e^2/\epsilon_0\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q$ and $\mathbf e\to\mathbf e^{\mathcal L}=-\hat Z^*\mathbf e/e$. So the whole Sec. 2 machinery gives the polar vertex $g^{\mathcal L}$, Eq. (131), for free. The author identifies it with Eq. (4) of Verdi & Giustino, PRL 115, 176401 (2015), which is Giustino RMP Eq. (180). In this range $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ is put in "ad hoc". It will later be removed and regenerated from the electronic RPA photon self-energy, to avoid double counting.

**Sec. 4 (start): the electron–phonon–photon Lagrangian.** The author picks the temporal gauge $\Phi=0$ ($\mathbf E=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}$, $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$, Gaussian units). He writes one Lagrangian, Eq. (134), for valence electrons, harmonic ions and the free field. Electrons couple through $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A$ and ionic dipoles through $\frac1c\dot{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf A$. The electron–electron *and* the electron–phonon interactions are now both mediated by $\mathbf A$, which includes the longitudinal (Coulomb) and transverse (photon, retarded) parts. Sec. 4.1 checks that the Euler–Lagrange equation for an electron reproduces the Lorentz force. This is the conceptual leap beyond Giustino's framework, where every interaction is the instantaneous, longitudinal Coulomb one.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **(p. 17) Heisenberg picture for phonons, Eqs. (89)–(91).** $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}(t)=a_{\mathbf k\alpha}e^{-i\omega t}$. Inserted into (83), this gives the time-dependent $Q_{ia\mu}(t)$, Eq. (90). Then $m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}$, Eq. (91), equals $P_{ia\mu}$ of Eq. (84). This closes Sec. 1: "We are now fully equipped to study electron-phonon coupling."
2. **(p. 17) Physical setting of Sec. 2.** Valence electrons, frozen cores, and a local, transferable ionic pseudopotential $v_a(\mathbf r)$. The crystal potential is $V_{\rm cr}=\sum_{ia}v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_{ia})$, Eq. (92), with $\mathbf R_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r_a$, Eq. (93).
3. **(pp. 17–18) Frozen lattice, Eqs. (94)–(97).** $H_0=T+V^0_{\rm cr}$ has Bloch eigenstates $\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}$ and energies $E^0_{n\mathbf k}$. There is no valence e–e interaction yet.
4. **(p. 18) Vibrating lattice, Eqs. (98)–(100).** $\mathbf R^0_{ia}\to\mathbf R^0_{ia}+\mathbf Q_{ia}(t)$ with $\mathbf Q$ a quantum operator. A first-order Taylor expansion gives $\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-\sum\partial_\mu v_a\,Q_{ia\mu}$.
5. **(p. 18) Fourier representation, Eqs. (101)–(102).** $v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_{ia})=\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}V_a(\mathbf q')e^{i\mathbf q'(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}$, with the basis phase absorbed in $V_a(\mathbf q')=e^{-i\mathbf q'\mathbf r^0_a}v_a(\mathbf q')$. The gradient becomes $i q'_\mu$.
6. **(p. 18) Second quantization, Eqs. (103)–(104).** One-body operator $\sum\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|\Delta V_{\rm cr}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k'}\rangle c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k'}$.
7. **(p. 19) Momentum conservation, Eq. (105).** $\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i\mathbf q'\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k'}\rangle=\delta_{\mathbf k',\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}(\mathbf G=0)$, with an explicit warning about umklapp.
8. **(p. 19) Insert phonons, then do the lattice sum.** Inserting (83) and using $\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf q-\mathbf q')\mathbf R^0_i}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q',\mathbf q+\mathbf G}$ turns the continuous $\mathbf q'$ integral into a $\mathbf G$ sum: $\mathbf q'=\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ with $\mathbf q\in$ BZ.
9. **(p. 19) Charge-vertex definition, Eq. (106), and relabelling.** $\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}(0)=\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G)$ and $c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}=c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}$ (periodic gauge).
10. **(p. 20) Result, Eqs. (107)–(108).** The boxed bare e–ph matrix element $g^0_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ and $\Delta V^0_{\rm cr}=\sum g^0c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha})$.
11. **(p. 20) Interpretation.** All quantities carry superscript 0: bare, frozen-core. $H_0+\Delta V_{\rm cr}$ already goes beyond Born–Oppenheimer because the nuclei are quantum operators. "$g^0$ represents the first non-adiabatic correction."
12. **(p. 20) Full model Hamiltonian, Eq. (109).** Bare bands, bare phonons and the bare linear coupling, plus the bare valence e–e Coulomb interaction written with field operators $\Psi_0=\sum\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}c_{n\mathbf k}$. The stated approximations are harmonic ions and frozen cores.
13. **(p. 21) Choice of starting point and double counting.** Around (109), "everyone can interact with everyone" through $v$ or $D^0$ with no double counting, but the perturbations are not small. The better starting point is DFT, $E^0\to E$, $\phi^0\to\phi$, $\omega^0\to\omega$. Then the adiabatic part of the electronic bubble in the phonon self-energy is already in $\omega$ and must be excluded, keeping only dynamic (nonadiabatic) effects. DFT states inside the RPA bubble (and hence inside $W$) are not double counting.
14. **(pp. 21–22) Long-range forces.** DFT force constants are assumed short-range, $k^{\mathcal S}=0$ beyond one cell. Displacements in polar crystals create dipoles $\mathbf p=\hat Z^*\mathbf Q$ whose fields reach far beyond a cell. Adding them "poses no threat of double counting, in most cases."
15. **(p. 22) Born effective charges, Eqs. (110)–(111), Fig. 2.** $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}=\delta p_\mu/\delta R_{a\nu}=\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\delta P_\mu/\delta R_{a\nu}$ is a cell-intrinsic tensor with the dimension of charge, so $p_{ia\mu}=Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}$.
16. **(pp. 22–24) Electrostatics of a point dipole in an anisotropic medium, Eqs. (112)–(120).** Dipole charge density; Gauss law with polarization $\mathbf P=\epsilon_0(\hat\epsilon^\infty-1)\mathbf E$; $\nabla\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\nabla\phi=-\rho/\epsilon_0$; Fourier transform; result $\phi(\mathbf q)=-\frac{i}{\epsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf p\mathbf q}{\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q}$ (SI). Here $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ is the electronic screening, inserted ad hoc and to be removed later.
17. **(p. 24) Potential of displaced atoms, Eqs. (121)–(123).** Combine (111), (117) and (120); sum over $a,\mu,\nu$ and over cells $i$; multiply by the electron charge $-e$. This gives $\Delta V^{\mathcal L}_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)$.
18. **(pp. 24–25) Mapping onto Sec. 2, Eqs. (124)–(129).** Define $V^{\mathcal L}_a(\mathbf q)=e^2/\epsilon_0\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q$ and $Q^{\mathcal L}=-\hat Z^*Q/e$, so that $\mathbf e^{\mathcal L}=-\hat Z^*\mathbf e/e$. Then $\Delta V^{\mathcal L}_{\rm cr}$, Eq. (128), has exactly the form of $\Delta V_{\rm cr}$, Eq. (102) = (129).
19. **(p. 25) Polar vertex, Eqs. (130)–(131).** Steps (102)→(107) are reused verbatim. The explicit form (131) is "exactly equal to Eq. 4" of Verdi & Giustino (2015), up to a factor $e$ that is hidden in $\hat Z^*$ (EPP's $Z^*$ has units of charge).
20. **(pp. 25–26) Sec. 4: gauge and Lagrangian, Eqs. (132)–(136).** $\Phi=0$. $L$ contains electrons in the frozen $V_{\rm cr}$, harmonic ions, the free field $\int(E^2-B^2)/8\pi$, the electron–field coupling $\frac ec\sum\mathbf v_e\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)$ (described as "the e–e interaction") and the ion-dipole–field coupling $\frac1c\sum\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}\mathbf A(\mathbf R^0_i)$ (described as "the e–ph interaction"), with $p_{ia\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}$.
21. **(p. 27) Sec. 4.1: Lorentz equation, Eqs. (137)–(138).** The Euler–Lagrange equation for electron $e$, the convective derivative of $\mathbf A$ and the BAC–CAB identity give $m_e\dot{\mathbf v}_e=-\nabla V_{\rm cr}+e\mathbf E+\frac ec\mathbf v_e\times\mathbf B$. Next (Sec. 4.2, Eq. 139) comes the ionic Euler–Lagrange equation, where the field couples to $p_{ia\mu}$ rather than to $Q_{ia\mu}$.

## 3. Key equations (faithful transcriptions with the paper's numbering)

### Conventions in this range (read first)

- **Units.**
  - Sec. 2 uses no specific unit system: $V_a(\mathbf q)$ has dimension energy × volume, and $g$ has dimension energy.
  - **Sec. 3 is explicitly SI**: "here we use SI units. In Gaussian units $\epsilon_0=1/4\pi$" (p. 23).
  - **Sec. 4 is Gaussian**: $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\partial_t\mathbf A$, field energy $\int(E^2-B^2)/8\pi$, couplings $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A$.
  - Eq. (131) is written in a hybrid "$\frac{4\pi i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{e}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$" form, so that it can be compared with Verdi–Giustino. In Gaussian units $e/4\pi\epsilon_0\to e$.
  - $\hbar$ is explicit throughout.
- **Electron charge.**
  - Sec. 3: the electron charge is $-e$ with $e>0$ ("multiply by the electron charge (−e)", before Eq. 123).
  - Sec. 4: the coupling is written $+\frac ec\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A$ and the Lorentz force $+e\mathbf E$, so $e$ there is the *signed* particle charge. See Source issues.
- **Born charge units.** EPP's $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$ **has the dimension of charge**, $Z^*_{\rm EPP}=|e|\,Z^*_{\rm standard}$, where $Z^*_{\rm standard}$ is dimensionless as in Giustino and Verdi–Giustino.
- **Volumes.**
  - $\Omega$ is the **crystal (BvK normalization) volume**: Bloch functions are normalized as $\phi=\Omega^{-1/2}e^{i\mathbf k\mathbf r}u$, Eq. (96), and the step $N/\Omega=1/\Omega_{\rm uc}$ appears on p. 19.
  - $\Omega_{\rm uc}=\Omega/N$ is the unit-cell volume.
  - **Caution: Giustino's $\Omega$ is the unit-cell volume**, so EPP $\Omega_{\rm uc}$ ↔ Giustino $\Omega$, and EPP $\Omega$ ↔ Giustino $N_p\Omega$.
- **Fourier conventions.**
  - Continuous: $f(\mathbf r)=\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}f(\mathbf q)$ (Eqs. 101, 117), so $f(\mathbf q)=\int d\mathbf r\,e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r}f(\mathbf r)$.
  - Lattice: $Q_{ia\mu}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathbf k}(\dots)e^{+i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i}$ (Eq. 83).
  - Discretization: $\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\to\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q'}$ and $\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf q-\mathbf q')\mathbf R^0_i}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q',\mathbf q+\mathbf G}$.
- **Coupling normalization.** EPP puts the factor $N^{-1/2}$ **inside** $g$ (as $\sqrt{\hbar/2Nm_a\omega}$), and the Hamiltonian (108)/(109) has **no** extra $N^{-1/2}$. This is self-consistent. Giustino Eq. (1) instead factors out $N_p^{-1/2}$.
- **Momentum labelling.**
  - EPP: $g_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha})$, i.e. $|m,\mathbf k-\mathbf q\rangle\to|n,\mathbf k\rangle$ by absorbing $\mathbf q$ or emitting $-\mathbf q$.
  - Giustino: $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,c^\dagger_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}c_{n\mathbf k}$.
  - Mapping: $g^{\rm EPP}_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=N_p^{-1/2}\,g^{\rm Gius}_{nm\nu}(\mathbf k-\mathbf q,\mathbf q)$, where Giustino's first band index is the final state.
- **Index dictionary, EPP ↔ Giustino.**

  | EPP | Giustino |
  |---|---|
  | cell $i$, $\mathbf R^0_i$ | $p$, $\mathbf R_p$ |
  | atom $a$ ($1..s$) | $\kappa$ |
  | Cartesian $\mu,\nu$ | $\alpha$ |
  | branch $\alpha$ ($1..3s$) | $\nu$ |
  | $m_a$ | $M_\kappa$ |
  | $e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ | $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)$ |
  | $E_{n\mathbf k},\phi_{n\mathbf k}$ | $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k},\psi_{n\mathbf k}$ |
  | $Q_{ia\mu}$ | $\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}$ |
  | $N$ | $N_p$ |
  | $\Omega_{\rm uc}$ | $\Omega$ |
  | $\mathbf r^0_a$ | $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$ |

- **Charge vertex (Eq. 106).** $\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf G)=\langle\phi_{n\mathbf k}|e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\rangle$ is dimensionless; it equals $\delta_{nm}$ for $\mathbf q+\mathbf G\to0$. The same object appears in RPA/BSE codes as the "oscillator strength" or "pair density".

### (89) Heisenberg-picture ladder operators
$$a_{\mathbf k\alpha}(t)=a_{\mathbf k\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t},\qquad a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}(t)=a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}e^{i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t}\tag{89}$$
Here $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}=a_{\mathbf k\alpha}(t=0)$. This follows from Eq. (88), $i\hbar\dot a=[a,H]=\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)a$, with the harmonic phonon Hamiltonian (78).

### (90) Time-dependent displacement operator
$$Q_{ia\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf k\in BZ}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}}\;e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)\left(a_{\mathbf k\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t}+a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}e^{i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t}\right)e^{i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i}\tag{90}$$
Symbols:
- $N$: number of cells.
- $s$: atoms per cell.
- $m_a$: mass of atom $a$.
- $e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)$: the $(a\mu)$ component of the eigenvector of the mass-scaled dynamical matrix, with $e_{a\mu\alpha}(-\mathbf k)=e^*_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)$ and $\omega_\alpha(-\mathbf k)=\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$.
- $\sqrt{\hbar/2m_a\omega}$: the zero-point amplitude.

### (91) Momentum check
$$m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}=\frac{i}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf k\in BZ}\sqrt{\frac{m_a\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}{2}}\;e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)\left(a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}e^{i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t}-a_{\mathbf k\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t}\right)e^{i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i}\tag{91}$$
At $t=0$ this is exactly $P_{ia\mu}$ of Eq. (84). This verifies $P=m\dot Q$, i.e. that (83)–(84) are the canonical pair.

### (92)–(94) Crystal potential from local pseudopotentials
$$V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_{ia}),\tag{92}$$
$$\mathbf R_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r_a,\tag{93}$$
$$\mathbf R_{ia}\equiv\mathbf R^0_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r^0_a\quad\text{(frozen lattice)}.\tag{94}$$
$v_a$ is a local, transferable ionic pseudopotential: nucleus plus frozen core, obtained from atomic all-electron DFT with the valence e–e part removed.

### (95)–(97) Frozen-lattice Bloch problem
$$H_0=T+V^0_{\rm cr},\tag{95}$$
$$\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)=\frac{1}{\sqrt\Omega}e^{i\mathbf k\mathbf r}u^0_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r),\quad E^0_{n\mathbf k};\quad\mathbf k\in1.B.Z.,\tag{96}$$
$$\left(T+V^0_{\rm cr}\right)\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)=E^0_{n\mathbf k}\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r).\tag{97}$$
$T$ is the kinetic energy and $V^0_{\rm cr}$ is (92) at $\mathbf R_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_{ia}$. **No valence e–e interaction** is included here. $\Omega$ is the crystal volume.

### (98)–(100) Vibrating lattice, first-order expansion
$$\mathbf R^0_{ia}\to\mathbf R_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_{ia}+\mathbf Q_{ia}(t),\tag{98}$$
$$V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=V^0_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)+\Delta V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r),\tag{99}$$
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=-\sum_{ia\mu}\partial_\mu v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_{ia})\,Q_{ia\mu}.\tag{100}$$
Assumptions:
- Small displacements; the text says $|\mathbf Q_{ia}|\ll|\mathbf r^0_a|$, see Source issues.
- **Linear order only**, so the second-order (Debye–Waller) term $\frac12\mathbf Q\mathbf Q:\nabla\nabla v_a$ is dropped.
- Rigid-ion: the pseudopotential moves rigidly with the nucleus.

### (101) Fourier transform of the ionic potential
$$v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_{ia})=\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}V_a(\mathbf q')e^{i\mathbf q'(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)},\qquad V_a(\mathbf q')=e^{-i\mathbf q'\mathbf r^0_a}v_a(\mathbf q').\tag{101}$$
$v_a(\mathbf q)$ is the FT of the potential of atom $a$ with its nucleus at the origin. $V_a(\mathbf q)$ is the FT with the nucleus at $\mathbf r^0_a$, i.e. it **includes the basis phase factor**. This is the analogue of Giustino's $V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$ in RMP Eq. (43).

### (102) Induced potential in Fourier form
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_{ia\mu}Q_{ia\mu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\,q'_\mu V_a(\mathbf q')e^{i\mathbf q'(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}.\tag{102}$$
The gradient $\partial_\mu$ becomes $iq'_\mu$, and the minus sign of (100) combines with it to give $-i$.

### (103)–(104) Second quantization
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}=\sum_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'nm}\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|\Delta V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k'}\rangle\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k'},\tag{103}$$
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-i\sum_{ia\mu}Q_{ia\mu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}q'_\mu V_a(\mathbf q')e^{-i\mathbf q'\mathbf R^0_i}\sum_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'nm}\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i\mathbf q'\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k'}\rangle\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k'}.\tag{104}$$
Spin is not written explicitly.

### (105) Charge vertex and momentum conservation
$$\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i\mathbf q'\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k'}\rangle=\delta_{\mathbf k',\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i\mathbf q'\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}\rangle=\delta_{\mathbf k',\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}\,\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}(\mathbf G=0).\tag{105}$$
The author notes: "pay attention to the umklapp process, i.e. the cases when $\mathbf k-\mathbf q'=\mathbf G$ will also contribute … no matter what $\mathbf k'\in1BZ$." The Kronecker delta holds modulo $\mathbf G$.

### Unnumbered intermediate results (p. 19)
After (105) in (104):
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-i\sum_{ia\mu}Q_{ia\mu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}q'_\mu V_a(\mathbf q')e^{-i\mathbf q'\mathbf R^0_i}\sum_{\mathbf knm}\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}(\mathbf G=0)\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}.$$
After inserting (83), with bare phonons $\omega^0_\alpha,\ e^0_{a\mu\alpha}$:
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-\frac{i}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{a\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_a\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}e^0_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}q'_\mu V_a(\mathbf q')\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf q-\mathbf q')\mathbf R^0_i}\sum_{\mathbf knm}\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}(\mathbf G=0)c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right).$$
Lattice sum (as printed):
$$\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf q-\mathbf q')\mathbf R^0_i}=N\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q',\mathbf q+\mathbf G}=\frac N\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q'}\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q',\mathbf q+\mathbf G}.$$
Result:
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-i\frac{\sqrt N}{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{a\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_a\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}e^0_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\sum_{\mathbf G}(q_\mu+G_\mu)V_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\sum_{\mathbf knm}\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}(\mathbf G=0)c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right).$$

### (106) Standard charge vertex
$$\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf G)=\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\rangle\tag{106}$$
Relabelling identity (unnumbered):
$$\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}(\mathbf G=0)=\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}\rangle=\langle\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|\phi^0_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}\rangle=\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G).$$
This uses the periodic gauge $\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf G}=\phi_{m\mathbf k}$ and $c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q-\mathbf G}=c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}$. With $N/\Omega=1/\Omega_{\rm uc}$:
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}=-\frac{i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{a\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}e^0_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\sum_{\mathbf G}(q_\mu+G_\mu)V_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\sum_{\mathbf knm}\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G)\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right).$$

### (107) Bare electron–phonon coupling matrix element (boxed in the paper)
$$g^0_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-\frac{i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\mathbf G}\sum_{a\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}\;e^0_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,(q_\mu+G_\mu)\,V_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\,\rho^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G)\tag{107}$$
Meaning: the amplitude for an electron to scatter $|m,\mathbf k-\mathbf q\rangle\to|n,\mathbf k\rangle$ by absorbing phonon $(\mathbf q,\alpha)$ or emitting $(-\mathbf q,\alpha)$. Dimension: energy, with $N^{-1/2}$ included.

Structure:
- zero-point amplitude $\sqrt{\hbar/2Nm\omega}$;
- **longitudinal projection** $(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf e_a$, so only displacement components along $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$ couple;
- bare form factor $V_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)$, including the basis phase;
- charge vertex $\rho$, i.e. the band overlap;
- umklapp sum over $\mathbf G$.

This is the **bare** (unscreened) vertex. For a Coulomb-tailed pseudopotential, $V_a(\mathbf q)\propto-1/q^2$, so $g^0\propto1/q$ at small $q$. That is a long-range divergence for *every* mode, which electronic screening must remove (in metals) or reduce to the Fröhlich form (in polar insulators).

Special case: $s=1$, $\mathbf G=0$, plane-wave states ($\rho=\delta_{nm}$) gives $g^0=-i\sqrt{\hbar/2Nm\omega}\,(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf e)V(\mathbf q)/\Omega_{\rm uc}$. This is Bloch's 1929 matrix element, Giustino Eq. (2), including the $N$ inside the square root, with $V_0\leftrightarrow V(\mathbf q)/\Omega_{\rm uc}$.

### (108) Coupling Hamiltonian
$$\Delta V^0_{\rm cr}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf k\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{nm}g^0_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right).\tag{108}$$

### (109) Full model Hamiltonian ("bare" version)
$$H=\sum_{n\mathbf k}E^0_{n\mathbf k}c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{n\mathbf k}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\hbar\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)\left(a^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+1/2\right)+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf k\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{nm}g^0_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\,c^+_{n\mathbf k}c_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}\left(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\right)+\frac12\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\,\Psi^+_0(x)\Psi^+_0(x')V(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\Psi_0(x')\Psi_0(x),\tag{109}$$
$$\Psi_0(x)=\sum_{n\mathbf k}\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)c_{n\mathbf k}(t).$$
- $V(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')$ is the bare Coulomb interaction between valence electrons (not written explicitly). $x=(\mathbf r,t)$ at equal times.
- Stated approximations: harmonic phonons and frozen cores. Implicit approximations: linear coupling only, local pseudopotential, rigid ions.
- The renormalized ("DFT") version, p. 21: $E^0_{n\mathbf k}\to E_{n\mathbf k}$, $\phi^0_{n\mathbf k}\to\phi_{n\mathbf k}$, $\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)\to\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$, with "Hamiltonian (109) can remain the same".

### Short-range DFT force constants (p. 21, unnumbered)
$$k^{\mathcal S}_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial R_{ia\mu}\partial R_{jb\nu}}\bigg|_{\{\mathbf R^0_{ia}\}}=0\quad\text{for }|\mathbf R^0_{ia}-\mathbf R^0_{jb}|>a$$
$a$ is the size of the unit cell and $\mathcal S$ means "short range". The induced dipole is $\mathbf p_{ia}=\hat Z^*\mathbf Q_{ia}$.

### (110)–(111) Born effective charges
$$p_{ia\mu}=\frac{\delta p_\mu}{\delta R_{a\nu}}Q_{ia\nu},\qquad Z^*_{a\mu\nu}=\frac{\delta p_\mu}{\delta R_{a\nu}}=\Omega_{\rm uc}\frac{\delta P_\mu}{\delta R_{a\nu}},\tag{110}$$
$$\delta P_\mu=\frac{p_\mu}{\Omega_{\rm uc}},\qquad p_{ia\mu}=Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}.\tag{111}$$
- $Z^*$ is a cell-intrinsic tensor, independent of $i$, with the dimension of charge. $P$ is the polarization (dipole per volume).
- The author calls this "the most important information we extract from DFT calculations because it connects the atomic displacement and the induced macroscopic polarization".
- Implicit convention: sum over the repeated $\nu$ in (111).
- Not stated: the derivative must be taken at zero macroscopic field, which defines the Born (transverse) charge.

### (112) Point dipole charge density
$$\rho(\mathbf r)=e\left[\delta(\mathbf r-\tfrac{\boldsymbol\delta}{2})-\delta(\mathbf r+\tfrac{\boldsymbol\delta}{2})\right],\qquad\mathbf p=e\boldsymbol\delta,\ |\boldsymbol\delta|=0^+.\tag{112}$$
The dipole sits in an anisotropic polarizable continuum with $\hat\epsilon^\infty\equiv\epsilon^\infty_{\mu\nu}$, the electronic, clamped-ion screening. The author says it is "put here only ad hoc and later we will remove it to avoid double-counting in e-e and e-ph perturbative expansion."

### (113)–(116) Electrostatics in SI units
$$\nabla\mathbf E=\frac{1}{\epsilon_0}\left[\rho(\mathbf r)-\nabla\mathbf P\right],\tag{113}$$
$$\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\left[\hat\epsilon^\infty-\mathbb 1\right]\mathbf E,\tag{114}$$
$$\nabla\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf E=\frac{1}{\epsilon_0}\rho(\mathbf r),\tag{115}$$
$$\nabla\hat\epsilon^\infty\nabla\phi=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_0}\rho(\mathbf r),\qquad\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi.\tag{116}$$
Here "$\nabla\mathbf E$" means divergence, and $-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ is the bound charge. Gaussian units follow from $\epsilon_0\to1/4\pi$.

### (117)–(120) Fourier solution: dipole potential in a polarizable medium
$$\phi(\mathbf r)=\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\phi(\mathbf q),\tag{117}$$
$$\rho(\mathbf r)=e\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\left[e^{-i\mathbf q\boldsymbol\delta/2}-e^{i\mathbf q\boldsymbol\delta/2}\right]=-i\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\,\mathbf p\mathbf q,$$
$$\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\nabla\hat\epsilon^\infty\nabla e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\phi(\mathbf q)=\frac{i}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\mathbf p\mathbf q,\tag{118}$$
$$-\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\,\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q\,\phi(\mathbf q)=\frac{i}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\mathbf r}\,\mathbf p\mathbf q,\tag{119}$$
$$\boxed{\phi(\mathbf q)=-\frac{i}{\epsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q}{\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q}}\tag{120}$$
- Check: for $\hat\epsilon^\infty=1$ this is the FT of $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r/4\pi\epsilon_0r^3$, since FT$[1/4\pi\epsilon_0r]=1/\epsilon_0q^2$ and FT$[-\mathbf p\cdot\nabla f]=-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q\,f(\mathbf q)$.
- Real-space form for anisotropic $\hat\epsilon$ (standard, not written in the text):
$$\phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\hat\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\mathbf r}{4\pi\epsilon_0\sqrt{\det\hat\epsilon}\,(\mathbf r\cdot\hat\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\mathbf r)^{3/2}}.$$
- Key property: $\phi(\mathbf q)\propto\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\mathbf p/(q\,\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\hat\epsilon\cdot\hat{\mathbf q})$ is **nonanalytic** at $\mathbf q\to0$ (its limit depends on direction) and **vanishes for $\mathbf p\perp\mathbf q$**. Only longitudinal dipole components produce a scalar potential.

### (121)–(123) Potential of displaced atoms and total induced potential energy
$$\phi_{ia\mu}(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_\mu\frac{Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}\frac{q_\mu}{\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q}\quad\text{[as printed; index labels inconsistent, see Source issues]},\tag{121}$$
$$\phi_i(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_{a\mu\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}\frac{q_\mu}{\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q},\tag{122}$$
$$\Delta V^{\mathcal L}_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=-e\sum_i\phi_i(\mathbf r)=i\sum_{ia\mu\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e\,q_\mu}{\epsilon_0\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}.\tag{123}$$
The superscript $\mathcal L$ means "long range". The dipole of atom $a$ is placed at the cell origin $\mathbf R^0_i$, not at $\mathbf R^0_{ia}$ (see Source issues).

### (124)–(127) Notation that maps the polar problem onto Sec. 2
$$V^{\mathcal L}_a(\mathbf q)=\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q},\tag{124}$$
$$Q^{\mathcal L}_{ia\mu}=-\frac1e\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu},\tag{125}$$
$$Q^{\mathcal L}_{ia\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf k\in BZ}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}}e^{\mathcal L}_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)\left(a_{\mathbf k\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}\right)e^{i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i},\tag{126}$$
$$e^{\mathcal L}_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)=-\frac1e\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf k).\tag{127}$$
- (126) is (83) with the single replacement $\mathbf e\to\mathbf e^{\mathcal L}$.
- Interpretation: the polar coupling acts *as if* each atom carried a point charge $-e$ (like the electron), displaced by $Q^{\mathcal L}$. Its dipole $-eQ^{\mathcal L}=\hat Z^*Q=\mathbf p$ interacts with the electron through the screened "Coulomb form factor" $V^{\mathcal L}$, which is $e^2/\epsilon_0\epsilon q^2$ in the isotropic case (a repulsive $(-e)(-e)$ interaction).
- $\mathbf e^{\mathcal L}$ is dimensionless (EPP's $Z^*$ carries the charge) and *not* normalized. $\sum_a\hat Z^*_a\mathbf e_{a\alpha}/\sqrt{m_a}$ is the standard "mode polarity" (mode effective charge) vector that sets IR oscillator strengths; that concept is not named in the text.

### (128)–(129) Formal identity with the pseudopotential case
$$\Delta V^{\mathcal L}_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_{ia\mu}Q^{\mathcal L}_{ia\mu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\,q_\mu V^{\mathcal L}_a(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)},\tag{128}$$
$$\Delta V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_{ia\mu}Q_{ia\mu}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\,q_\mu V_a(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}\quad(=\text{Eq. 102}).\tag{129}$$

### (130) Polar electron–phonon coupling, compact form
$$g^{\mathcal L}_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=-\frac{i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\mathbf G}\sum_{a\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}\,e^{\mathcal L}_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)(q_\mu+G_\mu)V^{\mathcal L}_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\,\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G).\tag{130}$$
The superscripts 0 are dropped here: renormalized (DFT) phonons and Bloch states are used.

### (131) Polar electron–phonon coupling, explicit form (boxed in the paper)
$$g^{\mathcal L}_{nm\alpha}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\frac{4\pi i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{e}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{\mathbf G}\sum_{a\mu\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}\;\frac{(q_\mu+G_\mu)Z^*_{a\mu\nu}e_{a\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)}{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\hat\epsilon^\infty(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)}\;\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G)\tag{131}$$
- Check: $-i\cdot(-\frac1e)\cdot\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0}=\frac{ie}{\epsilon_0}=\frac{4\pi i\,e}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$. Correct.
- Here $\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}(-\mathbf G)=\langle\phi_{n\mathbf k}|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|\phi_{m\mathbf k-\mathbf q}\rangle$.
- The author says this is "exactly equal to Eq. 4" of (Verdi &) Giustino, PRL 115, 176401 (2015), and that "the difference is in the factor $e$, but it is hidden in the tensor $\hat Z^*$, which has the dimension of charge."
- Giustino RMP Eq. (180), p. 015003-35, the corrected form:
$$g^{\mathcal L}_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=i\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_\kappa\left(\frac{\hbar}{2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf G\ne-\mathbf q}\frac{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}(\mathbf q)}{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)}\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa)}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm sc}.$$
  Compared with EPP (131), Giustino's form excludes $\mathbf G=-\mathbf q$ and carries the basis phase $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$, which EPP lacks.

### (132)–(133) Temporal gauge (Gaussian units)
$$\Phi=0,\qquad\mathbf E=-\frac1c\frac{\partial\mathbf A}{\partial t},\tag{132}$$
$$\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A.\tag{133}$$
In this gauge the **longitudinal part of $\mathbf A$ carries the Coulomb field**: $\mathbf E_L=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}_L$. The transverse part carries photons. Sec. 4.4.1 later separates "free transverse photons and longitudinal Coulomb interaction". Gauss's law is not an Euler–Lagrange equation here; it is recovered in Sec. 4.3 (p. ~37) from Ampère's law by taking the divergence and integrating in time.

### (134)–(136) Electron–phonon–photon Lagrangian
$$L=\sum_e\left[\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}-V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)\right]+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q^2_{ia\mu}}{2}-\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}\left\{\mathbf E^2(\mathbf r)-\mathbf B^2(\mathbf r)\right\}+\frac ec\sum_e\mathbf v_e\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)+\frac1c\sum_{ia}\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}\mathbf A(\mathbf R^0_i),\tag{134}$$
$$p_{ia\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu},\tag{135}$$
$$V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_{ia}).\tag{136}$$
The author's reading of the six terms:
1. Noninteracting valence electrons in the **frozen** local crystal potential (136).
2. and 3. Harmonic ions [Eq. (5)].
4. The free EM field.
5. The electron–field coupling, which "actually represents the electron-electron interaction".
6. The field–ion-dipole coupling, which "also couples (via $\mathbf A$) the motion of ions to electrons, so that it also represents the electron-phonon interaction. Thus in this way we have plausibly closed the scheme."

He also remarks: "Notice the symmetry of the fifth and sixth terms. Both are a consequence of binding energy between currents $\mathbf j_{e,i}$ and the vector potential $\mathbf A$" (refers to "Lecture 9", an external text). Sec. 4.3 makes this explicit, with $\mathbf j_e=e\sum_e\mathbf v_e\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_e)$ (Eq. 166) and $\mathbf P(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}\mathbf p_{ia}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$ (Eq. 167). The ionic "current" is $\dot{\mathbf P}$, the polarization current.

Implicit:
- $k$ must be the short-range $k^{\mathcal S}$; otherwise the dipole–dipole force would be counted twice, once in $k$ and once via $\mathbf A$.
- The short-range deformation coupling $\Delta V_{\rm cr}$ of Eq. (108) is **absent** from (134): electrons see only the frozen $V_{\rm cr}$.

### (137)–(138) and the Lorentz equation (Sec. 4.1, p. 27)
$$\nabla_eL-\frac{d}{dt}\nabla_{\mathbf v_e}L=0,\tag{137}$$
$$\nabla_eL=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)+\frac ec\nabla_e(\mathbf v_e\mathbf A_e),\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\nabla_{\mathbf v_e}L=m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}+\frac ec\frac{d\mathbf A_e}{dt}.\tag{138}$$
Using the convective derivative $\frac{d\mathbf A_e}{dt}=\frac{\partial\mathbf A_e}{\partial t}+(\mathbf v_e\nabla_e)\mathbf A_e$ and $\mathbf a\times(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)=\mathbf b(\mathbf a\mathbf c)-(\mathbf a\mathbf b)\mathbf c$:
$$m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)-\frac ec\frac{\partial\mathbf A_e}{\partial t}+\frac ec\,\mathbf v_e\times\nabla_e\times\mathbf A_e=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)+e\mathbf E_e+\frac ec\mathbf v_e\times\mathbf B_e.$$
- $\mathbf A_e\equiv\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e,t)$.
- The canonical momentum is $m_e\mathbf v_e+\frac ec\mathbf A_e$, which is the origin of the minimal coupling $(\mathbf p-\frac ec\mathbf A)^2/2m$ in Sec. 4.4, Eq. (186).
- Next: Sec. 4.2, Eq. (139), $\partial L/\partial Q_{ia\mu}-\frac{d}{dt}\partial L/\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}=0$. The field couples to $p_{ia\mu}$ rather than to $Q_{ia\mu}$, so the ionic canonical momentum becomes $\pi_{ia\mu}=m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}+\frac1c\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}A_\nu(\mathbf R^0_i)$, which appears in Eq. (186).

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Rigid-ion picture of the e–ph vertex.**
- Every vibrating ion drags its pseudopotential along. To first order the electron feels $-\mathbf Q\cdot\nabla v$, a "dipole-like" perturbation of the potential.
- In Fourier space $\nabla\to i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)$. This produces the factor $(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf e$: at $\mathbf G=0$ only longitudinal displacements couple (Bloch 1929). Umklapp terms ($\mathbf G\neq0$) let transverse modes couple too.
- The $\mathbf G$ sum converges because $V_a(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)$ decays at large $|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|$ (soft pseudopotentials) and because $\rho(-\mathbf G)$ decays.

**(b) Bare versus screened.**
- Eq. (107) uses the *bare* pseudopotential. For an ion of valence $Z_v$, $v_a(q)\to-Z_ve^2/\epsilon_0q^2$ at small $q$ (SI). Then $g^0\propto qV(q)\propto1/q$, a long-range Coulomb divergence for *any* mode, acoustic modes included.
- In a real solid the valence electrons screen it:
  - Metals: $\epsilon(q)\approx1+k_{\rm TF}^2/q^2$, so $g\propto q\to0$ (the Bardeen 1937 vertex; Giustino Eq. 61).
  - Insulators: the screened displacement potential of a whole cell reduces to the dipole field of the Born charges divided by $\epsilon^\infty$. That is Sec. 3.
- EPP does not screen $g^0$ explicitly in this range. Screening is supposed to emerge from the e–e term in (109), or be built into the DFT starting point.

**(c) "Everyone interacts with everyone": bare versus DFT reference.**
- Around the bare Hamiltonian (109) there is no double counting, but the expansion is useless because the bare phonons are very different from the real ones.
- Around DFT, the band energies and phonons already contain adiabatic (static) electronic screening, so the static part of the electronic bubble must be subtracted from the phonon self-energy.
- This is exactly Giustino's $\Pi^{\rm NA}=\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$, Eqs. (125), (140), (145).
- DFT orbitals in the RPA bubble are fine: they define $W$, and $W$ is not counted elsewhere.

**(d) Why polar crystals are special (long-range force constants).**
- Displacing a charged sublattice creates a dipole per cell, $p=Z^*Q$. The dipole field falls off as $1/r^3$ and its potential as $1/r^2$, which is long range.
- In $\mathbf q$-space the corresponding coupling diverges as $1/q$: the Fröhlich singularity.
- Numbers (standard literature values, **not from the manuscript**):

  | Material | $Z^*$ (in $|e|$) | $\epsilon^\infty$ |
  |---|---|---|
  | GaAs | 2.07 | 10.9 |
  | MgO | 1.98 | 2.96 |
  | NaCl | 1.12 | 2.34 |
  | GaN | ≈2.6 | ≈5.3 |
  | h-BN (in-plane) | ≈2.7 | ≈4.9 |

  - Anomalous charges: Ti in BaTiO$_3$ has $Z^*\approx7$.
  - Order of magnitude (my estimate): the GaAs zero-point LO relative amplitude is $\sqrt{\hbar/2\mu\omega_{\rm LO}}\approx0.040$ Å, which gives a dipole of $\approx0.083\,e$Å $\approx0.40$ D per cell.

**(e) Born charge is the "transverse" charge.**
- $Z^*=\Omega\,\partial P/\partial u$ at zero macroscopic field. Because EPP adds the $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ screening of the dipole field separately (Eq. 120), $Z^*$ must be this zero-field charge.
- The Callen (longitudinal) charge would be $Z^*/\epsilon^\infty$.
- Acoustic sum rule: $\sum_aZ^*_a=0$, so rigid translations create no dipole. Hence $g^{\mathcal L}$ vanishes at leading order for acoustic modes.

**(f) LO versus TO, and the seed of the photon story.**
- Eq. (120) gives $\phi(\mathbf q)\propto\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q$. A **longitudinal** optical mode ($\mathbf p\parallel\mathbf q$) produces a macroscopic scalar potential and longitudinal field $\mathbf E=-\mathbf P/\epsilon_0\epsilon^\infty$: the Fröhlich interaction and the LO–TO splitting.
- A **transverse** optical mode ($\mathbf p\perp\mathbf q$) produces *no* electrostatic potential at $\mathbf G=0$. Its macroscopic field is transverse and retarded: it is a photon, giving phonon-polaritons near $q\sim\omega_{\rm TO}\sqrt\epsilon/c$.
- So electrostatics captures only half the story. Sec. 4 restores the other half by coupling $\dot{\mathbf p}$ to the full vector potential.

**(g) The "Coulomb-form-factor trick" (Eqs. 124–129).**
- The polar potential has exactly the same structure as the pseudopotential one if the ion is replaced by a fictitious charge $-e$ displaced by $\mathbf Q^{\mathcal L}=-\hat Z^*\mathbf Q/e$, interacting with the electron through $V^{\mathcal L}=e^2/\epsilon_0\mathbf q\hat\epsilon\mathbf q$.
- Pedagogically, the polar vertex is the deformation-potential vertex with the pseudopotential replaced by the screened Coulomb kernel and the polarization vector replaced by a charge-weighted one.
- In the isotropic diatomic limit, with LO eigenvector $\mathbf e_1=\sqrt{m_2/M}\hat{\mathbf q}$, $\mathbf e_2=-\sqrt{m_1/M}\hat{\mathbf q}$ and $Z^*_1=-Z^*_2=Z|e|$, one gets $\sum_aZ_a\mathbf e_a\cdot\hat{\mathbf q}/\sqrt{m_a}=Z/\sqrt\mu$ with $\mu$ the reduced mass. Then (131) with $\mathbf G=0$ and $\rho=1$ gives
$$\sqrt N|g^{\mathcal L}|=\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{Z}{\epsilon^\infty q}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\mu\omega_{\rm LO}}}.$$
- With $\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2=Z^2e^2/(\epsilon_0\epsilon^\infty\Omega_{\rm uc}\mu)$ and Lyddane–Sachs–Teller, this is *identical* to the Fröhlich form $\sqrt N|g|=\frac1q\sqrt{\frac{e^2}{\epsilon_0\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}}{2}\left(\frac1{\epsilon^\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon^0}\right)}$ (Giustino Eq. 7).
- My numerical check with experimental inputs, EPP (131) versus Fröhlich, in eV Å$^{-1}/q$:

  | Material | EPP (131) | Fröhlich |
  |---|---|---|
  | GaAs | 0.030 | 0.032 |
  | MgO | 0.320 | 0.319 |
  | NaCl | 0.131 | 0.130 |

  The small GaAs mismatch reflects that the input $Z^*$ and the $Z$ implied by LO–TO are not perfectly consistent (2.07 vs 2.18).

**(h) Temporal gauge and "one field mediates everything".**
- With $\Phi=0$ the scalar potential is gone, and *all* electromagnetic interaction, Coulomb included, is carried by $\mathbf A$.
- The electron–electron interaction is "electron → $\mathbf A$ → electron". The polar electron–phonon interaction is "ion dipole → $\mathbf A$ → electron".
- This is the structural reason why EPP can later treat e–e screening ($W$) and polar e–ph coupling with a single photon propagator $D_{\mu\nu}$ and its RPA self-energies, which have electronic and ionic parts.
- The Lorentz-force check (Sec. 4.1) confirms that the minimal-coupling Lagrangian is correct.

**(i) Limiting cases to emphasize.**
- $\hat\epsilon^\infty\to1$: the bare polar vertex, which is what EPP later uses, with screening regenerated by the electronic photon self-energy.
- $\mathbf G=0$ and intraband ($\rho\to\delta_{nm}$): the Fröhlich vertex.
- $c\to\infty$ (in Sec. 7): the electrodynamic vertex reduces to (131). On p. 95, $\lim_{c\to\infty}g_{nm\sigma}=\frac{4\pi i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{a,\mu\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\sigma}}\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}e_{a\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)}{q^2}[\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}]^*$.

  - Acoustic mode ($\epsilon_1=\epsilon_2$): $\mathbf p=(Z^*_1+Z^*_2)\mathbf u=0$ by the acoustic sum rule.

  - $E_0=180.95\times1.83\times10^{-3}/10.9\approx0.030$ V/Å.

- **What to notice:**
  - Only LO produces $\phi$, and $\phi\propto1/q$: the Fröhlich singularity.
  - Increasing $\epsilon^\infty$ weakens it.
  - TO is "dark" for electrostatics but not for electrodynamics.
  - Acoustic modes carry no dipole.

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of EPP

**1. Hamiltonian (109) versus Giustino Eq. (1).**
- *Reproduces:* the first three terms of (109) have exactly the structure of the first two lines of Giustino Eq. (1): bands, harmonic phonons, and linear coupling $g\,c^+c(a+a^+_{-\mathbf q})$.
- *Departs:*
  - (i) EPP keeps an **explicit bare e–e Coulomb term**. Giustino's Eq. (1) has none; all Coulomb physics is hidden in $\varepsilon$, $\omega$ and the screened $g$.
  - (ii) All EPP parameters are **bare** (superscript 0, frozen-core pseudopotential, no valence screening).
  - (iii) **No Debye–Waller (second-order) term**, because Eq. (100) is linear in $Q$. Giustino's Eq. (1), lines 3–4, and Eqs. (39)–(40) keep it, and it is needed for $T$-dependent gaps (Sec. V.B).
  - (iv) Different momentum labelling and $N^{-1/2}$ placement (see Conventions). EPP's $N$ inside $\sqrt{\hbar/2Nm\omega}$ is consistent with its own Hamiltonian. It coincides with the convention of Giustino's Eqs. (2), (57), (58), (61) and (180), which the team's G1/G2 notes flagged as inconsistent with Giustino's own Eq. (1).
  - The bare-reference Hamiltonian is the "older partitioning with bare IFCs" (Grimvall 1981) that Giustino calls not useful in practice (Sec. IV.C / V.A). EPP reaches the same conclusion on p. 21 and switches to a DFT reference.

**2. $g^0$ (Eq. 107) versus Giustino's $g$.**
- Giustino's central $g$ is the **screened** DFPT matrix element $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=\langle u_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}|u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle_{\rm uc}$ (Eq. 38).
- EPP's $g^0$ is the plane-wave form of the **bare** vertex. It is Giustino's Eq. (43), $\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm en}(\mathbf G)=-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\alpha V_\kappa(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$, contracted with Bloch states and phonon amplitudes. Equivalently it is the bare $g^{\rm b}=\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm en}$ of Eq. (142).
- The DFPT vertex is $g=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b}$ (Giustino Eqs. 50–56, 143).
- Special cases of (107): Bloch's Eq. (2) ($s=1$, $\mathbf G=0$, free electrons) and Bardeen's Eq. (61) (same, divided by the Lindhard $\epsilon$).
- EPP does not do DFPT. The DFT route enters only through the "renormalized basis" remark on p. 21.

**3. Double counting, adiabatic versus nonadiabatic phonons (p. 21).**
- EPP's prescription ("in the chain of bubble diagrams exclude adiabatic effects and leave only dynamic effects") is Giustino's split $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}$ (Eq. 125). Concretely: $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ from $W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)$ (Eq. 140); the mode-resolved Eq. (141) with **one bare and one screened vertex**; and the practical Eq. (145) with the static subtraction.
- EPP's statement that DFT states inside the RPA bubble and $W$ are not double counting matches Giustino's use of KS states in $P_{\rm e}$ (approximations i–v leading to Eq. 145) and standard GW practice.
- EPP later (Sec. 7.2, Eqs. 411–418) builds a phonon self-energy from one bare electrodynamic vertex $g^{\rm dyn,0}$ and one screened vertex $g^{\rm dyn}$: the electrodynamic analogue of Giustino Eq. (141) (see the G4 notes).

**4. Polar coupling (Sec. 3) versus Giustino Sec. VI.A.3 and Verdi & Giustino (2015).**
- EPP Eq. (131) reproduces Giustino RMP Eq. (180) and PRL 115, 176401 Eq. (4) (cited by EPP as "F. Giustino, PRL 115, 176401"), up to:
  - (i) the units of $Z^*$ (EPP's carries $e$);
  - (ii) the momentum labelling;
  - (iii) Giustino's $\mathbf G\neq-\mathbf q$ exclusion;
  - (iv) the basis phase $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$, absent in both EPP and the PRL and restored in RMP Eq. (180), footnote 7.
- Its isotropic, $\mathbf G=0$, intraband limit is Giustino's Eq. (7) (Fröhlich, Pelzer & Zienau 1950); see Concepts (g) for the explicit proof and numbers.
- *Purpose differs:*
  - Giustino and Verdi–Giustino use $g^{\mathcal L}$ to make Wannier interpolation work in polar materials: subtract $g^{\mathcal L}$ on the coarse grid, interpolate the short-range remainder $g^{\mathcal S}$, add $g^{\mathcal L}$ back (Giustino Fig. 7, TiO$_2$).
  - EPP uses $g^{\mathcal L}$ as the **electrostatic seed** that its electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex must reproduce (Sec. 7.2–7.3). It inserts $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ only provisionally and later regenerates the screening from the electronic RPA photon self-energy (Sec. 5.1), so $\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\cdot\mathbf q\to\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)\cdot\mathbf q$ (Sec. 7.2.2).
- *Framing differs:* EPP says DFT force constants are strictly short-range and that "DFT neglects long-range correlations". Giustino states that DFPT matrix elements in polar materials *do* contain the $1/q$ Fröhlich divergence; the difficulty is interpolation, not DFT. The two can be reconciled: EPP's "short-range" objects are those without the macroscopic ($\mathbf G=0$, $\mathbf q\to0$) field, exactly like the $\bar v$ (no $\mathbf G=0$) kernel in BSE/TDDFT for $\epsilon_M$. The macroscopic part is then added through Born charges, and later through the photon propagator.

**5. Screened interaction $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$ (Giustino Eqs. 104–105, 110).**
- The polar vertex squared times the LO phonon propagator is the Fröhlich part of $W^{\rm ph}=g\,D\,g^*$ (team notes G3: $W_{\rm ph}=|g|^2\,2\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}/[(\hbar\omega)^2-(\hbar\omega_{\rm LO})^2]$ with $|g|^2=v(q)(\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}/2)(1/\epsilon^\infty-1/\epsilon^0)$).
- $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ in Eq. (120) plays the role of $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ in $W_{\rm e}=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}v$.
- In EPP's full theory, $W^{\rm e}$ becomes the electronically dressed photon propagator $D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}$ (longitudinal + transverse). The ionic RPA photon self-energy (Sec. 5.2) is the electrodynamic counterpart of the $W^{\rm ph}$ channel.

**6. Hedin–Baym equations and the many-body starting point.**
- Giustino (Sec. IV.A–IV.D) starts from the full electron–nucleus Coulomb Hamiltonian and derives Hedin–Baym equations with instantaneous Coulomb $v$. "Retardation" there means only the frequency dependence of $\epsilon_{\rm e}$.
- EPP's Lagrangian (134) replaces the instantaneous $v$ with the dynamical field $\mathbf A$. True retardation (finite $c$) and transverse photons enter, and the e–e and e–ph interactions are both photon-mediated.
- Electrostatic limit: $c\to\infty$, keeping only the longitudinal part. This recovers Giustino's framework (EPP Secs. 6, 7.3).

**7. What the EPP Lagrangian leaves out (relative to Giustino).**
- (134) contains no short-range deformation-potential coupling: electrons see only the frozen $V_{\rm cr}$ of Eq. (136).
- The e–ph coupling from Sec. 4 on is therefore purely the **dipolar (polar) channel** through $\mathbf A$.
- Giustino's $g$ contains both short- and long-range parts ($g=g^{\mathcal S}+g^{\mathcal L}$). A complete theory would add Eq. (108) (with a screened $g^{\mathcal S}$) to the EPP Hamiltonian.
- EPP later states (Sec. 4.4, p. ~39–40) that the short-range ion–electron–ion interaction is already in the DFT $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$.

**8. Internal EPP links.**
- Eq. (83)–(84) (Sec. 1.1) give the displacement and momentum used throughout; Eq. (4)/(5) give the elastic matrix and harmonic Hamiltonian; Eq. (1)/Fig. 1 give the geometry.
- The temporal gauge and Lagrangian (134) are re-expressed with $\mathbf j_e$ and $\dot{\mathbf P}$ in Sec. 4.3 (Eqs. 166–170), where Gauss's law is recovered.
- Sec. 4.2/4.2.1: ionic equation of motion (Eq. 139 ff.) and the diatomic crystal in the long-wavelength limit (Huang-type equations, nearest-neighbour short-range forces).
- Sec. 4.4: Hamiltonian with minimal coupling, Eq. (186), including the ionic terms $-\frac1c\sum Z^*\pi A/m_a+\frac{1}{2c^2}\sum Z^*Z^*AA/m_a$. Total Hamiltonian Eqs. (236)–(237).
- Sec. 5: electronic and ionic RPA photon self-energies (where $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ is regenerated). Sec. 6: connection formulas. Sec. 7.1: LO–TO splitting. Sec. 7.2: electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex; on p. 95 Eq. (131) is re-derived as the $c\to\infty$, $\hat\epsilon^\infty=1$, $\mathbf G=0$ limit. Sec. 7.3: electrostatic limit of the phonon self-energy.
- "Lecture 9" (cited on p. 26 and again on p. ~59, Sec. 5.1, for q2D crystals) is an external lecture note, not part of this manuscript.

## 8. Source issues

1. **Eq. (121): index labels.** The text says "displacement $Q_{ia\mu}$ of the atom $a$ in $\mu$ direction", and the left side is $\phi_{ia\mu}$. The right side sums over $\mu$ and contains $Q_{ia\nu}$. *Corrected (confident):* $\phi_{ia\nu}(\mathbf r)=-i\sum_\mu\frac{Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}}{\epsilon_0}\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}\frac{q_\mu}{\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q}$, the potential from displacing atom $a$ along $\nu$. Eq. (122) is then correct.

2. **Missing basis phase in the polar vertex (Eqs. 121–124, 128, 130–131; also 134 and 167).**
   - The dipole of atom $a$ is placed at the cell origin $\mathbf R^0_i$ instead of at $\mathbf R^0_{ia}=\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r^0_a$. In Sec. 2 the analogous phase was kept: $V_a(\mathbf q')=e^{-i\mathbf q'\mathbf r^0_a}v_a(\mathbf q')$, Eq. (101).
   - *Corrected (confident):* $e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}\to e^{i\mathbf q(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf r^0_a)}$ in (121)–(123), i.e. $V^{\mathcal L}_a(\mathbf q)=e^{-i\mathbf q\mathbf r^0_a}e^2/(\epsilon_0\mathbf q\hat\epsilon^\infty\mathbf q)$, so Eq. (131) acquires $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf r^0_a}$ inside the $a$ sum.
   - This is exactly the factor that Giustino (RMP 2017, footnote 7, p. 015003-35) says is missing from Verdi & Giustino PRL Eq. (4) and that "needs to be retained in order to correctly describe the acoustic modes near $\mathbf q=0$". So EPP's claim that (131) is "exactly equal" to the PRL is true, but both lack this factor.
   - The same simplification appears in the sixth term of (134), $\mathbf A(\mathbf R^0_i)$, and in Eq. (167). This is harmless at long wavelength (it is the dipole approximation within a cell), but should be noted.

3. **Citation.** "F. Giustino, Phys. Rev. Lett. 115, 176401 (2015)" (p. 25) should read C. Verdi and F. Giustino. It is cited correctly on p. 95.

4. **"Exactly equal" (p. 25) needs qualifiers.**
   - (i) $e$ vs $e^2$: EPP's $Z^*$ has units of charge. The author's own Croatian note on p. 95 ("U odnosu na Giustino-a fali ti taj $e^2$…": relative to Giustino, you are missing that $e^2$; probably it sits in the interaction $D$ or in $Z^*$) shows this was still being reconciled.
   - (ii) Momentum and band labelling ($g_{nm}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ with $\langle n\mathbf k|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|m\mathbf k-\mathbf q\rangle$ vs $g_{mn}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ with $\langle m\mathbf k+\mathbf q|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\mathbf r}|n\mathbf k\rangle$).
   - (iii) Verdi–Giustino exclude $\mathbf G=-\mathbf q$ and, in practice, damp the sum with $e^{-a|\mathbf q+\mathbf G|^2}$ (Giustino footnote 7). EPP's sum is unrestricted.
   - (iv) The phase factor of item 2.

5. **Electron-charge sign convention changes between Sec. 3 and Sec. 4.** Sec. 3 uses electron charge $-e$ with $e>0$ (Eq. 123). The Lagrangian (134) and the Lorentz equation (p. 27) use $+\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A$ and $+e\mathbf E$, i.e. $e$ there is the signed charge ($e<0$ for electrons). The page should state this explicitly.

6. **Units switch.** Sec. 3 is SI (stated). Sec. 4 is Gaussian (not announced on these pages; implied by $\frac1c\partial_t\mathbf A$, $1/8\pi$ and $e/c$). Eq. (131) is in a hybrid form. Page builders should pick one system per panel and say so.

7. **Small-displacement criterion (before Eq. 99).** "$|\mathbf Q_{ia}|\ll|\mathbf r^0_a|$" should be "small compared with interatomic distances / the range over which $v_a$ varies". $\mathbf r^0_a$ can be zero (atom at the cell origin).

8. **Lattice-sum step (p. 19).** The middle expression $N\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q',\mathbf q+\mathbf G}$ mixes a continuous integral with a Kronecker delta. Properly: in a BvK crystal $\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\to\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q'}$ and $\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf q-\mathbf q')\mathbf R^0_i}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q',\mathbf q+\mathbf G}$. The final result ($N/\Omega=1/\Omega_{\rm uc}$) is correct.

9. **Notation overloads.**
   - $\Delta V_{\rm cr}$ denotes both the function $\Delta V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)$ and the second-quantized operator (Eq. 103). A superscript 0 appears in (108) only.
   - $\rho^0$ in (107) vs $\rho$ in (130).
   - $\Omega$ is the crystal volume here, but the unit-cell volume in Giustino. In EPP Sec. 1, $\Omega^2$ is the dynamical matrix, and later $\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}$ are self-energies.
   - "$a$" is both the atom index and the cell size (p. 21, $|\mathbf R^0_{ia}-\mathbf R^0_{jb}|>a$).
   - Typos: "unti cell" (p. 20), "atomatic potential" (p. 18), "Of cours" (p. 21).

10. **Definition of $\omega^0_\alpha(\mathbf q)$ is unclear (p. 20).** "obtained from the elastic matrix [Eq. (4)] which is obtained by variations of $H_0$ (95) while the core electrons are left frozen" can be read two ways. If it means bare pseudo-ion forces only (no valence electrons), the "bare phonons" would be ionic plasma oscillations, with no acoustic branch. If it means noninteracting valence electrons in $V_{\rm cr}$, it includes a non-self-consistent electronic response. The text does not say which.

11. **Approximations of (109) are understated.** Besides "harmonic approximation and frozen core", the derivation also assumes:
    - linear coupling only, so the Debye–Waller (second-order) term is dropped (cf. Giustino Eq. 1, lines 3–4);
    - local pseudopotentials (nonlocal ones would modify Eqs. 101–107);
    - rigid ions.

    Spin is also not explicit.

12. **"$g^0$ represents the first non-adiabatic correction" (p. 20).** This is loose. $g^0$ is the bare linear coupling. Nonadiabatic physics comes from treating that coupling dynamically (phonon self-energy at finite $\omega$), not from $g^0$ itself.

13. **Long-range force constants and DFT (p. 21).** The statements "$k^{\mathcal S}=0$ for $|\mathbf R^0_{ia}-\mathbf R^0_{jb}|>a$" and "the DFT ion-ion restoring force will never have a long-range character … neglect of long-range electronic correlations" conflict with standard DFPT practice:
    - DFPT force constants of polar insulators contain the dipole–dipole tail (Gonze & Lee 1997).
    - DFPT e–ph matrix elements contain the $1/q$ Fröhlich singularity (Giustino Sec. VI.A.3).

    What is true is narrower: at exactly $\mathbf q=0$ a periodic calculation lacks the macroscopic field, which is the nonanalytic term added through $Z^*$ and $\epsilon^\infty$. The strict one-cell cutoff is a *model* assumption, used later in Sec. 4.2.1 (nearest neighbours). Present it as such, not as a property of DFT. "Long-range electronic correlations" (van der Waals) are a different issue from the electrostatic dipole field.

14. **Born charge definition (Eq. 110).** The text does not state that the derivative is at zero macroscopic electric field (the Born/transverse charge), as opposed to the Callen charge $Z^*/\epsilon^\infty$. This matters because $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ screening is added separately in Eq. (120). The standard definition also uses a sublattice ($\mathbf q=0$) displacement, $Z^*=\Omega\,\partial P/\partial u$, rather than a single-atom displacement.

15. **Lagrangian (134).**
    - (i) $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}$ must be the short-range $k^{\mathcal S}$. Otherwise the dipole–dipole interaction is counted twice: in $k$ and via the longitudinal $\mathbf A$. This is implied by p. 21 but not stated at (134).
    - (ii) The short-range e–ph term $\Delta V_{\rm cr}$ (Eq. 108) is not included (electrons move in the frozen $V_{\rm cr}$, Eq. 136). The phrase "Thus in this way we have plausibly closed the scheme" therefore refers to the polar channel only.
    - (iii) In the temporal gauge, Gauss's law is a constraint, not an equation of motion. It is addressed only later (Sec. 4.3). Fine, but worth a footnote on the page.

16. **Eq. (105) delta.** $\delta_{\mathbf k',\mathbf k-\mathbf q'}$ should be read modulo reciprocal-lattice vectors when $\mathbf k-\mathbf q'\notin$ BZ. The author's umklapp note says this in words, but the wording ("the cases when $\mathbf k-\mathbf q'=\mathbf G$") is imprecise: the condition is $\mathbf k'=\mathbf k-\mathbf q'+\mathbf G$ for some $\mathbf G$.

17. **Eq. (131) at $\mathbf q=0$.** The $\mathbf G=0$ term diverges at $\mathbf q=0$ (and so does the bare $g^0$ for Coulomb-tailed $V_a$). Giustino excludes $\mathbf G=-\mathbf q$. EPP does not comment.
