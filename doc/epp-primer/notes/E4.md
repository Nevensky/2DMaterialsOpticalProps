## 1. Overview (plain language)

Sections 1–3 of the EPP manuscript built the ingredients one by one: normal modes and phonons, the electron–phonon matrix element, and the polar (Born-charge) Fröhlich coupling in electrostatics. Section 4 now does something new. It puts **all three subsystems** into a single classical Lagrangian, Eq. (134):
- valence electrons moving in the frozen crystal potential;
- ions oscillating harmonically about their equilibrium sites;
- the **full dynamical electromagnetic field**, written in the gauge $\Phi=0$ so that everything (longitudinal *and* transverse) is carried by the vector potential $\mathbf A$.

The electrons couple to $\mathbf A$ through their current, $\frac{e}{c}\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A$. The ions couple to $\mathbf A$ through the current of their induced dipoles, $\frac1c\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}\cdot\mathbf A$, where $\mathbf p=\hat Z^*\mathbf Q$ comes from the Born effective charge. There is **no direct electron–ion coupling term** beyond the frozen potential. The (polar) electron–phonon interaction appears because electrons and ions share the same field $\mathbf A$; in diagram language it is mediated by exchange of photons. The author: "Thus in this way we have plausibly closed the scheme."

Before quantizing (Sec. 4.3 onwards), the author checks that this Lagrangian gives the right classical physics.

1. **Sec. 4.1:** Euler–Lagrange for an electron gives the **Lorentz force** $m\dot{\mathbf v}=-\nabla V_{\rm cr}+e\mathbf E+\frac ec\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$.
2. **Sec. 4.2:** Euler–Lagrange for an ion gives a **driven harmonic oscillator**, $m_a\ddot Q+\hat k Q=\hat Z^{*T}\mathbf E$. The field couples to the dipole, not to the bare coordinate.
3. **Sec. 4.2.1:** a diatomic polar crystal in the long-wavelength limit, which is the classic Born–Huang / Kittel textbook problem, worked out in full:
   - acoustic modes ($\omega=0$) and "bare" optical modes $\omega_O^2=k/M$;
   - the polarization response $P(\omega)$;
   - the Lorentz local-field correction;
   - the **ionic plasma frequency** $\omega_p^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}M)$;
   - the phonon dielectric function $\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty-\omega_p^2/(\omega^2-\tilde\omega_{TO}^2)$, with $\epsilon_\infty$ added ad hoc;
   - the identification of **LO modes as zeros** ($\epsilon=0$, $\mathbf D=0$, longitudinal) and **TO modes as poles** ($\epsilon\to\infty$, $\mathbf E=0$, transverse);
   - the **Lyddane–Sachs–Teller** relation.

The author insists that what is "often incorrectly" called LO–TO splitting is better viewed as the **shift of the dipole-active phonon dressed by its own macroscopic field**. This is the conceptual seed of EPP Sec. 7.1, where the same shift is re-derived as the lowest-order **photon-mediated phonon self-energy** (Eq. 410).

Finally (p. 35), the Lagrangian is rewritten in terms of field densities: the electronic current $\mathbf j_e$, the ionic polarization $\mathbf P$, and the Lagrangian density $\mathcal L_A$. This prepares Maxwell's equations and photon quantization.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **Gauge (p. 25, context).** Choose $\Phi=0$ (temporal or Weyl gauge), so $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\partial_t\mathbf A$ (132) and $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ (133).
2. **Lagrangian (134).** Six terms:
   1. free electrons in the frozen local pseudopotential crystal $V_{\rm cr}$ (136);
   2. ionic kinetic energy;
   3. harmonic ionic potential (from Eq. 5);
   4. free-field Lagrangian $\frac1{8\pi}\int(E^2-B^2)$;
   5. electron–field coupling $\frac ec\sum_e\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)$;
   6. ion–field coupling $\frac1c\sum_{ia}\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf R_i^0)$, with dipole $p_{ia\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}$ (135).

   Term 5 is said to represent electron–electron interaction (Coulomb plus transverse, via $\mathbf A$). Term 6 couples the ions to the field and, through $\mathbf A$, to the electrons, so it represents the electron–phonon interaction. Terms 5 and 6 are symmetric "current × vector potential" couplings (reference to an external "Lecture 9").
3. **Electron equation of motion (137)–(138).** Euler–Lagrange in $\mathbf r_e,\mathbf v_e$. The convective derivative $d\mathbf A_e/dt=\partial_t\mathbf A_e+(\mathbf v_e\cdot\nabla_e)\mathbf A_e$ and the BAC–CAB identity give $\mathbf v\times(\nabla\times\mathbf A)$. With (132)–(133) this becomes the **Lorentz equation**.
4. **Ion equation of motion (139)–(143).** Euler–Lagrange in $Q_{ia\mu}$. Because $\mathbf A$ is evaluated at the fixed site $\mathbf R_i^0$, $d\mathbf A/dt=\partial_t\mathbf A$ with no convective term. The coupling gives a time derivative of $\mathbf A$, which (132) turns into the electric force $\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}E_\nu(\mathbf R_i^0)$ (143). This is a driven harmonic lattice.
5. **Diatomic model (Fig. 3, Eqs. 144–145).** Two atoms ($a$, $b$) per cell, nearest-neighbour short-range forces, and the equations for cell $i$ written out.
6. **Simplifications.**
   - Diagonal Born charges $Z^*_{a\nu\mu}=Z^*_{a\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}$ and diagonal force constants $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=k^\mu_{iajb}\delta_{\mu\nu}$, so the three Cartesian directions decouple.
   - Long-wavelength limit $|\mathbf q|\ll1/a$: neighbour displacements equal on-site ones ($Q_{ja\mu}\approx Q_{ia\mu}$). This reduces the problem to $2\times2$ per direction with lattice-summed constants $k^\mu_{aa},k^\mu_{ab},k^\mu_{ba},k^\mu_{bb}$.
7. **Force-constant relations (Fig. 4).** $k_{ab}=-k_{aa}$, $k_{ba}=-k_{bb}$ and $k_{aa}=k_{bb}\equiv k_\mu$. Physically these are the acoustic sum rule plus the symmetry of the force-constant matrix; see Concepts.
8. **Harmonic drive (146).** $[Q_a,Q_b]=[A,B]e^{-i\omega t}$ gives a $2\times2$ linear system with source $[Z^*_a,Z^*_b]E$, solved in (147) with $\det=m_am_b\omega^2(\omega^2-\omega_{\mu O}^2)$ (148).
9. **Normal modes.** $\det=0$ gives 3 acoustic modes ($\omega=0$, in-phase $[1,1]$) and 3 **bare optical** modes ($\omega_{\mu O}^2=k_\mu/M$, $[1,-m_a/m_b]$), with $M$ the reduced mass. "Bare" means not yet dressed by the macroscopic field.
10. **Polarization (149).** $P_\mu=\Omega_{\rm uc}^{-1}(Z^*_{a\mu}Q_{a\mu}+Z^*_{b\mu}Q_{b\mu})$ evaluated with (147).
11. **Charge-neutrality simplification.** $Z^*_{a\mu}\approx-Z^*_{b\mu}\equiv Z^*_\mu$ (Fig. 5) gives the Lorentz-oscillator response $P=-\frac{Z^{*2}}{\Omega_{\rm uc}M}\frac{E}{\omega^2-\omega_O^2}$ (150). The acoustic pole cancels.
12. **Local field (151).** The dipoles feel the microscopic field $E^*=E+\frac{4\pi}3P$ rather than the macroscopic $E$. This gives (152), which defines $\omega_p^2$, and the solved form (153), where the TO pole is shifted down to $\omega_O^2-\omega_p^2/3$.
13. **Macroscopic electrodynamics (154)–(156).** $P=\chi E$ and $D=\epsilon E=E+4\pi P$, so $P=(\epsilon-1)E/4\pi$. Combined with (153) this gives the **phonon dielectric function** (157).
14. **Adding electrons ad hoc (158).** $1\to\epsilon_\infty$, justified by additivity of susceptibilities in RPA: $\epsilon_{12}=\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2-1$ and $W=v+v(\chi^0_1+\chi^0_2)W$.
15. **Factorized form (159)–(160).** $\epsilon=\epsilon_\infty\frac{\omega^2-\tilde\omega_{LO}^2}{\omega^2-\tilde\omega_{TO}^2}$, with $\tilde\omega^2_{LO}=\omega_O^2+(\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac13)\omega_p^2$ and $\tilde\omega_{TO}^2=\omega_O^2-\frac13\omega_p^2$.
16. **Coupled modes.**
    - **Zeros**, $\epsilon=0$ (161)–(162), are dressed **LO** phonons: $D=0$, so $E=-4\pi P$ (163)–(164), so $\nabla\times\mathbf P=0$ and the mode is longitudinal.
    - **Poles**, $\epsilon\to\infty$, are **TO**: $E=0$, and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=0$ requires a transverse mode.
    - Modes with $Z^*=0$ ($\omega_p=0$) are not shifted.
    - Commentary: "LO–TO splitting" vs "LO shift".
17. **Static limit.** $\omega\to0$ gives the **LST relation** $\epsilon(0)/\epsilon_\infty=\tilde\omega_{LO}^2/\tilde\omega_{TO}^2$ (165). The author notes that $\epsilon_\infty$ was ad hoc and will be generated consistently by the diagrammatic expansion. Conclusion: "the Lagrangian (134) leads to good ionic equations of motion."
18. **Sec. 4.3 start (p. 35).** The Lagrangian is rewritten with $\mathbf E=-\dot{\mathbf A}/c$ and $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$. The field part $L_A$ is expressed through the electronic current density $\mathbf j_e$ (166) and the polarization density $\mathbf P(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}\mathbf p_{ia}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_i^0)$ (167), which leads to the Lagrangian density $\mathcal L_A$ (168)–(169).

## 3. Key equations

### Conventions in this range

- **Units: Gaussian.** Evidence:
  - field energy $\frac1{8\pi}\int(E^2+B^2)$;
  - minimal coupling $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A$;
  - $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\partial_t\mathbf A$;
  - $\mathbf D=\mathbf E+4\pi\mathbf P$;
  - Lorentz field $\frac{4\pi}3\mathbf P$;
  - $\omega_p^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}M)$.

  This differs from Sec. 3, which states "SI units" and uses $\epsilon_0$ (Eqs. 113–131). The conversion is $4\pi\to1/\epsilon_0$ in Coulomb-type factors, so $\omega_p^2=Z^{*2}e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega_{\rm uc}M)$ in SI with dimensionless $Z^*$.
- **$\hbar$:** absent. Everything in this range is classical.
- **Gauge:** $\Phi=0$ (temporal or Weyl gauge), Eqs. (132)–(133).
- **Time dependence:** $e^{-i\omega t}$ (146). With this convention, absorption corresponds to $\mathrm{Im}\,\epsilon>0$ if damping is added as $\omega^2\to\omega^2+i\gamma\omega$.
- **Charges:** the electron couples with $+\frac ec$ and feels $+e\mathbf E$. Here **$e$ is the signed electron charge** ($e<0$), or a generic particle charge. Contrast Sec. 3 (Eq. 123), where the electron charge is written $(-e)$. See Source issues.
- **Born charges:** $Z^*$ "has the dimension of charge" (Sec. 3, Eq. 110: $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}=\delta p_\mu/\delta R_{a\nu}=\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\delta P_\mu/\delta R_{a\nu}$). The first index is the dipole (field) direction and the second the displacement direction, which is the standard convention.
- **Volumes:** $\Omega_{\rm uc}$ is the unit-cell volume. $N$ (number of cells) and the crystal volume do not appear in this range.
- **Indices:**
  - $e$ labels electrons;
  - $i,j$ label cells;
  - $a,b$ label atoms in the cell (in 4.2.1 they are specifically "atom a" with mass $m_a$ and "atom b" with mass $m_b$);
  - $\mu,\nu$ are Cartesian components.
- **Dot products:** written without a dot (e.g. $\mathbf v_e\mathbf A$, $(\mathbf v_e\nabla_e)$). Below I insert "$\cdot$" for clarity.
- **Dipole location:** the dipoles of all atoms of cell $i$ are placed at the **cell origin** $\mathbf R_i^0$, not at $\mathbf R^0_{ia}$. This is a dipole or long-wavelength approximation.

### Context: gauge (p. 25)
$$\Phi=0,\qquad \mathbf E=-\frac1c\frac{\partial\mathbf A}{\partial t}\tag{132}$$
$$\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A\tag{133}$$
In the temporal gauge both the longitudinal (Coulomb) and the transverse (radiation) parts of $\mathbf E$ live in $\mathbf A$. Gauss's law is not an Euler–Lagrange equation in this gauge. It must be imposed as a constraint, which is handled in Sec. 4.4.3, "Longitudinal electromagnetic field".

### The electron–phonon–photon Lagrangian (p. 26)
$$L=\sum_e\left[\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}-V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)\right]+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q^2_{ia\mu}}{2}-\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}\left\{\mathbf E^2(\mathbf r)-\mathbf B^2(\mathbf r)\right\}+\frac ec\sum_e\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e)+\frac1c\sum_{ia}\dot{\mathbf p}_{ia}\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf R_i^0)\tag{134}$$

Symbols:
- $\mathbf r_e,\mathbf v_e$: position and velocity of valence electron $e$, with mass $m_e$.
- $V_{\rm cr}$: frozen crystal potential (136).
- $Q_{ia\mu}$: displacement of atom $a$ in cell $i$ along $\mu$, with mass $m_a$.
- $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=\partial^2V/\partial R_{ia\mu}\partial R_{jb\nu}$: elastic matrix (interatomic force constants, Eq. 4/5).
- $\mathbf E,\mathbf B$: total (dynamical) electromagnetic fields, given by $\mathbf A$ through (132)–(133).
- $\mathbf p_{ia}$: induced dipole of atom $a$ in cell $i$.

Meaning of the six terms:
1. noninteracting electrons in a frozen lattice;
2. and 3. harmonic ions (Eq. 5 of Sec. 1);
4. free Maxwell field;
5. electron current coupled to $\mathbf A$, which the paper calls "electron–electron interaction";
6. ionic polarization current $\dot{\mathbf p}$ coupled to $\mathbf A$, which by also coupling to electrons through $\mathbf A$ "represents the electron–phonon interaction".

Assumptions:
- harmonic ions;
- rigid, local pseudopotentials;
- ions couple to the field only through their linear dipole $\hat Z^*\mathbf Q$ (no higher multipoles, no ionic monopole current);
- dipole approximation, with $\mathbf A$ evaluated at $\mathbf R_i^0$;
- nonrelativistic electrons, with no spin or Pauli term.

### Induced dipole (p. 26)
$$p_{ia\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\tag{135}$$
$Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$ is the Born effective charge tensor of atom $a$ (independent of the cell $i$; defined in Eq. 110). It is linear in displacement.

### Frozen crystal potential (p. 26)
$$V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_{ia})\tag{136}$$
$v_a$ is the local ionic pseudopotential (nucleus plus core electrons), evaluated at the **equilibrium** positions. This is the same object as Eq. (92). Note that the short-range electron–phonon coupling of Sec. 2 (from expanding $v_a(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_{ia}-\mathbf Q_{ia})$) is **not** in (134). Only the polar, field-mediated part is.

### Sec. 4.1: Lorentz equation (p. 27)
$$\nabla_eL-\frac{d}{dt}\nabla_{\mathbf v_e}L=0\tag{137}$$
$$\nabla_eL=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)+\frac ec\nabla_e(\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A_e),\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\nabla_{\mathbf v_e}L=m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}+\frac ec\frac{d\mathbf A_e}{dt}\tag{138}$$
Here $\mathbf A_e\equiv\mathbf A(\mathbf r_e,t)$. The steps that follow are unnumbered:
$$m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)-\frac ec\frac{d\mathbf A_e}{dt}+\frac ec\nabla_e(\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A_e)$$
$$\frac{d\mathbf A_e}{dt}=\frac{\partial\mathbf A_e}{\partial t}+(\mathbf v_e\cdot\nabla_e)\mathbf A_e\quad\text{(convective, "hydrodynamic" derivative)}$$
$$m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}-\frac ec\frac{\partial\mathbf A_e}{\partial t}+\frac ec\left[\nabla_e(\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A_e)-(\mathbf v_e\cdot\nabla_e)\mathbf A_e\right]$$
Using $\mathbf a\times(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)=\mathbf b(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)-(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)\mathbf c$, with $\nabla$ acting only on $\mathbf A$:
$$m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)-\frac ec\frac{\partial\mathbf A_e}{\partial t}+\frac ec\,\mathbf v_e\times\nabla_e\times\mathbf A_e$$
$$\boxed{m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)+e\mathbf E_e+\frac ec\,\mathbf v_e\times\mathbf B_e}\quad\text{(Lorentz equation)}$$
This confirms that term 5 of (134) is the correct minimal coupling. In the temporal gauge, $e\mathbf E$ includes the Coulomb force from all other charges.

### Sec. 4.2: ionic motion (pp. 27–28)
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q_{ia\mu}}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}}=0\tag{139}$$
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q_{ia\mu}}=-\sum_{jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}\tag{140}$$
$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}}=m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}+\frac1c\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}\dot A_\nu(\mathbf R_i^0)\tag{141}$$
$$m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}=-\sum_{jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}-\frac1c\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}\dot A_\nu(\mathbf R^0_i)\tag{142}$$
$$\boxed{m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}+\sum_{jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}E_\nu(\mathbf R_i^0)}\tag{143}$$

Notes:
- (140) uses $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=k_{jb\nu,ia\mu}$.
- In (141) the **transposed** Born charge $Z^*_{a\nu\mu}$ appears because $\partial(\dot p_{ia\nu}A_\nu)/\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}=Z^*_{a\nu\mu}A_\nu$.
- Since $\mathbf R_i^0$ is fixed, $\dot{\mathbf A}=\partial_t\mathbf A$ and there is no convective term. For the same reason **there is no magnetic force on the ions**: the dipole approximation drops the $O(v/c)$ $\dot{\mathbf p}\times\mathbf B$-type terms.
- Meaning: each ion is a harmonic oscillator driven by the electric field through its Born charge. The generalized force is $F_{a\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}E_\nu$, the standard result $Z^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}=\partial F_{\kappa\beta}/\partial E_\alpha$.
- "The electromagnetic field couples to the dipole moment $p_{ia\mu}$ and not to the coordinate $Q_{ia\mu}$."
- $E_\nu(\mathbf R_i^0)$ here is the (total) field at the site. In 4.2.1 it is first treated as macroscopic, then corrected by a local-field term (151).

### Sec. 4.2.1: diatomic crystal (pp. 28–30)
Nearest-neighbour equations of motion for cell $i$:
$$m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}=-\sum_\nu\left[k_{ia\mu,ia\nu}Q_{ia\nu}+k_{ia\mu,ib\nu}Q_{ib\nu}\right]-\sum_{\nu,\,j\in\text{1st neigh.}}\left[k_{ia\mu,ja\nu}Q_{ja\nu}+k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}\right]+\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}E_{i\nu}\tag{144}$$
$$m_b\ddot Q_{ib\mu}=-\sum_\nu\left[k_{ib\mu,ib\nu}Q_{ib\nu}+k_{ib\mu,ia\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\right]-\sum_{\nu,\,j\in\text{1st neigh.}}\left[k_{ib\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}+k_{ib\mu,ja\nu}Q_{ja\nu}\right]+\sum_\nu Z^*_{b\nu\mu}E_{i\nu}\tag{145}$$
Here $E_{i\nu}\equiv E_\nu(\mathbf R^0_i)$, and "$j\in$1st neigh." means the cells $j\neq i$ containing first-neighbour atoms, e.g. $(i-1)_x$, $(i+1)_x$ (Fig. 3). Short-range forces only.

Simplifying assumptions, called "not-so-dangerous":
$$Z^*_{a,b\,\nu\mu}=Z^*_{a,b\,\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}\ \ (\text{for }a\text{ or }b),\qquad k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=k^\mu_{iajb}\delta_{\mu\nu}.$$
These hold exactly for cubic site symmetry, and in the principal axes for uniaxial crystals. They decouple $x,y,z$:
$$m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}=-\left[k^\mu_{iaia}Q_{ia\mu}+k^\mu_{iaib}Q_{ib\mu}\right]-\sum_{j\in\text{1st neigh.}}\left[k^\mu_{iaja}Q_{ja\mu}+k^\mu_{iajb}Q_{jb\mu}\right]+Z^*_{a\mu}E_{i\mu}$$
$$m_b\ddot Q_{ib\mu}=-\left[k^\mu_{ibib}Q_{ib\mu}+k^\mu_{ibia}Q_{ia\mu}\right]-\sum_{j\in\text{1st neigh.}}\left[k^\mu_{ibjb}Q_{jb\mu}+k^\mu_{ibja}Q_{ja\mu}\right]+Z^*_{b\mu}E_{i\mu}$$
**Long-wavelength limit** $|\mathbf q|\ll1/a$: $Q_{ja\mu}\approx Q_{ia\mu}$ and $Q_{jb\mu}\approx Q_{ib\mu}$ for all first neighbours $j$. This gives
$$m_a\ddot Q_{a\mu}=-\left[k^\mu_{aa}Q_{a\mu}+k^\mu_{ab}Q_{b\mu}\right]+Z^*_{a\mu}E_\mu,\qquad m_b\ddot Q_{b\mu}=-\left[k^\mu_{bb}Q_{b\mu}+k^\mu_{ba}Q_{a\mu}\right]+Z^*_{b\mu}E_\mu,$$
with lattice-summed (i.e. $\mathbf q=0$) force constants
$$k^\mu_{aa}=k^\mu_{aiai}+\sum_{j}k^\mu_{iaja},\quad k^\mu_{ab}=k^\mu_{aibi}+\sum_jk^\mu_{iajb},\quad k^\mu_{ba}=k^\mu_{biai}+\sum_jk^\mu_{ibja},\quad k^\mu_{bb}=k^\mu_{bibi}+\sum_jk^\mu_{ibjb}.$$
The paper mislabels the last one as $k^\mu_{ba}$ and mixes the index orders $aiai$ and $iaja$; see Source issues. From Fig. 4:
$$k^\mu_{ab}=-k^\mu_{aa},\qquad k^\mu_{ba}=-k^\mu_{bb},\qquad k^\mu_{aa}=k^\mu_{bb}\ \Rightarrow\ k_\mu\equiv k^\mu_{aa}=k^\mu_{bb}=-k^\mu_{ab}=-k^\mu_{ba}.$$
The reduced equations become
$$m_a\ddot Q_{a\mu}=-k_\mu Q_{a\mu}+k_\mu Q_{b\mu}+Z^*_{a\mu}E_\mu,\qquad m_b\ddot Q_{b\mu}=-k_\mu Q_{b\mu}+k_\mu Q_{a\mu}+Z^*_{b\mu}E_\mu.$$
These are two masses joined by one spring of stiffness $k_\mu$ and driven by the field. They are exactly the $\mathbf q=0$ dynamical-matrix problem with $\hat\Omega^2(0)=\begin{pmatrix}k&-k\\-k&k\end{pmatrix}$.

### Harmonic response (p. 31)
$$\begin{bmatrix}Q_{a\mu}\\Q_{b\mu}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A\\B\end{bmatrix}e^{-i\omega t}\tag{146}$$
$$\begin{bmatrix}k_\mu-\omega^2m_a&-k_\mu\\-k_\mu&k_\mu-\omega^2m_b\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A\\B\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}Z^*_{a\mu}\\Z^*_{b\mu}\end{bmatrix}E_\mu$$
$$\begin{bmatrix}A\\B\end{bmatrix}=\frac{E_\mu}{\det}\begin{bmatrix}(Z^*_{a\mu}+Z^*_{b\mu})k_\mu-m_b\omega^2Z^*_{a\mu}\\(Z^*_{a\mu}+Z^*_{b\mu})k_\mu-m_a\omega^2Z^*_{b\mu}\end{bmatrix}\tag{147}$$
$$\det=m_am_b\,\omega^2\left[\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mu O}\right]\tag{148}$$
I verified (147) and (148) by explicit $2\times2$ inversion: $\det=(k-\omega^2m_a)(k-\omega^2m_b)-k^2$.

Normal modes ($\det=0$), with $\mu=x,y,z$:
$$\text{acoustic: }\ \omega^2_{\mu A}=0,\ \ \begin{bmatrix}A\\B\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix};\qquad\text{bare optical: }\ \omega^2_{\mu O}=\frac{k_\mu}{M},\ \ \begin{bmatrix}A\\B\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\-\frac{m_a}{m_b}\end{bmatrix},\qquad M=\frac{m_am_b}{m_a+m_b}.$$
In the optical mode the centre of mass is at rest ($m_aA+m_bB=0$). "$\omega_{\mu O}$ are still **bare phonons** that have not yet been dressed in [the] macroscopic electric field."

### Polarization (pp. 31–32)
$$p_{q\mu}=Z^*_{q\mu}Q_{q\mu}\ (q=a,b),\qquad P_\mu=\frac1{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\left[p_{a\mu}+p_{b\mu}\right]=\frac1{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\left[Z^*_{a\mu}Q_{a\mu}+Z^*_{b\mu}Q_{b\mu}\right]$$
Here $q$ is an atom label, not a wave vector.
$$P_\mu=\frac1{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\left[Z^*_{a\mu}A+Z^*_{b\mu}B\right]e^{-i\omega t}=\frac{E_\mu}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\det}\left\{\left(Z^*_{a\mu}+Z^*_{b\mu}\right)^2k_\mu-\omega^2\left[m_a\left(Z^*_{b\mu}\right)^2+m_b\left(Z^*_{a\mu}\right)^2\right]\right\}e^{-i\omega t}\tag{149}$$
Charge neutrality (Fig. 5) gives $Z^*_\mu=Z^*_{a\mu}\approx-Z^*_{b\mu}$, and then
$$\boxed{P_\mu=-\frac{\left[Z^*_\mu\right]^2}{\Omega_{\rm uc}M}\,\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mu O}}\,E_\mu}\tag{150}$$
This is a Lorentz oscillator with a resonance at the bare optical frequency. With $Z_a+Z_b=0$, the $\omega^2$ in $\det$ cancels against $\omega^2(m_a+m_b)Z^2$, so there is **no acoustic pole**: a rigid translation of a neutral cell carries no dipole.

### Lorentz local field (p. 32)
$$E_\mu\ \to\ E^*_\mu=E_\mu+\frac{4\pi}3P_\mu\tag{151}$$
$$P_\mu=-\frac{\omega^2_{p\mu}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mu O}}\left(E_\mu+\frac{4\pi}3P_\mu\right)\tag{152}$$
$$\boxed{\omega^2_{p\mu}=\frac{4\pi\left[Z^*_\mu\right]^2}{\Omega_{\rm uc}M}}\quad\text{(ionic plasmon frequency)}$$
$$P_\mu=-\frac{\omega^2_{p\mu}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mu O}+\frac13\omega^2_{p\mu}}E_\mu\tag{153}$$
The author says (151) "can be easily derived for a simple cubic lattice, however the same expression is valid for all other crystal symmetries". That is only true for cubic site symmetry; see Source issues. Note that $\omega_p$ has exactly the form of an electron-gas plasma frequency $4\pi ne^2/m$, with density $n=1/\Omega_{\rm uc}$, charge $Z^*$ and mass $M$.

### Macroscopic dielectric function (p. 33)
$$P_\mu=\chi_\mu(\omega)E_\mu\tag{154}$$
$$D_\mu=\epsilon_\mu(\omega)E_\mu=E_\mu+4\pi P_\mu\tag{155}$$
$$P_\mu=\frac1{4\pi}\left[\epsilon_\mu(\omega)-1\right]E_\mu\tag{156}$$
$$\epsilon_\mu(\omega)=1-\frac{\omega^2_{p\mu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mu O}+\frac13\omega^2_{p\mu}}\quad\text{(the phonon's macroscopic dielectric function)}\tag{157}$$
$$\boxed{\epsilon_\mu(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty-\frac{\omega^2_{p\mu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mu O}+\frac13\omega^2_{p\mu}}}\tag{158}$$
In (158), electronic polarization is added ad hoc via
$$\epsilon_{12}(\omega)=\epsilon_1(\omega)+\epsilon_2(\omega)-1,$$
with the RPA illustration
$$W=v+v(\chi^0_1+\chi^0_2)W,\qquad \epsilon_{12}=1-v(\chi^0_1+\chi^0_2)=\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2-1.$$
Here $\chi^0_{1,2}$ are the irreducible polarizabilities of the two subsystems (electrons, ions), $v$ is the bare Coulomb interaction, and $\epsilon=1-v\chi^0$, so $W=v/\epsilon$.

### Factorized form, LO and TO (pp. 33–34)
$$\epsilon_\mu(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty\frac{\omega^2-\tilde\omega^2_{LO\mu}}{\omega^2-\tilde\omega^2_{TO\mu}}\tag{159}$$
$$\tilde\omega^2_{LO\mu}=\omega^2_{\mu O}+\left[\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac13\right]\omega^2_{p\mu},\qquad \tilde\omega^2_{TO\mu}=\omega^2_{\mu O}-\frac13\omega^2_{p\mu}\tag{160}$$
Corollary (not written by the author, but immediate):
$$\tilde\omega^2_{LO\mu}-\tilde\omega^2_{TO\mu}=\frac{\omega^2_{p\mu}}{\epsilon_\infty}=\frac{4\pi[Z^*_\mu]^2}{\epsilon_\infty\Omega_{\rm uc}M},$$
which is independent of the local-field model.
$$\epsilon_\mu(\omega)=0\tag{161}$$
$$\tilde\omega^2_{LO\mu}=\omega^2_{\mu O}+\left[\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac13\right]\omega^2_{p\mu};\qquad\mu=x,y,z\tag{162}$$
"Dressed phonons" are shifted from the bare $\omega_{\mu O}$. If $\omega_{p\mu}=0$ ($Z^*_\mu=0$) there is no shift.
$$D_\mu=\epsilon_\mu(\omega)E_\mu=E_\mu+4\pi P_\mu=0\tag{163}$$
$$E_\mu=-4\pi P_\mu\tag{164}$$
$\nabla\times\mathbf E=0$ then requires $\nabla\times\mathbf P=0$, so these are **longitudinal electromagnetic modes**.

The other limit, $\epsilon_\mu(\omega)\to\infty$, gives $E_\mu=0$ at the TO frequency $\tilde\omega^2_{TO\mu}=\omega^2_{\mu O}-\frac13\omega^2_{p\mu}$. There $\nabla\times\mathbf E=0$ holds automatically, while $\mathbf D\neq0$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=0$ require **transverse electromagnetic modes**.

### Lyddane–Sachs–Teller relation (p. 35)
$$\boxed{\frac{\epsilon_\mu(0)}{\epsilon_\infty}=\frac{\tilde\omega^2_{LO\mu}}{\tilde\omega^2_{TO\mu}}}\tag{165}$$
This is (159) at $\omega\to0$.

### Sec. 4.3 start: Lagrangian in terms of fields (p. 35)
$$L=\sum_e\left[\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}2-V_{\rm cr}(\mathbf r_e)\right]+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q^2_{ia\mu}}2-\frac12\sum k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\underbrace{\int\frac{d\mathbf r}{8\pi}\left\{\frac1{c^2}\dot{\mathbf A}^2(\mathbf r)-[\nabla\times\mathbf A(\mathbf r)]^2\right\}+\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j_e(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\dot{\mathbf P}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)}_{L_A}$$
$$\mathbf j_e(\mathbf r)=e\sum_e\mathbf v_e\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_e)\tag{166}$$
$$\mathbf P(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia}\mathbf p_{ia}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_i^0)\tag{167}$$
$$L_A=\int d\mathbf r\,\mathcal L_A\left(\{A_\mu,\dot A_\mu,\partial_\mu A_\nu\}\right)\tag{168}$$
$$\mathcal L_A(\mathbf r)=\frac1{8\pi c^2}\dot{\mathbf A}^2(\mathbf r)-\frac1{8\pi}\left[\nabla\times\mathbf A(\mathbf r)\right]^2+\frac1c\mathbf j_e(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)+\frac1c\dot{\mathbf P}(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf A(\mathbf r)\tag{169}$$
The total current that sources $\mathbf A$ is $\mathbf j_e+\dot{\mathbf P}$: the free (electronic) current plus the **polarization current** of the lattice. Varying $\mathcal L_A$ (the next pages) gives the Ampère–Maxwell law with this source, so the photon "sees" ionic and electronic currents on an equal footing. This is the origin of the electronic and ionic parts of the RPA photon self-energy (Secs. 5.1, 5.2).

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

### 4.1 One field to rule them all
The key conceptual move of Sec. 4 is to stop treating the Coulomb interaction as an instantaneous potential, which is what Giustino's and all DFPT-based treatments do. Instead the electromagnetic field becomes a **dynamical degree of freedom** in its own right. In the gauge $\Phi=0$:
- the longitudinal part of $\mathbf A$ ($\nabla\times\mathbf A_L=0$) carries the Coulomb field;
- the transverse part carries photons.

Electrons and ions never interact directly in (134). They talk only through $\mathbf A$. Consequences:
- **Electron–electron interaction** = exchange of $\mathbf A$ between two electron currents. Its longitudinal part reduces to the Coulomb $v$ (Sec. 4.4.3).
- **Polar electron–phonon interaction** = exchange of $\mathbf A$ between an electron current and an ionic polarization current $\dot{\mathbf p}=\hat Z^*\dot{\mathbf Q}$.
- **Phonon–phonon (dipole–dipole) interaction** = exchange of $\mathbf A$ between two polarization currents. This is what produces LO–TO splitting (Sec. 7.1) and the local field.

The "symmetry of the fifth and sixth terms" the author points to is just that $\mathbf j_e$ and $\dot{\mathbf P}$ enter identically as sources.

### 4.2 Why the ion feels $\mathbf E$ and not $\mathbf A$
The ionic coupling is $\frac1c\dot{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf A$. Moving the time derivative from $\mathbf p$ onto $\mathbf A$ (Euler–Lagrange does this automatically) gives $-\mathbf p\cdot\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}=\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$. This is the familiar $-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$ dipole energy, up to a total time derivative: $\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A)/c$ does not change the equations of motion. So the "velocity gauge" form $\frac1c\dot{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf A$ and the "length gauge" form $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$ are equivalent for the ionic dipoles. This is the same equivalence as $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$ vs $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf E$ for electrons in optics, which RPA/BSE practitioners will recognise (velocity vs length gauge for optical matrix elements).

### 4.3 The diatomic polar crystal, step by step
- **Two masses, one spring.** At $\mathbf q\to0$, all cells move identically. The whole crystal behaves like one diatomic "molecule" per cell, with effective spring $k_\mu$ = the sum of all short-range springs.
- **Acoustic** ($\omega=0$): $a$ and $b$ move together. There is no restoring force (translation invariance) and **no dipole** ($Z_a+Z_b=0$).
- **Optical** ($\omega_O^2=k/M$): $a$ and $b$ move against each other with the centre of mass fixed. The dipole per cell is $Z^*(A-B)=Z^*(1+m_a/m_b)A\neq0$.
- **Bare vs dressed.** $\omega_O$ is set by the short-range springs only. The optical mode carries a dipole, which creates a macroscopic field, which acts back on the mode. This self-consistent feedback dresses the mode.
  - For a **longitudinal** wave, $\mathbf E=-4\pi\mathbf P/\epsilon_\infty$ opposes the motion, which adds stiffness and pushes the frequency up to $\omega_{LO}$.
  - For a **transverse** wave in the electrostatic limit, the macroscopic $\mathbf E$ vanishes. Only the Lorentz local field remains, and it softens the mode to $\omega_{TO}$.

### 4.4 The ionic plasma frequency
$\omega_p^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}M)$ is what the LO frequency would be if the springs were absent and there were no electronic screening or local field. It is a plasma of ions of charge $Z^*$, density $1/\Omega_{\rm uc}$ and mass $M$. Including screening, the robust combination is $\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2=\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$.

Orders of magnitude **[not in paper]**, from experimental $\omega_{TO}$, $\omega_{LO}$, $\epsilon_\infty$, inverted via $\omega_p^2=\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2)$. I computed these:

Typical scales: $\hbar\omega_{TO}\sim20$–$200$ meV and $\omega_p\sim$ a few hundred to ~2000 cm$^{-1}$. The LO–TO gap is small in covalent III–Vs (large $\epsilon_\infty$, ~10%) and large in ionic crystals (small $\epsilon_\infty$, ~60% in NaCl).

### 4.5 Local field and the soft-mode instability
The Lorentz term $\frac{4\pi}3\mathbf P$ is the field from all the *other* dipoles. It is positive (it reinforces the displacement) and softens the TO mode: $\tilde\omega_{TO}^2=\omega_O^2-\omega_p^2/3$.
- **Limiting case [not in paper, classic Cochran picture].** If $\omega_p^2>3\omega_O^2$, then $\tilde\omega_{TO}^2<0$: the long-range dipolar attraction beats the short-range restoring force, and the lattice spontaneously polarizes (ferroelectric instability).
- Approaching it, $\tilde\omega_{TO}\to0$ and by LST $\epsilon(0)\to\infty$. This is why incipient ferroelectrics such as SrTiO$_3$ have $\epsilon(0)\sim10^4$ at low $T$.
- This is a nice slider demonstration.

### 4.6 LO vs TO: zeros vs poles of $\epsilon$
- **Poles of $\epsilon$** (TO): finite $\mathbf D$ with $\mathbf E=0$. These are the frequencies where the crystal **absorbs** light: $\mathrm{Im}\,\epsilon$ peaks at $\omega_{TO}$. They are transverse, so they couple to transverse photons.
- **Zeros of $\epsilon$** (LO): $\mathbf E\neq0$ with $\mathbf D=0$, the self-sustained longitudinal field. They appear in **$-\mathrm{Im}(1/\epsilon)$**, the loss function, which governs EELS and the screened Coulomb interaction $W=v/\epsilon$. LO phonons are the modes that scatter electrons via the Fröhlich mechanism.
- **Between TO and LO**, $\epsilon<0$: the **Reststrahlen band**, with near-total reflectivity. In hBN, $\epsilon_\perp<0$ while $\epsilon_\parallel>0$ in 1370–1610 cm$^{-1}$, a type-II hyperbolic band **[not in paper]**.
- **Optics sees TO, Coulomb sees LO.** For readers working with $W$, BSE and RPA this is the key take-away.

### 4.7 "LO–TO splitting" vs "LO shift" (author's viewpoint)
The author argues the textbook term is misleading. The LO mode is the dipole-active phonon dressed by its own macroscopic longitudinal field, i.e. a shifted mode. A mode with $Z^*=0$ is not shifted. A more neutral way to say it: in the electrostatic limit, the macroscopic field couples only to longitudinal polarization, so only the LO branch is renormalized by the long-range (non-analytic) term. The TO branch keeps the "analytic" frequency. In DFPT terms:
$$C_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}(\mathbf q\to0)=C^{\rm an}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{(\mathbf q\cdot\hat Z^*_\kappa)_\alpha(\mathbf q\cdot\hat Z^*_{\kappa'})_\beta}{\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon_\infty\cdot\mathbf q}.$$
- The analytic part gives $\omega_{TO}$. It already contains the dipole–dipole local field, so it is *not* the paper's bare $\omega_O$.
- For a cubic diatomic, the non-analytic part adds exactly $\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$ to the longitudinal mode.

So the observable LO–TO splitting $\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$ is model-independent. Only the position of the unobservable bare $\omega_O$ depends on the local-field model.

### 4.8 Electrostatic vs electrodynamic (what is *not* in this range)
The L/T argument uses $\nabla\times\mathbf E=0$, i.e. electrostatics, valid for $cq\gg\omega$. With retardation, transverse optical phonons hybridize with photons into **phonon-polaritons**:
$$c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon(\omega).$$
This is the natural continuation once the transverse field of Sec. 4.3 is included. At exactly $\mathbf q=0$ (the opposite limit, $cq\ll\omega$), the upper polariton starts at $\omega_{LO}$ for *both* polarizations. EPP Sec. 7.1, Eq. (410), finds exactly this: at $\mathbf q=0$, every dipole-active mode is shifted by $\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$, the analogue of $\omega_p^2$ with $\epsilon_\infty=1$. Interpretation by me, consistent with the photon propagator $D^0(\mathbf q=0,\omega)=-4\pi c/\omega^2\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ (408), which is isotropic.

Polaritons live at $q\sim\omega_{TO}\sqrt{\epsilon_0}/c\approx2\pi\times10^3$ cm$^{-1}\approx6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$, about $10^{-4}$ of the Brillouin zone. That is why the electrostatic (Giustino/DFPT) picture is fine almost everywhere except a tiny region around Γ.

### 4.9 Electronic screening $\epsilon_\infty$ and the RPA additivity
The author adds $\epsilon_\infty$ ad hoc using $\epsilon_{12}=\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2-1$, which holds when the irreducible polarizabilities add ($\chi^0=\chi^0_{el}+\chi^0_{ion}$). Here $\epsilon_\infty$ is the clamped-ion, high-frequency electronic dielectric constant, the same as Giustino's $\epsilon^\infty$ in Eq. (180). Treating it as a constant is the **adiabatic approximation for the electrons**: electrons respond instantaneously on phonon time scales. The author promises that in the diagrammatic theory (Secs. 5–7) $\epsilon_\infty$ is replaced by the full electronic RPA photon self-energy, so no ad hoc input remains.

### 4.10 Derived connection to $W=W^e+W^{ph}$ and the Fröhlich constant (my derivation from Eqs. 159–165)
Using (159) and LST:
$$W(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{v(\mathbf q)}{\epsilon(\omega)},\qquad W^e=\frac{v}{\epsilon_\infty},\qquad W^{ph}\equiv W-W^e=v\left[\frac1{\epsilon(\omega)}-\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}\right]=\frac{v}{\epsilon_\infty^2}\,\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2-\tilde\omega_{LO}^2}.$$
With $v=4\pi e^2/q^2$ and $\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon(0)}=\frac{\omega_p^2}{\epsilon_\infty^2\tilde\omega_{LO}^2}$ (from LST):
$$W^{ph}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\underbrace{\frac{4\pi e^2}{q^2}\,\frac{\hbar\tilde\omega_{LO}}{2}\left(\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon(0)}\right)}_{|g_{\rm Fr}(q)|^2\ (\times N\Omega)}\ \underbrace{\frac{2\tilde\omega_{LO}}{\hbar(\omega^2-\tilde\omega_{LO}^2)}}_{D_{LO}(\omega)}.$$
- The phonon part of the screened interaction is the Fröhlich vertex squared times the LO-phonon propagator. This is precisely Giustino's $W^{ph}=W^eDW^e$ (his Eq. 105) in the long-wavelength polar limit.
- The pole of $W$ is at $\omega_{LO}$ and its zero at $\omega_{TO}$.
- The static limit is $v/\epsilon(0)$, the high-frequency limit $v/\epsilon_\infty$.
- Substituting $\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon(0)}=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\epsilon_\infty^2\Omega M\tilde\omega_{LO}^2)$ turns Fröhlich's empirical coupling (Giustino Eq. 7) into the Born-charge form of Verdi–Giustino (Giustino Eq. 180), $|g|^2\propto\frac{(4\pi)^2e^2}{\Omega^2}\frac{\hbar}{2NM\omega_{LO}}\frac{Z^{*2}}{\epsilon_\infty^2q^2}$, for an isotropic diatomic.

This is directly useful for BSE: exciton binding in polar materials uses an effective dielectric constant between $\epsilon_\infty$ and $\epsilon_0$ (Haken/Pollmann–Büttner) when $E_b\sim\hbar\omega_{LO}$.

### V3. Optical response of a polar crystal: $\epsilon(\omega)$, reflectivity, loss function and $W$
**Shows:** Eqs. (158)/(159) with damping, plus the $W=W^e+W^{ph}$ link from Concepts 4.10.

**Formulas** (with $e^{-i\omega t}$ convention):
$$\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty\frac{\omega_{LO}^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}{\omega_{TO}^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}\quad(\text{or }\ \epsilon_\infty+\frac{\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2)}{\omega_{TO}^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega})$$
$$R(\omega)=\left|\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}-1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}+1}\right|^2,\quad \sqrt{z}=\sqrt{\tfrac{|z|+\mathrm{Re}z}{2}}+i\,\mathrm{sgn}(\mathrm{Im}z)\sqrt{\tfrac{|z|-\mathrm{Re}z}{2}}$$
$$\mathrm{Loss}=-\mathrm{Im}\frac1{\epsilon(\omega)},\qquad \frac{W(\omega)}{v}=\frac1{\epsilon(\omega)}=\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}+\frac{W^{ph}}{v},\quad \frac{W^{ph}}{v}=\frac1{\epsilon_\infty}\frac{\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{LO}^2+i\gamma\omega}$$

**Plots:**
- Re and Im $\epsilon$;
- $R$, with the Reststrahlen band shaded between TO and LO;
- $-\mathrm{Im}(1/\epsilon)$;
- Re $W/v$ split into the constant $1/\epsilon_\infty$ ($W^e$) and the resonant $W^{ph}$.

**Controls:** material preset (GaAs: TO 267, LO 292, $\epsilon_\infty$ 10.9, $\gamma\approx2.5$; NaCl: 164, 264, 2.34, $\gamma\approx8$; SiC: 796, 972, 6.52, $\gamma\approx5$; hBN in-plane: 1370, 1610, 4.87, $\gamma\approx5$; all cm$^{-1}$), plus sliders for $\omega_{TO}$, $\omega_{LO}$, $\epsilon_\infty$ and $\gamma$.

**What to notice:**
- Im $\epsilon$ peaks at TO (optical absorption).
- The loss function and $W$ peak at LO (Coulomb/EELS/Fröhlich).
- $R\approx1$ in the band where $\epsilon<0$.
- $W(0)/v=1/\epsilon_0$ and $W(\infty)/v=1/\epsilon_\infty$, with LST ratio $\epsilon_0/\epsilon_\infty=\omega_{LO}^2/\omega_{TO}^2$ displayed live.

### V4. Electrostatic vs electrodynamic: phonon-polariton dispersion
**Shows:** this range's $\epsilon(\omega)$ (159) combined with Maxwell's equations (Sec. 4.3). It visualizes what EPP adds to Giustino's electrostatic picture.

**Formulas:** work with wavenumbers. Let $\tilde\nu=\omega/2\pi c$ in cm$^{-1}$ and $\kappa=q/2\pi$ in cm$^{-1}$, so the vacuum light line is $\tilde\nu=\kappa$. The transverse branches solve $\kappa^2=\tilde\nu^2\epsilon(\tilde\nu)$:
$$\tilde\nu_\pm^2=\frac{(\epsilon_\infty\tilde\nu_{LO}^2+\kappa^2)\pm\sqrt{(\epsilon_\infty\tilde\nu_{LO}^2+\kappa^2)^2-4\epsilon_\infty\kappa^2\tilde\nu_{TO}^2}}{2\epsilon_\infty}.$$
The longitudinal branch is flat at $\tilde\nu_{LO}$ (no dispersion in this model). Light lines: $\tilde\nu=\kappa/\sqrt{\epsilon_0}$ (low frequency) and $\tilde\nu=\kappa/\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}$ (high frequency).

**Controls:**
- material preset (as in V3);
- $\kappa$ range from 0 to $\sim5\sqrt{\epsilon_0}\tilde\nu_{LO}$ (GaAs: 0–5000 cm$^{-1}$);
- toggle "electrostatic limit ($c\to\infty$)", which collapses the T branches to the flat TO line;
- a secondary axis converting $\kappa$ to fraction of the BZ, using $q_{BZ}=2\pi/a$ with $a=5.65$ Å for GaAs.

**What to notice:**
- At large $q$ the lower polariton → TO and the upper → photon-like. The electrostatic Giustino/DFPT picture is the $cq\gg\omega$ limit.
- At $q=0$ the upper transverse branch starts at $\omega_{LO}$. This matches EPP Sec. 7.1, Eq. (410), where all polar modes are shifted at $q=0$.
- The entire polariton region spans only ~$10^{-4}$ of the BZ.
- There is a gap (the Reststrahlen band) between TO and LO for transverse waves.

### V5 (optional). Lorentz force from $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A$: gauge picture
**Shows:** Sec. 4.1.

A charged particle in a prescribed $\mathbf A(\mathbf r,t)$, for example a uniform $\mathbf B$ ($\mathbf A=\frac12\mathbf B\times\mathbf r$) plus a time-dependent uniform $\mathbf A(t)=-c\mathbf E_0\sin(\Omega t)/\Omega$ that gives $\mathbf E=\mathbf E_0\cos\Omega t$. Integrate $m\dot{\mathbf v}=e\mathbf E+\frac ec\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ (RK4), and alongside show the canonical momentum $\mathbf p_{\rm can}=m\mathbf v+\frac ec\mathbf A$.

**Purpose:** to show that in the gauge $\Phi=0$ the electric force comes entirely from $\partial_t\mathbf A$, and that the canonical momentum (not $m\mathbf v$) is what is conserved in a uniform $\mathbf A(t)$. This prepares the Hamiltonian $\frac1{2m}(\mathbf p-\frac ec\mathbf A)^2$ of Sec. 4.4.

Lower priority than V1–V4.

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of EPP

### Reproduces
- **Lattice dynamics.** Terms 2–3 of (134) are Giustino's harmonic nuclear Hamiltonian: the IFCs $C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$ (Giustino Eqs. 12–22) are EPP's $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}$. Phonons from these terms appear as the second term of Giustino's Eq. (1), $\sum\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+\frac12)$, and as EPP Eq. (236).
- **Born charges and $\epsilon^\infty$.** The same $Z^*_\kappa$ and $\epsilon^\infty$ that enter the Verdi–Giustino Fröhlich vertex (Giustino Eq. 180, RMP p. 015003-35; Verdi & Giustino PRL 115, 176401, Eq. 4) appear here:
  - in the dipole (135);
  - in the ionic force (143);
  - in the polarization (149)–(150);
  - in $\omega_p$ and $\epsilon(\omega)$ (152)–(160).

  EPP Sec. 3, Eq. (131), had already reproduced Verdi–Giustino electrostatically. The author notes it is "exactly equal to Eq. 4" of that PRL, with $e$ hidden in $Z^*$.
- **LO–TO splitting.** Giustino Sec. VI.A.3 says the polar-interpolation strategy "is analogous to that in use for calculating the splitting between LO and TO phonons in polar materials (Gonze and Lee, 1997)". Eqs. (160)–(162) are the elementary diatomic version: $\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\epsilon_\infty\Omega M)$. This is the Gonze–Lee non-analytic term for a cubic diatomic. EPP re-derives it diagrammatically in Sec. 7.1 (Eqs. 406–410), "completely analogous to the non-analytic dynamical matrix correction done by Quantum Espresso".
- **Fröhlich model.** Giustino Eq. (7) (Fröhlich, Pelzer, Zienau) contains $(1/\epsilon_\infty-1/\epsilon_0)$. With (160) and LST (165), $1/\epsilon_\infty-1/\epsilon(0)=\omega_p^2/(\epsilon_\infty^2\omega_{LO}^2)=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\epsilon_\infty^2\Omega M\omega_{LO}^2)$. This shows Fröhlich's empirical constant and the Born-charge vertex (180) agree for a diatomic. Derivation in Concepts 4.10 is mine.
- **Screened interaction.** Giustino Eqs. (104)–(105) split $W=W^e+W^{ph}$ with $W^{ph}=W^eDW^e$. The RPA remark on p. 33, $W=v+v(\chi_1^0+\chi^0_2)W$ and $\epsilon_{12}=\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2-1$, is the same idea at the level of dielectric functions. In the diatomic long-wavelength model, $W^{ph}=v[1/\epsilon(\omega)-1/\epsilon_\infty]=|g_{\rm Fr}|^2D_{LO}$ (Concepts 4.10, my derivation). Giustino also cites Botti & Marques' use of LST-based total dielectric matrices to compute $W$ including lattice screening (RMP p. 015003-43). That is exactly the $\epsilon(\omega)$ of Eq. (159).
- **Phonon self-energy.** The dressing $\omega_O\to\tilde\omega_{LO/TO}$ is a classical, self-consistent-field phonon self-energy from the macroscopic (Coulomb) field. In EPP Sec. 7 it becomes $\tilde\omega^2=\omega^2+2\omega\,\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}/\hbar$ (Eq. 407). That has the same structure as Giustino's $\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}=\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}+2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}\Pi^{NA}_{\mathbf q\nu\nu}(\tilde\Omega)$ (Giustino Eq. 137). The difference: EPP's self-energy is mediated by the photon propagator $D$ with ionic current vertices $J$ (Eq. 399), while Giustino's $\Pi$ is built from electron–hole bubbles with e-ph vertices.
- **Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic.** Using a constant $\epsilon_\infty$ (clamped ions, instantaneous electrons) is the adiabatic approximation for electronic screening of the ionic field. Giustino's nonadiabatic $\Pi^{NA}(\omega)$ corresponds to replacing $\epsilon_\infty$ by $\epsilon_{el}(\mathbf q,\omega)$. EPP does this in Sec. 7.2 via $\Delta\Omega^e$ with the screened electronic $jj$ correlator $\Pi^e$ (Eqs. 402–403, 411). Giustino also speculates about coupled plasmon–phonon modes, "the electronic analog of photon polaritons" (RMP p. 015003-22). EPP's framework treats both in one formalism.

### Generalizes
- **Hamiltonian.** Giustino's Eq. (1) has electrons, phonons and a phenomenological $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$. EPP's Lagrangian (134), and later its Hamiltonian (Eqs. 236–237), adds a **third quantum field** (photons $b_{\mathbf q\sigma}$ plus the longitudinal Coulomb field). It replaces the direct polar $g$ by **electron–photon** ($\frac ec\mathbf j_e\cdot\mathbf A$) and **ion–photon** ($\frac1c\dot{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf A$) vertices.
- **Polar e-ph coupling.** In EPP the polar coupling becomes a derived, second-order (photon-exchange) quantity. In the electrostatic limit it reduces to the Fröhlich/Verdi–Giustino vertex (EPP Sec. 7.3). With retardation it gives the "electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex" $g^{dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\omega)$ (Sec. 7.2).
- **Electrostatics vs retardation.** Giustino's (and DFPT's) treatment is purely electrostatic: $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$, $1/q^2$ Coulomb kernel. Section 4.2.1 is still electrostatic (it uses $\nabla\times\mathbf E=0$), but the Lagrangian (134) already contains the transverse field. The L/T classification by zeros and poles of $\epsilon$ is the bridge to phonon-polaritons, which have no counterpart in Giustino.

### Departs
- **Short-range e-ph coupling.** The short-range (deformation-potential) e-ph coupling of Sec. 2 / Giustino's DFPT $\partial V^{KS}/\partial\tau$ is **absent** from (134). $V_{\rm cr}$ is frozen at equilibrium, so only the dipolar part is kept.
- **Dipole location.** Dipoles sit at the cell origin $\mathbf R_i^0$ (Eqs. 134, 167), so the intracell phase $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$ is dropped. Giustino's footnote 7 notes that exactly this factor is missing in Verdi–Giustino Eq. (4) and "needs to be retained in order to correctly describe the acoustic modes near $q=0$". EPP inherits that simplification.
- **Local field.** The Lorentz local field (151) is added by hand on top of Born charges. In DFPT, $Z^*$ is defined at zero macroscopic field and already contains all local-field effects. The analytic dynamical matrix at Γ gives $\omega_{TO}$ directly, not the paper's "bare" $\omega_O$.

### Note for 2D materials (user context) [not in paper]
In a 2D crystal the macroscopic field of a polarization sheet decays out of plane. $v=4\pi e^2/q^2$ is replaced by $2\pi e^2/(Aq)$ with screening $\epsilon_{2D}(q)=1+2\pi\alpha_{2D}q$. As a result:
- the LO–TO splitting vanishes at $q\to0$;
- the LO branch rises linearly with $q$;
- the Fröhlich coupling stays finite at $q\to0$ (Sohier, Calandra, Mauri, PRB 94, 085415 (2016); Sohier et al., Nano Lett. 17, 3758 (2017)).

The 3D formulas of this range are the starting point that a 2D version would modify.

## 8. Source issues

1. **Sign convention of $e$ (Eq. 134, Lorentz equation).** The coupling $+\frac ec\mathbf v_e\cdot\mathbf A$ and the force $+e\mathbf E$ mean that $e$ is the *signed* electron charge ($e<0$). In Sec. 3, Eq. (123), the electron charge is written explicitly as $(-e)$ with $e>0$. This is not an error within Sec. 4, but it is a notational inconsistency between sections. The page should state "$e$ = electron charge (negative)" for Sec. 4.
2. **Units switch.** Sec. 3 is SI ($\epsilon_0$, Eqs. 113–131). Sec. 4 is Gaussian ($1/8\pi$, $e/c$, $4\pi P$, $\frac{4\pi}3P$). The switch is not announced at the start of Sec. 4. In SI: $\omega_p^2=Z^{*2}e^2/(\epsilon_0\Omega_{\rm uc}M)$ with dimensionless $Z^*$; $D=\epsilon_0E+P$; Lorentz field $E+P/(3\epsilon_0)$.
3. **Vector identity (p. 27).** It is written "$\mathbf a\times\mathbf b\times\mathbf c=\mathbf b(\mathbf a\mathbf c)-(\mathbf a\mathbf b)\mathbf c$", which needs parentheses: $\mathbf a\times(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)=\mathbf b(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)-(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)\mathbf c$. When applied with $\mathbf b=\nabla_e$, $\nabla$ acts only on $\mathbf A$, and $\mathbf v_e$ is an independent variable. The resulting "$\mathbf v_e\times\nabla_e\times\mathbf A_e$" means $\mathbf v_e\times(\nabla_e\times\mathbf A_e)$. Dot products are written without "·" throughout ($\mathbf v_e\mathbf A_e$, $(\mathbf v_e\nabla_e)$).
4. **Dipole position.** $\mathbf A(\mathbf R_i^0)$ in (134) and $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$ in (167) place every atom's dipole at the cell origin, while $V_{\rm cr}$ (136) uses $\mathbf R^0_{ia}$. This is fine for $q\to0$. At finite $q$ it drops the phase $e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r_a}$, the same omission as in Verdi–Giustino Eq. (4) flagged in Giustino's footnote 7. Not stated explicitly as an approximation.
5. **Mislabelled force constant (p. 30).** The fourth definition is printed as "$k^\mu_{\rm ba}=k^\mu_{bibi}+\sum_jk^\mu_{ibjb}$" but should read **$k^\mu_{\rm bb}$**. The index order is also inconsistent within the same lines: on-site terms are written $k^\mu_{aiai}$, $k^\mu_{aibi}$, $k^\mu_{biai}$, $k^\mu_{bibi}$ (atom-cell), while the neighbour terms are $k^\mu_{iaja}$ (cell-atom). They should be $k^\mu_{iaia}$, $k^\mu_{iaib}$, $k^\mu_{ibia}$, $k^\mu_{ibib}$ for consistency with Eqs. (144)–(145).
6. **Justification of $k_{ab}=-k_{aa}$, $k_{ba}=-k_{bb}$ and $k_{aa}=k_{bb}$ (pp. 30–31).** The author derives these "from Fig. 4", says they hold equally for intra- and intercell constants, and gets $k_{aa}=k_{bb}$ from "the very definition of the second derivative". The rigorous origin is different:
   - The **acoustic sum rule** (translational invariance), $\sum_{jb}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=0$, gives $k^\mu_{aa}+k^\mu_{ab}=0$ and $k^\mu_{bb}+k^\mu_{ba}=0$ for the **lattice-summed** constants.
   - The **symmetry** $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=k_{jb\nu,ia\mu}$ together with translation invariance gives $k^\mu_{ab}=k^\mu_{ba}$.
   - Together these give $k_{aa}=k_{bb}$.

   Individually (per cell pair) $k_{iaib}\neq-k_{iaia}$ in general, so the parenthetical remark that "exactly same applies to all intercell constants" should be read for the sums only.
7. **$Z^*_a\approx-Z^*_b$ (p. 32).** For a diatomic crystal this is **exact**, by the charge-neutrality (acoustic) sum rule for Born charges, $\sum_\kappa Z^*_{\kappa,\mu\nu}=0$. It is not an approximation "clear from Fig. 5". It is also what removes the spurious $\omega=0$ pole from (149).
8. **Local field "valid for all crystal symmetries" (p. 32).** The Lorentz factor $4\pi/3$ in (151) holds for sites of cubic symmetry, where the near-field dipole sum vanishes. For lower symmetry it is replaced by a local-field tensor (trace $4\pi$ in Gaussian units), and the near-field sum does not vanish in general. The statement as written is too strong.
9. **Local field applied to Born charges.** The Born charge $Z^*$ (Eq. 110, $\Omega\,\partial P/\partial u$) is the response to displacement in the presence of the *macroscopic* field condition. It already contains electronic and local-field effects. The charge that couples to the Lorentz local field in a Clausius–Mossotti model is the Szigeti charge, $Z_s=3Z^*/(\epsilon_\infty+2)$. Applying (151) with $Z^*$ therefore double-counts at the level of the "bare" $\omega_O$. The observable splitting $\tilde\omega^2_{LO}-\tilde\omega^2_{TO}=\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$ is nevertheless correct.
10. **$\epsilon_\infty$ ad hoc with the vacuum Lorentz factor (158)–(160).** The author acknowledges that $\epsilon_\infty$ is ad hoc. A side effect: keeping $\frac13\omega_p^2$ while inserting $\epsilon_\infty$ gives $\tilde\omega_{LO}<\omega_O$ whenever $\epsilon_\infty>3$, which covers most semiconductors (GaAs, SiC, hBN). That conflicts with the narrative that the LO mode is "shifted" up while the TO "remained where it is". In (160) the TO is itself shifted by $-\omega_p^2/3$.

    A consistent Clausius–Mossotti treatment with electronic polarizability gives $\tilde\omega^2_{TO}=\omega_O^2-\frac{\epsilon_\infty+2}9\omega^2_{p,s}$ and $\tilde\omega^2_{LO}=\omega_O^2+\frac{2(\epsilon_\infty+2)}{9\epsilon_\infty}\omega^2_{p,s}$, with $\omega_{p,s}$ from the Szigeti charge. These reduce to (160) for $\epsilon_\infty=1$. I verified this numerically.
11. **"LO + TO" wording (p. 34).** "According to (159) these are three LO + TO phonons", followed by Eq. (162), which gives only $\tilde\omega_{LO}$. The condition $\epsilon=0$ (161) yields the LO modes only. The TO modes come from $\epsilon\to\infty$, discussed a few lines later.
12. **LO–TO discussion (p. 34).** The paragraph arguing that "LO–TO splitting" is a misnomer, but "makes sense" because "the LO phonon was shifted and the TO remained where it is", is internally inconsistent with (160), where both are shifted from $\omega_O$. It is best presented as the author's opinion. The physically clean statement: the *analytic* (TO) frequency vs the *non-analytic, macroscopic-field-dressed* (LO) frequency.
13. **Electrostatic assumption not stated.** The L/T identification uses $\nabla\times\mathbf E=0$. That is valid only in the quasi-static regime $cq\gg\omega$; with Faraday's law, $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac1c\partial_t\mathbf B$. Retardation (polaritons) is not discussed in this range, although the Lagrangian contains the transverse field.
14. **Gauss's law in the temporal gauge.** Not an error in this range, but worth noting for the page. With $\Phi=0$, Gauss's law is a constraint, not an Euler–Lagrange equation of (134). It is handled later (Sec. 4.4.3).
15. **"Lecture 9" (p. 26).** A reference to external lecture notes, not part of the manuscript.
16. **Typos** (no physics impact): "dipole momentum" (dipole moment), "elestic", "oftenly", "Frolich", "spliting", "macroscopinc", "intercation", "they becomes", "an diagonal tensors", "Lyddane–Sachs–Teller" hyphenation. Also "(i−1)$_{xy}$" in Fig. 3 versus the text's examples.
17. **Born-charge notation (p. 29).** "$Z^*_{a,b\nu\mu}=Z^*_{a,b\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}$" uses "a,b" to mean "for atom a or b", which is easy to misread as a two-atom tensor. Also, (141)–(145) use the transposed $Z^*_{a\nu\mu}$ while (135) uses $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$. Both are correct, but the transpose should be pointed out.
