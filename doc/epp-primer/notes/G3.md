## 1. Overview (plain language)

Section III computed the electron–phonon ingredients ($\varepsilon_{n\mathbf k}$, $\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$, $g_{mn\nu}$) with DFT and DFPT. Section IV asks what the *exact* theory looks like, so that we know what DFT/DFPT approximate. Three weak spots motivate it: (i) $g$ depends on the Kohn–Sham potential, and hence on the xc functional; (ii) phonons rest on the Born–Oppenheimer (adiabatic) approximation, which is questionable in metals and narrow-gap semiconductors; (iii) "retardation", meaning the finite response time of the electrons, is ignored. More fundamentally, electrons and phonons renormalize each other, so the theory must be self-consistent.

The construction follows Schwinger, Kato–Kobayashi–Namiki, Baym (1961) and Hedin–Lundqvist (1969), in modern notation. Electrons are indistinguishable fermions and get field operators. Nuclei are distinguishable and are described by their displacements $\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}$. Phonons emerge as bosons when those displacements are quantized. The Hamiltonian contains only the **instantaneous Coulomb interaction** $v=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|)$ among all charges. There is no vector potential and there are no photons.

A fictitious scalar potential $\varphi$ couples to the **total** (electron + nuclear) charge density. Functional derivatives with respect to $\varphi$ (Schwinger's trick) turn the equation of motion for the electron Green function $G$ into Hedin-like equations: $G$, self-energy $\Sigma=i\hbar G\Gamma W$, vertex $\Gamma$, polarization $P$, and screened interaction $W=\epsilon^{-1}v$.

The key new step is to split the polarization into electronic and nuclear parts. Doing so gives the exact decomposition $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$ with $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$. Here $D$ is the nuclear density–density correlation function. In the harmonic approximation $D$ reduces to the displacement–displacement correlation function $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$, the "phonon Green function". The electron–phonon vertex then appears as the **electronically screened gradient of the nuclear potential**, $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$.

The phonon Green function obeys a Dyson-like equation $[M\omega^2-\Pi(\omega)]D=1$. Its self-energy $\Pi$ is the second derivative of the electronically screened interaction $W_{\rm e}$ evaluated at the nuclear positions: nuclei are point charges interacting through $W_{\rm e}$. Table I collects everything into the closed **Hedin–Baym equations**. Setting $D=0$ recovers Hedin's GW equations for clamped nuclei. Taking $\Pi(\omega=0)$ recovers Born–Oppenheimer/DFPT force constants.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **Motivation (p. 13).** DFT-based EPIs depend on the xc functional, rest on the BO approximation, and miss retardation. EPIs also modify both subsystems, so self-consistency is needed. The historical lineage is Schwinger 1951, Hedin 1965, Nakajima 1954, Bardeen–Pines 1955, Migdal 1958, Engelsberg–Schrieffer 1963 (homogeneous electron gas), Baym 1961, Hedin–Lundqvist 1969 (most complete), and later work by Sjölander–Johnson, Keating, Gillis, Maksimov, Vogl, van Leeuwen 2004 (translational and rotational invariance) and Marini–Poncé–Gonze 2015.
2. **IV.A Operators and distinguishability (pp. 13–14).** Electrons get second quantization. Nuclei get first quantization in their displacements, with equilibrium positions $\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}$ as external parameters. Phonons are quantized displacements. The section works with zero-temperature equilibrium Green functions in the coupled ground state $|0\rangle$. Write $\hat H=\hat T_{\rm e}+\hat T_{\rm n}+\hat U_{\rm ee}+\hat U_{\rm nn}+\hat U_{\rm en}$ (Eqs. 62–67) and treat the crystal as infinite.
3. **IV.B.1 EOM for $G$ (pp. 14–16).** Define $G$ (Eq. 70) and the Heisenberg EOM (Eqs. 71–73). Add the probe $\varphi$ coupled to the total density and obtain the EOM for $G$ containing a two-particle term (Eq. 74). Use Schwinger's identity (Eq. 75) to replace that term by $\delta G/\delta\varphi$ (Eq. 76) and switch to compact notation (Eqs. 78–79). Define $\epsilon^{-1}=\delta V_{\rm tot}/\delta\varphi$ (Eq. 80). Use $\delta G=-G\,\delta G^{-1}G$ and the chain rule (Eqs. 81–82) and define $\Gamma=-\delta G^{-1}/\delta V_{\rm tot}$ (Eq. 83). This gives the Dyson-type EOM with $\Sigma=i\hbar G\Gamma W$ and $W=\epsilon^{-1}v$ (Eqs. 84–86) and the vertex equation (Eq. 87).
4. **IV.B.2 Screened Coulomb interaction (pp. 16–17).** Derive $W=v+vPW$ with total polarization $P=\delta\langle\hat n\rangle/\delta V_{\rm tot}$ (Eqs. 88–91). Define the electronic polarization $P_{\rm e}=-i\hbar GG\Gamma$, the electron-only $W_{\rm e}$ and $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ (Eqs. 92–95). Equations (84)–(87) with $W\to W_{\rm e}$ are Hedin's equations for clamped nuclei. Then include nuclear motion: $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}(\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle/\delta\varphi)v$ (Eq. 96). A second probe $J$ coupled only to nuclei, together with a reciprocity relation (Eqs. 97–99), gives the screening of the nuclear response by electrons (Eq. 100) and the nuclear density–density correlation $D$ (Eqs. 101–103). The result is $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$ with $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$ (Eqs. 104–105, Hedin–Lundqvist 1969).
5. **IV.B.3 Nuclear contribution made explicit (pp. 17–18).** Taylor-expand the nuclear density in displacements to second order (Eqs. 106–107). This gives $D(12)$ in terms of the displacement–displacement correlation function (Eqs. 108–109) and $W_{\rm ph}=[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V]\,D\,[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V]$ (Eq. 110). The only approximation so far is the harmonic truncation. Hedin–Lundqvist's static-$\epsilon_{\rm e}$ (adiabatic) approximation is *not* made.
6. **IV.C Phonon Green function (pp. 18–19).** Newton-like EOM for $\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}$ (Eqs. 111–112). Add a force probe $\mathbf F_{\kappa p}$ so that $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\delta\langle\Delta\hat\tau\rangle/\delta F$ (Eq. 113). Functional differentiation gives Eqs. (114)–(115). This yields the Dyson-like EOM for $D$ with phonon self-energy $\Pi$ (Eqs. 116–117). In frequency space (Eqs. 118–119), the acoustic sum rule (Eq. 120) gives the compact $\Pi=ZZ'\partial\partial'[W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)\cdots]$ (Eq. 121) and the nuclear density with zero-point smearing (Eq. 122).
7. **IV.D Hedin–Baym equations (p. 20, Table I).** The closed self-consistent set. It is nonperturbative, it does not assume BO, and it reduces to Hedin's equations when $D=0$.
8. **V.A.1 (pp. 20–21).** Formal solution $\mathbf D(\omega)=[\mathbf M\omega^2-\Pi(\omega)]^{-1}$ (Eq. 123). Eigenmodes are roots of $\Omega_\nu(\omega)=\omega$ (Eq. 124). Split $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)$ with $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(0)$ (Eq. 125). Show that $\Pi^{\rm A}$ equals the BO/Hellmann–Feynman force constants (Eqs. 126–128), but dressed by the exact many-body $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)$.

## 3. Key equations (faithful transcriptions with the paper's numbering)

### 3.0 Conventions (state these on the page)

- **Units: SI, $\hbar$ explicit.** $v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|)$ is the Coulomb interaction *energy* between two particles of charge $e$ (p. 14). All potentials ($V_{\rm tot}$, $V_{\rm n}$, $\varphi$, $V_\kappa$, $V^{\rm en}$) are **potential energies of an electron**.
- **Charge-density units (p. 14):** density operators are "in units of the electron charge". $\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)=\sum_\sigma\hat\psi^\dagger\hat\psi\ge0$, while $\hat n_{\rm n}=-\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\delta(\cdots)\le0$. The total charge density is $-e\langle 0|\hat n(\mathbf r)|0\rangle$ with $\hat n=\hat n_{\rm e}+\hat n_{\rm n}$.
- **Compact 4-point notation (pp. 15–16):** $1\equiv(\mathbf x_1t_1)=(\mathbf r_1\sigma_1t_1)$, $2\equiv(\mathbf x_2t_2)$, and $1^+\equiv(\mathbf r_1,t_1+\eta)$ with $\eta\to0^+$. $\int d\mathbf x=\sum_\sigma\int d\mathbf r$. $\int d(34)\equiv\int d3\,d4$. **Instantaneous Coulomb:** $v(12)=v(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2)\delta(t_1-t_2)$ (stated under Eq. 74). This $\delta(t_1-t_2)$ is the "electrostatic" baseline.
- **Time Fourier transform (p. 19):** $f(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,f(t)e^{i\omega t}$, hence $\partial_t^2\to-\omega^2$. Equilibrium quantities depend only on time differences: $W_{\rm e}(\mathbf rt,\mathbf r't')=W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',t-t')$.
- **Infinite crystal (p. 14):** $N_p^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf q}\to\Omega_{\rm BZ}^{-1}\int d\mathbf q$, $\int d\mathbf q\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}=\Omega_{\rm BZ}\delta_{p0}$, and $\sum_pe^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}=\Omega_{\rm BZ}\delta(\mathbf q)$. Supercell lattice vectors $\mathbf T$ drop out.
- **Indices:** $\kappa$ = atom in the cell, $p$ = cell, $\alpha,\alpha',\gamma$ = Cartesian directions. $\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}$ = equilibrium position (external parameter), $\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}$ = displacement operator. $Z_\kappa$ = nuclear (or ionic) charge, $M_\kappa$ = mass.
- **Temperature:** zero-temperature, equilibrium, time-ordered Green functions in the exact coupled electron–nuclear ground state $|0\rangle$. $\langle\cdots\rangle\equiv\langle0|\cdots|0\rangle$. Finite $T$ is treated in Sec. V by Matsubara continuation.
- **$\hbar$ placement:** $G=-(i/\hbar)\langle T\psi\psi^\dagger\rangle$ carries $1/\hbar$. Therefore $\Sigma=+i\hbar G\Gamma W$, $P_{\rm e}=-i\hbar GG\Gamma$ and $n_{\rm e}=-i\hbar G(11^+)$. In $\hbar=1$ texts these read $\Sigma=iGW\Gamma$ and $P=-iGG\Gamma$.
- **Dimensions** (useful for checks): $G\sim$ 1/(J·s·m³); $v(12),W(12)\sim$ J/s (because of $\delta(t)$); $W(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)\sim$ J; $\epsilon^{-1}(12)\sim$ 1/(m³·s); $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)\sim$ m²/J; $\Pi\sim$ J/m² (force constants).

### 3.2 IV.A Operators and Hamiltonian (pp. 13–14)

Field operators obey $\{\hat\psi(\mathbf x),\hat\psi(\mathbf x')\}=\{\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x),\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\}=0$ and $\{\hat\psi(\mathbf x),\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\}=\delta(\mathbf x-\mathbf x')$.

$$\hat H=\hat T_{\rm e}+\hat T_{\rm n}+\hat U_{\rm ee}+\hat U_{\rm nn}+\hat U_{\rm en} \qquad (62)$$

The most general **nonrelativistic** Hamiltonian of coupled electrons and nuclei. It has no spin–orbit coupling, no vector potential and no radiation field.

$$\hat T_{\rm e}=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\int d\mathbf x\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\nabla^2\hat\psi(\mathbf x) \qquad (63)$$

$$\hat U_{\rm ee}=\frac12\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\,\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)[\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r')-\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')]v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r') \qquad (64)$$

- $\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)=\sum_\sigma\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$. The $-\delta$ removes the unphysical self-interaction of a continuous charge distribution.
- $\hat T_{\rm n}$ is the nuclear kinetic energy, i.e. the last term of Eq. (19): $-\sum_{\kappa\alpha p}(\hbar^2/2M_\kappa)\partial^2/\partial\tau^2_{\kappa\alpha p}$.

$$\hat U_{\rm nn}=\frac12\sum_{\kappa'p'\neq\kappa p}Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa'}\,v(\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}+\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p},\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}+\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa'p'}) \qquad (65)$$

- Nuclei are distinguishable, so all pairwise interactions are counted individually and the self-term is excluded. This is the "asymmetry" between Eqs. (64) and (65) stressed on p. 14.

$$\hat U_{\rm en}=\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\,\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf r')v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r') \qquad (66)$$

$$\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)=-\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\,\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}-\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}) \qquad (67)$$

- Point nuclei. The displacement *operator* sits inside the delta function and is expanded in Eq. (107). Later the bare $Z_\kappa v$ is replaced by pseudopotentials $V_\kappa$ (see Eq. 110).

### 3.3 IV.B.1 Electron Green function, equation of motion and self-energy (pp. 14–16)

$$\hat H=\hat T_{\rm n}+\hat U_{\rm nn}+\int d\mathbf x\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2+\hat V_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)\right]\hat\psi(\mathbf x)+\frac12\int d\mathbf x\,d\mathbf x'\,v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x) \qquad (68)$$

$$\hat V_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)=\int d\mathbf r'\,v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf r') \qquad (69)$$

- $\hat V_{\rm n}$ is an *operator*, because it depends on the nuclear displacements. It is negative near nuclei.

$$G(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle0|\hat T\hat\psi(\mathbf xt)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x't')|0\rangle \qquad (70)$$

- The time-ordered one-electron Green function. Wick's fermionic time ordering is $\hat T\psi(\mathbf xt)\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x't')=\theta(t-t')\psi\psi^\dagger-\theta(t'-t)\psi^\dagger\psi$.
- For $t>t'$, $iG$ is the amplitude to find an electron at $\mathbf x$ at time $t$ after one was added at $\mathbf x'$ at $t'$. For $t<t'$ it describes hole propagation.

$$\hat\psi(\mathbf xt)=e^{it\hat H/\hbar}\hat\psi(\mathbf x)e^{-it\hat H/\hbar} \qquad (71)$$
$$i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat\psi(\mathbf xt)=[\hat\psi(\mathbf xt),\hat H] \qquad (72)$$
$$i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat\psi(\mathbf xt)=\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2+\int d\mathbf r'\,v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\hat n(\mathbf r't)\right]\hat\psi(\mathbf xt) \qquad (73)$$

- In Eq. (73) **$\hat n=\hat n_{\rm e}+\hat n_{\rm n}$ is the total density**. Electron–electron and electron–nucleus Coulomb terms merge into one term. This merger is why the probe $\varphi$ and the dielectric response below involve *all* charges.

$$\left[i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2-\varphi(\mathbf rt)\right]G(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't')=\delta(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't')-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int d\mathbf r''dt''\,v(\mathbf rt,\mathbf r''t'')\langle\hat T\hat n(\mathbf r''t'')\psi(\mathbf xt)\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x't')\rangle \qquad (74)$$

- $v(\mathbf rt,\mathbf r''t'')=v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r'')\delta(t-t'')$, which is **instantaneous**. $\varphi(\mathbf rt)$ is a fictitious scalar potential entering through $\hat H_1(t)=\int d\mathbf r\,\hat n(\mathbf r)\varphi(\mathbf rt)$. It couples to the total charge, induces "forced oscillations" whose resonances are many-body eigenmodes, and is set to zero at the end.
- Strictly, $\varphi$ makes $\hat H$ time dependent and requires the interaction picture, with $\hat U(t)=\hat T\exp[-i\hbar^{-1}\int_0^t\hat H(t')dt']$. The paper skips this and refers to Aryasetiawan and Gunnarsson (1998).

$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat T\hat a(t_1)\hat b(t_2)\rangle}{\delta\varphi(\mathbf r''t'')}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T[\hat n(\mathbf r''t'')-\langle\hat n(\mathbf r''t'')\rangle]\hat a(t_1)\hat b(t_2)\rangle \qquad (75)$$

- **Schwinger's identity** (Kato–Kobayashi–Namiki 1960, App. II; Hedin–Lundqvist 1969, App. B.a). It converts a three-operator correlation into a functional derivative of a two-operator one. $\delta/\delta\varphi$ is a *functional* derivative, not a Dirac delta.

$$\left[i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2-V_{\rm tot}(\mathbf rt)-i\hbar\int d\mathbf r''dt''\,v(\mathbf rt+\eta,\mathbf r''t'')\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi(\mathbf r''t'')}\right]G(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't')=\delta(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't') \qquad (76)$$
$$V_{\rm tot}(\mathbf rt)=\int d\mathbf r'\,v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\langle\hat n(\mathbf r't)\rangle+\varphi(\mathbf rt) \qquad (77)$$

- Derivation check: with $\hat a\hat b=\psi\psi^\dagger$ and $\langle T\psi\psi^\dagger\rangle=i\hbar G$, one gets $-\tfrac{i}{\hbar}\langle T\hat n\psi\psi^\dagger\rangle=\langle\hat n\rangle G+i\hbar\,\delta G/\delta\varphi$. The mean-field part builds $V_{\rm tot}$ (Hartree potential of electrons **and** nuclei, plus $\varphi$). The fluctuation part becomes the functional derivative. Equation (76) is due to Kato, Kobayashi and Namiki (1960).

Compact form:
$$\left[i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t_1}+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2(1)-V_{\rm tot}(1)-i\hbar\int d3\,v(1^+3)\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi(3)}\right]G(12)=\delta(12) \qquad (78)$$
$$V_{\rm tot}(1)=\int d2\,v(12)\langle\hat n(2)\rangle+\varphi(1) \qquad (79)$$

It is the many-body counterpart of the Sec. III.B.4 dielectric matrix. From Eq. (79), $\epsilon^{-1}=1+v\,\delta\langle\hat n\rangle/\delta\varphi=1+v\chi$, where $\chi$ is the *reducible* density response.

$$\frac{\delta G^{-1}(45)}{\delta\varphi(3)}=\int d6\,\frac{\delta G^{-1}(45)}{\delta V_{\rm tot}(6)}\frac{\delta V_{\rm tot}(6)}{\delta\varphi(3)} \qquad (82)$$

### 3.4 IV.B.2 The screened Coulomb interaction (pp. 16–17)

$$W(12)=v(12)+\int d(34)\,v(13)\frac{\delta\langle\hat n(3)\rangle}{\delta V_{\rm tot}(4)}W(42) \qquad (88)$$

**Polarization propagator (total, electrons + nuclei; irreducible with respect to $v$ because $V_{\rm tot}$ already contains the Hartree field):**
$$P(12)=\frac{\delta\langle\hat n(1)\rangle}{\delta V_{\rm tot}(2)} \qquad (89)$$
$$W(12)=v(12)+\int d(34)\,v(13)P(34)W(42) \qquad (90)$$

**Nuclei allowed to move.** Combining Eqs. (80), (86), (88), (92), (94), (95):
$$W(12)=W_{\rm e}(12)+\int d(34)\,W_{\rm e}(13)\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle}{\delta\varphi(4)}v(42) \qquad (96)$$

- My reformulation, which helps when teaching it: write $P=P_{\rm e}+P_{\rm n}$ with $P_{\rm n}\equiv\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle/\delta V_{\rm tot}$. Then $W=v+v(P_{\rm e}+P_{\rm n})W$ implies $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}P_{\rm n}W$. Since $P_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}=\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle/\delta\varphi$ and $W=\epsilon^{-1}v$, this is Eq. (96).

**The "delicate passage": reciprocity with a second probe $J$ coupling only to nuclei.**
$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle}{\delta\varphi(2)}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T[\hat n(2)-\langle\hat n(2)\rangle][\hat n_{\rm n}(1)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle]\rangle \qquad (97)$$

With $\hat H_2(t)=\int d\mathbf r\,\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf rt)J(\mathbf rt)$:
$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat n(1)\rangle}{\delta J(2)}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T[\hat n_{\rm n}(2)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(2)\rangle][\hat n(1)-\langle\hat n(1)\rangle]\rangle \qquad (98)$$
$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle}{\delta\varphi(2)}=\frac{\delta\langle\hat n(2)\rangle}{\delta J(1)} \qquad (99)$$

- Equation (99) is a reciprocity (Onsager-like) relation for time-ordered correlation functions. The nuclear response to a probe of all charges equals the total-charge response to a probe of the nuclei only.

Chain rule $\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm e}\rangle/\delta J=\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm e}\rangle/\delta V_{\rm tot}\times\delta V_{\rm tot}/\delta\langle n\rangle\times\delta\langle n\rangle/\delta J$ (then add $\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle/\delta J$):
$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat n(1)\rangle}{\delta J(2)}=\int d3\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle}{\delta J(2)} \qquad (100)$$

- Meaning: when the nuclei move, the electrons follow and screen the change. The *total* charge response is the bare nuclear response screened by $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$. See Source issues for the index order: it should be $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(31)$.

$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle}{\delta J(2)}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T[\hat n_{\rm n}(2)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(2)\rangle]\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle \qquad (101)$$
$$\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle/\delta J(2)=D(21) \qquad (102)$$

**Nuclear density–density correlation function:**
$$D(12)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T[\hat n_{\rm n}(1)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle][\hat n_{\rm n}(2)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(2)\rangle]\rangle \qquad (103)$$

- $D(12)=D(21)$. It is the fully interacting nuclear charge-fluctuation propagator, in which the nuclei interact through the electron-screened $W_{\rm e}$.

**Main result of IV.B (Hedin–Lundqvist 1969):**
$$W(12)=W_{\rm e}(12)+W_{\rm ph}(12) \qquad (104)$$
$$W_{\rm ph}(12)=\int d(34)\,W_{\rm e}(13)D(34)W_{\rm e}(24) \qquad (105)$$

- Physically, an electron at 1 polarizes the electrons, giving the screened field $W_{\rm e}$. That field pushes the nuclei, which propagate a charge fluctuation $D$. The nuclear charge fluctuation acts back on an electron at 2, again screened by the electrons ($W_{\rm e}$). In diagrams this is: (dashed $W_{\rm e}$)–(wavy $D$)–(dashed $W_{\rm e}$).

### 3.5 IV.B.3 Nuclear contribution to $W$ made explicit (pp. 17–18)

$$\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf u)=\delta(\mathbf r)-\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\delta(\mathbf r)+\tfrac12\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\nabla\delta(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf u \qquad (106)$$

- $\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\nabla\cdot\mathbf u\equiv\sum_{\alpha\alpha'}u_\alpha u_{\alpha'}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_{\alpha'}$. Follows from the Fourier representation of $\delta$.

$$\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)=n^0_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)+\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}\cdot\nabla\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})-\frac12\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}\cdot\nabla\nabla\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\cdot\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p} \qquad (107)$$

- $n^0_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)=-\sum Z_\kappa\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})$ is the classical point-charge lattice. The linear term is a lattice of **point dipoles** $Z_\kappa\Delta\boldsymbol\tau$. This is the seed of polar (Fröhlich) coupling and of the ionic dielectric response. The quadratic term gives zero-point/thermal smearing (Debye–Waller-like, Eq. 122). This is the **harmonic approximation** (second-order truncation).

$$D(12)=\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}Z_\kappa\nabla_{1,\alpha}\delta(\mathbf r_1-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\,D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t_1t_2)\,Z_{\kappa'}\nabla_{2,\alpha'}\delta(\mathbf r_2-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}) \qquad (108)$$

**Displacement–displacement correlation function ("phonon Green function"):**
$$D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}(t)\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')\rangle \qquad (109)$$

- Heisenberg time evolution as in Eq. (71). Strictly, "phonon Green function" should be reserved for $-(i/\hbar)\langle\hat T\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}(t)\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q'\nu'}(t')\rangle$ (Sec. V.A.1). Equation (108) keeps only the linear terms of Eq. (107) in each density fluctuation (see Source issues).

**$W_{\rm ph}$ in terms of the phonon propagator and the screened e–ph vertex:**
$$W_{\rm ph}(12)=\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}\int d(34)\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)\nabla_{3,\alpha}V_\kappa(\mathbf r_3-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\,D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t_3t_4)\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(24)\nabla_{4,\alpha'}V_{\kappa'}(\mathbf r_4-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}) \qquad (110)$$

- $V_\kappa$ is the bare Coulomb potential of a nucleus, $V_\kappa(\mathbf r)=-Z_\kappa e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r|)$, or its ionic pseudopotential.
- Derivation check (I verified signs): $\int d\mathbf r_3\,W_{\rm e}(1,3)Z_\kappa\nabla_{3,\alpha}\delta(\mathbf r_3-\boldsymbol\tau^0)=-\int d3\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)\nabla_{3,\alpha}V_\kappa(\mathbf r_3-\boldsymbol\tau^0)$, using $Z_\kappa v(\mathbf r,\boldsymbol\tau)=-V_\kappa(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau)$. Two such factors give the $+$ sign.
- **This is the central identification of the e–ph vertex.** The coupling function is $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$: the gradient of the nuclear potential, screened **dynamically** by the full many-body $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)$, which depends on $t_1-t_3$. In Sec. V this becomes $g_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r,\omega)=\int d\mathbf r'\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)g^{\rm b}_{\mathbf q\nu}(\mathbf r')$ with $g^{\rm b}_{\mathbf q\nu}=\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V^{\rm en}$ (Eqs. 142–144, p. 24). Symbolically, **$W_{\rm ph}=g\,D\,g^*$** with *dressed vertices at both ends* (Eq. 156, p. 27: $\Sigma^{\rm FM}\propto G\Gamma g^{\rm cc}Dg$).
- **Approximation not made:** Hedin–Lundqvist replaced $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$ in Eq. (110) by its static limit, which amounts to the BO adiabatic approximation for the vertex. Giustino keeps the "retardation effects" (Bardeen–Pines 1955; Baym 1961). Here "retardation" means **frequency dependence of electronic screening**, not finite speed of light. The only approximation up to this point is the harmonic truncation. Gillis (1970) generalized beyond harmonic, but the result is "exceedingly complex".

### 3.6 IV.C Phonon Green function (pp. 18–19)

Heisenberg EOM: $i\hbar\,\partial_t\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}(t)=[\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}(t),\hat H]$. Differentiating once more:
$$M_\kappa\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}=-\frac{M_\kappa}{\hbar^2}[[\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p},\hat H],\hat H] \qquad (111)$$

$$M_\kappa\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}(t)=Z_\kappa\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\,\hat n^{(\kappa p)}(\mathbf rt)v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\times\{-\nabla'\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})+\nabla'[\nabla'\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\cdot\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}(t)]\}\qquad (112)$$

- This is **Newton's equation**: mass × acceleration = Coulomb force on nucleus $\kappa p$ from the total charge density $\hat n^{(\kappa p)}$ of electrons and all *other* nuclei, with the nucleus's own position expanded to first order in $\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}$ via Eq. (106). $\nabla'$ acts on $\mathbf r'$. The force is purely electrostatic (Coulomb). There is no Lorentz force and no radiation reaction.
- Check: $-\partial_{\Delta\tau}$ of $-Z_\kappa\int\!\!\int\hat n^{(\kappa p)}v\,\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0-\Delta\boldsymbol\tau)$ reproduces Eq. (112).

Third probe: $\hat H_3(t)=\sum_{\kappa p}\mathbf F_{\kappa p}(t)\cdot\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}(t)$, where $\mathbf F$ is an external force. Note the sign: this adds a force $-\mathbf F_{\kappa p}$ to Eq. (112).
$$\frac{\delta\langle\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}(t)\rangle}{\delta F_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')}=D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt') \qquad (113)$$

- Derived by Baym (1961) at finite temperature, in the interaction picture with $\hat H+\hat H_3(t)$. The details are omitted in the paper.

Expectation value of Eq. (112) with the $-\mathbf F$ term, then $\delta/\delta\mathbf F_{\kappa'p'}(t')$:
$$M_\kappa\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')=-\delta_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}\delta(tt')+Z_\kappa\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\left[-\frac{\delta\langle\hat n^{(\kappa p)}(\mathbf rt)\rangle}{\delta F_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')}v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\nabla'_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})+\langle\hat n^{(\kappa p)}(\mathbf rt)\rangle v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\nabla'_\alpha\nabla'_\gamma\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})D_{\kappa\gamma p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')\right] \qquad (114)$$

- Implied sum over $\gamma$. Ingredients: (i) the $\delta$'s come from $-\mathbf F$; (ii) harmonic factorization $\langle\hat n^{(\kappa p)}\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}\rangle\to\langle\hat n^{(\kappa p)}\rangle\langle\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}\rangle$ (Baym 1961); (iii) $\langle\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}\rangle\to0$ at the end because $|\mathbf F|\to0$. Hedin–Lundqvist (1969) omitted the last line. Baym (1961) and Maksimov (1976) kept it.

$$\frac{\delta\langle\hat n^{(\kappa p)}(\mathbf rt)\rangle}{\delta F_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')}=\int d\mathbf r''dt''\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf rt,\mathbf r''t'')\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf r''t'')\rangle}{\delta F_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')}-\sum_\gamma Z_\kappa D_{\kappa\gamma p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')\nabla_\gamma\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}) \qquad (115)$$

- First term: the total charge response (nuclear response screened by electrons, as in Eq. 100). Second term: removes the contribution of nucleus $\kappa p$ itself. The index-order caveat of Eq. (100) applies here too.

**Dyson-like EOM for the phonon propagator:**
$$M_\kappa\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')=-\delta_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}\delta(tt')-\sum_{\kappa''\alpha''p''}\int dt''\,\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa''\alpha''p''}(tt'')D_{\kappa''\alpha''p'',\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t''t') \qquad (116)$$

**Phonon self-energy (time domain):**
$$\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')=\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'\left[Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})W_{\rm e}(\mathbf rt,\mathbf r't')Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'_{\alpha'}\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'})+\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}\delta(tt')\nabla_\alpha\langle\hat n(\mathbf r)\rangle v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'_{\alpha'}\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'})\right] \qquad (117)$$

- First term: two nuclei (point charges) interacting through the **electronically screened, retarded** $W_{\rm e}$. It is the dynamic part. Second term: Baym's "static force", from the forces on nuclei in the equilibrium configuration. It is instantaneous and local in the nucleus.
- The derivation "is not found consistently in the literature". It involves converting $\nabla_{\mathbf r}$ into nuclear-coordinate derivatives, integrating by parts, using the harmonic approximation, and setting $\mathbf F=0$ and $\varphi=0$, so that $\langle\hat n(\mathbf rt)\rangle$ is time independent.
- Self-interaction remark (my check): the static term uses the total $\langle\hat n\rangle$ (including nucleus $\kappa p$), whereas Eq. (114) used $\langle\hat n^{(\kappa p)}\rangle$. The divergent self-terms cancel against the second line of Eq. (115), so the forms are consistent.

**Frequency domain:**
$$\sum_{\kappa''\alpha''p''}[M_\kappa\omega^2\delta_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa''\alpha''p''}-\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa''\alpha''p''}(\omega)]D_{\kappa''\alpha''p'',\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\delta_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'} \qquad (118)$$
$$\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\int d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r'[Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)+\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}\nabla_\alpha\langle\hat n(\mathbf r)\rangle v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')]Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'_{\alpha'}\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}) \qquad (119)$$

- Equation (118) follows from Eq. (116) with $\partial_t^2\to-\omega^2$: $-M\omega^2D=-1-\Pi D$, i.e. $(M\omega^2-\Pi)D=1$.

**Acoustic sum rule (many-body generalization, Hedin–Lundqvist 1969; first derived by Baym 1961):**
$$\sum_{\kappa'p'}\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega=0)=0\quad\text{for any }\alpha,\alpha' \qquad (120)$$

- This expresses the condition that the crystal is held fixed in the laboratory frame by fictitious restoring forces, so it cannot translate or rotate as a whole. Related work: Sjölander–Johnson (1965), Gillis (1970). It is the many-body version of $\sum_{\kappa'p'}C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=0$ (Born–Huang 1954).

**Compact phonon self-energy (key equation):**
$$\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\sum_{\kappa''p''}Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa''}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial r_\alpha\partial r'_{\alpha'}}\bigg|_{\mathbf r=\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p},\,\mathbf r'=\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa''p''}}[\delta_{\kappa'p',\kappa''p''}W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)-\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)] \qquad (121)$$

- **Meaning:** the phonon self-energy (the dynamical force-constant matrix) is the mixed second derivative of the electronically screened Coulomb interaction between point nuclei. The second term enforces Eq. (120). For $\kappa'p'=\kappa p$ the singular bare $v$ cancels in $W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)$, because $v$ is frequency independent.
- **Corollary (p. 20):** the coupling of nuclear displacements to electrons is entirely defined by $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ through $W_{\rm e}$. The electrons feel the nuclei through $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ in $W_{\rm ph}$ (Eq. 110) and through $\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle$ in $V_{\rm tot}$ (Eq. 79). Hence the electronic dielectric matrix $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$ plays "an absolutely central role".

**Nuclear density with zero-point smearing:**
$$\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(\mathbf rt)\rangle=n^0_{\rm n}(\mathbf r)-\frac{i\hbar}{2}\sum_{\kappa p,\alpha\alpha'}Z_\kappa\frac{\partial^2\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})}{\partial r_\alpha\partial r_{\alpha'}}D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa\alpha'p}(t^+t) \qquad (122)$$

- Check: $i\hbar D(t^+t)=\langle\Delta\hat\tau_\alpha\Delta\hat\tau_{\alpha'}\rangle$, so this is $n^0_{\rm n}-\tfrac12\sum Z\langle\Delta\tau_\alpha\Delta\tau_{\alpha'}\rangle\partial_\alpha\partial_{\alpha'}\delta$, the second-order expansion of a Gaussian-smeared nucleus. It enters $V_{\rm tot}$ and produces the Debye–Waller self-energy in Sec. V.B (Eq. 154).

### 3.8 V.A.1 Phonons in the BO adiabatic approximation (pp. 20–21)

$$\mathbf D(\omega)=[\mathbf M\omega^2-\Pi(\omega)]^{-1} \qquad (123)$$

- $\mathbf M$ is the diagonal matrix of nuclear masses, and $\mathbf D$ has elements $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$.

$$\Omega_\nu(\omega)-\omega=0,\quad\text{with }\nu=1,\dots,3M \qquad (124)$$

- $\Omega_\nu^2(\omega)$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathbf M^{-1/2}\Pi(\omega)\mathbf M^{-1/2}$, parametric in $\omega$. Because $\Pi$ is **complex and frequency dependent**, roots can lie off the real axis (finite lifetimes) and there can be **multiple roots for the same eigenmode** (satellites). $\Pi(\omega)$ is the many-body analog of the IFC matrix $C$ (Eq. 13).

$$\Pi(\omega)=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega) \qquad (125)$$

- $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(\omega=0)$ is adiabatic: the nuclei are considered immobile during electronic time scales, i.e. $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)\to\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)$ in Eq. (121) (Keating 1968). $\Pi^{\rm A}$ defines the noninteracting (adiabatic) phonons, and $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ carries the nonadiabatic EPI effects (Sec. V.A.2). The older partitioning used bare IFCs, i.e. nuclei without electrons (Grimvall 1981). It is not useful today, because bare dispersions are very different from the real ones.

$$\Pi^{\rm A}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\sum_{\kappa''p''}(\delta_{\kappa'p',\kappa''p''}-\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'})\left[\int d\mathbf r\,\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\partial\tau_{\kappa''\alpha'p''}}\frac{\partial V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}+\frac{\partial^2U_{\rm nn}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa''\alpha'p''}}\right] \qquad (126)$$

- $U_{\rm nn}$ comes from Eq. (65) and $V^{\rm en}$ is the electron–nuclei potential of Eq. (25). All derivatives are taken at equilibrium.

$$\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\partial\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}}=-Z_\kappa\int d\mathbf r'[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';0)-\delta(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')]\nabla'_\alpha\delta(\mathbf r'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}) \qquad (127)$$

- Same reasoning as Eq. (100), with an external potential that moves nucleus $\kappa p$. **See Source issues: I believe the overall sign should be $+Z_\kappa$, with $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r;0)$.**

$$\Pi^{\rm A}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\int d\mathbf r\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\frac{\partial V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}+\int d\mathbf r\,\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\mathbf r)\rangle\frac{\partial^2V^{\rm en}(\mathbf r)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}+\frac{\partial^2U_{\rm nn}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}} \qquad (128)$$

- This is exactly the BO + Hellmann–Feynman IFC expression (cf. Baroni *et al.* 2001, Eq. 10): density response × potential derivative, plus density × second derivative of potential, plus ion–ion term. It is obtained from Eq. (126) via the sum rule (120).
- **Difference from DFPT's $C$ (Eq. 13):** in $\Pi^{\rm A}$ the density response is governed by the *exact* many-body $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)$ and the exact $\langle\hat n_{\rm e}\rangle$, dressed by e–e and e–ph interactions. In DFPT it is $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}$ (RPA + xc, Sec. III.B.4) at clamped nuclei (continued on p. 22).

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Why distinguishable nuclei and indistinguishable phonons.** Nuclear statistics would depend on isotopes and spins, giving a mess of fermions and bosons. In a solid far from melting, nuclei can be labelled individually (STM and electron diffraction "see" them). So nuclei are treated in first quantization through displacements from known equilibrium positions, which are external parameters taken from crystallography or DFT. Phonons, the quanta of the displacement field, are bosons, and for them the first/second-quantization distinction is irrelevant. This choice is why the theory is a *harmonic lattice dynamics* theory, not a theory of nuclear liquids.

**(b) One Coulomb interaction for all charges.** After Eq. (73) electrons see $\int v\,\hat n$ with $\hat n=\hat n_{\rm e}+\hat n_{\rm n}$. The probe $\varphi$ couples to all charges, so $\epsilon^{-1}=\delta V_{\rm tot}/\delta\varphi$ is the *full* dielectric response, electronic and ionic. In an infrared-active crystal, $\epsilon^{-1}(\mathbf q\to0,\omega)$ is the familiar $1/\epsilon(\omega)$, with $\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega^2)/(\omega_{\rm TO}^2-\omega^2)$. $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$ is only the $\epsilon_\infty$-like electronic part, which is still dynamic and still has local fields.

**(c) Schwinger's functional-derivative trick in one sentence.** A source field $\varphi$ that couples to $\hat n$ generates density fluctuations: $\delta/\delta\varphi$ inserts $-(i/\hbar)(\hat n-\langle\hat n\rangle)$ into any time-ordered product (Eq. 75). So the four-point function in the EOM becomes $\delta G/\delta\varphi$, and chain rules organize everything into $\Sigma=i\hbar G\Gamma W$. The probes $\varphi$, $J$ and $\mathbf F$ couple to total charge, nuclear charge and nuclear displacement respectively. Each generates its own correlation function: $\chi$, $D(12)$ and $D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}$.

**(d) $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$: the lattice as an extra, slow polarizable medium.** In a homogeneous model (macroscopic, $\mathbf q\to0$ for a polar insulator):
- $W_{\rm e}=v/\epsilon_\infty$ (frequency independent below the gap).
- $W=v/\epsilon(\omega)$.
- $W_{\rm ph}=W-W_{\rm e}=\dfrac{v}{\epsilon_\infty}\dfrac{\omega_{\rm TO}^2-\omega_{\rm LO}^2}{\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega^2}=v\left(\dfrac1{\epsilon_\infty}-\dfrac1{\epsilon_0}\right)\dfrac{\omega_{\rm LO}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{\rm LO}^2}$ (using Lyddane–Sachs–Teller, $\epsilon_0/\epsilon_\infty=\omega_{\rm LO}^2/\omega_{\rm TO}^2$).
- This equals $|g|^2\,2\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}/[(\hbar\omega)^2-(\hbar\omega_{\rm LO})^2]$ with $|g|^2=v(q)\,(\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}/2)(1/\epsilon_\infty-1/\epsilon_0)$, which is the **Fröhlich coupling** squared. Hence $W_{\rm ph}=g\,D_{\rm LO}\,g^*$, as Eq. (110) says.
- Limits: at $\omega\to0$, $W\to v/\epsilon_0$ (electrons and ions both screen) and $W_{\rm ph}(0)=v(1/\epsilon_0-1/\epsilon_\infty)<0$. At $\omega\gg\omega_{\rm LO}$, $W\to v/\epsilon_\infty$ (ions too slow). The pole of $W_{\rm ph}$ sits at **$\omega_{\rm LO}$, not $\omega_{\rm TO}$**: $W$ is the longitudinal (Coulomb) propagator, so its poles are zeros of $\epsilon$.
- Metals (jellium): $W=\dfrac{v}{\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)}\dfrac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-\omega_q^2}$ with $\omega_q^2=\Omega_p^2/\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)$. This is the Bardeen–Pines effective interaction. It is attractive ($W<0$) for $\omega<\omega_q$, which is the seed of phonon-mediated pairing. My derivation: $\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm e}-\Omega_p^2/\omega^2$ with ions as a classical plasma.

**(e) Where the e–ph vertex lives.** Table I contains no symbol $g$. The coupling is (i) $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$ in $W_{\rm ph}$ (Eq. 110), the dressed vertex at both ends, and (ii) $Z_\kappa\nabla\delta(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau^0)$ contracted with $W_{\rm e}$ in $\Pi$ (Eq. 121). In Sec. V.A.3 (p. 23–24, Eq. 141) the nonadiabatic part becomes $\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}=g^{\rm b}[P_{\rm e}(\omega)-P_{\rm e}(0)]g^{\rm cc}$: one **bare** vertex $g^{\rm b}=\Delta V^{\rm en}$ and one **dressed** vertex $g=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b}$. The electron self-energy, by contrast, has two dressed vertices (Eq. 156). Common practice ($|g|^2$ in both, Allen 1972; Eq. 146) is therefore an approximation for phonons. DFPT's $g$ (Eq. 38) is the static RPA+xc approximation to the dressed vertex (Eq. 53, Sec. III.B.4).

**(f) Phonon self-energy = second derivative of screened interaction.** Equation (121) says: take two point nuclei, let them interact through $W_{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$, and take the mixed second derivative. That gives the force-constant matrix at frequency $\omega$. Taking $\omega=0$ gives BO/DFPT force constants (Eq. 128). In jellium this gives $M\omega^2=\Pi(q)\Rightarrow\omega^2(q)=\Omega_p^2/\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)$:
- Without electrons ($\epsilon_{\rm e}=1$), the ions oscillate at the ion plasma frequency $\Omega_p=\sqrt{Zn_{\rm e}e^2/(\epsilon_0M)}$ (≈30 meV for Na).
- Thomas–Fermi screening gives sound, $\omega=\Omega_pq/k_{\rm TF}$, with velocity $v_s=\sqrt{Zm/(3M)}\,v_{\rm F}$ (Bohm–Staver). Na: ≈3 km/s (expt. ≈3.2 km/s).
- The Lindhard $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ (Eq. 60) adds the Kohn anomaly at $q=2k_{\rm F}$.

**(g) Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic.** $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(0)$ assumes that electrons respond instantaneously ($\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)\approx\epsilon_{\rm e}(0)$ at phonon frequencies). The adiabatic parameter is $\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}/E_{\rm el}$ with $\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}\sim10$–200 meV:
- In insulators, $E_{\rm el}\sim$ gap $\sim$ eV, and the corrections scale like $(\hbar\omega/E_g)^2$, which is tiny.
- In metals, $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\mathbf q\to0,\omega)$ is singular. Doped polar semiconductors can have plasmon energies near phonon energies, giving coupled plasmon–phonon modes. Graphene's $\Gamma$/K modes (Lazzeri–Mauri 2006) are another case.
- The BO approximation is *not* assumed in Table I. It is recovered by setting $\omega=0$ in $W_{\rm e}$ inside $\Pi$, and by Hedin–Lundqvist's static $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ inside $W_{\rm ph}$.

**(h) Sum rules and invariance.** The acoustic sum rule (Eq. 120) ensures zero-frequency acoustic modes at $\mathbf q=0$. In Eq. (121) it is built in by the $-\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}W_{\rm e}(0)$ term, which plays the role of the diagonal "self" force constant. van Leeuwen (2004) addressed translational and rotational invariance more generally.

**(i) Orders of magnitude** (not quoted in this range; standard values for the page):
- Zero-point displacement $\sqrt{\hbar/2M\omega}$: ≈0.03 Å for Si (64 meV), ≈0.07 Å for H in a 400 meV mode.
- Electronic energies are eV and phonon energies are 10–200 meV. The mass ratio $m_{\rm e}/M$ is $\sim10^{-4}$–$10^{-5}$, and the BO expansion parameter is $(m_{\rm e}/M)^{1/4}\sim0.1$.
- LO–TO splittings: GaAs 33.3→36.2 meV, MgO 49→89 meV, hBN (in-plane) 170→200 meV.

**(j) Relevance for the user (RPA/BSE/W).** $P_{\rm e}$ with $\Gamma=\delta\delta$ is the RPA $\chi^0$, and $W_{\rm e}=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}v$ is the $W$ of GW/BSE. The Hedin–Baym $W$ adds $W_{\rm ph}$, the lattice screening. For excitons in polar materials, whether $\epsilon_\infty$ or $\epsilon_0$ applies depends on exciton binding vs $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$. That is precisely the frequency structure of $W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$. "Phonon screening of excitons" in BSE (e.g. Filip, Haber, Neaton, PRL 2021) is an outside-source pointer, not from Giustino.

## 7. Connections: the electron–phonon-only baseline that EPP generalizes

1. **Purely instantaneous, longitudinal Coulomb interaction.**
   - The Hamiltonian (Eqs. 62–69) is nonrelativistic. It contains only $v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|)$, and in space-time $v(12)=v(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2)\delta(t_1-t_2)$ (p. 15). There is no vector potential $\mathbf A$, no $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$ or $\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$ coupling, no transverse photons, no magnetic or Lorentz forces, and no radiation reaction.
   - The Newton equation for nuclei (Eq. 112) contains only Coulomb forces.
   - The probe is a **scalar** potential $\varphi$ coupling to charge density (Eq. 74). An electrodynamic theory adds a vector-potential probe coupling to the current density, giving current–current responses and a polarization tensor.
   - **"Retardation" in Giustino means frequency-dependent screening** ($\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)$, Bardeen–Pines/Baym, p. 18), *not* finite $c$. In EPP language this whole section is the electrostatic ($c\to\infty$, Coulomb-only) limit.
2. **Screened interaction $W$ and RPA-type self-energies.**
   - $W=\epsilon^{-1}v$ (Eq. 86) and $W=v+vPW$ (Eq. 90) form the Dyson equation for the screened longitudinal propagator. The total polarization splits into an electronic part ($P_{\rm e}=-i\hbar GG\Gamma$, Eq. 92; RPA when $\Gamma=\delta\delta$) and a nuclear part. The nuclear part enters through $D$ (Eqs. 96, 103, 105).
   - EPP's "RPA photon self-energies with electronic and ionic parts" generalize exactly this split to the full photon propagator. $W_{\rm e}$ is the electronic-only screened propagator. $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}DW_{\rm e}$ is the ionic (phonon) correction. The structure "screened propagator = electronic-screened propagator + (electronic-screened) × (ionic response) × (electronic-screened)" is the template for **connection formulas** between bare, electronically dressed and fully dressed quantities.
3. **Nuclear contribution to $W$ (Hedin–Lundqvist 1969, Eqs. 104–110).** This is the baseline for any "ionic photon self-energy". In the polar homogeneous limit it reproduces $1/\epsilon(\omega)=1/\epsilon_\infty+(\text{LO pole})$ (Sec. 4d). With transverse photons the TO resonance would also enter, through the transverse propagator $\sim1/[\epsilon(\omega)\omega^2/c^2-q^2]$, giving **phonon polaritons**. In the Coulomb-only $W$, TO appears only as a *zero* of $W$.
4. **Phonon self-energy (Eqs. 116–121).**
   - $\Pi=ZZ'\partial\partial'W_{\rm e}$: nuclei are point charges coupled by the electronically screened *longitudinal* interaction. EPP's "electrodynamic phonon self-energy" must add the coupling of nuclear currents $Z_\kappa e\,\Delta\dot{\boldsymbol\tau}$ to the transverse field.
   - Its electrostatic limit should recover Eq. (121) and, at $\omega=0$, the DFPT force constants (Eq. 128). The sum rule (Eq. 120) and the cancellation of the bare self-interaction in Eq. (121) are properties any generalization must keep.
5. **LO–TO splitting, non-analytic terms, Born effective charges, dielectric screening.**
   - None of these is written explicitly in Sec. IV. They are *contained* in Eq. (121) through the long-range (macroscopic, $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$, $\mathbf q\to0$) part of $W_{\rm e}$.
   - Evaluating it with local fields turns the bare $Z_\kappa$ into Born effective charges $Z^*_\kappa$ and gives the non-analytic term $\propto(\mathbf q\cdot Z^*)(\mathbf q\cdot Z^*)/(\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\mathbf q)$. This is my statement, following Pick–Cohen–Martin 1970, which Giustino cites in Sec. III.B.4.
   - The point-dipole term in Eq. (107), $Z_\kappa\Delta\boldsymbol\tau\cdot\nabla\delta$, is the microscopic origin of the ionic polarization. Giustino treats polar matrix elements in Sec. VI.A.3 (p. 34; Vogl 1976; Verdi–Giustino 2015).
6. **Polar (Fröhlich) coupling.**
   - The general vertex is $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$ in Eq. (110). For polar materials at long wavelength it reduces to the Fröhlich vertex $|g|^2=v(q)(\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}/2)(1/\epsilon_\infty-1/\epsilon_0)$ (Sec. 4d; Giustino Secs. II.A.3 and VI.A.3).
   - EPP's "electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex" replaces the electrostatic $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$ (dipole field of a displaced ion, screened by electrons) with its electrodynamic counterpart, which includes the transverse field and finite-$c$ retardation. It should reduce to Eq. (110)'s long-wavelength limit when $c\to\infty$.
7. **Hedin–Baym equations (Table I).** These are the closed electron–phonon baseline. Setting $D=0$ gives Hedin. An electron–phonon–photon theory adds a photon propagator and current vertices to this loop. Viz 4's "where is $v$" toggle shows the insertion points.
8. **Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic.**
   - $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi(0)$ (Eq. 125) corresponds to BO and DFPT (Eq. 128). The nonadiabatic part $\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)$ is treated in Sec. V.A.2–3 (Eqs. 135–146).
   - Hedin–Lundqvist's static-$\epsilon_{\rm e}$ approximation in $W_{\rm ph}$ is the BO vertex. Giustino keeps dynamic $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)$.
   - In an electrodynamic theory there are two distinct "retardations": dynamic screening (electron response time) and light propagation (finite $c$). Keep them separate on the page.
9. **Bare vs dressed vertices.** $W_{\rm ph}$ has two dressed vertices. $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ has one bare ($g^{\rm b}=\Delta V^{\rm en}$) and one dressed ($g=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b}$) vertex (Eq. 141, p. 24; Fig. 1c,d). Any electrodynamic generalization must keep track of which vertex is screened by what, to avoid double counting.
