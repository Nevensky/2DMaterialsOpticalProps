## 0. Conventions used in this range (read first)

- **Units: Gaussian.** Coulomb kernel $v(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')=1/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ (Eq. 229, 317), Poisson $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi\rho$ (Eq. 325), matter–field coupling $-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$ (Eq. 312). $\hbar$ is kept explicitly. Charges sit inside $\rho$ and $\mathbf j$ (and inside $Z^*$), not in $v$.
- **Born effective charges** $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}=\delta p_\mu/\delta R_{a\nu}=\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\delta P_\mu/\delta R_{a\nu}$ (Eq. 110, p. 22) **have the dimension of charge** (they include $e$). The first index is the polarization/current direction and the second is the displacement direction.
- **Time Fourier transform:** $f(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}f(t-t')$ (Eq. 255 and the definition on p. 60). So $f(t)\propto e^{-i\omega t}$ and $\partial_t\to-i\omega$. This gives $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}\Rightarrow \mathbf E=i\frac{\omega}{c}\mathbf A$ (Eq. 334), and continuity $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0\Rightarrow\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=i\omega\rho$ (Eq. 335).
- **Gauge:** $\Phi=0$ (temporal gauge) for the external fields (Eq. 324). The longitudinal field is handled by the instantaneous Coulomb propagator $v$ (Eq. 235). The free longitudinal photon propagator vanishes, $D^{0l}=0$ (Eq. 263). The resulting $4\times4$ propagator (Eq. 317) has the Coulomb-gauge structure of QED.
- **Spatial Fourier convention in a 3D crystal (Eq. 294):**
  $f(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\,f_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)\,e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf r}e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\cdot\mathbf r'}$.
- **Normalization:** $N$ = number of unit cells (Born–von Kármán), $\Omega$ = crystal volume, $\Omega_{\rm uc}=\Omega/N$ = unit-cell volume. $\sum_{\mathbf q\in\rm BZ}\leftrightarrow\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{\rm BZ}d\mathbf q$ and $\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\leftrightarrow\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q}$. In 2D: $S$ = crystal area, $S_{\rm uc}$ = unit-cell area, $\sum_{\mathbf q}\to\frac{S}{(2\pi)^2}\int_{\rm SBZ}d\mathbf Q$, and the sheet lies at $z=0$.
- **Phonons** (Eqs. 242–243, 274): $s$ atoms per cell (index a, b), Cartesian $\mu,\nu$, and $3s$ branches (index $\alpha$ in early equations, renamed $\sigma$ from p. 61 on).
  $Q_{ia\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha\mathbf q}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}}e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha})e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R^0_i}$,
  $\pi_{a\mu}(\mathbf r)=-\frac{i}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in\rm BZ}\sqrt{\frac{m_a\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}{2}}e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}-a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha})e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}$ (Eq. 274).
  The eigenvectors are orthonormal, $\sum_{a\mu}e^*_{a\mu\alpha}e_{a\mu\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ (Eq. 27), and complete, $\sum_\alpha e^*_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)e_{b\nu\alpha}(\mathbf k)=\delta_{a\mu,b\nu}$ (Eq. 28). Also $e_{a\mu\alpha}(-\mathbf q)=e^*_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ and $\omega_\alpha(-\mathbf q)=\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$ (Eqs. 31, 32, 38, 288).
- **Temperature:** time-ordered $T=0$ formalism. For bosons one sets $n_{\mathbf q\sigma}=0$ (Sec. 4.5, p. 50). This is the author's prescription for recovering the physical, $T$-independent bosonic propagator.
- **Response normalization:** $\Pi_{jj}$ carries $\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{jj\}\rangle$, so the Kubo relation reads $j_\alpha=\sum_\beta\int\Pi_{\alpha\beta}A_\beta$ with no extra $c$ (p. 65). $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ carries $-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle T\{\rho\rho\}\rangle$ (Eq. 320). The mixed correlators carry $-\frac{i}{\hbar}$ for $\Pi_{j\rho}$ and $+\frac{i}{\hbar c}$ for $\Pi_{\rho j}$ (Eqs. 345–346).
- **Point-dipole model of the ions:** every atom of cell $i$ is placed at the lattice point $\mathbf R^0_i$, so the basis positions $\tau_a$ are dropped (Eqs. 241, 271, 272). The author notes that this makes the ionic response constant in $\mathbf G,\mathbf G'$ and that the resulting Bragg/local-field components are unphysical (p. 62).

**Useful derived dictionary (my derivation, consistent with the paper's conventions; check before quoting as the paper's):**
In the $\Phi=0$ gauge $\mathbf A=\frac{c}{i\omega}\mathbf E$, so $\mathbf j=\Pi\mathbf A$ gives
$\sigma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=-\frac{ic}{\omega}\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$, $\chi_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{c}{\omega^2}\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$, and $\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)$.
Check with free electrons: $\Pi^{\rm dia}=-\frac{e^2n}{mc}$ gives $\varepsilon=1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2$. The quantity $-4\pi c\,\Pi$ therefore has units of frequency squared. The free transverse photon propagator is $D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-4\pi c\,[\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/q^2]/(\omega^2-c^2q^2+i\eta)$ (Eq. 261). The pole condition $1-D^{0t}\Pi=0$ is equivalent to $c^2q^2=\omega^2\varepsilon(\omega)$ with the same $\varepsilon=1+4\pi c\Pi/\omega^2$.

## 1. Overview

Section 5 builds the RPA self-energy $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ of the **transverse photon**: the current–current response of matter that dresses the free photon through the Dyson equation $D^t=D^{0t}+D^{0t}\Pi^{\rm RPA}D^t$ (Eq. 277). The self-energy has an electronic part (Sec. 5.1, the usual Kubo/RPA current–current bubble) and an **ionic part** (Sec. 5.2, this range). The ions couple to light because a vibrating polar lattice carries a polarization current $\mathbf j^{\rm ion}=\dot{\mathbf P}$. With Born charges $Z^*$, $j^{\rm ion}_\alpha=\sum Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\pi_{a\mu}/m_a$: charge × velocity (Eq. 272). Like electrons, the ions also produce an $A^2$ ("diamagnetic") term, with the ionic density tensor $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}\propto\sum Z^*Z^*/m_a$.

Sec. 5.2.1 evaluates the **paramagnetic** ionic self-energy exactly for harmonic phonons. The current–current correlator of the ions reduces to a phonon propagator $F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)$ sandwiched between two **ionic current vertices** $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{a\mu}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)/\sqrt{m_a}$ (Eqs. 295–299). These vertices are the photon analogue of the electron–phonon matrix element. Sec. 5.2.2 evaluates the **diamagnetic** term, a frequency-independent constant $-\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum Z^*Z^*/m_a$ (Eq. 304). Sec. 5.2.3 shows, using only the completeness of the phonon eigenvectors, that the diamagnetic term is exactly minus the static paramagnetic term (Eq. 310). This is the ionic version of gauge invariance and of the f-sum rule: a static, uniform $\mathbf A$ (which carries no electric field in the $\Phi=0$ gauge) must not drive a current. The total ionic self-energy then takes the "subtract the static value" form $\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}(\omega)=\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ (Eq. 311), the same structure as the electronic Eq. (281). Strictly 2D versions with $\delta(z)\delta(z')$ are given (Eqs. 300, 305). The result is the ionic (infrared) dielectric response; it coincides with the Gonze–Lee mode-oscillator-strength formula used in DFPT.

Sec. 6 then lifts the restriction to transverse photons. The total propagator $D$ is a $4\times4$ matrix with a longitudinal (Coulomb $v$) block and a transverse ($D^{0t}$) block. Its first-order corrections involve four matter correlators $\Pi_{\rho\rho},\Pi_{\rho j},\Pi_{j\rho},\Pi_{jj}$ for both electrons and ions (Eq. 319, Fig. 7). Sec. 6.0.1 expresses the induced charges and currents through these correlators and the screened $W$ (Eqs. 330–333). The continuity equation then yields **connection formulas** (Eqs. 341, 344, and after the range 347–350): every correlator follows from $\Pi_{jj}$. In particular $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}$. So the ionic $\Pi_{jj}$ of Sec. 5.2 also fixes the ionic density response that drives LO phonons and the Fröhlich interaction.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **Starting point (p. 58, Eq. 280).** The ionic RPA photon self-energy is $\Pi^{\rm ion}=\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}+\Pi^{\rm ion,para}$. The dia term comes from the $A^2$ ion term, with $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}$ (Eq. 271). The para term is the time-ordered current–current correlator in the phonon vacuum.
2. **Insert the ionic current** (Eq. 272) into the para correlator, giving Eq. 286. This reduces it to a momentum–momentum correlator $\langle T\{\pi_{a\mu}\pi_{b\nu}\}\rangle$ of point ions at the lattice sites.
3. **Expand $\pi$ in phonon operators** (Eq. 274), giving Eq. 287. Use interaction-picture evolution $a(t)=ae^{-i\omega t}$. Only the $aa^+$ and $a^+a$ contractions survive, which forces $\mathbf q'=-\mathbf q$ and equal branches. With $T\neq0$ occupation factors this gives the standard bosonic time-ordered function (p. 61).
4. **Use the symmetry** $e(-\mathbf q)=e^*(\mathbf q)$, $\omega(-\mathbf q)=\omega(\mathbf q)$ (Eq. 288). Fourier-transform in time to get poles at $\omega=\pm\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)$ (p. 61). Recombine into $\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2\pm i\eta$ denominators (Eq. 289). Set $n_{\mathbf q\sigma}=0$ (Sec. 4.5) to get Eq. 290.
5. **Physical remark.** Each lattice site acts as a polarizable point dipole, so the response function is that of a **dipole grating**. Bragg (G ≠ 0) components are artefacts of the point-dipole model and should be avoided.
6. **Bring Eq. 290 to the standard periodic form** (Eq. 294). Replace the $\mathbf q$-sum by an integral and the $\delta$'s by Fourier integrals (Eqs. 291–292). Do the lattice sums $\sum_i e^{-i\mathbf q_1\cdot\mathbf R^0_i}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q_1\mathbf G}$, giving Eq. 293. Then read off $\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)$, which is **independent of $\mathbf G,\mathbf G'$**.
7. **Define ionic current vertices** $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ and $j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ (Eqs. 295–296). This gives the compact form Eq. 297. Recognizing the free phonon Green's function $F_\sigma$ (Eq. 298 = Eq. 256) gives Eq. 299, "vertex × phonon propagator × vertex".
8. **2D version.** Use 2D substitutions with $\delta(z)$ factors to get Eq. 300. The author argues this is an *exact* 2D polarizability within the point-dipole model, because crystal local-field effects (CLFEs) are already absorbed into the $\delta$-functions.
9. **Diamagnetic part (5.2.2).** There are no free ionic charges, which the author takes to mean $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j^{\rm ion}=0$ (Eq. 301; see Source issues). From the Kubo relation $\mathbf j=\Pi\mathbf A$ with $\Pi=\Pi^{\rm dia}+\Pi^{\rm para}$, the author first states the gauge-invariance claim $\Pi^{\rm dia}=-\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega=0)$ and then sets out to prove it directly.
10. **Write $\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}$ in real space** (Eq. 302): local, $\propto\sum_i\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R_i^0)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$. Fourier-transform the $\delta$'s (Eq. 303), do the lattice sum, and fold the $\mathbf q'$ integral into BZ + $\mathbf G'$. The result has the standard form, with $\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{a\mu}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}/m_a$ (Eq. 304), a constant in $\mathbf q,\omega,\mathbf G,\mathbf G'$. The 2D analogue is Eq. 305.
11. **Dia from para (5.2.3).** Take Eq. 297 at $\omega=0$ (Eqs. 306–307) and insert the vertices (Eq. 308). The completeness relation (Eq. 28) collapses $\sum_\sigma e_{a\mu\sigma}e^*_{b\nu\sigma}=\delta_{a\mu,b\nu}$, giving Eq. 309. Comparing with Eq. 304 gives the exact identity $\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}=-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}(\omega=0)$ (Eq. 310). Hence $\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}=\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ (Eq. 311), mirroring the electronic Eq. 281.
12. **Sec. 6: full field.** Restore the longitudinal (Coulomb) interaction in $V'$ (Eq. 312), which contains electron and ion densities $\rho^e$ and $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ (Eqs. 313–315). Expand $D=D^0+D^{1,\rm ion}+D^{1,e}+\dots$ (Eq. 316). Mixed electron–ion correlators vanish at lowest order.
13. **$4\times4$ structure.** $D^0=\mathrm{diag}(v,\hat D^{0,t})$ (Eqs. 317–318). The first-order block matrix for each species $i$ is Eq. 319, with correlators $\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}$ (Eq. 320), $\Pi^i_{\rho j}$, $\Pi^i_{j\rho}$ and $\Pi^i_{jj}$ (Eqs. 321–322, which include the dia terms). Each sector (ll, lt, tl, tt) has its own perturbation series. Higher orders mix $v$ and $D^{0t}$ inside each sector (Fig. 7).
14. **Induced sources (6.0.1).** Split the external field into longitudinal and transverse parts in the $\Phi=0$ gauge (Eqs. 323–325). Read induced densities and currents off the corners of Fig. 7: bare versions in Eqs. 326–329, then screened versions with $W$ and $A^{\rm ext,sc}$ in Eqs. 330–333.
15. **Connection formulas.** Go to $\omega$-space (Eqs. 334–339) and impose continuity on the longitudinal and the total induced sources (Eqs. 340, 342). For arbitrary sources this gives $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{i}{\omega}\partial_\mu\Pi_{j\rho\mu}$ (Eq. 341) and $\Pi_{\rho j\nu}=-\frac{i}{\omega}\partial_\mu\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}$ (Eq. 344). The definitions in Eqs. 345–346 and the "small digression" explain the $-i/\hbar$ vs $i/(\hbar c)$ prefactors.
16. **Just after the range (pp. 74–77).** The symmetry relations (Eqs. 347–348) give $\Pi_{j\rho\mu}=-i\frac{c}{\omega}\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}$ (Eq. 349) and $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}$ (Eq. 350). Sec. 6.0.2 verifies Eq. 350 explicitly for the ionic case, using the ionic $\Pi_{jj}$ of Eq. 352, which is Eq. 311 written out.

## 3. Key equations (faithful transcriptions; paper's numbering)

### 3.0 Context definitions (pp. 56–58, needed to follow the range)

**(271) Ionic density tensor**
$$\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)=\frac{m_0}{e^2}\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$$
$m_0$ is "some standard nucleon mass". It is pure bookkeeping, chosen so that the ionic $A^2$ term looks like the electronic one, and it cancels in every physical result. $m_a$ is the mass of atom a and $\mathbf R^0_i$ is the lattice vector of cell $i$.

**(272) Ionic current density**
$$j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\,\pi_{a\mu}(\mathbf r)\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$$
This is the polarization current $\dot P_\alpha=\sum Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\dot Q_{a\mu}$ with $\dot Q=\pi/m$, carried by point dipoles at the lattice sites.

**(273) Electron–photon–phonon interaction (transverse part only)**
$$V=-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^e\cdot\mathbf A+\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho^e\mathbf A^2-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm ion}\cdot\mathbf A+\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d\mathbf r\,A_\alpha\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}A_\beta$$

**(280) Ionic RPA photon self-energy (definition)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}}+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|T\{j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r,t)j^{\rm ion}_\beta(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,para}}$$
$|\Phi_0^{\rm phon}\rangle$ is the free-phonon ground state. The total RPA self-energy is $\Pi^{\rm RPA}=\Pi^e+\Pi^{\rm ion}$ (Eq. 278).

### 3.1 End of Sec. 5.1 (top of p. 60)

**(284) Electronic current matrix element**
$$j^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)=\frac{e\hbar}{2im}\left\{\phi^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)\partial_\alpha\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)-[\partial_\alpha\phi^*_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)]\phi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\mathbf r)\right\}$$
This enters Eqs. 282–283: $\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\mathbf G\mathbf G'}=-\frac{2}{c\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf k nm}\frac{f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+i\eta}j^\alpha(\mathbf G)[j^\beta(\mathbf G')]^*$, and $\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}=\Pi^{\rm para,e}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para,e}(0)$ (Eq. 281).

**(285) Quasi-2D (q2D) transition and the purely 2D limit**
$$\mathbf q\to\mathbf Q=(Q_x,Q_y),\quad\mathbf k\to\mathbf K=(K_x,K_y),\quad\sum_{\mathbf k\in1.BZ}\to\sum_{\mathbf K\in1.SBZ};\qquad \Pi^{\rm RPA,e,2D}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf Q,\omega)=l\,\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}_{\alpha\beta\,\mathbf G=0\mathbf G'=0}(\mathbf Q,\omega)$$
$l$ is the period of the vertical superlattice. Multiplying the $G_z=G_z'=0$ component by $l$ turns a supercell average into a sheet quantity. This is the same content as the $\delta(z)\delta(z')$ prescription used for the ions in Eqs. 300 and 305.

### 3.2 Sec. 5.2.1 — paramagnetic ionic self-energy

**Definition (p. 60, unnumbered; the para part of Eq. 280)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|T\{j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r,t)j^{\rm ion}_\beta(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle$$

**(286) After inserting Eq. 272**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\sum_{ia\mu}\sum_{jb\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{m_am_b}\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|T\{\pi_{a\mu}(\mathbf r,t)\pi_{b\nu}(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\delta(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)$$

**(287) Momentum–momentum correlator in phonon operators**
$$\langle T\{\pi_{a\mu}(\mathbf r,t)\pi_{b\nu}(\mathbf r',t')\}\rangle=-\frac1N\sum_{\alpha\beta=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\mathbf q'\in BZ}\sqrt{\frac{m_a\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{m_b\hbar\omega_\beta(\mathbf q')}{2}}\,e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e_{b\nu\beta}(\mathbf q')e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}e^{i\mathbf q'\cdot\mathbf r'}\,\langle T\{[a_{\mathbf q\alpha}(t)-a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}(t)][a_{\mathbf q'\beta}(t')-a^+_{-\mathbf q'\beta}(t')]\}\rangle$$
The prefactor $-1/N$ comes from $(-i)^2$. Here $\alpha,\beta$ are **branch** indices, which clashes with the Cartesian $\alpha\beta$ of $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$. Interaction picture: $a_{\mathbf q\alpha}(t)=a_{\mathbf q\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)t}$ and $a^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}(t)=a^+_{\mathbf q\alpha}e^{i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)t}$.

**Bosonic T-product (p. 61, unnumbered).** Only the $aa^+$ and $a^+a$ terms survive:
$$\langle T\{[\dots][\dots]\}\rangle=-\{[1+n_{\mathbf q\alpha}]\theta(t-t')+n_{\mathbf q\alpha}\theta(t'-t)\}e^{-i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)(t-t')}\delta_{\mathbf q',-\mathbf q}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\{n_{\mathbf q\alpha}\theta(t-t')+[1+n_{\mathbf q\alpha}]\theta(t'-t)\}e^{i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)(t-t')}\delta_{\mathbf q',-\mathbf q}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$$
$n_{\mathbf q\alpha}$ is the Bose occupation (Eq. 253).

**Result in time (p. 61, unnumbered)**
$$\langle T\{\pi_{a\mu}(\mathbf r,t)\pi_{b\nu}(\mathbf r',t')\}\rangle=\frac1N\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac{\sqrt{m_am_b}\,\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)}{2}e_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\alpha}(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\Big\{n_{\mathbf q\alpha}\big[\theta(t-t')e^{i\omega_\alpha(t-t')}+\theta(t'-t)e^{-i\omega_\alpha(t-t')}\big]+[1+n_{\mathbf q\alpha}]\big[\theta(t-t')e^{-i\omega_\alpha(t-t')}+\theta(t'-t)e^{i\omega_\alpha(t-t')}\big]\Big\}$$

**(288)** $e_{a\mu\alpha}(-\mathbf q)=e^*_{a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf q)$ and $\omega_\alpha(-\mathbf q)=\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)$.

**After the time integration (p. 61, unnumbered)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{1}{\hbar c}\frac1N\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{ia\mu}\sum_{jb\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\frac{\hbar\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{2}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\times$$
$$\left\{n_{\mathbf q\sigma}\left[\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)-i\eta}\right]+(1+n_{\mathbf q\sigma})\left[\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)-i\eta}\right]\right\}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\delta(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)$$
I verified this: $\int d\tau\,e^{i\omega\tau}\theta(\tau)e^{-i\omega_\sigma\tau}=i/(\omega-\omega_\sigma+i\eta)$, and so on. The overall sign comes from $\frac{i}{\hbar c}\cdot i=-\frac{1}{\hbar c}$. From here on $\sigma$ labels the $3s$ branches and Greek letters $\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu$ are Cartesian.

**(289) Rearranged**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{1}{\hbar c}\frac1N\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{ia\mu}\sum_{jb\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\hbar\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^{i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\left\{\frac{1+n_{\mathbf q\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}-\frac{n_{\mathbf q\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)-i\eta}\right\}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\delta(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)$$
This has the same bracket as the $T\neq0$ time-ordered phonon propagator, Eq. 249.

**$n_{\mathbf q}=0$ prescription (Sec. 4.5) → (290)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{1}{\hbar c}\frac1N\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\sum_{ia\mu}\sum_{jb\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\frac{\hbar\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\delta(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)$$
This is the "current propagator of a lattice of polarizable point dipoles" (dipole grating).

**(291) Substitutions**
$$\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\to\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{BZ}d\mathbf q,\qquad\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\to\int\frac{d\mathbf q_1}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_1\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)},\qquad\delta(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)\to\int\frac{d\mathbf q_2}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_2\cdot(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)}$$

**(292)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac1c\frac{\Omega}{N}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\int_{BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_{ia\mu}\sum_{jb\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\frac{\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)+i\eta}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}\int\frac{d\mathbf q_1}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_1\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}\int\frac{d\mathbf q_2}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_2\cdot(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)}$$

**Lattice sums (p. 63, unnumbered)**
$$\sum_i\int\frac{d\mathbf q_1}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_1\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\int\frac{d\mathbf q_1}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_1\cdot\mathbf r}\delta_{\mathbf q_1\mathbf G}=\frac{N}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf G}e^{i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf r},\qquad\sum_j\int\frac{d\mathbf q_2}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q_2\cdot(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)}=\frac{N}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf G'}e^{-i\mathbf G'\cdot\mathbf r'}$$
The second uses the relabeling $\mathbf G'\to-\mathbf G'$. This is just $\sum_i\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)=\Omega_{\rm uc}^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf G}e^{i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf r}$.

**(293)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf G}\sum_{\mathbf G'}\int_{BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf r}e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\cdot\mathbf r'}\left\{-\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\sum_{a\mu}\sum_{b\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\frac{\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)+i\eta}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)\right\}$$
with $\Omega_{\rm uc}=\Omega/N$. The prefactor is $\frac{\Omega}{N}\cdot\frac{N^2}{\Omega^2}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}$.

**(294) General nonlocal propagator in a 3D periodic crystal**
$$f(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf G}\sum_{\mathbf G'}\int_{BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}f_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf r}e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\cdot\mathbf r'}$$

**Fourier components (p. 63, unnumbered)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\sum_{a\mu}\sum_{b\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\frac{\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)+i\eta}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$
This is independent of $\mathbf G,\mathbf G'$. In real space, $\Pi^{\rm ion,para}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')$ is a function of $\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf R^0_j$ only: "more like the response function of a dipole grating".

**(295)–(296) Ionic current vertices (boxed)**
$$j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{a\mu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}}{\sqrt{m_a}}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q),\qquad j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{b\nu}\frac{Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_b}}e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$
This is the dipole-current "polarity" of mode $\sigma$: the effective charge of the mode divided by $\sqrt{\rm mass}$. Its units are charge/$\sqrt{\rm mass}$. $j_{\alpha\sigma}j^*_{\beta\sigma}$ is exactly the Gonze–Lee **mode oscillator strength** $S_{\sigma,\alpha\beta}$. For a diatomic crystal with $Z^*_1=-Z^*_2=Z$, the optical mode has $|j|^2=Z^2/\mu$, with $\mu$ the reduced mass.

**(297) Paramagnetic ionic photon self-energy (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}\,j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$

**(298) Free phonon Green's function (boxed; = Eq. 256)**
$$F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}$$
Defined in Eq. 251 as $F_\sigma(\mathbf q;t-t')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle T\{Q_{\mathbf q\sigma}(t)Q_{-\mathbf q\sigma}(t')\}\rangle$ with $Q_{\mathbf q\sigma}=a_{\mathbf q\sigma}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\sigma}$ dimensionless, and $n=0$.

**(299) Vertex × phonon propagator × vertex (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{\hbar}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}{2}F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)\,j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$
The $\mathbf G\mathbf G'$ labels are dropped as unnecessary.

**2D substitutions (p. 64)**
$$\sum_{\mathbf q\in BZ}\to\frac{S}{(2\pi)^2}\int_{S.B.Z}d\mathbf Q,\qquad\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\to\delta(z)\int\frac{d\mathbf Q_1}{(2\pi)^2}e^{i\mathbf Q_1\cdot(\boldsymbol\rho-\mathbf R^0_i)},\qquad\delta(\mathbf r'-\mathbf R^0_j)\to\delta(z')\int\frac{d\mathbf Q_2}{(2\pi)^2}e^{i\mathbf Q_2\cdot(\boldsymbol\rho'-\mathbf R^0_j)}$$
$\mathbf Q,\mathbf Q_1,\mathbf Q_2$ are 2D wavevectors, $\mathbf R^0_i$ are 2D Bravais vectors, and the crystal sits at $z=0$.

**(300) 2D paramagnetic ionic photon self-energy (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf Q,\omega,z,z')=-\frac{\hbar}{cS_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega_\sigma(\mathbf Q)}{2}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf Q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf Q)F_\sigma(\mathbf Q,\omega)\,\delta(z)\delta(z')$$
The author calls this an "exact 2D polarizability". Within the point-dipole model, dipoles confined to $z=0$ are consistent with the 3D derivation, and "CLFEs are incorporated into non-physical $\delta$-functions". $\alpha,\beta$ still run over $x,y,z$, so out-of-plane polar modes (ZO, with $Z^*_{zz}$) are included.

### 3.3 Sec. 5.2.2 — diamagnetic ionic self-energy

**(301)** $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j^{\rm ion}(\mathbf r,\omega)=0$. The author's justification is "there are no free charges $\rho^{\rm ion}=0$". This is questionable; see Source issues.

**Kubo relation and decomposition (p. 65, unnumbered)**
$$j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r,\omega)=\sum_\beta\int d\mathbf r_1\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)A_\beta(\mathbf r_1,\omega),\qquad\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')+\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$$
**Claim (unnumbered):** $\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega=0)$. The stated reasons are that the dia term is frequency independent (local in time) and continuity.

**(302) Real-space diamagnetic term** (from Eqs. 271 and 280)
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac1c\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$$
$m_0$ and $e^2$ cancel. The term is local and diagonal in space, a contact or "free-particle" term.

**(303)** $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)=\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)}$ and $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')=\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q'\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}$.

**Intermediate steps (pp. 65–66, unnumbered)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac1c\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_ie^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R^0_i}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}e^{-i\mathbf q'\cdot\mathbf r'}e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf q')\cdot\mathbf r},\qquad\sum_ie^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R^0_i}=N\sum_{\mathbf G}\delta_{\mathbf q\mathbf G}$$
$$\Rightarrow-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{\mathbf G}\int\frac{d\mathbf q'}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*Z^*e^{i(\mathbf q'+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf r}e^{-i\mathbf q'\cdot\mathbf r'}$$
This uses $\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\to\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q}$. Then rename $\mathbf q'\to\mathbf q$, use $\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}f(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathbf G'}\int_{BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}f(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')$, and (implicitly) shift $\mathbf G+\mathbf G'\to\mathbf G$:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf G\mathbf G'}\int_{BZ}\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf r}e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\cdot\mathbf r'}\left\{-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\right\}$$

**(304) Diamagnetic ionic photon self-energy (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}$$
It is constant in $\mathbf q$, $\omega$, $\mathbf G$ and $\mathbf G'$. Define the **ionic plasma-frequency tensor** $\Omega^2_{p,\alpha\beta}\equiv-4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{a\mu}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}/m_a$. This name is mine; on p. 34 the paper speaks of a "plasmon frequency" $\omega_{p\mu}$ of modes.

**2D substitutions (p. 66)**
$$\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)=\delta(z)\int\frac{d\mathbf Q}{(2\pi)^2}e^{i\mathbf Q\cdot(\boldsymbol\rho-\mathbf R^0_i)},\qquad\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')=\delta(z-z')\int\frac{d\mathbf Q'}{(2\pi)^2}e^{i\mathbf Q'\cdot(\boldsymbol\rho-\boldsymbol\rho')}$$

**(305) 2D diamagnetic ionic photon self-energy (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf Q,\omega,z,z')=-\frac{1}{S_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\,\delta(z)\delta(z')$$
This uses $\delta(z)\delta(z-z')=\delta(z)\delta(z')$.

### 3.4 Sec. 5.2.3 — diamagnetic from paramagnetic

**(306)** Static limit of Eq. 297:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)=\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)-i\eta}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$

**(307)** There are no real excitations at $\omega=0$, so $\eta\to0$:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)=\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$

**(308)** Inserting Eqs. 295–296:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)=\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{a\mu}\sum_{b\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}}{\sqrt{m_a}}\frac{Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_b}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$$

**(309)** Completeness (Eq. 28), $\sum_\sigma e_{a\mu\sigma}e^*_{b\nu\sigma}=\delta_{a\mu,b\nu}$, gives
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)=\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}}{m_a}$$
The result is independent of $\mathbf q$ and of all force constants. It is a **sum rule for the mode oscillator strengths**: $\sum_\sigma j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{a\mu}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}/m_a$.

**(310) Exact para/dia connection (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)$$

**(311) Total ionic RPA photon self-energy (boxed)**
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega=0)$$
Written out, which is what the paper uses as Eq. 352 on p. 75:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q)}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)+i\eta}$$
(I checked the algebra: $\frac{\omega_\sigma^2}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}+1=\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}$.)

**Derived consequence (mine, using the dictionary in Sec. 0):** the ionic contribution to the dielectric tensor is
$$\varepsilon^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)-\text{(background)}=\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\frac{j_{\alpha\sigma}j^*_{\beta\sigma}}{\omega_\sigma^2-\omega^2-i\eta},$$
which is the Gonze–Lee infrared formula. In 2D the corresponding sheet quantities are $\sigma^{2D}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=\frac{i\omega}{S_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\frac{j_{\alpha\sigma}j^*_{\beta\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}$ and $\alpha^{2D}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=\frac{1}{S_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\frac{j_{\alpha\sigma}j^*_{\beta\sigma}}{\omega^2_\sigma-\omega^2-i\eta}$ (a length in Gaussian units).

### 3.5 Sec. 6 — total (longitudinal + transverse) propagator

**(312) Full interaction** (Eq. 273 plus the longitudinal Coulomb term, Eq. 235)
$$V'=-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^e\cdot\mathbf A+\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int d\mathbf r\,\rho^e\mathbf A^2-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j^{\rm ion}\cdot\mathbf A+\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d\mathbf r\,A_\alpha\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}A_\beta+\frac12\int d\mathbf r\int d\mathbf r'\frac{[\rho^e(\mathbf r)+\rho^{\rm ion}(\mathbf r)][\rho^e(\mathbf r')+\rho^{\rm ion}(\mathbf r')]}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}$$
Here $\mathbf A$ is the total field $\mathbf A_t+\mathbf A_l$.

**(313)** Ionic charge density $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$.

**(314)** Ionic dipole-moment density (= Eq. 241): $P_\mu(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$.

**(315)** Ionic density tensor (= Eq. 271): $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)=\frac{m_0}{e^2}\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$.
Two different "ion densities" appear: $\rho^{\rm ion}$ (a scalar bound charge, linear in phonon operators) and $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}$ (a c-number tensor).

**(316)** Lowest-order expansion: $D=D^0+D^{1,\rm ion}+D^{1,e}+\dots$. Mixed electron–ion correlators do not contribute at this order.

**(317)** $D^0=\begin{bmatrix}v&0\\0&\hat D^{0,t}\end{bmatrix}$, a $4\times4$ matrix with $v=1/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ the bare Coulomb scalar propagator.

**(318)** Transverse photon propagator ($3\times3$):
$$\hat D^{0,t}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t-t')=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle\Phi^{\rm phot}_0|T\{A_\mu(\mathbf r,t)A_\nu(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\rangle$$
The total $A$ is paired, but its longitudinal contribution vanishes (Eq. 263). In $(\mathbf q,\omega)$ this is Eq. 261.

**(319) First-order block**
$$D^{1,i}=\begin{bmatrix}v\,\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}\,v&v\,\Pi^i_{\rho j}\,\hat D^{0,t}\\\hat D^{0,t}\,\Pi^i_{j\rho}\,v&\hat D^{0,t}\,\Pi^i_{jj}\,\hat D^{0,t}\end{bmatrix},\qquad i=e,\ \text{ion}$$
$\Pi_{\rho j}$ is a row (1×3) and $\Pi_{j\rho}$ a column (3×1).

**(320)** Density–density correlator:
$$\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r';t,t')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\Phi^i_0|T\{\rho^i(\mathbf r,t)\rho^i(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^i_0\rangle,\qquad i=e,\ \text{ion}$$

**(321)** Electronic current–current correlator (dia + para):
$$\Pi^e_{jj\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{mc}\rho^e(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')\delta_{\alpha\beta}}_{\Pi^{e,\rm dia}}+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\langle\Phi^{\rm el}_0|T\{j^e_\alpha(\mathbf r,t)j^e_\beta(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^{\rm el}_0\rangle}_{\Pi^{e,\rm para}}$$

**(322)** Ionic current–current correlator:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf r)\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}}+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0|T\{j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\mathbf r,t)j^{\rm ion}_\beta(\mathbf r',t')\}|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\rangle}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,para}}$$
Key statement (p. 70): $\Pi^i_{jj}(\omega)=\Pi^{{\rm para},i}_{jj}(\omega)-\Pi^{{\rm para},i}_{jj}(\omega=0)$ has the mathematical properties of a simple $\langle T\{A(t)B(t')\}\rangle$ correlator, and the author **assumes** the same holds for $\Pi_{\rho\rho},\Pi_{\rho j},\Pi_{j\rho}$.

### 3.6 Sec. 6.0.1 — induced sources and connection formulas

**(323)** $\mathbf A^{\rm ext}(t)=\mathbf A^{{\rm ext},l}(t)+\mathbf A^{{\rm ext},t}(t)$.
**(324)** $\mathbf E^{{\rm ext},\sigma}(t)=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}^{{\rm ext},\sigma}(t)$ for $\sigma=l,t$ ($\Phi=0$ gauge). Here $\sigma$ labels polarization type, not a phonon branch.
**(325)** $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E^l(t)=4\pi\rho^{\rm ext}(t)$, solved by $\mathbf E^l(t)=-\nabla\int v(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1)\rho^{\rm ext}(\mathbf r_1,t)$. $v$ enters as a propagator, not as a scalar potential.

**(326)–(329) Induced sources with bare propagators** ($\otimes$ = convolution in space and time; $i=e$, ion):
$$\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}=\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}\otimes v\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}\quad(326),\qquad\rho^{i,\rm ind}=\sum_\mu\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}\otimes A^{\rm ext}_\mu\quad(327),$$
$$j^{i,{\rm ind},l}_\mu=\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}\otimes v\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}\quad(328),\qquad j^{i,\rm ind}_\mu=\sum_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}\otimes A^{\rm ext}_\nu\quad(329)$$
Diagrammatic recipe: in a corner of Fig. 7, remove the external propagator on the left and attach the external source on the right. Eq. 327 uses the coupling $-\frac1c\int\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$, which does not distinguish $l$ from $t$, so it gives the density induced by the **total** field.

**(330)–(333) Screened versions.** The $\Pi$'s are bare, i.e. irreducible:
$$\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}=\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}\ (330),\quad\rho^{i,\rm ind}=\sum_\mu\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}\otimes A^{{\rm ext,sc}}_\mu\ (331),\quad j^{i,{\rm ind},l}_\mu=\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}\ (332),\quad j^{i,\rm ind}_\mu=\sum_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}\otimes A^{{\rm ext,sc}}_\nu\ (333)$$
$W$ is the screened longitudinal interaction and $A^{\rm ext,sc}$ the screened external field.

**(334)** $\mathbf E^{{\rm ext},\sigma,{\rm sc}}(\omega)=i\frac{\omega}{c}\mathbf A^{{\rm ext},\sigma,{\rm sc}}(\omega)$, $\sigma=l,t$.
**(335)** $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=i\omega\rho$.

**(336)–(339)** The same relations in $\omega$-space with explicit coordinates, for example (336): $\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}(\mathbf r,\omega)=\int d\mathbf r_1d\mathbf r_2\,\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)W(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\omega)\rho^{\rm ext}(\mathbf r_2,\omega)$. Likewise (337) uses $\Pi_{\rho j}$ with $A^{\rm ext,sc}$, (338) uses $\Pi_{j\rho}$ with $W\rho^{\rm ext}$, and (339) uses $\Pi_{jj}$ with $A^{\rm ext,sc}$.

**(340)** $\sum_\mu\partial_\mu j^{i,{\rm ind},l}_\mu(\mathbf r,\omega)=i\omega\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}(\mathbf r,\omega)$.

**(341) Connection formula 1 (boxed)**
$$\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega),\qquad i=e,\ \text{ion}$$
This follows from Eqs. 336, 338 and 340 for arbitrary $\rho^{\rm ext}$.

**(342)** $\sum_\mu\partial_\mu j^{i,\rm ind}_\mu(\mathbf r,\omega)=i\omega\rho^{i,\rm ind}(\mathbf r,\omega)$.
**(343)** $\sum_{\mu\nu}\int d\mathbf r_1\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)A^{\rm ext,sc}_\nu(\mathbf r_1,\omega)=i\omega\sum_\nu\int d\mathbf r_1\Pi^i_{\rho j\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r_1,\omega)A^{\rm ext,sc}_\nu(\mathbf r_1,\omega)$.

**(344) Connection formula 2 (boxed)**
$$\Pi^i_{\rho j\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)$$

**(345)–(346) Mixed correlators**
$$\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega t}\langle T\{j^i_\mu(\mathbf r,t)\rho^i(\mathbf r',0)\}\rangle,\qquad\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega t}\langle T\{\rho^i(\mathbf r,t)j^i_\mu(\mathbf r',0)\}\rangle$$
"Small digression" (pp. 73–74): $\Pi_{j\rho}$ is paired with the scalar coupling $\int d\mathbf r\,\rho V\phi^{\rm ext}$, which gives the standard factor $-i/\hbar$. $\Pi_{\rho j}$ is paired with $-\frac1c\int d\mathbf r\,\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A^{\rm ext}$, which gives $\frac{i}{\hbar c}$. Every current vertex brings a factor $-1/c$ (Fig. 8, p. 74).

**Just beyond the range (pp. 74–75), needed to close the argument:**
$\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)=-\frac1c\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega)$ (347); $\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',-\omega)=\Pi^i_{jj\nu\mu}(\mathbf r',\mathbf r,\omega)$ (348); $\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}=-i\frac{c}{\omega}\sum_\nu\partial'_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}$ (349);
$$\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)\qquad(350)$$
**In Fourier space** (my translation, with $\partial_\mu\to i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu$ and $\partial'_\nu\to-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')_\nu$ under Eq. 294):
$\Pi_{\rho\rho,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)_\mu\Pi_{jj\mu\nu,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')_\nu$. For ions, using Eq. 311:
$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho,\mathbf G\mathbf G'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\frac{[(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma(\mathbf q)][(\mathbf q+\mathbf G')\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma(\mathbf q)]^*}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta}$$
This is negative at $\omega=0$, as a static density response must be. The paper verifies this route in Sec. 6.0.2 (pp. 75–77).

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Why ions dress photons.** In a polar crystal a displacement $Q$ of atom a creates a dipole $Z^*Q$ (Eq. 111). Its time derivative is a current $Z^*\dot Q=Z^*\pi/m$ (Eq. 272), which couples to $\mathbf A$ exactly as an electron current does, via $-\frac1c\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf A$, plus an $A^2$ term. Ions are just "heavy, bound, oscillating charges". Their current response has the same para + dia anatomy as electrons, with $e^2/m_e\to Z^{*2}/m_a$.

**(b) The paramagnetic term is a phonon propagator.** Because the momentum is linear in $a,a^+$, the ionic current–current correlator of harmonic phonons is exactly one free phonon line between two vertices (Eq. 299). This is the same structure as phonon-mediated interactions in electron–phonon theory: vertex × $D$ × vertex. The vertex $j_{\alpha\sigma}=\sum Z^*e/\sqrt m$ is large only for **polar, optically active** modes. For non-polar modes, or for acoustic modes at $\mathbf q\to0$ (where the acoustic sum rule gives $\sum_aZ^*_a=0$), $j_{\alpha\sigma}=0$.

**(c) The diamagnetic term is the response of free ions.** $\Pi^{\rm dia}$ (Eq. 304) is what ions of charge $Z^*$ and mass $m_a$ would do if there were no springs: an inertial (Drude-like) response. At $\omega\gg\omega_\sigma$, $\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}\to\Pi^{\rm dia}$ (from Eq. 311), so the lattice responds like a free "ionic plasma" with $\Omega_p^2=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_aZ^{*2}_a/m_a$.

**(d) Gauge invariance / f-sum rule (Eqs. 309–311).** In the $\Phi=0$ gauge a static uniform $\mathbf A$ carries no electric field ($\mathbf E=i\omega\mathbf A/c=0$), so it must not drive any current: $\Pi^{\rm RPA}(\omega=0)=0$. The cancellation $\Pi^{\rm dia}=-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ guarantees this. For the ionic model it holds **exactly** for every $\mathbf q$ and every tensor component, as a purely algebraic consequence of the completeness of the dynamical-matrix eigenvectors. The spring constants drop out entirely. It is equivalently the **oscillator-strength sum rule** $\sum_\sigma|j_\sigma|^2=\sum_aZ^{*2}_a/m_a$, the ionic analogue of the Thomas–Reiche–Kuhn f-sum rule, and it implies $\int_0^\infty\omega\,{\rm Im}\,\varepsilon^{\rm ion}(\omega)\,d\omega=\frac{\pi}{2}\Omega_p^2$. The springs only redistribute weight among modes; they cannot change the total.

**(e) What goes wrong without the dia term.** If one keeps only $\Pi^{\rm para}$, then $\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\rm correct}-\Omega_p^2/\omega^2$: a spurious Drude divergence. The insulator would look like a metal whose "free carriers" are the ions. This is the classic pitfall in velocity-gauge (current–current) optical calculations. The same issue appears for electrons in RPA/BSE codes that use $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf A$ (velocity) matrix elements, where Eq. 281 does the subtraction.

**(f) $\Pi(\omega)-\Pi(0)$ is a recurring motif.** Electronic photon self-energy: Eq. 281. Ionic: Eq. 311. In Giustino's phonon self-energy (RMP Eq. 121): $\Pi(\omega)\propto ZZ\partial\partial'[W^e(\omega)-W^e(0)]$, enforced by the acoustic sum rule (RMP Eq. 120). The common thread is a symmetry (gauge or translation) that forces the static response to a pure gauge or translation to vanish. A local "contact" term (the $A^2$ term, or the $\nabla\langle n\rangle v$ term) therefore equals minus the static nonlocal response.

**(g) Point dipoles and the dipole grating.** All atoms of a cell sit at $\mathbf R^0_i$, so the ionic response is constant in $\mathbf G,\mathbf G'$ and would Bragg-scatter at every $\mathbf q+\mathbf G$. The author warns that this is unphysical: intra-cell local fields have been squeezed into a point. In practice, use only $\mathbf G=\mathbf G'=0$ (macroscopic) components of the ionic part.

**(h) Bare phonons and the LO frequency.** The $\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)$ in $F_\sigma$ are **bare** phonons. In the EPP construction the long-range Coulomb interaction (Eq. 235) has been moved from $H_0$ into $V'$. So the free phonons are TO-like at $\mathbf q\to0$, with no LO–TO splitting. The LO frequency is generated by dressing with $v$ (or $W$) in the longitudinal sector (top-left of Fig. 7). See also p. 34 (Eq. 162, "LO shift") and Sec. 7.1, p. 90 ("analogous to the non-analytic dynamical matrix correction done by Quantum Espresso"). In the transverse sector, the same $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ produces phonon-polaritons. For an isotropic or cubic crystal at $q\to0$, $\Pi^L_{jj}=\Pi^T_{jj}$, so the longitudinal $\varepsilon_L=1-v\Pi_{\rho\rho}=1+4\pi c\Pi^L_{jj}/\omega^2$ (via Eq. 350) and the transverse $\varepsilon_T=1+4\pi c\Pi^T_{jj}/\omega^2$ coincide. LO modes (zeros of $\varepsilon$) and polaritons ($c^2q^2=\omega^2\varepsilon$) come from one function.

**(i) The $4\times4$ structure is Coulomb-gauge QED in a crystal.** Index 0 is the longitudinal/Coulomb channel (scalar, instantaneous $v$). Indices 1–3 are the transverse, retarded photon $D^{0t}\propto(\omega^2-c^2q^2)^{-1}$. The matter polarization tensor is $\Pi^{\mu\nu}=\{\Pi_{\rho\rho},\Pi_{\rho j},\Pi_{j\rho},\Pi_{jj}\}$. The connection formulas (Eqs. 341, 344, 349, 350) are the Ward identities, i.e. current conservation $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ applied to $\Pi^{\mu\nu}$. Only $\Pi_{jj}$ is independent. For a 2D-materials person used to $W=v+vPW$ (longitudinal only), the message is that the same current–current kernel controls both $W$ (through $q\cdot\Pi_{jj}\cdot q$) and the transverse photon (through the transverse part of $\Pi_{jj}$).

**(j) Screening of induced sources (Eqs. 330–333).** Bare (irreducible) $\Pi$'s give induced sources when the external probe is replaced by its screened version: $v\rho^{\rm ext}\to W\rho^{\rm ext}$ and $A^{\rm ext}\to A^{\rm ext,sc}$. This is the usual $\chi v=PW$ identity, now for both longitudinal and transverse probes.

**Orders of magnitude.** I computed these with Eq. 304 and $\omega_{LO}^2-\omega_{TO}^2=\Omega_p^2/\varepsilon_\infty$ (single mode). The numbers are not in the manuscript. Useful constant: $\Omega_p^2\,[\mathrm{cm^{-2}}]=4.9206\times10^{7}\,Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}[\mathrm{\AA^3}]\,\mu[\mathrm u])$, with $Z^*$ in units of $e$.

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of the EPP manuscript

**Reproduces or parallels Giustino:**
- **Phonon propagator.** EPP $F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}$ (Eqs. 256, 298) has the same mode structure as Giustino's adiabatic displacement–displacement propagator, RMP Eqs. (130)–(131): $D^A=\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}S\,S^*\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu}}$ with $S=e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}(2M_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu})^{-1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}$. Both use the same normal-mode normalization ($\sqrt{\hbar/2M\omega}$ zero-point amplitudes), cf. Giustino Eq. (34).
- **Ionic current vertex ↔ Fröhlich vertex.** The projection $\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma(\mathbf q)=\sum_\kappa\mathbf q\cdot Z^*_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\sigma}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$ is exactly the numerator of the long-range Verdi–Giustino vertex, RMP Eq. (180) (= Eq. 4 of Verdi & Giustino PRL 115, 176401 (2015)): $g^L\propto i\frac{4\pi e^2}{\Omega}\sum_\kappa\left(\frac{\hbar}{2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf G}\frac{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot Z_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}}{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\varepsilon_\infty\cdot(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)}\dots$
  - Via Eq. 350, the ionic density response is $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\frac{|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}$. Hence $v\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}\,v$ (top-left of Fig. 7) equals, after the usual $1/(N\Omega_{\rm uc})$ normalization, $\sum_\sigma|g^L_\sigma|^2\hbar F_\sigma$ with bare $v$ in place of the $\varepsilon_\infty$-screened Coulomb interaction. Replacing $v\to W^e$ restores the $\varepsilon_\infty$ denominators. I checked the prefactors: $(4\pi)^2|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j|^2/(N\Omega_{\rm uc}^2q^4(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2))$ on both sides.
  - So the EPP longitudinal sector at lowest order reproduces **Giustino's $W^{ph}=W^eDW^e$** (RMP Eqs. 104–105, Table I) restricted to the dipolar (Fröhlich) channel.
  - The manuscript itself compares with Giustino PRL Eq. 4 on p. 25 (Sec. 3, polar coupling) and with Verdi & Giustino on pp. 96 and 99 (Sec. 7.2, "electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex"). One remark in Croatian on p. 96 says that, compared with Giustino, a factor $e^2$ is missing, probably in the interaction D or in $Z^*$. Those pages belong to other note-takers.
  - Departure: the EPP vertex has no basis phase $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\tau_\kappa}$, because of the point-dipole model. Giustino's footnote 7 stresses that this factor (missing in Verdi–Giustino Eq. 4) matters for acoustic modes near $q=0$.
- **The "$X(\omega)-X(0)$" structure.** EPP Eq. 311 (and the electronic Eq. 281) parallels Giustino's phonon self-energy, RMP Eq. (121): $\Pi(\omega)=\sum ZZ\,\partial\partial'[\delta\,W^e(\omega)-\delta\,W^e(0)]$, obtained from the Baym / Hedin–Lundqvist **acoustic sum rule** $\sum_{\kappa'p'}\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega=0)=0$ (RMP Eq. 120).
  - Giustino: electrons dress phonons; translation invariance kills the static response to a rigid shift.
  - EPP Sec. 5.2: phonons dress photons; gauge invariance kills the static response to a uniform static $\mathbf A$.
  - A good slide for the page: "the photon self-energy is the dual of the phonon self-energy".
- **DFPT inputs.** Everything in Eqs. 295–311 ($Z^*$, $e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$, $\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}$) is standard DFPT output. EPP p. 5 notes that elastic and dynamical matrices are "standard outputs of most ab initio codes". Giustino Sec. III.B and VI.A.3 discuss computing them, and RMP Sec. VI.A.3 cites Gonze & Lee (1997) for LO–TO. $\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}$ is the Gonze–Lee ionic dielectric tensor.

**Generalizes Giustino:**
- Giustino's Eq. (1)/(37) Hamiltonian couples electrons to phonon displacements through $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)(a+a^+)$. EPP Eq. 312 instead couples ions to the **full electromagnetic field**:
  - **Coulomb channel:** displacements via $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P\propto Z^*(a+a^+)$, which gives the Fröhlich/longitudinal physics.
  - **Transverse channel:** momenta via $\mathbf j^{\rm ion}\cdot\mathbf A\propto Z^*(a-a^+)$, plus an $A^2$ term. This is new relative to Giustino; it gives retardation and polaritons.
  - Electrons and ions interact only through $v$ and $D^{0t}$ in Eq. 312. There is no explicit short-range deformation-potential vertex $g^S$ in this interaction, so the EPP extension concerns the long-range (polar) channel.
- **Hedin–Baym ↔ $4\times4$ $D$.** Giustino's Hedin–Baym set (RMP Table I) is built on the scalar screened interaction $W=W^e+W^{ph}$. EPP replaces $W$ by the $4\times4$ propagator $D$ (Eqs. 316–319, Fig. 7), with longitudinal, transverse and mixed sectors. The electrostatic $W$ is the ll element; retarded transverse photons appear inside it at higher order (Fig. 8). The screened $W$ already appears in Eqs. 330–333.
- **Adiabatic vs non-adiabatic.** The ionic self-energy uses free harmonic phonons and is intrinsically frequency dependent: the full $F_\sigma(\omega)$ is kept. There is no adiabatic (static) approximation on the ionic side. The phonons are "bare": the LO shift is produced by the Coulomb dressing (EPP p. 34 and Sec. 7.1, p. 90). In Giustino's language this is like starting from $D$ without the non-analytic term and generating it via $\Pi$.

**Departs or differs:**
- Point-dipole ions (no $\tau_a$, $\mathbf G$-independent ionic kernel). The author warns against using its Bragg components.
- Gaussian units and $\hbar$ explicit (Giustino mixes SI with $4\pi\varepsilon_0$).
- $T=0$ time-ordered formalism with the $n=0$ prescription for bosons.

## 8. Source issues

1. **Eq. 301 is inconsistent with later use.** The author writes $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j^{\rm ion}=0$ because "there are no free charges $\rho^{\rm ion}=0$". But Eq. 313 defines a nonzero bound charge $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$, and Eqs. 340–342 apply continuity with $i=$ ion and nonzero $\rho^{\rm ion,ind}$. If Eq. 301 held, Eq. 350 would force $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}=0$, contradicting Eqs. 351–353. The correct justification for $\Pi^{\rm dia}=-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ is gauge invariance: a static $\mathbf A$ in the $\Phi=0$ gauge has $\mathbf E=0$ and drives no current. Continuity alone constrains only the longitudinal part. In any case, Sec. 5.2.3 proves the identity independently, via completeness, so the result stands.
2. **Index clash in Eq. 287 (p. 60).** $\alpha,\beta$ are used both as Cartesian indices (of $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ and $Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}$) and as phonon-branch summation indices. The paper switches to $\sigma$ for branches on p. 61. Builders should use $\sigma$ throughout.
3. **Wrong equation reference on p. 64.** "after (290), instead of (354), we need to make the following substitutions" should read "instead of (291)". Eq. 354 (p. 76) is an identical substitution set used later for $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$, so this looks like a leftover from reordering.
4. **Time-ordered vs retarded.** $F_\sigma=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}$ (Eqs. 290, 297–298, after $n=0$) is the $T=0$ time-ordered form, with poles at $\pm(\omega_\sigma-i0)$. It equals the retarded function (Eq. 247, $(\omega+i\eta)^2$) only for $\omega>0$. This is fine for optics at $\omega>0$, but plots of ${\rm Im}$ for $\omega<0$ would have the wrong sign. Use $(\omega+i\gamma)^2$ or $\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\gamma\omega$ in the visualizations.
5. **Implicit relabeling before Eq. 304 (p. 66).** The step to the final form needs $\mathbf G+\mathbf G'\to\mathbf G$, which is not stated. Also, the Kronecker $\delta_{\mathbf q\mathbf G}$ inside $\int d\mathbf q$ is handled loosely via $\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\to\frac1\Omega\sum_{\mathbf q}$. The result is correct.
6. **Acoustic modes in Eqs. 306–307.** At exactly $\mathbf q=0$, $\omega_\sigma\to0$ for acoustic branches, so $\omega_\sigma^2/\omega_\sigma^2$ is 0/0. This is harmless: take $\omega\to0$ before $\mathbf q\to0$. Also $j_{\alpha\sigma}^{\rm ac}(\mathbf q=0)\propto\sum_aZ^*_a=0$ by the acoustic sum rule (charge neutrality). Eq. 309 relies only on completeness. The ASR is used implicitly elsewhere but never stated in this range.
7. **Point-dipole approximation.** Basis positions $\tau_a$ are dropped; all atoms sit at $\mathbf R^0_i$ (Eqs. 271, 272, 314). The author acknowledges the unphysical $\mathbf G$-independence (p. 62). The statement under Eq. 300 that the 2D result is an "exact 2D polarizability" is exact only within this model.
8. **Units and charge bookkeeping in Eq. 312 (and Eq. 235).**
   - $\rho^e=\Psi^+\Psi$ is a **number** density (Eq. 239), while $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ is a **charge** density ($Z^*$ carries charge). The Coulomb term $\frac12\int\int(\rho^e+\rho^{\rm ion})(\rho^e+\rho^{\rm ion})/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$ therefore mixes units. Write it with the electron charge density $-e\rho^e$.
   - The continuity relations (Eqs. 335, 340–342) for $i=e$ likewise need $\rho^e$ to be a charge density, since $\mathbf j^e$ in Eq. 238 includes $e$.
   - The electron current (Eq. 238) uses $+e$. The sign is immaterial for $\Pi$, which is quadratic in it.
   - Eq. 321 writes $m$ where Eq. 312 has $m_e$.
9. **Assumed properties of the mixed correlators (p. 70).** "we assume that the same holds for other correlators". The time-ordered-correlator symmetry properties used later in Eqs. 347–348 are proven only for $\Pi_{jj}$ (as $\Pi^{\rm para}(\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$), not for $\Pi_{\rho j}$ or $\Pi_{j\rho}$. Sec. 6.0.2 checks only Eq. 351 explicitly, for ions, and then concludes "inductively" that all the formulas hold.
10. **"Equal only if" steps (Eqs. 341, 344).** Equality of the integrated expressions implies equality of the kernels only for arbitrary $\rho^{\rm ext}$ (Eq. 341, also assuming $W$ is invertible) or arbitrary $\mathbf A^{\rm ext,sc}$ (Eq. 344). Fine physically, but worth a footnote.
11. **Prefactor asymmetry (Eqs. 345–346).** $-\frac{i}{\hbar}$ for $\Pi_{j\rho}$ vs $+\frac{i}{\hbar c}$ for $\Pi_{\rho j}$ is intentional (explained in the digression) but leads to the non-obvious $-1/c$ in Eq. 347. Readers should keep the convention that each $j$ vertex carries $-1/c$.
12. **Notation.** $\sigma$ means phonon branch in Eqs. 289–311 but polarization type ($l,t$) in Eqs. 324 and 334. $D$ (photon) vs $D$ (Giustino's phonon propagator) is a possible confusion when connecting the two papers. The hat on $\hat D^{0,t}$ (Eqs. 317–319) is used inconsistently: no hat in Eqs. 261 and 277.
13. **Typos:** "paramegnetic", "diamagnatic", "diamagnate", "denity", "Moreower", "Derivation the diamagnetic self-energy from paramegnetic self-energy" (section title), "the the ionic conjugated momenta", "Faynman", "Poison's equation", "transfersal", "perurbative", "tranfersal-longitudinal", "Eqs.38" (should be Eq. 38). Bold "**2**" on p. 59 is the combinatorial factor.
14. **Dimensional sanity check (mine, all consistent).** $\Pi_{jj}(\mathbf q)$ has units of 1/(c·time²), so $-4\pi c\Pi$ is a frequency². $\Pi_{\rho\rho}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')$ has units of length⁻⁵, consistent with $\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial\partial'\Pi_{jj}$. $j_{\alpha\sigma}$ has units of charge/$\sqrt{\rm mass}$. No missing factors found in Eqs. 286–311 after verifying each step.
