## 0. Conventions (read first)

- **Units: Gaussian, $\hbar$ explicit.** Coulomb kernel $v(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')=1/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, so $v(\mathbf q)=4\pi/q^2$ in 3D and $v_Q=2\pi/Q$ for a strictly 2D sheet (p. 101). The charges sit in the vertices ($Z^*$ and $j^\mu$), never in $v$. On p. 96 the text states $e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0\equiv e^2$.
- **Born effective charges** $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$ **carry the dimension of charge** (they include $e$). This is stated after Eq. 131 on p. 25. They are real tensors: first index = dipole/current direction, second = displacement direction.
- **Gauge and fields:** $\Phi=0$ gauge, so $\mathbf E=-\frac1c\dot{\mathbf A}$ (Eq. 132). $D_{\mu\nu}$ is the $3\times3$ propagator of the *total* vector potential $\mathbf A=\mathbf A^l+\mathbf A^t$. It is defined as $D=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{A_\mu A_\nu\}\rangle$ (Eq. 318 for the transverse part). The longitudinal part is introduced ad hoc as $D^{0,l}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu v$ (Eq. 364).
- **Fourier conventions:** $f(\mathbf r)=\int\frac{d\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}f(\mathbf q)$ (Eq. 117). For translationally invariant kernels $\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\to q_\mu q_\nu$. In time, $F(\omega)=\int dt\,e^{i\omega t}F(t)$ (Eq. 255). Partial 2D transform: $(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\to(\mathbf Q,z,z')$.
- **Normalization:** crystal volume $\Omega=N\Omega_{\rm uc}$ ($N$ cells, cell volume $\Omega_{\rm uc}$). Bloch states $\phi_{n\mathbf k}=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}u_{n\mathbf k}/\sqrt\Omega$ (Eq. 96). Displacements $Q_{ia\mu}=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{\sigma\mathbf q}\sqrt{\hbar/2m_a\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)}\,e_{a\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)(a_{\mathbf q\sigma}+a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\sigma})e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_i^0}$ (Eq. 90). In 2D, $\Omega\to S$ and $\Omega_{\rm uc}\to S_{\rm uc}$ (surface areas).
- **Point dipoles at lattice sites:** the ionic polarization is $P_\mu(\mathbf r)=\sum_{ia\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf R^0_i)$ (Eq. 314). All dipoles of a cell sit at the cell origin $\mathbf R_i^0$, not at the atomic positions $\boldsymbol\tau_a$, so there is no $e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_a}$ phase (see Sec. 8, source issues).
- **Propagators are time-ordered and $T=0$** (phonon occupations $n_{\mathbf q}=0$, Sec. 4.5). The retarded version is obtained by $i\eta\to i\eta\omega$ (p. 88). Electronic occupations $f_{n\mathbf k}$ may be at finite $T$.
- **Indices:** $\sigma,\sigma'=1..3s$ phonon branches ($s$ atoms per cell). $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\mu,\nu\in\{x,y,z\}$ Cartesian. $a,b$ atoms in the cell. $i,j$ cells. $n,m$ bands. A spin factor of 2 sits explicitly in $\chi^0$ and in Eq. 383.
- **Two "self-energies", two symbols:** $\Pi_{jj}$ is the *photon* self-energy (current-current correlator). $\Omega^{e}_{\sigma\sigma'}$ is the *phonon* self-energy, with the dimension of energy. Mapping to Giustino: $\Omega^e=\hbar\,\Pi^{\rm Giustino}$, because Giustino's $\Pi$ has frequency units (compare Eq. 405 here with Giustino Eq. 137).
- **Notation switch at the start of Sec. 7 (p. 87):** bare (irreducible RPA) correlators get a superscript 0, $\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj},\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj}$. Screened ones (reducible with respect to $D^0$) have none: $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj},\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$. In Eqs. 385 and 391 the old notation, where $\Pi^{\rm ion}$ means the bare one, is still used.

## 1. Overview

The preceding chapters built one object, the RPA propagator $D_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ of the *total* electromagnetic field. It contains the longitudinal Coulomb part and the transverse photons, and it is dressed by two photon self-energies: an electronic one, $\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$, and an ionic one, $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$, which comes from the Born-charge dipoles of vibrating ions. This last part of the manuscript turns that construction around. It asks what the electromagnetic field does to the phonons.

The trick is a simple rearrangement of diagrams. Take every diagram of $D$ that starts and ends on an ionic current bubble and amputate the outer photon lines. Resum all electronic bubbles into an electron-dressed photon line $D^{\rm e}$. Then write each bare ionic bubble as "vertex × bare phonon propagator × vertex". The series becomes a matrix Dyson equation for the phonon Green's function, $F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F$. The phonon self-energy has a very transparent form:
$$\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}J_{\beta\sigma'}.$$
A phonon emits a (longitudinal or transverse) photon through its Born-charge current, the photon propagates through the polarizable electron gas, and it is reabsorbed.

Three physically distinct pieces come out:
1. **No electrons ($D^{\rm e}\to D^0$).** The vacuum electromagnetic field shifts the polar modes. At $\mathbf q=0$, inside the light cone, the shift is $\tilde\omega^2=\omega^2+(4\pi/\Omega_{\rm uc})\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$. The manuscript calls this the LO-TO splitting.
2. **Electronic correction.** Splitting off the electron loop gives $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}=\sum g^{\rm dyn}\chi^0g^{\rm dyn,0}$. This is a "screened vertex – electron-hole bubble – bare vertex" structure that defines a *bare* and a *screened electrodynamic electron-phonon vertex*. For $c\to\infty$ these reduce to the Fröhlich vertex of Verdi & Giustino (PRL 115, 176401, 2015). The bare one has $\epsilon_\infty=1$. In the screened one $\epsilon_\infty$ is replaced by the dynamical RPA tensor $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$.
3. **Electrostatic limit.** For $c\to\infty$ the whole self-energy collapses to $\Omega^{\rm e}=\hbar W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2/(2\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma)$. With static screening this gives the textbook non-analytic LO-TO term. In 2D it gives the $Q$-linear LO dispersion of Sohier et al. (Nano Lett. 17, 3758, 2017), with no splitting at $\Gamma$.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **Goal (p. 86).** Find the perturbative expansion of the phonon Green's function $F_\sigma(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (Eq. 251) without re-deriving all diagrams from $H$ (Eq. 312). Instead, reuse the known expansion of $D_{\mu\nu}$.
2. **Ionic jj expansion (Eq. 386, Fig. 12a).** $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ is obtained from all $D$-diagrams that start and end with $\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj}$, with the outer $D^0$ removed. Interior bubbles can be ionic or electronic.
3. **Resum the electrons (Eqs. 387–388, Fig. 12b).** Collect the electronic insertions between ionic bubbles into $D^{\rm e}=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj}D^{\rm e}$. This gives $\Pi^{\rm ion}=\Pi^{\rm ion,0}+\Pi^{\rm ion,0}D^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}+\dots$ The analogy given: the response $\chi_A$ of medium A embedded in a polarizable medium B.
4. **Phonons inside the ionic bubble (Eqs. 389–393).** The bare ionic correlator is rewritten with the bare phonon propagator $F^0_\sigma$ and a new *dynamical ionic current vertex* $J_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q,\omega)\propto\omega j_{\mu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$. This gives $\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj}=-\sum_\sigma J F^0 J^*$.
5. **Dyson equation for phonons (Eqs. 394–399, Figs. 12c, 13).** Insert step 4 into step 3 and recognize the geometric series $F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0+\dots$, i.e. $F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F$. The phonon self-energy is $\Omega^{\rm e}=-J^*D^{\rm e}J$.
6. **Split (Eqs. 400–403):** $\Omega^{\rm e}=\Omega^0+\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$. $\Omega^0$ uses $D^0$ (electron-free). $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ contains the screened electronic correlator $\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$.
7. **Diagonal approximation (Eqs. 404–405).** Neglect phonon mixing ($F_{\sigma\sigma'}\approx F_\sigma\delta_{\sigma\sigma'}$). Then $F_\sigma=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2-2\omega_\sigma\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}/\hbar}$.
8. **Sec. 7.1, vacuum photons only (Eqs. 406–410).** $D^{\rm e}\to D^0$. The pole condition (Eq. 407) is self-consistent. At $\mathbf q=0$, $D^0=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ (Eq. 408), so $\Omega^0$ is frequency independent (Eq. 409) and $\tilde\omega_\sigma^2=\omega_\sigma^2+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$ (Eq. 410).
9. **Sec. 7.2, electron-induced part (Eqs. 411–416, Fig. 14).** Rewrite $D^0\Pi^{\rm e}D^0=D^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0$ (Eq. 412). Insert the explicit RPA $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj}$ (Eq. 383), factorized into a *dynamical electron current vertex* $J^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)$ and a *bare electron-hole propagator* $\chi^0$ (Eqs. 413–414). Define the **bare** vertex $g^{\rm dyn,0}=i[J^{\rm e}]^*D^0J^{\rm ion}$ (Eq. 415) and the **screened** vertex $g^{\rm dyn}=iJ^{\rm ion*}D^{\rm e}J^{\rm e}$ (Eq. 416). Then $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}=\sum_{\mathbf k nm}g^{\rm dyn}\chi^0g^{\rm dyn,0}$.
10. **Sec. 7.2.1, bare vertex for $c\to\infty$ (Eqs. 417–428).** Insert the vertices and $D^0$, then take $c\to\infty$ (Eq. 418). Derive the *connection between charge and current vertices*, $\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=-\sum_\mu\hbar q_\mu j^\mu/(E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})$ (Eq. 428). This follows from the continuity equation with free-electron Heisenberg evolution (Eqs. 419–427). The result is the Verdi–Giustino Fröhlich vertex with $\epsilon_\infty=1$, $\mathbf G=0$ (pp. 95–96).
11. **Sec. 7.2.2, screened vertex for $c\to\infty$ (Eqs. 429–448).** Write the $D^{\rm e}$ Dyson equation as $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}D^{\rm e}=D^0$ (Eqs. 431–432). For $c\to\infty$ only the $ll$ sector survives (Eqs. 433–434). It equals $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu W^{\rm e}$, where $W^{\rm e}=D^{\rm e}_{11}$ is the RPA-screened Coulomb interaction (Eqs. 435–441). Then $W^{\rm e}=4\pi/(\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf q)$ (Eq. 445) and $D^{\rm e}\to-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf q}$ (Eq. 446). The result is the Verdi–Giustino vertex with $\epsilon_\infty\to\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (p. 98, boxed). It is "the complex conjugate" of the bare one.
12. **Sec. 7.3, full self-energy for $c\to\infty$ (Eqs. 449–454).** $\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}\to\frac{\hbar W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)}{2\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2$ (Eq. 452). With static screening, $\tilde\omega^2_\sigma=\omega^2_\sigma+\frac{W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,0)}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2$ (Eq. 454). This is "very similar (probably identical)" to Sohier et al., Eq. 2.
13. **Sec. 8, 2D (Eqs. 455–459).** A 2D crystal is a sheet $\propto\delta(z)\delta(z')$ of ionic currents. The same Dyson structure holds with $D^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)\to D^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,0,0)$ and $\Omega_{\rm uc}\to S_{\rm uc}$. $D^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,z,z')$ solves a $z$-resolved Dyson equation, which is the origin of crystal local-field effects (CLFE) along $z$.
14. **Sec. 8.1, 2D electrostatic limit (Eqs. 460–472).** Neglect the electronic $z$-LFE. Then $W^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,z,z')=v_Qe^{-Q|z-z'|}+v_Q^2e^{-Q(|z|+|z'|)}\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}$ (Eq. 461) and $W^{\rm e,2D}=v_Q/\epsilon^{\rm e}_{2D}$ (Eqs. 469–470). The result is $\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf Q)=\omega_\sigma^2+\frac{1}{S_{\rm uc}}\frac{v_Q}{\epsilon^{\rm e}_{2D}(Q,0)}|\sum_{\alpha=x,y}j_{\alpha\sigma}Q_\alpha|^2$ (Eq. 472), "consistent with Eq. 3" of Sohier et al.

$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)=\omega_\sigma^2(\mathbf q)+2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)\,\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}[\mathbf q,\tilde\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q)]/\hbar.$$
**(408)** Optical limit $\mathbf q=0$ of Eq. 375: $D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q=0,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$. This is the regime $cq\ll\omega$; the result is the same for any direction of approach.
**(409)** $\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}(\mathbf q=0)=\dfrac{2\pi\hbar}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q=0)}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q=0)|^2$. It is frequency independent because $J^*J\propto\omega^2$ cancels the $1/\omega^2$ of $D^0$.
**(410)**
$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q=0)=\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q=0)+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q=0)|^2 .$$
The text says this is "completely analogous to the non-analytic dynamical matrix correction done by Quantum Espresso". Note the *isotropic* sum $\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$. For a diatomic cubic crystal ($Z^*_a=-Z^*_b=Z^*$, reduced mass $\mu$), $\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2=Z^{*2}/\mu$, so the shift is the ionic plasma frequency $\omega_p^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}\mu)$ of Eq. 153 (p. 33). The interpretation is discussed in Secs. 4 and 8.

**General-$\mathbf q$ form (my derivation from Eqs. 375, 392, 405, 406; not in the text, useful for V1).** Decompose $\mathbf j_\sigma=\mathbf j_\parallel+\mathbf j_\perp$ with respect to $\hat{\mathbf q}$:
$$\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}\Big[|\mathbf j_\parallel|^2+|\mathbf j_\perp|^2\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-c^2q^2}\Big]\;\Rightarrow\;\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+\omega_{p\parallel}^2+\omega_{p\perp}^2\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-c^2q^2},\quad \omega_{p\parallel,\perp}^2=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}|\mathbf j_{\parallel,\perp}|^2 .$$
- Longitudinal: $\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+\omega_p^2$ for all $q$. There is no retardation (LO).
- Transverse: $(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)(\omega^2-c^2q^2)=\omega_p^2\omega^2$, i.e. $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon(\omega)$ with $\epsilon(\omega)=1+\omega_p^2/(\omega_\sigma^2-\omega^2)$. These are the phonon polaritons. At $q=0$ the upper branch reproduces Eq. 410. For $cq\gg\omega$ the TO mode stays at $\omega_\sigma$.

### 3.4 Sec. 7.2: electron-induced correction and the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertices

**(411)** = Eq. 402. Using $\Pi^{\rm e}=\Pi^{\rm e,0}+\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}+\dots$ and $D^{\rm e}=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0+\dots$:

$$\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=-\sum_{\mathbf k\in BZ,nm}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\alpha_1\beta_1}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q,\omega)D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\alpha_1}(\mathbf q,\omega)J^{\alpha_1}_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)\,\chi^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)\,[J^{\beta_1}_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)]^*D^0_{\beta_1\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)J_{\beta\sigma'}(\mathbf q,\omega).$$
**(414)** Dynamical electron current vertex (boxed):
$$j^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\to J^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)=\sqrt{\frac1{c\Omega}}\;\frac{\hbar\omega}{E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}\;j^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}.$$
**Bare electron-hole propagator** (boxed, unnumbered):
$$\chi^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)=2\,\frac{E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\hbar\omega}\;\frac{f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}+i\eta}.$$
Check: $J\chi^0J^*$ reproduces Eq. 383 exactly. The factor 2 is spin. This $\chi^0$ is *not* the plain Lindhard term. Using $\frac{\Delta E}{\hbar\omega(\hbar\omega+\Delta E)}=\frac1{\hbar\omega}-\frac1{\hbar\omega+\Delta E}$, it differs from $-$Lindhard by a term $\propto\sum(f_{n\mathbf k}-f_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})|\rho|^2/\hbar\omega$. That term vanishes for a complete band set (an f-sum-rule/gauge-invariance identity).
**(415)** Bare electrodynamic e-ph vertex (boxed):
$$g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\omega)=i\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}[J^\alpha_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)]^*\,D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf q,\omega).$$
**(416)** Screened electrodynamic e-ph vertex (boxed):
$$g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\omega)=i\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q,\omega)\,J^\beta_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega).$$
**Self-energy in vertex form** (unnumbered, p. 92; Fig. 14):
$$\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\mathbf q,\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf k\in BZ,nm}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\omega)\,\chi^0_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}(\omega)\,g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma'}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\omega).$$
The sign checks: $i\cdot i=-1$ reproduces the minus in Eq. 413. Interpretation: an electron (or hole) at $n\mathbf k$ scatters to $m\mathbf k+\mathbf q$ by absorbing a photon emitted by the phonon. Both vertices are frequency dependent, which goes beyond the adiabatic $g(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$.

**(463)** Ignoring the divergent local term $\sim\delta(z-z')$: $\big[i\mathbf Q+\partial_z\big]\big[-i\mathbf Q+\partial_{z'}\big]W^{\rm e}\big|_{z=z'=0}\equiv Q_\mu Q_\nu W^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,0,0)$, $\mu,\nu=x,y$.
**(464)** $\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf Q,\omega,0,0)\equiv-\frac{c}{\omega^2}Q_\mu Q_\nu W^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,0,0)$, $\mu,\nu=x,y$.
**(465)** $\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\mathbf Q,\omega)=-\frac{\hbar}{2cS_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma(\mathbf Q)}\omega^2\sum_{\alpha\beta}j^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf Q)D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf Q,\omega,0,0)j_{\beta\sigma}(\mathbf Q)$.
**(466)** $\displaystyle\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\mathbf Q,\omega)=\frac{\hbar W^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,z=0,z'=0)}{2S_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma(\mathbf Q)}\Big|\sum_{\alpha=x,y}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf Q)Q_\alpha\Big|^2$.
**(467)** $F_\sigma(\mathbf Q,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf Q)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf Q)-2\omega_\sigma(\mathbf Q)\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\mathbf Q,\omega)/\hbar}$ (Eq. 405 unchanged in 2D).
**(468)** $\displaystyle\omega^2=\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf Q)+\frac{W^{\rm e}(\mathbf Q,\omega,0,0)}{S_{\rm uc}}\Big|\sum_{\alpha=x,y}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf Q)Q_\alpha\Big|^2$.

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) The phonon self-energy is "emit a photon, propagate it through the electrons, reabsorb it".** A polar phonon carries an oscillating dipole $p_\mu=\sum Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{a\nu}$, i.e. a current $J\propto\omega Z^*Q$. That current radiates into the total EM field: a longitudinal Coulomb part plus transverse photons. The field propagates (dressed by the electronic polarizability, $D^{\rm e}$) and acts back on the same or another phonon mode. Everything long-range in polar lattice dynamics comes from this single loop: LO-TO splitting, polaritons, plasmon-phonon coupling, and the 2D LO dispersion. The bare $\omega_\sigma$ contain only short-range forces.

**(b) Why a Dyson equation, and why the diagonal approximation is not trivial.** Each finite order of Eq. 397 has poles only at the *bare* $\omega_\sigma$, with higher-order poles. Only the resummation (Eq. 398) moves the pole. Because $D^{\rm e}$ is a tensor, the same photon couples different branches (off-diagonal $\Omega_{\sigma\sigma'}$). The diagonal approximation (Eq. 404) is first-order degenerate-free perturbation theory in the mode basis, which is exactly what is done in Giustino Eq. 137. It is accurate when the shift is small compared with branch separations. For several polar modes with similar frequencies (e.g. perovskites, multiple IR modes) the matrix form matters.

**(c) Order of limits: retarded $q\to0$ versus electrostatic.** Eq. 410 takes $\mathbf q=0$ at finite $c$, i.e. $cq\ll\omega$, inside the light cone. There, $D^0=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ is isotropic, so *every* polar mode shifts by $\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$, transverse ones included. This is the $q=0$ end of the upper phonon-polariton branch. In polariton theory, transverse and longitudinal modes are degenerate at $\omega_{LO}$ at $q=0$, while the lower polariton is photon-like and goes to zero. The textbook "LO-TO splitting" (and the QE non-analytic correction) is the opposite order: $c\to\infty$ first, then $q\to0$ (Eq. 454). That gives a shift $\propto|\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2/(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q})$ that depends on the direction of approach (non-analytic). TO modes are unshifted. In real crystals the crossover happens at $q\sim\omega_{TO}/c$. For GaAs, $\hbar\omega_{TO}\approx33$ meV gives $q\approx0.17\ \mu$m$^{-1}$, about $10^{-5}$ of the BZ, which is why DFPT can ignore retardation. The general-$\mathbf q$ formula in Sec. 3.3 contains both limits.

**(d) Size of the bare shift and how electrons tame it.** Eq. 410 has no electrons ($\epsilon_\infty=1$). In GaAs, $Z^*\approx2.07$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}=a^3/4\approx45.2$ Å$^3$ and $\mu\approx36.1$ amu give $\hbar\omega_p\approx45$ meV. The vacuum-only formula would therefore put $\omega_{LO}\approx\sqrt{33^2+45^2}\approx56$ meV. The electronic screening in $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ divides the shift by $\epsilon_\infty\approx10.9$, giving the measured 36 meV. (These are standard literature values, not from the manuscript.) $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ is negative for an insulator: screening always reduces the bare dipolar stiffening.

**(e) Bare vs screened vertex, and why the self-energy has one of each.** Eq. 412 puts $D^{\rm e}$ on one side and $D^0$ on the other. If both were screened, the electronic bubbles would be double-counted. This is the electrodynamic version of the statement in Giustino (Sec. V.A.3, Eq. 145) that the phonon self-energy is $\Pi=g^b\,GG\,g$, with one bare and one dressed vertex. The common practice of using $|g|^2$ (both screened) is an approximation. EPP's vertices are additionally *frequency dependent* through $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$. This keeps what Giustino's approximation (iv) drops: the frequency dependence of the dressed vertex $g^{cc}(\mathbf r,\omega)$, Eq. 144.

**(f) Why the Fröhlich $1/q$ survives retardation, and what is new for TO modes.** Contract $D^0$ (Eq. 375) with a longitudinal ionic current $\mathbf j_\sigma\parallel\hat{\mathbf q}$: $(-q_\alpha q_\beta+\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\alpha\beta})\hat q_\beta/(q^2-\omega^2/c^2)=-\hat q_\alpha$, independent of $c$. The LO coupling therefore keeps exactly its Coulomb form $\propto\rho^*/q$ at every $q$. For a transverse ionic current the kernel is $\frac{\omega^2/c^2}{q^2-\omega^2/c^2}$. It vanishes as $c\to\infty$ (TO phonons do not couple to electrons by Fröhlich's mechanism), becomes $-1$ for $cq\ll\omega$, and is resonant at the light line. In EPP, TO phonons acquire a photon-mediated coupling to *transverse* electronic currents near the light cone. This is outside standard e-ph theory.

**(g) The charge–current connection (Eq. 428) is gauge invariance in matrix-element form.** $\hbar\,\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=(E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}-E_{n\mathbf k})\,\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}$ (up to $e$). It lets every longitudinal $jj$ quantity be rewritten in $\rho\rho$ language. It is the same identity that makes velocity-gauge and length-gauge optical matrix elements equivalent in BSE/RPA codes ($\langle v|\mathbf p|c\rangle=im\omega_{cv}\langle v|\mathbf r|c\rangle$). Caveat: it holds for a local Hamiltonian $H^e_0$. With nonlocal pseudopotentials or scissor-shifted energies the commutator acquires extra terms, a familiar issue in optical codes.

**(h) The electrostatic limit is $W^{\rm e}$-mediated dipole–dipole interaction.** Eq. 452, $\Omega^{\rm e}=\frac{\hbar}{2\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)|\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2$, is the energy of two macroscopic polarization waves interacting through the screened Coulomb interaction. Only the component of the mode dipole along $\mathbf q$ couples (longitudinal field). For insulators $W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega\approx\omega_{ph})\approx4\pi/(\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\mathbf q)$, which gives the LST relation and LO-TO splitting. For doped semiconductors or metals, $W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ has a plasmon pole near phonon frequencies. Eq. 453 must then be kept dynamical, giving coupled LO-phonon–plasmon modes (see V3). This is precisely the nonadiabatic physics Giustino's review "speculates" could come out of the frequency-dependent self-energy.

**(i) RPA-without-LFE caveat.** EPP keeps only $\mathbf G=0$ ("CLFs are mostly neglected here"). So $\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf q$ is the *head* $\epsilon_{00}$ of the RPA dielectric matrix, not the macroscopic $\epsilon_M=1/\epsilon^{-1}_{00}$ that DFPT's $\epsilon_\infty$ represents (which also contains xc). For bulk Si, for example, RPA without LFE overestimates $\epsilon_\infty$ by roughly 10% relative to with-LFE values. Same remark for Born charges.

**(j) 2D: no LO-TO splitting at $\Gamma$.** In a strictly 2D sheet, $v_Q=2\pi/Q$ while $|\mathbf Q\cdot\mathbf j|^2\propto Q^2$, so the shift is $\propto Q/\epsilon_{2D}(Q)$ and vanishes linearly as $Q\to0$. The LO and TO branches are degenerate at $\Gamma$, and LO disperses linearly with a finite slope (Sohier et al. 2017). For a 2D semiconductor, $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(Q,0)\approx-\alpha_{2D}Q^2$ at small $Q$, so $\epsilon_{2D}=1+r_0Q$ with $r_0=2\pi\alpha_{2D}$, the Keldysh/Rytova screening length. $W^{\rm e,2D}=2\pi/[Q(1+r_0Q)]$ is exactly the Rytova–Keldysh interaction used in 2D BSE for excitons. The same $W$ that binds excitons sets the 2D LO dispersion: slope $\propto|{\hat{\mathbf Q}}\cdot\mathbf j|^2/(2\omega_\sigma S_{\rm uc})$ for $Q\ll1/r_0$, saturation to $\omega^2_\sigma+\frac{2\pi}{S_{\rm uc}r_0}|\hat{\mathbf Q}\cdot\mathbf j|^2$ for $Q\gg1/r_0$. Illustrative hBN monolayer numbers (not from the manuscript): $S_{\rm uc}\approx5.4$ Å$^2$, $Z^*\approx2.7$, $\mu\approx6.1$ amu, $\hbar\omega_{TO}\approx170$ meV, $r_0\sim5$–$10$ Å. The shift term is then $(\hbar\omega)^2\approx0.083$ eV$^2$·Å$\times Q/(1+r_0Q)$. That gives $\hbar\omega_{LO}\approx182$ meV at $Q=0.1$ Å$^{-1}$ ($r_0=10$ Å), saturating at about 193 meV for $Q\gg1/r_0$, close to bulk LO (about 200 meV). The initial slope is $\hbar\,d\omega/dQ\approx C/(2\hbar\omega_T)\approx0.25$ eV·Å.

**(k) Out-of-plane dipoles in 2D.** Eqs. 463–466 keep only $\alpha=x,y$. The dropped $zz$ term contains $\partial_z\partial_{z'}e^{-Q|z-z'|}=-Q^2e^{-Q|z-z'|}+2Q\delta(z-z')$. The $\delta$ term is the depolarization field of a dipole sheet. Physically it gives a $Q$-independent shift of out-of-plane polar (ZO-type) modes, not a negligible contribution. The 2D result as written applies to in-plane LO modes.

**(l) Structural analogy for the user.** Eq. 388 versus Eq. 387 is exactly "screening of system A by environment B". It is the same logic as embedding a 2D layer's $\chi^0$ in a dielectric environment, or splitting $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$. In the electrostatic limit, the total longitudinal propagator with both bubbles is $W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}W^{\rm e}$, where $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ is built from the dressed $F$. The second term is Giustino's phonon-mediated $W^{\rm ph}$ (Table I, Eq. 110), with the screened Fröhlich vertex at each end. (This identification is mine; the text does not spell it out.)

  - doping gives a $\sqrt Q$ 2D plasmon that anticrosses the LO branch.

### V5. "Why resum? Dyson series for the phonon propagator" (Eqs. 397, 398, 404, 405)
- **Plot:** $-\mathrm{Im}\,F_N(\omega)$ versus $\hbar\omega$ for truncation orders $N=0..10$, against the exact Dyson result.
- **Formulas:**
  - $F^0(\omega)=\frac{2\omega_\sigma/\hbar}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\gamma\omega}$;
  - $F_N=F^0\sum_{n=0}^N(F^0\Omega)^n$ with a constant $\Omega$ (static, from Eq. 409 or 452);
  - exact $F=F^0/(1-F^0\Omega)$, peak at $\tilde\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+2\omega_\sigma\Omega/\hbar$.
- **Controls:** $\hbar\omega_\sigma$ (meV), shift strength $2\omega_\sigma\Omega/\hbar$ (as $\Delta(\omega^2)$), broadening $\gamma$, order $N$.
- **Notice:**
  - finite orders never move the peak; they pile up increasingly singular structure at the bare $\omega_\sigma$;
  - the series diverges where $|F^0\Omega|>1$ (near the bare pole);
  - only the infinite sum (Eq. 398) produces the renormalized pole.
  - This is the diagrammatic reason behind Figs. 12c and 13.

## 7. Connections to Giustino (2017) and to the rest of the EPP manuscript

- **Phonon Dyson equation and frequency shift.** EPP Eq. 398 $F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F$ is the analog of Giustino Eqs. 135–136 (phonon Dyson equation, Fig. 1a) and Eq. 118 (equation of motion for nuclei). The diagonal pole condition from Eq. 405, $\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+2\omega_\sigma\Omega_{\sigma\sigma}/\hbar$, is Giustino Eq. 137, $\tilde\Omega^2=\omega^2+2\omega\Pi^{NA}(\tilde\Omega)$, with $\Omega=\hbar\Pi$. EPP likewise solves it self-consistently (Eq. 407) and drops off-diagonal elements, which Giustino lists as effect (iii), mode coupling. EPP's full $3s\times3s$ matrix (Eq. 398) keeps them.
- **Phonon self-energy through $W^{\rm e}$.** Giustino Eq. 121 (Hedin–Baym, Table I) is $\Pi_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\sum Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa''}\partial_\alpha\partial'_{\alpha'}[\delta\,W^{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',\omega)-\delta\,W^{\rm e}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r',0)]$. Bare point nuclei interact through the electronically screened Coulomb interaction. EPP Eq. 452 has the same structure, $\Omega^{\rm e}\propto(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma)^*W^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma)$, but with three differences:
  - Born-charge point *dipoles* at cell origins replace bare nuclear charges, so EPP describes only the long-range ($\mathbf G=0$) dipolar part, and the short-range part sits in the bare $\omega_\sigma$;
  - the electromagnetic field includes transverse photons. Eq. 399 at finite $c$ is a genuine extension of the Hedin–Baym equations, which use the instantaneous Coulomb $v$ only;
  - $W^{\rm e}$ is RPA (Giustino's approximation (i), $\Gamma=\delta\delta$) without local fields.
- **Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic.** Giustino Eq. 125 splits $\Pi=\Pi^A+\Pi^{NA}$ with $\Pi^A=\Pi(\omega=0)$ (DFPT force constants), and Eq. 140 has $\Pi^{NA}\propto W^{\rm e}(\omega)-W^{\rm e}(0)$. In EPP, $\Omega^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,0)$ (Eq. 454) is the long-range part of $\Pi^A$, i.e. the non-analytic term that DFPT adds via $Z^*$ and $\epsilon_\infty$. $\Omega^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)-\Omega^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,0)$ is the dipolar part of $\Pi^{NA}$. EPP's argument for static screening in 3D (before Eq. 454) is Giustino's adiabatic approximation ("setting $\omega=0$ in Eq. 121", Keating 1968). Giustino notes that coupled plasmon-phonon modes should emerge from the $\omega$-dependence of the self-energy (p. 015003-22, citing Varga 1965, Mooradian & Wright 1966) and calls them "the electronic analog of phonon polaritons". EPP contains both: real phonon polaritons from $\Omega^0$ with transverse $D^0$, and plasmon-phonon modes from Eq. 453 with dynamical $W^{\rm e}$.
- **Bare + screened vertex in $\Pi$.** Giustino Eq. 145: $\hbar\Pi^{NA}_{q\nu\nu'}=2\sum\int g^{b*}_{mn\nu}g_{mn\nu'}[\dots]$, one bare and one screened vertex, in agreement with Calandra, Profeta & Mauri (2010). He stresses that most calculations replace $g^b\to g$. EPP's $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}=\sum g^{\rm dyn}\chi^0g^{\rm dyn,0}$ (Fig. 14) has the same one-bare-one-screened structure, derived diagrammatically from photon lines. EPP also keeps the frequency dependence of the dressed vertex through $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$. Giustino's approximation (iv), $g^{cc}(\mathbf r,\omega)\approx g(\mathbf r,0)$, "ubiquitous but never mentioned explicitly", is dropped. Giustino Eq. 144, $g=\epsilon_e^{-1}g^b$, is mirrored by $D^{\rm e}=(\hat\epsilon^{\rm e})^{-1}D^0$ (Eq. 431) entering $g^{\rm dyn}$ in place of $D^0$ in $g^{\rm dyn,0}$.
- **Fröhlich vertex.** EPP's two electrostatic limits (pp. 95 and 98) are the Verdi–Giustino PRL 115, 176401 (2015) Eq. 4 = Giustino RMP Eq. 180 long-range vertex $g^L_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)=i\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_\kappa(\frac{\hbar}{2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{q\nu}})^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf G\ne-\mathbf q}\frac{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\mathbf Z_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu}}{(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)}\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|e^{i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa)}|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$, in three variants:
  - the bare one with $\epsilon_\infty=1$, $\mathbf G=0$;
  - the screened one with $\epsilon_\infty\to\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ (dynamical RPA, no LFE);
  - at finite $c$, the generalization Eqs. 415–416 (the "electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex").
  EPP also derived the same VG vertex earlier from electrostatics as Eq. 131 (p. 25), and Sec. 7.2.1 cites that equation. Note Giustino's footnote 7: VG Eq. 4 is missing the factor $e^{-i(\mathbf q+\mathbf G)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$, needed for acoustic modes near $q=0$. EPP inherits this omission because its dipoles sit at $\mathbf R_i^0$ (Eq. 314).
- **DFPT.** EPP's Eq. 454 with $W^{\rm e}=4\pi/(\mathbf q\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\mathbf q)$ is the dipole–dipole non-analytic term of the DFPT dynamical matrix (Gonze & Lee 1997, referenced by Giustino in Sec. VI.A.3 when introducing Eq. 180). Here it is derived diagrammatically and evaluated in first-order perturbation theory on the mode basis. In DFPT it is added to the dynamical matrix before diagonalization.
- **$W=W^{\rm e}+W^{\rm ph}$.** The reorganization in Fig. 12 (ionic bubbles linked by $D^{\rm e}$) is, in the longitudinal limit, the decomposition of the total screened interaction into the electronic part $W^{\rm e}$ (Giustino Eq. 94) and the phonon-mediated part $W^{\rm ph}$ (Giustino Eq. 110: $\epsilon_e^{-1}\nabla V\,D\,\epsilon_e^{-1}\nabla V$), with the screened Fröhlich vertex at both ends. EPP generalizes $W^{\rm ph}$ to include transverse photon exchange (polaritonic exchange).
- **Electron-phonon Hamiltonian.** In Giustino Eq. 1 / EPP Eq. 109, $g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)$ is a static (adiabatic) matrix element of $\partial V/\partial u$. In EPP the e-ph coupling is not an input. It is generated by exchange of one photon (Eq. 415), so $g^{\rm dyn}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q,\omega)$ is intrinsically frequency dependent and contains the light-cone physics. For $c\to\infty$ and $\omega\to0$ it reduces to the polar part of $g_{mn\nu}$.
- **Links inside EPP:**
  - Eq. 385 ← Sec. 5.2 (Eqs. 297–311, notes E7);
  - $D^0$ (Eq. 375), Dyson Eqs. 379–384 and the connection formulas Eqs. 341–350 ← Sec. 6 (notes E7/E8);
  - the sector decomposition Eqs. 355–368 / Fig. 10 underpins Eqs. 433–437;
  - $F_\sigma$ ← Sec. 4.5–4.6 (Eqs. 246–256);
  - LO/TO via $\epsilon(\omega)=0$ and LST ← Sec. 4.2 (Eqs. 150–165, p. 33–35). There the author already argued that "LO-TO splitting" is better called "LO shift", the same viewpoint as in Sec. 7.1;
  - the ad-hoc $\epsilon_\infty$ of Eq. 112ff (p. 23), "later we will remove it to avoid double counting", is fulfilled here: $\epsilon_\infty$ re-emerges as $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\mathbf q,\omega)$ from the diagrams;
  - the 2D transcription parallels the 2D ionic self-energy (Eq. 300) and the 2D electronic self-energy (Sec. 5.1).
- **External references in the range:** Verdi & Giustino PRL 115, 176401 (2015) (pp. 96, 99); Sohier, Gibertini, Calandra, Mauri, Marzari, Nano Lett. 17, 3758 (2017) (pp. 99, 102); Quantum Espresso's non-analytic correction (p. 90); Eq. 72 of "Lecture 9" (internal course notes, cited in Sec. 6).

## 8. Source issues

1. **Notation switch for bare correlators.** Eqs. 385 and 391 use $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$ for the *bare* ionic correlator, while Eqs. 386–389 and 393 use $\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj}$ (the switch is announced on p. 87). Eq. 391 should read $\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\nu}$.
2. **"Photon" for "phonon".** The text before Eq. 406 says "the photon self-energy is" and Eq. 407 "the new corrected photon energy". Both refer to the *phonon* self-energy and energy. Eq. 406 also writes $\Omega^0_\sigma$ (single index) while Eq. 407 uses $\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}$.
3. **Interpretation of Eq. 410 as "LO-TO splitting".** I am confident of the following conceptual point. Eq. 410 is the retarded $q\to0$ limit ($cq\ll\omega$). It shifts *every* polar mode by the isotropic $\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$, transverse modes included: it is the $q=0$ point of the upper polariton. The QE non-analytic correction it is said to be "completely analogous to" is $\propto|\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2/(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\hat{\mathbf q})$ and leaves TO modes unshifted. That corresponds to Eq. 454, obtained with $c\to\infty$ first. The two coincide only for a mode polarized along $\hat{\mathbf q}$ and with $\epsilon_\infty=1$. Sec. 7.1 also has no electrons, so its shift is the unscreened $\omega_p^2$, about 11× too large for GaAs.
4. **"Retarded limit $c\to\infty$" (before Eq. 418).** $c\to\infty$ is the *non-retarded* (instantaneous, electrostatic) limit. Section 7.2.1's own heading also says "electrostatic limit".
5. **Missing factor $e$ in the charge–current connection (Eqs. 420, 426, 428).** $\mathbf j_{\rm e}$ (Eq. 419) and $j^\mu$ (Eq. 384) include $e$, but $\rho_{\rm e}$ (Eq. 420) and $\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}$ (Eq. 106/426) are number densities. The continuity equation $\dot\rho=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf j$ then mixes number and charge. Free-electron check: $\phi=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}/\sqrt\Omega$ gives $j=\frac{e\hbar}{2m}(2\mathbf k+\mathbf q)$, $\rho=1$, $E_{\mathbf k}-E_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(2\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf q+q^2)$. So $-\hbar\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf j/\Delta E=e$, not 1. Corrected (confident): $e\,\rho_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}=-\sum_\mu\hbar q_\mu j^\mu_{n\mathbf k,m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}/(E_{n\mathbf k}-E_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q})$. Both electrostatic vertices (pp. 95, 98) then acquire a factor $e$. Together with $Z^*$ carrying one $e$, this exactly resolves the "missing $e^2$" relative to Verdi–Giustino. The sign of $e$ (electron charge negative) only changes an overall phase of $g$.
6. **Untranslated author's note (p. 96, blue Croatian text).** "U odnosu na Giustino-a fali ti taj $e^2$. Vjerojatno je u interakciji D ili u $Z^*$. Također njegova je oznaka tog matričnog elementa $g^0_{mn\sigma}$, a naša $g^0_{nm\sigma}$, međutim to je nebitno." Translation: "Compared with Giustino, you are missing that $e^2$. It is probably in the interaction D or in $Z^*$. Also, his notation for that matrix element is $g^0_{mn\sigma}$ and ours is $g^0_{nm\sigma}$, but that is irrelevant." This is a draft/internal note; see item 5 for the resolution.
7. **Heisenberg evolution without $\hbar$ (p. 93):** $c_{n\mathbf k}(t)=e^{iH^e_0t}c_{n\mathbf k}e^{-iH^e_0t}$ should be $e^{iH^e_0t/\hbar}\dots$ This is cosmetic, since Eq. 422 uses $i\hbar\dot\rho=[\rho,H]$ correctly.
8. **Power counting in Eq. 440.** At fixed $q$, $D^{0,t}\propto1/c$ (Eq. 372), $D^{0,l}\propto c$ (Eq. 374), and $\Pi_{jj}\propto1/c$ (Eq. 383, 385). Then $D^{lt}\sim D^{0,l}\Pi D^{0,t}\sim c\cdot c^{-1}\cdot c^{-1}=c^{-1}$, not $c^0$ as stated (same for $tl$). This is likely a slip. It does not affect Eq. 434 or Eq. 438, which only need $lt,tl,tt$ to be subleading relative to $ll\sim c$.
9. **Comparison target in 7.2.1.** The text says the bare vertex reduces to "standard bare Fröhlich vertex Eq. (131)". Eq. 131 contains $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ and a $\mathbf G$ sum, i.e. it is the *screened* VG vertex. The match is only with $\epsilon_\infty=1$, $\mathbf G=0$, which p. 96 acknowledges. The screened limit (p. 98) is the proper counterpart of Eq. 131 / VG.
10. **Missing atomic-position phase.** Because dipoles sit at $\mathbf R_i^0$ (Eq. 314), $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ and both Fröhlich limits lack $e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_a}$. This mirrors the omission in VG Eq. 4 noted in Giustino's footnote 7. It is harmless as $q\to0$ but matters for acoustic modes and finite $q$ in multi-atom cells (e.g. Eq. 454 used away from $\Gamma$).
11. **No local fields.** $\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\mathbf q$ is the head of the RPA dielectric matrix, not $1/\epsilon^{-1}_{00}$. The "almost identical to VG" statement holds up to LFE and xc. The text acknowledges "CLFs are mostly neglected here" (p. 95).
12. **Unnumbered key results.** The following carry no equation numbers:

14. **Eq. 463 and dropped out-of-plane currents.** The "divergent local term $\sim\delta(z-z')$" arises only in the $zz$ component ($\partial_z\partial_{z'}e^{-Q|z-z'|}\ni2Q\delta(z-z')$). For in-plane $\mu,\nu$, Eq. 463 holds trivially without derivatives. Restricting the sum in Eqs. 466–472 to $\alpha=x,y$ silently drops the coupling of out-of-plane dipoles (Born charges $Z^*_{zz}$, ZO-type modes). Physically that coupling is a $Q$-independent depolarization shift, not zero. State it as a limitation.
15. **"Static screening" justification (before Eq. 454)** holds for gapped systems ($E_g\gg\hbar\omega_{ph}$). It fails for doped polar semiconductors and metals (plasmon near $\omega_{ph}$), where Eq. 453 must be kept dynamical.

In truncated-band calculations the two forms differ, as with the velocity- vs length-gauge issue.
