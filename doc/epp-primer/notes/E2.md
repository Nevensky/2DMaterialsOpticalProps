## 1. Overview (plain language)

Pages 2–8 took the harmonic lattice Hamiltonian (5), with $3sN$ coupled Cartesian displacements $Q_{i\mathrm a\mu}$, and applied two transformations. Step 1 is a lattice Fourier transform (Eqs. 7–10). It decouples different wave vectors $\mathbf k$. Step 2 combines mass-weighting (Eqs. 18–19) with a unitary rotation onto the eigenvectors $\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf k)$ of the mass-weighted dynamical matrix (Eqs. 25–29, 39–40). It decouples the $3s$ atom-and-direction components at each $\mathbf k$.

Page 9 finishes step 2. The classical Hamiltonian becomes a sum over **normal coordinates** $\{q_{\mathbf k\alpha},p_{\mathbf k\alpha}\}$, each with frequency $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$ (Eqs. 44–45). Each term still couples $\mathbf k$ to $-\mathbf k$, because the normal coordinates are complex and $q_{-\mathbf k\alpha}=q^\dagger_{\mathbf k\alpha}$.

Step 3 (pp. 9–14) quantizes the problem. The author first works through the 1D quantum harmonic oscillator step by step: canonical commutator, ladder operators, $[a,a^+]=1$, spectrum $\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$, and the matrix elements $\sqrt n$ and $\sqrt{n+1}$ (Eqs. 46–59). The same machinery is then applied to the crystal:
- The fundamental commutator $[Q_{i\mathrm a\mu},P_{j\mathrm b\nu}]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}\delta_{\mathrm{ab}}\delta_{\mu\nu}$ fixes the commutators of the normal coordinates, $[q_{\mathbf k\alpha},p_{\mathbf k'\beta}]=i\hbar\delta_{\mathbf k',-\mathbf k}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ (Eqs. 61–71).
- Ladder operators $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}$, $a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ are defined by pairing $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ with $a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$ (Eqs. 72–73). This pairing removes the $\mathbf k\leftrightarrow-\mathbf k$ coupling.
- The result is $H=\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)(a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}a_{\mathbf k\alpha}+1/2)$ (Eq. 78): $3Ns$ independent quantum oscillators. Their quanta are **phonons**.

Sec. 1.1 (pp. 15–17) states the goal of the whole construction. The physical displacement of atom $\mathrm a$ in cell $i$ is written as a superposition of phonon creation and annihilation operators (Eq. 83, boxed). The conjugate momentum follows in Eq. (84), also boxed. The author proves that both are Hermitian, that they satisfy the canonical commutator, and that $P=m\dot Q$ follows from the Heisenberg equation of motion (Eqs. 85–91).

Eq. (83) is the key result of these pages. In the electron-phonon coupling of Sec. 2 it is inserted into the first-order change of the crystal potential (Eqs. 100–107). It is inserted into the polar (Born-charge) displacement (Eqs. 125–131) to obtain the Verdi–Giustino Fröhlich vertex. In the electron-phonon-photon Hamiltonian it is "identical" to the ionic displacement (p. 40). Finally, the ionic part of the RPA photon self-energy is built from these operators. The momentum–momentum correlator $\langle T\{\pi\pi\}\rangle$ from (84) gives the paramagnetic ionic self-energy (Sec. 5.2.1, Eqs. 286–287, pp. 59–60). The displacement–displacement correlator $\langle T\{QQ\}\rangle$ from (83) gives $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ (Sec. 6.0.2, Eq. 353 ff., pp. 75–76).

The content is standard textbook lattice dynamics, essentially the same as Giustino (2017), Eqs. (12)–(22) and Appendix B. The manuscript derives it in full detail and with its own notation, and that notation is used for the rest of the 102 pages.

## 2. Logical flow

1. **(p. 8 → p. 9) End of step 2.** Insert normal coordinates (39)–(40) into the mass-weighted Hamiltonian (23) to obtain (43). The kinetic term becomes diagonal by orthonormality (27) and the relation $e(-\mathbf k)=e^*(\mathbf k)$ (38). The potential term becomes diagonal by the eigenvalue property (29), using the claimed symmetry $\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm b\nu\mathrm a\mu}(\mathbf k)=\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(\mathbf k)$ (but see Source issue S1). The result is $H=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}p_{\mathbf k\alpha}p_{-\mathbf k\alpha}+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}\omega^2_\alpha(\mathbf k)q_{\mathbf k\alpha}q_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$.
2. **Eqs. (44)–(45).** $H=\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}H_{\mathbf k\alpha}$, but each $H_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ still couples $\mathbf k$ and $-\mathbf k$. The author promises this will be removed "later".
3. **Step 3 begins: quantization.** $Q$, $P$ and $H$ become operators. The plan is to find a further transformation that makes the oscillators fully independent and then to find the spectrum.
4. **1D harmonic oscillator detour (Eqs. 46–59).**
   - (46) $[q,p]=i\hbar$.
   - (47)–(48) ladder-operator substitution.
   - (49)–(50) $[a,a^+]=1$.
   - (51) $H=\hbar\omega(a^+a+1/2)$.
   - (52)–(54) $a$ and $a^+$ lower and raise the energy by $\hbar\omega$.
   - (55)–(56) $a^+a$ has integer eigenvalues, giving the number operator $N$.
   - (57) $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$.
   - (58)–(59) $a|n\rangle=\sqrt n|n-1\rangle$ and $a^+|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle$.
5. **Fundamental commutators of the lattice (Eqs. 60–61).** Each physical degree of freedom satisfies $[q_i,p_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}$. For the lattice this is $[Q_{i\mathrm a\mu},P_{j\mathrm b\nu}]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}\delta_{\mathrm{ab}}\delta_{\mu\nu}$.
6. **Express $Q$ and $P$ directly through the normal coordinates (Eqs. 62–63).** Combine (7), (18), (39) and (8), (19), (40).
7. **Find the normal-coordinate commutators (Eqs. 64–71).** Insert (62)–(63) into (61). Consistency requires $[q_{\mathbf k\alpha},p_{\mathbf k'\beta}]=i\hbar\delta_{\mathbf k',-\mathbf k}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ (65, 69), with $[q,q]=[p,p]=0$ (70–71). The verification (66)–(68) uses $\sum_{\mathbf k}e^{i\mathbf k(\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf R^0_j)}=N\delta_{ij}$, the relation (32) and completeness (28).
8. **Ladder operators for the crystal (Eqs. 72–73).** These mirror (47)–(48). There is no mass factor because $q$ and $p$ are already mass-weighted. $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ is always paired with $a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$.
9. **Bosonic commutators (Eqs. 74–75).** Inserting (72)–(73) into (69) requires $[a_{\mathbf k\alpha},a^+_{\mathbf k'\beta}]=\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ and $[a,a]=[a^+,a^+]=0$. The author notes that uniqueness needs (70)–(71) as well.
10. **Hamiltonian in ladder operators (unnumbered p. 13 → Eq. 76).** The anomalous $aa$ and $a^+a^+$ terms cancel because $\omega_\alpha(-\mathbf k)=\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$ (31). This leaves $H_{\mathbf k\alpha}=\frac{\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}4(2a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}a_{\mathbf k\alpha}+2a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}a_{-\mathbf k\alpha}+2)$. This still mixes $\mathbf k$ and $-\mathbf k$.
11. **Sum over $\mathbf k$ (Eqs. 77–78).** Relabel $\mathbf k\to-\mathbf k$ in the second term to get $H=\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)(a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}a_{\mathbf k\alpha}+1/2)$. These are $3Ns$ independent 1D oscillators.
12. **Fock states and spectrum (Eqs. 79–82).** $|n_1,\dots,n_{\mathbf k\alpha},\dots,n_{3Ns}\rangle$ has energy $\sum\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)(n_{\mathbf k\alpha}+1/2)$. A phonon is one quantum of a normal mode. $a$ and $a^+$ annihilate and create phonons.
13. **Sec. 1.1: displacement and momentum operators (Eqs. 83–84).** Insert (72) into (62) and (73) into (63).
14. **Hermiticity (Eq. 86 and the unnumbered lines after it).** Use $e^*(\mathbf k)=e(-\mathbf k)$, relabel $\mathbf k\to-\mathbf k$, and use $\omega(-\mathbf k)=\omega(\mathbf k)$.
15. **Canonical commutator check (p. 16, Eq. 87).** Use (75), relabel $\mathbf k'\to-\mathbf k'$, then completeness (87), then the lattice sum $N\delta_{ij}$. This recovers (85).
16. **$P=m_\mathrm a\dot Q$ (Eqs. 88–91).** The Heisenberg equation gives $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}(t)=a_{\mathbf k\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)t}$. Differentiating (90) reproduces (84). "We are now fully equipped to study electron-phonon coupling."
17. **(p. 17, start of Sec. 2) Setup for the electron-phonon interaction.** Frozen-core, local, transferable ionic pseudopotentials $v_\mathrm a(\mathbf r)$ give the crystal potential (92). With frozen ions (94) the unperturbed electronic Hamiltonian is $H_0=T+V^0_{\rm cr}$ (95). On p. 18 the ions are then allowed to move, $\mathbf R^0_{i\mathrm a}\to\mathbf R^0_{i\mathrm a}+\mathbf Q_{i\mathrm a}(t)$ (98), and (83) is inserted (p. 19). The next set of notes covers this.

## 3. Key equations

### Conventions (apply to the whole range)

- **Units.** Section 1 contains no electromagnetism, so it uses no Gaussian or SI constants. $\hbar$ is explicit. Masses $m_\mathrm a$ are physical ionic masses; there is no reference mass $M_0$, unlike Giustino. The electromagnetic parts of the manuscript (Sec. 4 onwards) use **Gaussian units**: e.g. $H=\frac1{8\pi}\int(E^2+B^2)$ and $2\pi c^2\,\tilde p\tilde p$ in Eqs. (204), (212), (213) on pp. 42–43. One later formula, Eq. (131) on p. 24, shows a $4\pi\varepsilon_0$, so the units are mixed in places (outside this range).
- **Dimensions.**
  - $Q_{i\mathrm a\mu}$: length. $P_{i\mathrm a\mu}$: momentum.
  - Mass-weighted $\tilde Q=\sqrt{m}\,Q$ and the normal coordinates $q_{\mathbf k\alpha}$: $\sqrt{\text{mass}}\times$length.
  - $p_{\mathbf k\alpha}=\dot q_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ (42): $\sqrt{\text{mass}}\times$length/time.
  - $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$: angular frequency (rad/s).
  - $k_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(\boldsymbol\delta)$ and $\Omega^2$ (Eq. 15): energy/length². $\tilde\Omega^2$ (Eq. 24): 1/time².
- **Lattice and normalization.**
  - $i=1,\dots,N$ labels Bravais cells, with cell vector $\mathbf R^0_i$. $\mathrm a=1,\dots,s$ labels atoms in the cell, $\mu,\nu\in\{x,y,z\}$ label Cartesian directions, and $\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,3s$ label branches (polarizations).
  - Equilibrium atomic position: $\mathbf R^0_{i\mathrm a}=\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r^0_\mathrm a$.
  - $\mathbf k\in$ BZ takes $N$ values (Born–von Kármán periodicity is implicit).
  - Lattice sums: $\sum_{\mathbf k\in BZ}e^{i\mathbf k(\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf R^0_j)}=N\delta_{ij}$ and $\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf k+\mathbf k')\mathbf R^0_i}=N\delta_{\mathbf k',-\mathbf k}$. Umklapp ($\mathbf k+\mathbf k'=\mathbf G$) is ignored "because $\mathbf k\in BZ$".
  - No cell volume $\Omega$ or crystal volume $V$ appears in this range.
- **Fourier convention.** Unitary, with $1/\sqrt N$ in both directions: $Q_{i\mathrm a\mu}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathbf k}e^{+i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i}Q_{\mathbf k\mathrm a\mu}$ (7) and $Q_{\mathbf k\mathrm a\mu}=N^{-1/2}\sum_ie^{-i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i}Q_{i\mathrm a\mu}$ (9). The phase contains only the **cell vector** $\mathbf R^0_i$, not the atom position $\mathbf R^0_i+\mathbf r^0_\mathrm a$. Intra-cell phases therefore live in the polarization vectors. This is the same convention as Giustino's Eq. (20), $e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}$.
- **Time convention.** Heisenberg picture with $a(t)=a\,e^{-i\omega t}$ and $a^+(t)=a^+e^{+i\omega t}$ (89).
- **Notation.** Hermitian conjugate is written with a superscript $+$ ($a^+$, $Q^+$), not $\dagger$. Atom indices are roman a, b; ladder operators are italic $a$. In Sec. 1 the phonon wave vector is $\mathbf k$. From Sec. 2 onwards it is renamed $\mathbf q$ (e.g. $a_{\mathbf q\alpha}$ in Eq. 219) and $\mathbf k$ is reused for electrons.

### pp. 9–11: quantization of the 1D harmonic oscillator (classical start)

$$H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2q^2,\qquad p=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot q}=m\dot q\qquad\text{(unnumbered, p. 9)}$$

$$[q,p]=i\hbar\qquad(46)$$

$$q=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(a+a^+\right)\qquad(47)$$

$$p=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega m}{2}}\left(a^+-a\right)\qquad(48)$$

- This is a canonical transformation from $(q,p)$ to $(a,a^+)$, with $a^+$ the Hermitian conjugate of $a$. The prefactor $\sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}$ is the **zero-point length**: the rms of $q$ in the ground state, since $\langle0|q^2|0\rangle=\hbar/2m\omega$.

$$\frac12\left[a+a^+,\,a^+-a\right]=1\qquad(49)$$

$$\left[a,a^+\right]=1\qquad(50)$$

- (49) follows from (46)–(48): $[q,p]=\frac{i\hbar}2[a+a^+,a^+-a]$. Expanding gives $[a+a^+,a^+-a]=2[a,a^+]$, hence (50).

Unnumbered (p. 10): $H=\frac{\hbar\omega}2\left(a^+a+aa^+\right)$, then, "after using (46)" (it should be (50); see S2):
$$H=\hbar\omega\left(a^+a+1/2\right)\qquad(51)$$

$$H|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle\qquad(52)$$

Unnumbered: $Ha|n\rangle=(E_n-\hbar\omega)a|n\rangle$ and $Ha^+|n\rangle=(E_n+\hbar\omega)a^+|n\rangle$. These follow from $[H,a]=-\hbar\omega a$ and $[H,a^+]=+\hbar\omega a^+$.

$$a|n\rangle=C_-|n-1\rangle\qquad(53)$$
$$a^+|n\rangle=C_+|n+1\rangle\qquad(54)$$
$$a^+a|n\rangle=a_n|n\rangle\qquad(55)$$

Unnumbered (p. 11): $a^+a\,a^m|n\rangle=(a_n-m)a^m|n\rangle$, i.e. $a^+a|n-m\rangle=(a_n-m)|n-m\rangle$. Hence $a_n=n\in\mathbb N$ (see S3 for the missing positivity step).

$$N|n\rangle=a^+a|n\rangle=n|n\rangle\qquad(56)$$

$$H|n\rangle=\hbar\omega\left(a^+a+1/2\right)|n\rangle=\hbar\omega\left(n+1/2\right)|n\rangle\qquad(57)$$

$$E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2),\qquad n=0,1,2,3,\dots\qquad\text{(unnumbered)}$$

### p. 13: ladder operators for the normal modes

$$q_{\mathbf k\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}}\left(a_{\mathbf k\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}\right)\qquad(72)$$

$$p_{\mathbf k\alpha}=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}2}\left(a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}-a_{\mathbf k\alpha}\right)\qquad(73)$$

- These are (47)–(48) with $m\to1$, because the coordinates are mass-weighted, and with $a^+\to a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$. Pairing $a_{\mathbf k}$ with $a^+_{-\mathbf k}$ is what makes $q^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}=q_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$ hold, given $\omega(-\mathbf k)=\omega(\mathbf k)$. Physically, a displacement wave with wave vector $\mathbf k$ is produced either by destroying a phonon of momentum $\hbar\mathbf k$ or by creating one of momentum $-\hbar\mathbf k$.
- Inverse (not written in the text, but useful for builders and implied by the bracketed remark): $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}=\dfrac{\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)q_{\mathbf k\alpha}+ip_{\mathbf k\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}}$ and $a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}=\dfrac{\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)q_{\mathbf k\alpha}-ip_{\mathbf k\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}}$.

$$[q_{-\mathbf k\alpha},p_{\mathbf k'\beta}]=i\frac\hbar2\sqrt{\frac{\omega_\beta(\mathbf k')}{\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}}\Big\{[a_{-\mathbf k\alpha},a^+_{-\mathbf k'\beta}]-[a_{-\mathbf k\alpha},a_{\mathbf k'\beta}]+[a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha},a^+_{-\mathbf k'\beta}]-[a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha},a_{\mathbf k'\beta}]\Big\}=i\hbar\delta_{\mathbf k',\mathbf k}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\qquad(74)$$

(This is (69) after the substitution $-\mathbf k\to\mathbf k$; it uses $\omega_\alpha(-\mathbf k)=\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$.)

$$\left[a_{\mathbf k\alpha},a^+_{\mathbf k'\beta}\right]=\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}\delta_{\alpha\beta},\qquad[a_{\mathbf k\alpha},a_{\mathbf k'\beta}]=0,\qquad\left[a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha},a^+_{\mathbf k'\beta}\right]=0\qquad(75)$$

- These are bosonic commutators: phonons obey Bose–Einstein statistics. Author's bracketed note: "[The completeness of this commutation relations is probably only guaranteed after use (70) and (71).]" This is correct. (69) alone fixes only one combination; with (70)–(71) and the inverse formulas above one gets (75) uniquely.
- Check given in the text: $i\frac\hbar2\sqrt{\omega_\beta(\mathbf k')/\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}\{[a_{-\mathbf k\alpha},a^+_{-\mathbf k'\beta}]+[a_{\mathbf k'\beta},a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}]\}=i\frac\hbar2\sqrt{\cdot}\,2\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}\delta_{\alpha\beta}=i\hbar\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$.

## 4. Concepts and physical intuition

**(a) Three steps that decouple everything.**
- Step 1, the Fourier transform: lattice translation symmetry means different $\mathbf k$ do not mix, so $3sN$ coupled coordinates become $N$ blocks of size $3s$.
- Step 2, mass-weighting plus diagonalization of the Hermitian $3s\times3s$ dynamical matrix: the $3s$ components inside each block decouple.
- Step 3, ladder operators: the residual $\mathbf k\leftrightarrow-\mathbf k$ pairing goes away and each mode becomes a quantum oscillator.

The general lesson is that **a free (quadratic) field is diagonalized by a symmetry-adapted Fourier transform plus an eigenvector rotation plus ladder operators**. The manuscript repeats exactly this recipe for the transverse photon field in Sec. 4: Eqs. (202)–(213) on pp. 42–43, where it says explicitly "see phonon's **3rd step**". The photon analogue of (72) is $\tilde A_{\alpha\mathbf q}=\sqrt{2\pi c\hbar/q}\{a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha}\}$ (Eq. 208, Gaussian units). The result is $\sum\hbar qc(a^+a+1/2)$ plus a longitudinal piece (Eq. 213). For students this parallel is the main pedagogical value of these pages: phonons and photons are quantized by the same algebra.

**(b) Why mass-weighting.** The classical equation of motion $m_\mathrm a\ddot Q=-kQ$ is a *generalized* eigenproblem $K\mathbf u=\omega^2M\mathbf u$. Rescaling $\tilde Q=\sqrt m\,Q$ turns it into the *standard* Hermitian eigenproblem $\tilde\Omega^2\mathbf e=\omega^2\mathbf e$. The kinetic energy becomes the identity, which "remains immune to the unitary transformation" (p. 5). That is why (72)–(73) contain no mass, while (83)–(84) contain $1/\sqrt{m_\mathrm a}$ and $\sqrt{m_\mathrm a}$.

**(c) Complex normal coordinates and the $\mathbf k,-\mathbf k$ pairing.** $q_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ is complex with $q^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}=q_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$, so the conjugate momentum of $q_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ is $p_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$ (Eq. 65).
- The operator $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ destroys a travelling-wave phonon of crystal momentum $\hbar\mathbf k$.
- $q_{\mathbf k\alpha}\propto a_{\mathbf k\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$ lowers the crystal momentum by $\hbar\mathbf k$ in both ways: absorb $+\mathbf k$ or emit $-\mathbf k$.
- This is why every electron-phonon vertex contains the combination $(a_{\mathbf q\nu}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\nu})$, in Giustino's Eq. (1) and in the manuscript's Sec. 2.

Giustino's Appendix B does the same thing in a more pedestrian way. He builds real standing-wave coordinates for the inversion-related pairs and takes circular combinations $(\hat a_x+i\hat a_y)/\sqrt2$. The manuscript's (72)–(73) is the compact complex version.

**(d) Ladder-operator logic.** $[H,a]=-\hbar\omega a$ means that $a$ lowers the energy by one quantum. $\langle a^+a\rangle=\|a|n\rangle\|^2\ge0$ forces the ladder to stop at $|0\rangle$ (S3), so the spectrum is evenly spaced, $\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$. The ground-state energy $\hbar\omega/2$ is nonzero: the uncertainty principle forbids $q=p=0$. Summed over modes this gives the zero-point energy $\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)/2$. It is a constant for the dynamics, but the associated zero-point *motion* ($\langle Q^2\rangle\neq0$ at $T=0$) causes zero-point renormalization of band gaps (Giustino Sec. IX).

**(e) The displacement operator as a field.** Eq. (83) is the lattice analogue of a quantized field operator: a sum over modes of (amplitude) × (polarization) × ($a+a^+$) × (plane wave). The amplitude per mode is the zero-point length $l=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m_\mathrm a\omega)}$, and the $1/\sqrt N$ spreads one phonon over $N$ cells. Useful numbers (computed here from $l^2[\text{Å}^2]=2.0901/(m[\text{amu}]\,\hbar\omega[\text{meV}])$):
- Si, $m=28.09$ amu, $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO/TO}\approx64$ meV: $l\approx0.034$ Å.
- C in graphene, $\hbar\omega\approx196$ meV ($G$/$K$ optical): $l\approx0.030$ Å.
- S in MoS$_2$, $A_{1g}$ at about 50.6 meV (408 cm$^{-1}$): $l\approx0.036$ Å.
- B and N in hBN, about 170 meV: $l\approx0.030$–$0.034$ Å.
- H, about 400 meV: $l\approx0.07$ Å.

These are about 1–2% of a bond length ($\sim1.4$–$2.5$ Å), which justifies both the harmonic expansion and the linear coupling of Sec. 2 ($|\mathbf Q_{i\mathrm a}|\ll|\mathbf r^0_\mathrm a|$, Eq. 99).

**(f) Thermal occupations (not in the text, but they follow from (80)–(82)).** In equilibrium $\langle a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}a_{\mathbf k'\beta}\rangle=n_B(\omega)\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ with $n_B=1/(e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}-1)$. Then from (83):
$$\langle Q^2_{i\mathrm a\mu}\rangle=\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf k\alpha}\frac{\hbar}{2m_\mathrm a\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)}|e_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)|^2\,[2n_B(\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k))+1].$$
- At 300 K ($k_BT=25.85$ meV): $n_B=0.092$ for 64 meV (Si optical), 0.16 for 50.6 meV, $5\times10^{-4}$ for 196 meV (graphene), and 2.1 for 10 meV (acoustic).
- Optical phonons in Si, graphene and hBN are therefore essentially in their quantum ground state at room temperature. Their motion is dominated by zero-point fluctuations.

**(g) Counting.** There are $3s$ branches, 3 acoustic (with $\omega\to0$ as $\mathbf k\to0$) and $3s-3$ optical, and $3sN$ oscillators in total.
- Monolayer MoS$_2$ ($s=3$): 9 branches.
- hBN or graphene ($s=2$): 6 branches.

**(h) Hermiticity of $Q$ and P = mQ̇.** $Q$ must be an observable, and Hermiticity relies on $e(-\mathbf k)=e^*(\mathbf k)$ and $\omega(-\mathbf k)=\omega(\mathbf k)$. Both follow from the reality of the force constants (time-reversal symmetry) together with a phase (gauge) choice for the eigenvectors (S7). $P=m\dot Q$ holds here because $H$ (78) has no velocity-dependent terms. Once the ions couple to the vector potential (p. 39, Eq. 186, through $Z^*$ and $\mathbf A(\mathbf R^0_i)$), the operator in (84), written $\pi_{i\mathrm a\mu}$ on p. 40, is the **canonical** momentum. The kinetic momentum is then $m\dot Q=\pi-\frac1cZ^*\mathbf A$ (forward-looking remark).

**(i) Limiting cases.**
- One atom per cell ($s=1$, Bravais lattice): only acoustic branches. $e_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)$ is real (it can be chosen real), and (83) reduces to the textbook monatomic form.
- $\mathbf k\to0$ acoustic: $\omega\to0$ and $l\to\infty$. The three uniform translations at $\mathbf k=0$ are not oscillators and must be excluded (S6).
- Einstein model (dispersionless $\omega_0$): $\langle Q^2\rangle=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_0}\coth\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{2k_BT}$. This goes to $k_BT/(m\omega_0^2)$ at high $T$, the classical equipartition result.

**(j) Frequency conversions (for builders).** $\hbar\omega[\text{meV}]=64.654\sqrt{\omega^2[\text{eV Å}^{-2}\text{amu}^{-1}]}$; 1 meV $=8.0655$ cm$^{-1}=0.24180$ THz.

  - For the chain, use the bond-length fluctuation to avoid the 1D infrared divergence of the absolute displacement: $\langle(Q_{i2}-Q_{i1})^2\rangle=\frac1N\sum_{k\alpha}\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_\alpha(k)}\left|\frac{e_{2\alpha}(k)}{\sqrt{m_2}}-\frac{e_{1\alpha}(k)}{\sqrt{m_1}}\right|^2\coth\frac{\hbar\omega_\alpha(k)}{2k_BT}$. Sum over 200–2000 $k$ points and skip $k=0$ for the acoustic branch. The integrand vanishes there anyway, since the bracket goes as $k$ and $\omega$ goes as $k$.
  - In SI, $\hbar/(2m\omega)$ in Å² is $2.0901/(m[\text{amu}]\hbar\omega[\text{meV}])$.
- **Controls.** $m$, $\hbar\omega_0$ (or $C$, $m_1$, $m_2$), $T$.
- **Presets.** Si (28.09 amu, 64 meV); graphene C (12.01, 196 meV); MoS$_2$ S (32.06, 50.6 meV); H (1.008, 400 meV).
- **What to notice.** Light, stiff atoms stay at their zero-point level up to high $T$. This is the origin of large zero-point renormalizations in diamond, graphene and hBN, as in Giustino Sec. IX, which requires the full $\langle Q^2\rangle$ from (83).

### V5 (optional). Cancellation of anomalous terms: why the prefactors in (72)–(73) are forced
- **Plot.** Coefficient of the anomalous terms $a_{\mathbf k}a_{-\mathbf k}$ and $a^+_{-\mathbf k}a^+_{\mathbf k}$ in $H_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ for a trial transformation $q=\sqrt{\hbar/2\lambda}(a+a^+_{-})$, $p=i\sqrt{\hbar\lambda/2}(a^+_{-}-a)$, with a slider for $\lambda/\omega$.
- **Formula** (from the unnumbered H on p. 13 with $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)\to\lambda$ in the prefactors): the anomalous coefficient is $\frac\hbar4\left(\frac{\omega^2}{\lambda}-\lambda\right)$ and the normal coefficient is $\frac\hbar4\left(\frac{\omega^2}{\lambda}+\lambda\right)$. These are the Bogoliubov-like terms.
- **What to notice.** Only $\lambda=\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$ removes the pair terms and gives $\hbar\omega(a^+a+1/2)$. The ladder operators are tied to the frequencies. This is the same logic that makes "bare" phonons not diagonal once self-energies are added (EPP Sec. 7, Giustino's $\Pi$).

**Reproduces Giustino (2017) Sec. III.A and Appendix B almost line by line.** Notation dictionary (EPP → Giustino):

- EPP Eq. (83) is **identical** to Giustino Eq. (20), $\Delta\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}=(M_0/N_pM_\kappa)^{1/2}\sum_{\mathbf q\nu}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_p}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)l_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})$, once the reference mass $M_0$ cancels. Giustino introduces $M_0$, typically the proton mass, only so that $l$ has the dimension of length; EPP avoids it.
- EPP Eq. (78) is **identical** to Giustino Eq. (22), $\hat H_{\rm p}=\sum\hbar\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+1/2)$, i.e. the phonon line of Giustino's Eq. (1).
- EPP (31)–(32) correspond to Giustino Eq. (18): $\omega_{-\mathbf q\nu}=\omega_{\mathbf q\nu}$ and $e(-\mathbf q)=e^*(\mathbf q)$, the Maradudin–Vosko phase convention. EPP (27)–(28), (87) correspond to Giustino (16)–(17).
- EPP (75) matches the commutators below Giustino's (B14). Giustino builds the ladder operators carefully: the $\mathbf q$ grid is split into self-inverse points and pairs, real standing-wave coordinates are used first, and then circular combinations are taken. EPP's (72)–(73) take the compact complex route, which is equivalent and leaves the special points implicit.
- Giustino's Eq. (1) linear vertex $N_p^{-1/2}g_{mn\nu}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)\hat c^\dagger_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}\hat c_{n\mathbf k}(\hat a_{\mathbf q\nu}+\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q\nu})$ inherits its operator structure, its $N^{-1/2}$ and its $\sqrt{\hbar/2M\omega}$ factor from (83). The manuscript makes this explicit on p. 19 by inserting (83) into $\Delta V_{\rm cr}$. Giustino's matrix element is $g=\langle\psi_{m\mathbf k+\mathbf q}|\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}V|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$ with $\Delta_{\mathbf q\nu}v^{\rm KS}=l_{\mathbf q\nu}\sum_{\kappa\alpha}(M_0/M_\kappa)^{1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\mathbf q)\partial_{\kappa\alpha,\mathbf q}v^{\rm KS}$ (his Eq. 34). The manuscript's Sec. 2 version uses the bare local ionic pseudopotential $v_\mathrm a$ (rigid-ion type) in (92)–(100), not the self-consistent DFPT $\Delta v^{\rm KS}$. Screening enters later through the dielectric and photon self-energy machinery.
- Giustino's Eq. (37) (the second-quantized perturbation) and the DW term (lines 3–4 of his Eq. 1) would need $Q^2$, i.e. (83) squared. The manuscript's Sec. 2 is linear in $Q$ (Eq. 100).

**Departures and generalizations relevant to EPP.**
- *What is $\omega_\alpha(\mathbf k)$?*
  - In Sec. 1 it comes from a generic harmonic potential $V$ (interatomic potentials, or "standard outputs of most ab initio codes", p. 5).
  - On p. 2 the manuscript notes that in polar crystals the long-range part of $U$ is "treated separately".
  - Later (p. 40) it warns that DFT phonon frequencies already include the short-range, adiabatically screened ion–electron–ion interaction.
  - In Sec. 7.1 (p. 90) the **LO–TO splitting is obtained as the lowest-order self-energy correction from coupling to the electromagnetic field**, "completely analogous to the non-analytic dynamical matrix correction done by Quantum Espresso".

  So the phonons quantized here play the role of the **bare, analytic (short-range) phonons** $D^0$. In EPP they are dressed by an **electrodynamic phonon self-energy**: transverse photons give phonon-polaritons, and the longitudinal field gives the LO shift. This generalizes Giustino's phonon self-energy $\Pi$ and his discussion of adiabatic vs nonadiabatic phonons (Sec. IV.C–D, Eqs. 116–121), where the electrons screen the bare ion–ion interaction instantaneously (adiabatic) or with retardation $W^e(\omega)$ (nonadiabatic). Giustino warns about double counting when DFPT phonons are used as $D^0$; EPP's p. 40 remark is the same caveat.
- *Free phonon propagator.* (89)–(90) give the free propagator $\langle T\{(a_{\mathbf k}+a^+_{-\mathbf k})(t)(a^+_{\mathbf k}+a_{-\mathbf k})(0)\}\rangle\to2\omega_\alpha/(\omega^2-\omega^2_\alpha+i\eta)$. This is the structure of Giustino's $D$ and of EPP's $F_\sigma(q,\omega)$ (Eq. 405, p. 90). The ionic part of the RPA photon self-energy is built from the same operators in two places. The paramagnetic $\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}$ (Sec. 5.2.1, Eqs. 286–287, pp. 59–60) uses $\langle T\{\pi_{\mathrm a\mu}\pi_{\mathrm b\nu}\}\rangle$ with the ionic momenta from (84), weighted by $Z^*Z^*/m_\mathrm am_\mathrm b$. $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ (Sec. 6.0.2, Eq. 353 ff., pp. 75–76) uses $\langle T\{Q_{i\mathrm a\alpha}(t)Q_{j\mathrm b\beta}(t')\}\rangle$ by "insert[ing] the displacement operators (83)". In Giustino's language this is $W^{\rm ph}$: the phonon-mediated screened interaction $W=W^e+W^{\rm ph}$. The ionic photon self-energy is the electrodynamic analogue of the lattice contribution to the dielectric response.
- *Polar (Fröhlich) coupling.* On p. 24, (83) with $e\to e^L$ (Born effective charges, Eqs. 125–127) gives the polar e–ph vertex (130)–(131). The text states that (131) is "exactly equal to Eq.4 published in F. Giustino, Phys. Rev. Lett 115, 176401 (2015)" (Verdi & Giustino, Fröhlich vertex). The dynamical (electrodynamic) Fröhlich vertex later in EPP, $\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}$ on p. 95 and the version with $\mathbf q\cdot\hat\epsilon(\mathbf q,\omega)\cdot\mathbf q$ on p. 98, has the same factor $\sqrt{\hbar/(2Nm_\mathrm a\omega_\sigma(\mathbf q))}\,Z^*_{\mathrm a\mu\nu}e_{\mathrm a\nu\sigma}(\mathbf q)$ inherited from (83). It is compared with Verdi & Giustino again on pp. 96 and 99. The ionic current correlator $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}(\mathbf q,\omega)\propto\sum_\sigma j^*_{\mu\sigma}j_{\nu\sigma}/(\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\mathbf q)+i\eta)$ (Eq. 352, p. 75) shows the phonon poles that come from (89)–(90).
- *Photons quantized the same way.* Eqs. (202)–(213) (pp. 42–43) repeat step 2 and step 3 for the vector potential: polarizations $\mathbf e_{1,2,3}(\mathbf q)$ (Fig. 6), $\tilde A\propto(a_{\mathbf q\alpha}+a^+_{-\mathbf q\alpha})$ (208), and bosonic commutators (210), giving $\sum\hbar qc(a^+a+1/2)$ (213). The free EPP Hamiltonian (219, p. 45) is $\sum E_{n\mathbf k}c^+c+\sum\hbar\omega_\alpha(\mathbf q)(a^+a+1/2)+\sum_{\sigma=1,2}\hbar qc(b^+b+1/2)+H'_{\rm Lph}$, with (78) as its middle term. Giustino has no photon field at all. All electromagnetism is hidden in his screened parameters $\varepsilon$, $\omega$ and $g$, and in the electrostatic $W$.
- *Canonical vs kinetic momentum.* With the field present (Eq. 186, p. 39), the ions couple via $-\frac1c\sum\frac1{m_\mathrm a}Z^*_{\mathrm a\alpha\mu}\pi_{i\mathrm a\mu}A_\alpha(\mathbf R^0_i)+\frac1{c^2}\sum\frac1{2m_\mathrm a}Z^*Z^*AA$. (84) is reinterpreted as the canonical momentum $\pi$, and (83)–(84) "remain identical" (p. 40). Giustino has no such term.

**Citations inside the range.** None. The range is self-contained textbook material with no references. Giustino is cited later, on p. 24 (PRL 115, 176401), and on pp. 96 and 99 (Verdi & Giustino).

## 8. Source issues

**S1. (p. 9, with the last line of p. 8) "Symmetry of the dynamical matrix" is incorrect in general.** The text uses $\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm b\nu\mathrm a\mu}(\mathbf k)=\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(\mathbf k)$.
- From (16) and translational invariance, $k_{\mathrm b\nu\mathrm a\mu}(\boldsymbol\delta)=k_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(-\boldsymbol\delta)$. With (35)–(36) this gives only **Hermiticity**: $\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm b\nu\mathrm a\mu}(\mathbf k)=\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(-\mathbf k)=[\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(\mathbf k)]^*$. The matrix is symmetric only where it is real.
- The 1D diatomic chain is a counterexample: $\tilde\Omega^2_{12}=-C(1+e^{ika})/\sqrt{m_1m_2}\neq\tilde\Omega^2_{21}=-C(1+e^{-ika})/\sqrt{m_1m_2}$.
- **Consequence.** In (43) the potential term is $\sum e_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)\tilde\Omega^2_{\mathrm a\mu\mathrm b\nu}(\mathbf k)e^*_{\mathrm b\nu\beta}(\mathbf k)=\mathbf e^\dagger_\beta(\mathbf k)\,\tilde\Omega^{2}(-\mathbf k)\,\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf k)$. This is diagonal only if $\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf k)$ are eigenvectors of $\tilde\Omega^2(-\mathbf k)=[\tilde\Omega^2(\mathbf k)]^*$, not of $\tilde\Omega^2(\mathbf k)$ as in (26)/(33). Equivalently, the displacement pattern $e^{+i\mathbf k\mathbf R^0_i}\mathbf e(\mathbf k)$ in (62)/(83) solves the classical equation of motion only if $\mathbf e(\mathbf k)$ diagonalizes $\tilde\Omega^2(-\mathbf k)$.
- **Fix (high confidence).** Either (i) define the dynamical matrix with $e^{-i\mathbf k\boldsymbol\delta}$ in (15), which is the conventional $D(\mathbf k)$ = Giustino's Eq. (14); or (ii) keep (15) and (26) but replace $e_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)\to e^*_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)$ in (39)–(40) and everywhere downstream.
- The final forms (78), (83), (84) are unchanged when $\mathbf e_\alpha(\mathbf k)$ is understood as an eigenvector of the conventional $D(\mathbf k)$. This is what ab initio codes such as Quantum ESPRESSO return, and it matches Giustino's Eq. (20). Visualization builders should use the conventional $D(k)$.

**S2. (p. 10) Wrong equation reference.** "or after using (46)" should read "after using (50)". The step from $\frac{\hbar\omega}2(a^+a+aa^+)$ to (51) uses $[a,a^+]=1$.

**S3. (pp. 10–11) Missing positivity argument.** From $a^+a|n-m\rangle=(a_n-m)|n-m\rangle$ the text concludes "$a_n$ is a natural number" without justification. The missing step: $a_n=\langle n|a^+a|n\rangle=\|a|n\rangle\|^2\ge0$, so the descending ladder must terminate at a state $|0\rangle$ with $a|0\rangle=0$, which forces $a_n\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$. The sentence "Since energy can only change in quantum jumps, we conclude that $\hbar\omega$ is that quantum jump" is heuristic. The rigorous statement is $[H,a]=-\hbar\omega a$.

**S4. (p. 12, Eq. 67) Order of operations.** The lattice sum $\sum_{\mathbf k}e^{i\mathbf k(\mathbf R^0_i-\mathbf R^0_j)}=N\delta_{ij}$ is "evaluated" while the summand still contains the $\mathbf k$-dependent $\sum_\alpha e_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)e^*_{\mathrm b\nu\alpha}(\mathbf k)$. Eq. (67) is left with a dangling $\mathbf k$. The correct order is the one used on p. 16: first apply completeness (28)/(87) to remove the $\mathbf k$ dependence, then do the lattice sum. The final result (68) is correct.

**S5. (p. 14) Terminology.** (31), $\omega^2_\alpha(-\mathbf k)=\omega^2_\alpha(\mathbf k)$, is called the "isotropy property". It follows from the reality of the force constants (time-reversal symmetry), not from isotropy.

**S6. (pp. 13–15) Zero-frequency modes not addressed.** For the three acoustic modes at $\mathbf k=0$, $\omega_\alpha=0$. Then (72) and (83) contain $1/\sqrt{\omega}\to\infty$ and (73) vanishes. These uniform translations are free-particle (centre-of-mass) degrees of freedom, not oscillators, and must be excluded from the sums, as Giustino does explicitly. Correspondingly, "$3\times N\times s$ independent 1D harmonic oscillators" (p. 14) should strictly be $3Ns-3$.

**S7. (used on pp. 13–16; stated on p. 7, Eqs. 32/38) Gauge condition presented as automatic.** $e_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(-\mathbf k)=e^*_{\mathrm a\mu\alpha}(\mathbf k)$ is a **phase (and degenerate-subspace) convention** that must be imposed on numerically obtained eigenvectors. Eq. (37) only shows that $\mathbf e^*(\mathbf k)$ is *an* eigenvector of $\tilde\Omega^2(-\mathbf k)$. At points with $-\mathbf k\equiv\mathbf k$ (mod $\mathbf G$) the convention requires real eigenvectors. It is needed for the Hermiticity proof (86), for the cancellation in (76), and for $q^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}=q_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$.

**S8. Implicit assumption.** $q^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}=q_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$ and $p^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}=p_{-\mathbf k\alpha}$ are never stated, but they are needed for $a^+_{\mathbf k\alpha}$ defined by (72)–(73) to be the Hermitian conjugate of $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}$. They follow from the reality of $Q$ and $P$, the unitarity (27)–(28) and (32).

**S9. (p. 13) Draft remark left in the text.** "[The completeness of this commutation relations is probably only guaranteed after use (70) and (71).]" The remark is correct and can be stated firmly. Inverting (72)–(73) gives $a_{\mathbf k\alpha}=(\omega_\alpha q_{\mathbf k\alpha}+ip_{\mathbf k\alpha})/\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_\alpha}$, and (69)–(71) together then imply (75) uniquely.

**S10. Umklapp.** "$\sum_ie^{i(\mathbf k+\mathbf k')\mathbf R^0_i}=N\delta_{\mathbf k',-\mathbf k}$ (here the sum over reciprocal vectors does not yet appear because $\mathbf k\in BZ$)" (p. 4, used throughout). Strictly, the condition is $\mathbf k+\mathbf k'=\mathbf G$. This is harmless if $-\mathbf k$ is always mapped back into the BZ.
