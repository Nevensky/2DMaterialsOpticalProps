B.6EPP §6 · Eqs. 312–385

Charge conservation, the total propagator and polaritons

Coulomb lines and photon lines, charge vertices and current vertices: EPP §6 shows that charge conservation collapses all of them into one $3\times3$ Dyson equation, $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D$. By the end you can derive it, read $D^0$ as the vacuum Maxwell propagator, and find plasmons, LO phonons and polaritons as its poles.

§B.5 dressed the transverse photon (E Eq. 277) and kept the Coulomb interaction (E Eq. 235′) as a separate, instantaneous scalar line $v$. EPP §6 puts both into one perturbation series. Units are Gaussian, $v(q)=4\pi/q^2$, with the charges in the vertices; the Born charges $Z^*$ carry the dimension of charge. Giustino's nuclear density correlator $D$ (G Eq. 103) is written $\mathcal D$, so that $D$ always means EPP's photon propagator.

Physical picture: one current, two ways to look at it

A crystal has one response to the field: an induced current. Charge piles up only where that current converges, $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\bj=0$. So the density response of GW, BSE and DFPT is the longitudinal part of the current response, and the instantaneous Coulomb line is the longitudinal part of the photon line. With both statements, every line is a $3\times3$ tensor, every bubble a current–current correlator, and the series sums into one Dyson equation. In an isotropic medium it splits into a longitudinal channel, whose poles are plasmons and LO phonons, and a transverse channel, whose poles are polaritons.

One interaction, two kinds of lines

Adding the longitudinal term of E Eq. 235′ to the transverse couplings of §B.5 gives the full interaction (EPP p. 68, with the factor $e$ of §Z.3):

$$\begin{aligned}V'={}&-\frac1c\int d\br\,\bj^{\rm e}(\br)\cdot\bA(\br)+\frac{e^2}{2m_{\rm e}c^2}\int d\br\,\rho^{\rm e}(\br)\,\bA^2(\br)\\&-\frac1c\int d\br\,\bj^{\rm ion}(\br)\cdot\bA(\br)+\frac{e^2}{2m_0c^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d\br\,A_\alpha(\br)\,\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\br)\,A_\beta(\br)\\&+\frac12\int d\br\int d\br'\,\frac{[e\rho^{\rm e}(\br)+\rho^{\rm ion}(\br)][e\rho^{\rm e}(\br')+\rho^{\rm ion}(\br')]}{|\br-\br'|}\end{aligned}\tag{E.312$'$}$$

Here $\rho^{\rm e}=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ is the electron number density, which the $\bA^2$ term needs, and $e\rho^{\rm e}$ the electronic charge density, which the Coulomb term needs. The ionic bound charge $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\bP$ of the point dipoles $P_\mu(\br)=\sum_{ia\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\,\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia})$ (E Eqs. 313 and 314′), each at its atom, is linear in the phonon operators. The c-number tensor $\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}\propto\sum_aZ^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}/m_a$ (E Eq. 315′) belongs to the diamagnetic term. The electronic charge vertex is therefore $e\rho^{\rm e}$, and $\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}$, $\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho j}$, $\Pi^{\rm e}_{j\rho}$ in E Eqs. 320–350 below are correlators of $e\rho^{\rm e}$, as the continuity equation E Eq. 335 requires. Mixed electron–ion bubbles vanish at lowest order, so $D=D^0+D^{1,\rm ion}+D^{1,\rm e}+\dots$ (E Eq. 316), each term a $4\times4$ matrix with one scalar (Coulomb) slot and a $3\times3$ vector block:

$$D^0=\begin{bmatrix}v&0\\0&\hat D^{0t}\end{bmatrix}\tag{E.317}$$
$$D^{1,i}=\begin{bmatrix}v\,\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}\,v&v\,\Pi^i_{\rho j}\,\hat D^{0t}\\ \hat D^{0t}\,\Pi^i_{j\rho}\,v&\hat D^{0t}\,\Pi^i_{jj}\,\hat D^{0t}\end{bmatrix},\qquad i={\rm e,\,ion}\tag{E.319}$$

$\hat D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{A_\mu A_\nu\}\rangle$ (E Eq. 318) is the transverse photon. The longitudinal pairing of the quantized field vanishes (E Eq. 263), so $v$ alone carries the longitudinal interaction. $\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle T\{\rho^i\rho^i\}\rangle$ (E Eq. 320) is Hedin's irreducible polarizability $P$, here the bare RPA bubble, and $\Pi^i_{jj}$ the gauge-invariant (para plus dia) current correlator (E Eqs. 321–322). Each sector has its own series, and at higher order the lines mix: a transverse photon can sit inside the Coulomb sector (EPP Fig. 8).

Induced charges and currents

Cut a diagram open and attach an external source. It is either a charge $\rho^{\rm ext}$, acting through $v$ (or $W$ once screened), or a vector potential coupled through $-\frac1c\int\bj\cdot\bA^{\rm ext}$, which does not distinguish $l$ from $t$. The bubbles are irreducible, so (EPP p. 72; $\otimes$ is a convolution in space and time)

$$\rho^{i,{\rm ind},l}=\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext},\qquad i={\rm e,\,ion}\tag{E.330}$$
$$j^{i,{\rm ind}}_\mu=\sum_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}\otimes A^{{\rm ext,sc}}_\nu\tag{E.333}$$

and likewise $\rho^{i,\rm ind}=\sum_\mu\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}\otimes A^{\rm ext,sc}_\mu$ and $j^{i,{\rm ind},l}_\mu=\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}\otimes W\otimes\rho^{\rm ext}$ (E Eqs. 331–332), with $\bA^{\rm ext,sc}$ the screened external field. This is the identity $\chi v=PW$ for both kinds of probe. With time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$, the temporal gauge and continuity read

$$\bE^{{\rm ext},\sigma,{\rm sc}}(\omega)=i\frac{\omega}{c}\,\bA^{{\rm ext},\sigma,{\rm sc}}(\omega),\qquad\sigma=l,t\tag{E.334}$$
$$\nabla\cdot\bj=i\omega\rho\tag{E.335}$$

Charge conservation: the connection formulas

Impose continuity on the pair induced by the longitudinal probe (E Eq. 340) and on the pair induced by the total field (E Eq. 342). Since $\rho^{\rm ext}$ and $\bA^{\rm ext,sc}$ are arbitrary and $W$ is invertible, the kernels themselves must agree (EPP p. 73):

$$\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\br,\br',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\br,\br',\omega)\tag{E.341}$$
$$\Pi^i_{\rho j\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)=-\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\mu\partial_\mu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)\tag{E.344}$$

The mixed correlators carry different prefactors, $\Pi_{j\rho}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle T\{j\rho\}\rangle$ and $\Pi_{\rho j}=+\frac{i}{\hbar c}\langle T\{\rho j\}\rangle$ (E Eqs. 345–346), because a current vertex always comes from $-\frac1c\int\bj\cdot\bA$. Reversing the operator order in a time-ordered correlator gives

$$\Pi^i_{\rho j\mu}(\br',\br,\omega)=-\frac1c\,\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\br,\br',-\omega)\tag{E.347}$$

and $\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\br,\br',-\omega)=\Pi^i_{jj\nu\mu}(\br',\br,\omega)$ (E Eq. 348). These move the derivative of E Eq. 344 onto $\br'$, and E Eq. 341 closes the set:

$$\Pi^i_{j\rho\mu}(\br,\br',\omega)=-i\frac{c}{\omega}\sum_\nu\partial'_\nu\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)\tag{E.349}$$
$$\boxed{\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\br,\br',\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\,\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)}\tag{E.350}$$

In a homogeneous medium, with $\partial_\mu\to iq_\mu$ and $\partial'_\nu\to-iq_\nu$ (derived from E Eqs. 344, 349 and 350):

$$\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,\bq\cdot\Pi_{jj}\cdot\bq,\qquad \Pi_{\rho j}=\frac1\omega\,\bq\cdot\Pi_{jj},\qquad \Pi_{j\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega}\,\Pi_{jj}\cdot\bq .$$

These are the Ward identities of QED, $q_\mu\Pi^{\mu\nu}=0$ for the four-current, written for a crystal. Only $\Pi_{jj}$ is independent. The density response is its longitudinal projection, and a transverse current ($\bq\cdot\bj=0$) carries no charge.

EPP §6.0.2 verifies E Eq. 350 for the ions. $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ computed directly from $\rho^{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\bP$ is, at $\bG=\bG'=0$ (cf. EPP p. 77, E p.77c′) and with the vertex $j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)$ of E p.86a′,

$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}(\bq,\omega)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\frac{|\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma(\bq)|^2}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta},$$

which is exactly $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}\cdot\bq$ for the gauge-invariant correlator of §B.5,

$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\,\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq+\bG)\,j^*_{\nu\sigma}(\bq+\bG')}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta},\tag{E.352$'$}$$

here at $\bG=\bG'=0$. With every element kept, the same check gives the matrix form $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho,\bG\bG'}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\mu\nu}(q_\mu+G_\mu)(q_\nu+G'_\nu)\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu,\bG\bG'}$ (E Eq. 354a), because the vertices (E Eq. 295′) carry the basis phases $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_a}$ of dipoles at their atoms (§Z.3). The overall $\omega^2$ comes from the diamagnetic subtraction $-\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$. The paramagnetic part alone would add $-\sum_\sigma|\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2/(\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega^2)$, a static density response that diverges (third tab of the figure below). The numerator $\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma=\sum_a\bq\cdot Z^*_a\cdot\be_{a\sigma}\,e^{-i\bq\cdot\br^0_a}/\sqrt{m_a}$ is that of the Verdi–Giustino vertex (G Eq. 180), basis phase included.

Derivation: from E Eqs. 341 and 344 to E Eqs. 349 and 350, and the ionic check

By E Eq. 347, $\Pi_{j\rho\mu}(\br,\br',\omega)=-c\,\Pi_{\rho j\mu}(\br',\br,-\omega)$. E Eq. 344 with $\br\leftrightarrow\br'$ and $\omega\to-\omega$ gives $\Pi_{\rho j\mu}(\br',\br,-\omega)=\frac{i}{\omega}\sum_\nu\partial'_\nu\Pi_{jj\nu\mu}(\br',\br,-\omega)$, and E Eq. 348 turns the last factor into $\Pi_{jj\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)$: this is E Eq. 349. Inserted into E Eq. 341 it gives the prefactor $(-\frac{i}{\omega})(-i\frac{c}{\omega})=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}$: E Eq. 350.

Ionic check: $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\langle T\{P_\mu P_\nu\}\rangle$ (E Eq. 353). The displacement operator brings $\hbar/(2m_a\omega_\sigma)$ and the Fourier-transformed phonon pairing $2i\omega_\sigma/(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta)$, so the prefactor is $-\frac{i}{N\hbar}\cdot\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_\sigma}\cdot2i\omega_\sigma=\frac1N$, and the lattice sums (E Eq. 354, with the dipoles at $\bR^0_{ia}$) turn $\frac1N$ into $\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}$. The kernel $\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma j_{\mu\sigma}j^*_{\nu\sigma}/(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)$ is $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu}$ by E Eq. 352′. With the paramagnetic part alone, $\Pi^{\rm para}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma\omega_\sigma^2j_{\mu\sigma}j^*_{\nu\sigma}/(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)$ (E Eq. 297′), the same contraction gives $\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\sigma|\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2\frac{\omega_\sigma^2}{\omega^2(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)}$, the direct result minus $\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega^2}\sum_\sigma|\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2$.

For the GW/BSE practitioner: length and velocity gauge

E Eq. 350 is why a length-gauge calculation with $\langle c|e^{-i\bq\cdot\br}|v\rangle$ and a velocity-gauge one with $\langle c|\hat\bp|v\rangle=im\omega_{cv}\langle c|\br|v\rangle$ give the same $\epsilon$. In E Eq. 383 below, the diamagnetic subtraction that makes it hold appears as the factor $\hbar\omega/(E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq})$. The $1/\omega^2$ divergence of velocity-gauge spectra computed without the diamagnetic term violates it. The current must also obey continuity with the Hamiltonian: nonlocal pseudopotentials require the correction $\frac{ie}{\hbar}[V_{\rm NL},\br]$ to the current of E Eq. 384. EPP verifies E Eq. 350 only for the ionic RPA correlator and concludes "inductively" that all connection formulas hold. They do, provided $\Pi_{jj}$ contains the diamagnetic term and the current operator obeys continuity with the Hamiltonian.

From four sectors to one tensor (EPP §6.0.3)

With E Eqs. 344, 349 and 350 every lowest-order sector contains only $\Pi_{jj}$ (EPP Fig. 9). EPP labels the scalar (Coulomb) slot of the $4\times4$ matrix "1", so $D_{11}$ is the scalar–scalar entry, not the $xx$ component. The Coulomb sector reads

$$\begin{aligned}D_{11}(\br,\br',\omega)={}&v(\br,\br')\\&-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{i={\rm e,ion}}\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}\int d\br_1d\br_2\,v(\br,\br_1)\big[\partial_{1\alpha}\partial_{2\beta}\Pi^i_{jj\alpha\beta}(\br_1,\br_2,\omega)\big]v(\br_2,\br')+\dots\end{aligned}\tag{E.355}$$

Integrating by parts moves the derivatives onto the Coulomb lines (EPP p. 79), but the sectors remain a scalar, a row, a column and a matrix. EPP acts on them with $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu$ (E Eq. 359), $i\frac{c}{\omega}\partial_\mu$, $\frac{i}{\omega}\partial'_\nu$ and $1$. In our reading (EPP does not say so) this is a change to the temporal gauge: a potential $\phi(\br)$ becomes $\bA_l=\frac{ic}{\omega}\nabla\phi$, and a charge source becomes a current source through $\rho=-\frac{i}{\omega}\nabla'\cdot\bj$. Every Coulomb line then appears as

$$\boxed{D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\,v(\br-\br')}\tag{E.364}$$

and all four sectors share the form $D^{ab}=\delta_{ab}D^{0a}+D^{0a}\Pi_{jj}D^{0b}+\dots$ with $a,b\in\{l,t\}$ (E Eqs. 365–368, EPP Fig. 10). The rule is

$$\rho^i\to j^i_\mu\quad(i={\rm ion,e}),\qquad v\to D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\omega),\tag{E.377}$$

and the sum is $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D^0+\dots$ with $D^0=D^{0l}+D^{0t}$ and $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$ (E Eqs. 369–371). The second tab of the figure below steps through EPP Figs. 9–11.

What $D^0$ is: the vacuum Maxwell propagator (EPP §6.0.4)

Fourier-transforming E Eq. 364 (E Eq. 373) and adding the transverse photon, with $\omega_\bq=cq$:

$$D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{q^2}\,\frac{q^2\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu}{\omega^2-\omega_\bq^2+i\eta},\tag{E.372}$$
$$D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{q^2}\,\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{\omega^2},\tag{E.374}$$
$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)+D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\frac{-q_\mu q_\nu+\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\mu\nu}}{q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}-i\eta}.\tag{E.375}$$

In projector form, $D^0=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}P^L+\frac{4\pi}{c}\frac{P^T}{q^2-\omega^2/c^2}$ with $P^L=\hat q\hat q$ and $P^T=1-\hat q\hat q$. This is the inverse of the temporal-gauge Maxwell operator: $[(q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2})\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu]A_\nu=\frac{4\pi}{c}j_\mu$ (derived here; EPP cites its "Lecture 9"). So $D^0\bj^{\rm ext}$ is the field of an external current, and $D^0\Pi_{jj}D^0\bj^{\rm ext}$ the first induced field (E Eq. 376). The longitudinal part $-4\pi c/\omega^2$ is independent of $q$: it has no light cone. Where $D^{0t}$ competes with it is the subject of §X.1.

Is the longitudinal propagator zero or not? E Eq. 263 vs E Eq. 374

Both statements are correct, and they are about different objects.

  • E Eq. 263, $D^{0l}=0$ (§B.5), is the free propagator of the interaction-picture operator $\bA_l$, which has no restoring force. Its field energy $\frac{1}{8\pi}\int\bE_l^2$ became, through Gauss's law, the Coulomb term of E Eq. 312′ and was moved into $V'$ (§B.4). An operator with no dynamics in $H_0$ is static in the interaction picture, so its free propagator vanishes at every $\omega\neq0$ (only a $\delta(\omega)$ term remains). Hence $\hat D^{0t}$ is the only vector line in E Eq. 317.
  • E Eq. 374 is not a new line. It is the Coulomb line $v$ of E Eq. 312′, carried by the connection formulas from charge vertices to current vertices. EPP calls E Eq. 364 "ad hoc", but $\bA_l=\frac{ic}{\omega}\nabla\phi$ and continuity fix it.

Keeping $v$ and adding $D^{0l}$ would count Coulomb twice. The $1/\omega^2$ double pole of E Eq. 374, for which EPP gives no $i\eta$, describes a static charge in the temporal gauge, where $\bA_l$ grows linearly in time at constant $\bE$. It is harmless because $D^{0l}$ only meets conserved currents, and $\bq\cdot\bj=\omega\rho$ supplies the compensating powers of $\omega$. Likewise, "identical to the vacuum Maxwell propagator" holds in the temporal gauge: photon propagators are gauge dependent, and only their action on conserved currents is not.

One Dyson equation

At higher order each sector mixes both lines and all vertex types (EPP §6.0.5, Fig. 11a). E Eq. 377 maps every diagram to current vertices joined by $D^{0l}$ and $D^{0t}$. Compatible diagrams sum into $D^0$ lines and the bubbles into $\Pi_{jj}$, so the series is geometric (E Eq. 378):

$$D=D^0+D^0\,\Pi_{jj}\,D,\qquad D^0=D^{0l}+D^{0t},\qquad \Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}.\tag{E.379}$$

In $(\bq,\omega)$ space, with $D^0$ from E Eq. 375 (repeated as E Eq. 381) and $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$ (E Eq. 382):

$$D_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)+\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}D^0_{\mu\alpha}(\bq,\omega)\,\Pi_{jj\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,D_{\beta\nu}(\bq,\omega).\tag{E.380}$$

The ionic part is the $\bG=\bG'=0$ element of E Eq. 352′ (repeated as E Eq. 385), with the vertex $j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)$ of E p.86a′, which carries the basis phase $e^{-i\bq\cdot\br^0_a}$ (§Z.3). The electronic part is the "para minus static para" form of §B.5, with the current vertices of E Eq. 384:

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)&=\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega=0)\\&=\frac{2}{c\Omega}\sum_{\bk\in{\rm BZ}}\sum_{nm}\frac{\hbar\omega}{E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}}\,\frac{f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}+i\eta}\,j^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big[j^\nu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big]^*\end{aligned}\tag{E.383}$$

Here $\Omega=N\Omega_{\rm uc}$ is the crystal volume, $f$ are occupations and the 2 counts spin. $D$ propagates the total field $\bA^l+\bA^t$ in a crystal of electrons and phonons: "$W$ with photons". It is RPA: lowest-order irreducible bubbles, no mixed electron–ion bubbles, no exchange–correlation kernel and, from E Eq. 380 on, no crystal local fields ($\bG=\bG'=0$): neither those of the electrons nor, through the structure factors of E Eq. 295′, those of the ions.

Isotropic medium: plasmons, LO phonons and polaritons

In an isotropic medium, or a cubic crystal at small $q$, both $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi_LP^L+\Pi_TP^T$ and $D^0$ are diagonal in the projectors. E Eq. 380 decouples and $D^{lt}=D^{tl}=0$ to all orders (derived here, not in the manuscript):

$$D_L(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2\,\epsilon_L(\bq,\omega)},\qquad D_T(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2\epsilon_T(\bq,\omega)-c^2q^2},\qquad \epsilon_{L,T}=1+\frac{4\pi c\,\Pi_{L,T}}{\omega^2}.$$

By E Eq. 350, $\epsilon_L=1-v\Pi_{\rho\rho}$, the RPA dielectric function of GW and BSE. The two channels have different poles.

  • $\epsilon_L(\bq,\omega)=0$: longitudinal modes, Coulomb dressed by matter. They do not depend on $c$, because $c\,\Pi_{jj}$ does not. A Drude metal gives the plasmon at $\omega_p$. A polar crystal, $\epsilon=\epsilon_\infty+\Omega_p^2/(\omega_{TO}^2-\omega^2)$ with $\Omega_p^2=4\pi|j|^2/\Omega_{\rm uc}$ for one mode of E Eq. 352′, gives the LO phonon at $\omega_{LO}^2=\omega_{TO}^2+\Omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$. Here $\omega_{TO}$ is the bare $\omega_\sigma$, since the Dyson route has no local field.
  • $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon_T(\bq,\omega)$: transverse modes, light dressed by matter. For the polar crystal these are the phonon polaritons
$$\omega_\pm^2=\frac{(\epsilon_\infty\omega_{LO}^2+c^2q^2)\pm\sqrt{(\epsilon_\infty\omega_{LO}^2+c^2q^2)^2-4\epsilon_\infty c^2q^2\omega_{TO}^2}}{2\epsilon_\infty}.$$

The upper branch starts at $\omega_{LO}$ at $q=0$, where $L$ and $T$ cannot be told apart. The lower branch tends to $\omega_{TO}$ for $cq\gg\sqrt{\epsilon_0}\,\omega_{TO}$. Between them $\epsilon\lt0$ and no transverse mode exists: the Reststrahlen band. §B.7 obtains the same branches from the phonon side and follows the order of the limits $q\to0$ and $c\to\infty$.

Derivation: L and T decouple, $D_{11}=W$, and the polariton roots

Write $D^0=d_LP^L+d_TP^T$ with $d_L=-4\pi c/\omega^2$ and $d_T=-4\pi c/(\omega^2-c^2q^2)$ (E Eq. 375). Since $P^LP^L=P^L$, $P^TP^T=P^T$ and $P^LP^T=0$, the series E Eq. 378 sums channel by channel, $D_{L,T}=d_{L,T}/(1-d_{L,T}\Pi_{L,T})$, which gives the forms in the text; no product contains $P^LP^T$, so $D^{lt}=D^{tl}=0$. With E Eq. 350, $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{cq^2}{\omega^2}\Pi_L$, hence $1-v\Pi_{\rho\rho}=\epsilon_L$.

In $\bq$-space E Eq. 359 is $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}q_\mu q_\nu$, so $D^{ll}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}q_\mu q_\nu D_{11}$. Comparing with $D^{ll}=D_LP^L$ gives $D_{11}=-\frac{\omega^2}{cq^2}D_L=\frac{4\pi}{q^2\epsilon_L}$.

For the polar model, $\epsilon=\epsilon_\infty(\omega_{LO}^2-\omega^2)/(\omega_{TO}^2-\omega^2)$, and $\omega^2\epsilon=c^2q^2$ is the quadratic $\epsilon_\infty\omega^4-(\epsilon_\infty\omega_{LO}^2+c^2q^2)\,\omega^2+c^2q^2\omega_{TO}^2=0$ with roots $\omega_\pm^2$. The same algebra with $\epsilon=(\omega_L^2-\omega^2)/(\Delta^2-\omega^2)$ gives the polaritons of the two-level insulator in the figure.

Bridge to Giustino: $W$ is the $ll$ sector of $D$

The scalar slot of EPP's propagator is Giustino's screened Coulomb interaction (§A.5). In an isotropic medium, exactly,

$$D^{ll}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,q_\mu q_\nu\,W(\bq,\omega),\qquad W=D_{11}=\frac{4\pi}{q^2\,\epsilon_L(\bq,\omega)} .$$

Resumming only electronic bubbles gives Giustino's

$$W_{\rm e}(12)=v(12)+\int d(34)\,v(13)\,P_{\rm e}(34)\,W_{\rm e}(42),\tag{G.94}$$

with $P_{\rm e}\leftrightarrow\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}\cdot\bq$. Adding the ions, $\epsilon_L=\epsilon_\infty-v\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ for the polar model, and expanding $1/\epsilon_L$ with $W_{\rm e}=4\pi/(q^2\epsilon_\infty)$:

$$W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}\,W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}\,W_{\rm e}\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}\,W_{\rm e}+\dots\equiv W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}\,\mathcal D\,W_{\rm e}.$$

This is $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$ with $W_{\rm ph}=\int d(34)\,W_{\rm e}(13)\mathcal D(34)W_{\rm e}(24)$ (G Eqs. 104–105). Giustino's nuclear density correlator $\mathcal D$ (G Eq. 103) is represented by the Born-charge $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}$ dressed by $W_{\rm e}$. Three things differ on EPP's side, one addition and two restrictions:

  • the vector block ($lt$, $tl$, $tt$), which carries transverse photons;
  • Born-charge point dipoles instead of bare nuclei with $\nabla V_\kappa$ (G Eq. 110), so only the long-range part of $\mathcal D$ is represented;
  • RPA without xc or local fields.

In an isotropic medium the $ll$ sector never sees $c$. Retardation in EPP's sense (finite speed of light) enters $W$ only through $l$–$t$ mixing, the diagrams of EPP Fig. 8: in anisotropic crystals off the principal axes, with local fields, and in 2D sheets (§B.10). The formal $c\to\infty$ statement is E Eqs. 437 and 441 (§B.9).

What the spectral map leaves out

The response is local. That is accurate here, since the plotted $q\lesssim10\,\omega/c$ is tiny on the scale of $k_F$ or the Brillouin zone; at larger $q$ plasmons disperse and $\Pi_L\neq\Pi_T$. The polar model uses a constant $\epsilon_\infty$ for the electrons. Uniaxial hBN mixes $L$ and $T$ off its axes, $\hat\bq\cdot\Pi_{jj}\cdot\hat{\mathbf t}\propto(\Pi_\parallel-\Pi_\perp)\sin\theta\cos\theta$. This mixing, with $\epsilon_\parallel$ and $\epsilon_\perp$ of opposite sign inside its Reststrahlen bands, produces its hyperbolic phonon polaritons, which the isotropic map cannot show. A 2D sheet needs the sheet propagator $D^0(\bQ,\omega;0,0)$, not just $\Omega\to S$ (§B.10).
