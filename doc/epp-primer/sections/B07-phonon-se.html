B.7EPP §7, §7.1 · Eqs. 386–410

The electrodynamic phonon self-energy and the LO–TO splitting

A polar phonon dresses itself by emitting and reabsorbing an electron-screened photon. By the end you can derive EPP's phonon Dyson equation, recognise it as Giustino's Eqs. (135) and (137) with photons, and explain why EPP's $\bq=0$ "LO–TO splitting" (E Eq. 410) is really the upper phonon polariton of a crystal without electrons.

In §B.6 the whole crystal was folded into one field propagator, $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D$ with $\Pi_{jj}=\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}$. EPP §7 reads the same diagram series the other way round. Instead of asking how matter dresses light, it asks how light dresses the lattice. Units are Gaussian throughout, and the Born charges $Z^*$ carry the dimension of charge.

Physical picture

A long-wavelength polar phonon is a lattice of identical oscillating Born-charge dipoles. An oscillating dipole is a current, $\dot{\bp}$, and a current radiates: it creates a longitudinal (Coulomb) field and a transverse photon field. That field crosses the polarizable electron gas, which screens it, and acts back on the ions. The loop phonon → current → screened field → current → phonon contains everything long-range in polar lattice dynamics: the LO–TO splitting, phonon polaritons and, in doped crystals, LO-phonon–plasmon modes. The bare frequencies $\omega_\sigma(\bq)$ keep only the short-range forces (§B.1).

Step 1: resum the electrons into the photon line

Take every diagram of $D$ that starts and ends on a bare ionic bubble and remove the outer $D^0$ lines. What remains is the screened ionic current–current correlator. From here on a superscript 0 marks a bare (irreducible) bubble (EPP p. 87). With the arguments $(\bq,\omega)$ written only in the first line,

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)&=\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)+\sum_{\alpha\beta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\alpha}(\bq,\omega)D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\beta\nu}(\bq,\omega)\\&\quad+\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\alpha}D^0_{\alpha\beta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\beta\gamma}D^0_{\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\delta\nu}+\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\alpha}D^0_{\alpha\beta}\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\beta\gamma}D^0_{\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\delta\nu}+\dots\end{aligned}\tag{E.386}$$

Between two ionic bubbles any chain of electronic bubbles may appear. Collecting those chains into an electron-dressed photon line $D^{\rm e}$ leaves only ionic bubbles:

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)&=\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)+\sum_{\alpha\beta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\alpha}(\bq,\omega)D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\beta\nu}(\bq,\omega)\\&\quad+\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\alpha}D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\beta\gamma}D^{\rm e}_{\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\delta\nu}+\dots\end{aligned}\tag{E.387}$$
$$D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)+\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}D^0_{\mu\alpha}(\bq,\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\beta\nu}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.388}$$

E Eq. 388 is the Dyson equation of §B.6 with the electronic self-energy only. The author's analogy is the response of a medium A embedded in a polarizable medium B: $\chi_A=\chi^0_A+\chi^0_AW_B\chi^0_A+\dots$ with $W_B=v+v\chi^0_BW_B$. If you have screened a 2D layer by its substrate, this is the same move. The ions are system A, the electrons are the environment B, and $D^{\rm e}$ plays the role of $W_B$, now with transverse photons included.

Step 2: open the ionic bubble

The bare ionic bubble is a sum over the $3s$ bare phonon branches ($s$ atoms of mass $m_a$ per cell of volume $\Omega_{\rm uc}$, eigenvectors $e_{a\alpha\sigma}$). It is built from the ionic current vertex $j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)=\sum_{a\alpha}Z^*_{a\mu\alpha}e_{a\alpha\sigma}(\bq)/\sqrt{m_a}$ (E Eq. 295). The bare phonon propagator is the second line:

$$\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\,\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)\,j^*_{\nu\sigma}(\bq)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}\tag{E.389}$$
$$F^0_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=2\,\frac{\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{\hbar}\,\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}\tag{E.390}$$

The bubble becomes "vertex × phonon line × vertex" once one defines the dynamical ionic current vertex:

$$j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)\;\to\;J_{\mu\sigma}(\bq,\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2c\,\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\;\omega\,j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)\tag{E.392}$$
$$\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}J_{\mu\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,F^0_\sigma(\bq,\omega)\,J^*_{\nu\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.393}$$

The factor $\omega$ is physical. A Born-charge dipole oscillating as $e^{-i\omega t}$ carries the current $\dot{\bp}=-i\omega\bp$, and the field couples to currents through $-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA$. A static displacement carries no current, so $J(\omega=0)=0$. The two vertices carry the overall $\omega^2$ of the gauge-invariant bubble, which came from subtracting the static paramagnetic term (§B.5). The square root collects the zero-point amplitude $\sqrt{\hbar/2m_a\omega_\sigma}$ (the mass is already inside $j$) and the $1/\sqrt{c\,\Omega_{\rm uc}}$ normalization of the current coupling.

Step 3: a Dyson equation for phonons

Insert E Eq. 393 into E Eq. 387. Each bare ionic bubble becomes $-JF^0J^*$, and between two phonon lines sits the combination $J^*D^{\rm e}J$. Defining minus that combination as the phonon self-energy, the series reads (unnumbered, EPP p. 89)

$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu}=-\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}J_{\mu\sigma}\left\{F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0+\dots\right\}J^*_{\nu\sigma'},$$

and the curly bracket is the perturbative expansion of the phonon Green's matrix:

$$F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F^0+\dots\tag{E.397}$$
$$F=F^0+F^0\,\Omega^{\rm e}\,F\tag{E.398}$$
$$\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.399}$$

Derivation: E Eq. 393 reproduces E Eq. 389, and the signs of the Dyson series

Insert E Eqs. 390 and 392:

$$-J_{\mu\sigma}F^0_\sigma J^*_{\nu\sigma}=-\frac{\hbar\,\omega^2}{2c\,\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\omega_\sigma}\,j_{\mu\sigma}j^*_{\nu\sigma}\cdot\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\hbar}\,\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\,\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{j_{\mu\sigma}j^*_{\nu\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2+i\eta}.$$

The term of E Eq. 387 with $n$ lines $D^{\rm e}$ contains $n+1$ bare bubbles, so $(-1)^{n+1}$ from E Eq. 393, and $n$ factors $J^*D^{\rm e}J=-\Omega^{\rm e}$, so another $(-1)^n$. The product is always $-1$: the term equals $-J\,F^0(\Omega^{\rm e}F^0)^n J^*$, which is the bracket above. Summing the geometric series gives E Eq. 398.

$\Omega^{\rm e}$ is a $3s\times3s$ matrix. Off-diagonal elements mix branches, because one photon line can connect two different polar modes. EPP splits it as $\Omega^{\rm e}=\Omega^0+\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ (E Eq. 400), with an electron-free part

$$\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.401}$$

and an electron-induced remainder $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$, where the electrodynamic Fröhlich vertices live (§B.8). Neglecting phonon mixing, $F_{\sigma\sigma'}\approx F_\sigma\delta_{\sigma\sigma'}$, E Eq. 398 becomes algebra:

$$F_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=\frac{F^0_\sigma(\bq,\omega)}{1-F^0_\sigma(\bq,\omega)\,\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\bq,\omega)}\tag{E.404}$$
$$F_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)-2\omega_\sigma(\bq)\,\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\bq,\omega)/\hbar}\tag{E.405}$$

The renormalized frequencies solve $\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+2\omega_\sigma\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\bq,\omega)/\hbar$, a self-consistent condition because $\Omega^{\rm e}$ depends on $\omega$. For the optical modes of a cubic diatomic crystal near $\Gamma$, choosing the bare modes along and across $\hat\bq$ makes $\Omega^{\rm e}$ diagonal among the optical modes. The acoustic modes carry no current at $\Gamma$, so E Eq. 404 is exact at $\Gamma$ and accurate near it. The figure below uses this.

Why the infinite sum matters. Each finite order of E Eq. 397 is a polynomial in $F^0$ and $\Omega^{\rm e}$. Its poles are those of $F^0$, at the bare $\omega_\sigma$ with growing multiplicity, plus any poles of $\Omega^{\rm e}$ itself, such as the bare photon. None of them is a renormalized mode. The geometric series converges only where $|F^0\Omega^{\rm e}|\lt 1$, and the renormalized pole sits exactly on the boundary, where $F^0\Omega^{\rm e}=1$. No truncation reaches it. The "Why resum?" tab of the figure shows this numerically.

Bridge to Giustino: the same Dyson equation

Here Giustino's phonon Green's function is written $\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}$ and his phonon self-energy $\Pi^{\rm ph}$ (in the PDF they are $\mathbf D$ and $\Pi$; see §A.6), with nonadiabatic part $\Pi^{\rm NA}=\Pi^{\rm ph}-\Pi^{\rm A}$. His adiabatic propagator $\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu\nu'}=2\omega_{\bq\nu}\delta_{\nu\nu'}/(\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu})$ (G Eq. 132) has the form of $\hbar F^0_\sigma$, with adiabatic frequencies in place of EPP's short-range ones. Giustino §V.A.2 writes

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}(\omega)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}^{\rm A}(\omega)+\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}^{\rm A}(\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}(\omega)\tag{G.135}$$
$$\tilde\Omega^2_{\bq\nu}=\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+2\omega_{\bq\nu}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu}(\tilde\Omega_{\bq\nu})\tag{G.137}$$

These are E Eq. 398 and the pole condition of E Eq. 405, with $\Omega^{\rm e}/\hbar$ in the place of $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ (in general $\Omega^{\rm e}\leftrightarrow\hbar\,\Pi^{\rm ph}$): Giustino's self-energy is a frequency, EPP's an energy. Mind the letters: Giustino's $\tilde\Omega$ is a renormalized frequency, EPP's $\Omega^{\rm e}$ a self-energy and $\Omega_{\rm uc}$ a cell volume. Both papers drop the off-diagonal elements, Giustino's effect (iii) "coupling between the adiabatic vibrational eigenmodes", and both solve the pole condition self-consistently (E Eq. 407 below). The theories differ in what they call the unperturbed phonon:

So $\Omega^{\rm e}(\bq,0)$, which is purely longitudinal (at $\omega=0$ the $\omega^2$ of $J^*J$ cancels the $1/\omega^2$ of the longitudinal $D^{\rm e}$ and removes the transverse part), is the long-range part of Giustino's $\Pi^{\rm A}$. $\Omega^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)-\Omega^{\rm e}(\bq,0)$ is the dipolar part of his $\Pi^{\rm NA}$, plus the transverse-photon part that his theory does not have.

§7.1: switch the electrons off and set q = 0

With $D^{\rm e}\to D^0$ the self-energy is E Eq. 401 on the diagonal, and the pole condition must be solved self-consistently:

$$\Omega^0_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.406}$$
$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)=\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+2\omega_\sigma(\bq)\,\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}\big[\bq,\tilde\omega_\sigma(\bq)\big]/\hbar\tag{E.407}$$

The free propagator of §B.6,

$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\frac{-q_\mu q_\nu+\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\mu\nu}}{q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}-i\eta},\tag{E.381}$$

loses its direction dependence in the optical limit $\bq=0$:

$$D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bq=0,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}\tag{E.408}$$

The $\omega^2$ in $J^*J$ cancels the $1/\omega^2$ of $D^0$. The self-energy is frequency independent, and the self-consistency becomes trivial:

$$\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}(\bq=0)=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\omega_\sigma(\bq=0)}\sum_\alpha\big|j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq=0)\big|^2\tag{E.409}$$
$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\bq=0)=\omega^2_\sigma(\bq=0)+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha\big|j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq=0)\big|^2\tag{E.410}$$

For a cubic diatomic crystal ($Z^*_a=-Z^*_b=Z^*$, reduced mass $\mu$) every polar optical mode has $\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2=Z^{*2}/\mu$. The shift is then the ionic plasma frequency of §B.3, $\omega_p^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}\mu)$. Note the size of E Eq. 409: for GaAs ($Z^*=2.07$, $\hbar\omega_\sigma=33.3$ meV; literature values, illustrative) it is about 30 meV (33 meV in the figure, whose $\omega_p$ comes from the measured splitting), comparable to $\hbar\omega_\sigma$ itself. There is no small parameter, which is why the resummation matters.

Derivation: from E Eq. 406 to E Eq. 410, and the same at finite q

With E Eq. 392, $\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}=-\frac{\hbar\omega^2}{2c\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}\sum_{\alpha\beta}j^*_{\alpha\sigma}D^0_{\alpha\beta}j_{\beta\sigma}$. At $\bq=0$, E Eq. 408 gives $\frac{\hbar\omega^2}{2c\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}\cdot\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$, which is E Eq. 409. Then $2\omega_\sigma\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}/\hbar=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$, and E Eq. 407 becomes E Eq. 410.

At finite $\bq$, project E Eq. 381 on $\hat\bq$ and on a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf t}\perp\bq$. The mixed elements vanish and

$$\hat\bq\cdot D^0\cdot\hat\bq=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2},\qquad \hat{\mathbf t}\cdot D^0\cdot\hat{\mathbf t}=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2-c^2q^2}.$$

Split $\bj_\sigma=\bj_{\sigma\parallel}+\bj_{\sigma\perp}$ and write $\omega^2_{p\parallel,\perp}=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}|\bj_{\sigma\parallel,\perp}|^2$. Then $2\omega_\sigma\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}/\hbar=\omega^2_{p\parallel}+\omega^2_{p\perp}\,\omega^2/(\omega^2-c^2q^2)$, which gives the finite-$q$ condition in the text.

Electrons below the gap of an insulator can be modelled by $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\mu\nu}=\frac{(\epsilon_\infty-1)\omega^2}{4\pi c}\delta_{\mu\nu}$, the small-$\omega$ form of the electronic bubble (§B.5). E Eq. 388 then gives $\hat\bq\cdot D^{\rm e}\cdot\hat\bq=-\frac{4\pi c}{\epsilon_\infty\omega^2}$ and $\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot D^{\rm e}\cdot\hat{\mathbf t}=-\frac{4\pi c}{\epsilon_\infty\omega^2-c^2q^2}$. For the transverse modes of a cubic diatomic crystal, the poles of E Eq. 405 then satisfy $(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)(\epsilon_\infty\omega^2-c^2q^2)=\omega_p^2\omega^2$, whose roots are

$$\omega_\pm^2=\frac{(\epsilon_\infty\omega_L^2+c^2q^2)\pm\sqrt{(\epsilon_\infty\omega_L^2+c^2q^2)^2-4\epsilon_\infty c^2q^2\omega_\sigma^2}}{2\epsilon_\infty},\qquad \omega_L^2=\omega_\sigma^2+\frac{\omega_p^2}{\epsilon_\infty}.$$

The residue of $F_\sigma$ at each pole in the variable $\omega^2$, relative to the bare residue $2\omega_\sigma/\hbar$, is the phonon weight $Z_\pm=\big[1+\omega_p^2c^2q^2/(\epsilon_\infty\omega_\pm^2-c^2q^2)^2\big]^{-1}$, and $Z_++Z_-=1$.

What E Eq. 410 is, and what it is not

EPP opens §7.1 with "This procedure is completely analogous to the non-analytic dynamical matrix correction done by Quantum Espresso." The analogy holds in form: both shifts are built from Born charges and the cell volume. But E Eq. 410 is a different limit, and it is not the LO–TO splitting that DFPT adds. Three features give it away.

  • It is isotropic. $\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$ counts all three components of the mode current. At $\bq=0$ the two transverse modes shift exactly as much as the longitudinal one. The DFPT non-analytic term depends on $|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2$ and leaves TO modes where they are.
  • It is unscreened. No electrons means $\epsilon_\infty=1$. For GaAs ($Z^*\approx2.07$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}=a^3/4=45.2$ Å$^3$, $\mu=36.1$ amu, $\hbar\omega_{\rm TO}=33.3$ meV, $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}=36.2$ meV; literature values, illustrative) and Gaussian $e^2=14.40$ eV Å, $\hbar\omega_p\approx44.6$ meV. E Eq. 410 then puts the shifted mode at $\sqrt{33.3^2+44.6^2}\approx56$ meV, against the measured LO phonon at 36.2 meV. The electrons divide the shift by $\epsilon_\infty\approx10.9$, and that factor only arrives with $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ (§B.8).
  • It is retarded, in EPP's sense of a finite speed of light (not Giustino's sense of frequency-dependent screening). E Eq. 408 is the regime $cq\ll\omega$, inside the light cone. There the shifted "phonon" is the $\bq=0$ end of the upper phonon polariton.

Keeping $\bq$ finite shows all three at once. With $\bj_\sigma$ split along and across $\hat\bq$, E Eqs. 381, 392 and 406 give (derived here, not in the manuscript)

$$\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+\omega_{p\parallel}^2+\omega_{p\perp}^2\,\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-c^2q^2},\qquad \omega^2_{p\parallel,\perp}=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\,|\bj_{\sigma\parallel,\perp}|^2 .$$

The longitudinal part never sees $c$: $\hat\bq\cdot D^0\cdot\hat\bq=-4\pi c/\omega^2$ at every $q$, so the LO mode sits at $\sqrt{\omega_\sigma^2+\omega_{p\parallel}^2}$ for all $q$. The transverse part is the polariton condition $c^2q^2=\omega^2\epsilon(\omega)$ with $\epsilon(\omega)=1+\omega_p^2/(\omega_\sigma^2-\omega^2)$, the photon-side picture of §B.6 seen from the phonon. Its upper branch starts at $\sqrt{\omega_\sigma^2+\omega_p^2}$ at $q=0$, which is E Eq. 410, while the lower branch is photon-like there. Electrons, modelled by a constant $\epsilon_\infty$, divide $\omega_{p\parallel}^2$ by $\epsilon_\infty$ and turn $\omega^2-c^2q^2$ into $\epsilon_\infty\omega^2-c^2q^2$.

The order of limits

In this result $c$ and $q$ enter only through the product $cq$. The limits $q\to0$ and $c\to\infty$ therefore do not commute:

  • $q\to0$ at fixed $c$ (E Eq. 410; with electrons, $\omega_p^2\to\omega_p^2/\epsilon_\infty$): all three polar modes sit at $\omega_L$, whatever the direction of approach. The transverse ones are the upper polariton.
  • $c\to\infty$ at fixed $q$, then $q\to0$ (E Eq. 454, §B.9): only the $ll$ sector of $D^{\rm e}$ survives, the TO modes stay at $\omega_\sigma$, and the LO shift carries $|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2/(\hat\bq\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\hat\bq)$. This is the non-analytic LO–TO splitting of DFPT.

A real crystal has one value of $c$, and the crossover sits at the light-cone wave vector of §X.1, $q_c=\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega/c$ evaluated at $\omega_\sigma$, about $5.6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$ in GaAs, or $5\times10^{-5}$ of $2\pi/a$. Taking $c\to\infty$ first is the right description of essentially the whole Brillouin zone, which is why electrostatic DFPT works. The limit behind E Eq. 410 takes $q\to0$ at finite $c$ and describes the tiny region on the other side of $q_c$, the region that optics probes. §X.1 makes the same argument from the propagator side.

What the model leaves out

The figure keeps the electrons static, a constant $\epsilon_\infty$. That is the adiabatic approximation for electronic screening and is fine in a gapped crystal. With free carriers, $\Pi^{\rm e,0}$ acquires a Drude term, $D^{\rm e}$ acquires a plasmon pole near phonon energies, and the dynamical $D^{\rm e}$ gives coupled LO-phonon–plasmon modes (§B.9). The Dyson route has no Lorentz local field, unlike the classical model of §B.3, so TO equals the bare $\omega_\sigma$. hBN is uniaxial; using one $\epsilon_\infty$ is a simplification. Real polaritons also carry damping, which only the second tab includes.

Bridge to Giustino: where his LO–TO splitting lives

Giustino's theory has only the instantaneous Coulomb interaction, so it lives in the $\lambda_c\to\infty$ limit of the figure. His LO–TO splitting sits inside the adiabatic force constants $\Pi^{\rm A}$, as the Gonze–Lee non-analytic term with $Z^*$ and $\epsilon^\infty$ that he cites in §VI.A.3 (§A.4). EPP derives that term as a self-energy (E Eq. 454) and adds what electrostatics cannot see: phonon polaritons for $q\lesssim q_c$, and an isotropic $\bq=0$ limit.
