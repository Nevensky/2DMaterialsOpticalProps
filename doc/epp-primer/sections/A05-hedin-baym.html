Hedin–Baym: the lattice inside $W$

The $W$ of a GW calculation is the Coulomb interaction screened by electrons around clamped nuclei. Letting the nuclei move adds a phonon-mediated piece, $W_{\rm ph}=W_{\rm e}\mathcal DW_{\rm e}$, whose vertex is the screened gradient of the nuclear potential. Together with a Dyson equation for the phonons, this closes into the Hedin–Baym equations, the exact framework from which Giustino derives the phonon and electron self-energies used in practice (§A.6–§A.8).

Sections A.2–A.4 took the Hamiltonian (G.1) as given and computed its parameters with DFT and DFPT. Giustino §IV asks which exact theory those numbers approximate. The answer is a field theory of electrons and nuclei that interact only through the Coulomb potential, built with Schwinger's functional derivatives exactly as in Hedin's derivation of GW. Units are SI with $\hbar$ explicit. All potentials ($V_{\rm tot}$, $\varphi$, $V_\kappa$) are potential energies of an electron, and densities are counted in units of the electron charge, so $\hat n_{\rm e}\ge0$ while the nuclear density $\hat n_{\rm n}\le0$. Giustino writes both the nuclear density correlator and the phonon Green function as $D$, and the phonon self-energy as $\Pi$. On this page they are $\mathcal D$ and $\Pi^{\rm ph}$, which keeps them apart from EPP's photon propagator $D$ and photon self-energy $\Pi_{jj}$.

Physical picture · two screening clouds

Place a test charge in a polar crystal. Within a femtosecond the electrons rearrange and reduce its field by a factor $\epsilon^\infty$; that reduced interaction is the $W$ of a GW calculation. If the charge stays put for longer than a phonon period, about 0.1 ps, the ions shift too and the field drops further, to $1/\epsilon^0$ of its bare value. A moving charge therefore carries a fast electronic cloud and a slow ionic one. Hedin–Baym theory puts both clouds into one screened interaction and treats the ionic cloud as a propagating quantum object: the phonon.

One Hamiltonian, one instantaneous interaction

Electrons are indistinguishable fermions with field operators $\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$, $\mathbf x=(\br,\sigma)$. Nuclei in a solid can be labelled individually, so they are treated in first quantization through their displacements $\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}$ from equilibrium positions $\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}$, which are external parameters. Phonons appear later as quanta of these displacements. The most general nonrelativistic Hamiltonian is

$$\hat H=\hat T_{\rm e}+\hat T_{\rm n}+\hat U_{\rm ee}+\hat U_{\rm nn}+\hat U_{\rm en}, \tag{G.62}$$

with the kinetic energies $\hat T_{\rm e}$, $\hat T_{\rm n}$ and three Coulomb terms,

$$\hat U_{\rm ee}=\frac12\int d\br\int d\br'\,\hat n_{\rm e}(\br)\big[\hat n_{\rm e}(\br')-\delta(\br-\br')\big]v(\br,\br'), \tag{G.64}$$
$$\hat U_{\rm nn}=\frac12\sum_{\kappa'p'\neq\kappa p}Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa'}\,v\big(\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}+\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p},\,\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}+\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa'p'}\big), \tag{G.65}$$

and $\hat U_{\rm en}=\int d\br\,d\br'\,\hat n_{\rm e}(\br)\hat n_{\rm n}(\br')v(\br,\br')$ (G Eq. 66), with point nuclei

$$\hat n_{\rm n}(\br)=-\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\,\delta\big(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}-\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}\big). \tag{G.67}$$

Here $v(\br,\br')=e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0|\br-\br'|)$, $\hat n_{\rm e}=\sum_\sigma\hat\psi^\dagger\hat\psi$, and $Z_\kappa$ and $M_\kappa$ are the charge (in units of $e$) and mass of nucleus $\kappa$ in unit cell $p$. The $-\delta$ in (G.64) removes the self-interaction of the continuous electron charge, while the distinguishable nuclei in (G.65) are counted pair by pair. There is no vector potential, no current coupling and no photon. In the space-time notation used below, $1\equiv(\br_1\sigma_1t_1)$, $1^+$ is $1$ with $t_1+\eta$ ($\eta\to0^+$) and $\int d(34)\equiv\int d3\,d4$. The interaction is

$$v(12)=v(\br_1,\br_2)\,\delta(t_1-t_2),$$

so a charge moved at time $t_1$ is felt everywhere at $t_1$. Every bare interaction line in this section is this one. The screened lines $W_{\rm e}$ and $W$ are not instantaneous, because the screening clouds take time to form.

The electron Green function and its self-energy

The central object is the time-ordered Green function in the exact coupled electron–nuclear ground state $|0\rangle$, at zero temperature:

$$G(\mathbf xt,\mathbf x't')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle0|\hat T\hat\psi(\mathbf xt)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x't')|0\rangle. \tag{G.70}$$

To derive its equation of motion, Giustino adds a fictitious scalar potential $\varphi(\br t)$ that couples to the total charge, $\hat H_1(t)=\int d\br\,\hat n(\br)\varphi(\br t)$ with $\hat n=\hat n_{\rm e}+\hat n_{\rm n}$, and sets it to zero at the end. Electrons feel electrons and nuclei through the same $v$, so the potential they move in, and its response to $\varphi$, involve all charges:

$$V_{\rm tot}(1)=\int d2\,v(12)\langle\hat n(2)\rangle+\varphi(1), \tag{G.79}$$
$$\epsilon^{-1}(12)=\delta V_{\rm tot}(1)/\delta\varphi(2). \tag{G.80}$$

Equation (G.80) is the most general dielectric response: because $\langle\hat n\rangle$ contains the nuclei, $\epsilon^{-1}$ includes electronic and lattice screening. With the vertex $\Gamma(123)=-\delta G^{-1}(12)/\delta V_{\rm tot}(3)$ (G Eq. 83), the equation of motion takes the Dyson form (G.84), listed in Table I below, with

$$\Sigma(12)=i\hbar\int d(34)\,G(13)\Gamma(324)W(41^+), \tag{G.85}$$
$$W(12)=\int d3\,\epsilon^{-1}(13)v(32)=\int d(3)\,v(13)\epsilon^{-1}(23). \tag{G.86}$$

The $\hbar$ in (G.85) appears because $G$ carries $1/\hbar$; with $\hbar=1$ it is the familiar $\Sigma=iGW\Gamma$. The vertex obeys Hedin's integral equation (G.87). The structure is that of GW, with one difference hidden in (G.80): $W$ is screened by the lattice as well as by the electrons.

Derivation: from the equation of motion to $\Sigma=i\hbar G\Gamma W$

The Heisenberg equation for $\hat\psi$ (G Eq. 73) contains $\int d\br'\,v(\br,\br')\hat n(\br't)\hat\psi$ with the total density, so the equation for $G$ acquires a two-particle term $-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int d3\,v(13)\langle\hat T\hat n(3)\hat\psi(1)\hat\psi^\dagger(2)\rangle$ (G Eq. 74). Schwinger's identity, $\delta\langle\hat T\hat a\hat b\rangle/\delta\varphi(3)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\hat T[\hat n(3)-\langle\hat n(3)\rangle]\hat a\hat b\rangle$ (G Eq. 75), splits it into a mean-field part, which builds the Hartree potential of electrons and nuclei inside $V_{\rm tot}$, and a fluctuation part:

$$\Big[i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t_1}+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\rm e}}\nabla^2(1)-V_{\rm tot}(1)-i\hbar\int d3\,v(1^+3)\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi(3)}\Big]G(12)=\delta(12). \tag{G.78}$$

Now (G Eq. 81)

$$\frac{\delta G(12)}{\delta\varphi(3)}=-\int d(45)\,G(14)\,\frac{\delta G^{-1}(45)}{\delta\varphi(3)}\,G(52),$$

and the chain rule through $V_{\rm tot}$ (G Eq. 82) with (G.80) and (G.83) gives $\delta G^{-1}(45)/\delta\varphi(3)=-\int d6\,\Gamma(456)\epsilon^{-1}(63)$. The last term of (G.78) becomes

$$-i\hbar\int d(456)\,G(14)\Gamma(456)\Big[\int d3\,\epsilon^{-1}(63)v(31^+)\Big]G(52)=-\int d5\,\Sigma(15)G(52),$$

and the bracket is $W(61^+)$ by the first form of (G.86). Relabelling gives (G.85). The second form of (G.86), with $\epsilon^{-1}(23)$, is correct as printed: it uses $\delta\langle\hat n(1)\rangle/\delta\varphi(2)=\delta\langle\hat n(2)\rangle/\delta\varphi(1)$, so $W(12)=W(21)$. Inverting (G.84) and differentiating with respect to $V_{\rm tot}$ gives the vertex equation (G.87).

Screening by everything: $W=v+vPW$

Differentiating (G.79) with the chain rule through $\langle\hat n\rangle$ gives a Dyson equation for $W$ with the polarization propagator

$$P(12)=\frac{\delta\langle\hat n(1)\rangle}{\delta V_{\rm tot}(2)}, \tag{G.89}$$
$$W(12)=v(12)+\int d(34)\,v(13)P(34)W(42). \tag{G.90}$$

$P$ is irreducible with respect to $v$ because $V_{\rm tot}$ already contains the Hartree field, and $\epsilon=1-vP$ (G Eq. 91). Since $\hat n=\hat n_{\rm e}+\hat n_{\rm n}$, the polarization splits as $P=P_{\rm e}+P_{\rm n}$, with $P_{\rm n}\equiv\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle/\delta V_{\rm tot}$ (this page's notation). If the nuclei are classical point charges clamped at $\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}$, only the electrons respond:

$$P_{\rm e}(12)=\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(1)\rangle}{\delta V_{\rm tot}(2)}=-i\hbar\sum_{\sigma_1}\int d(34)\,G(13)G(41^+)\Gamma(342), \tag{G.92}$$
$$W_{\rm e}(12)=v(12)+\int d(34)\,v(13)P_{\rm e}(34)W_{\rm e}(42), \tag{G.94}$$

with $\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(1)\rangle=-i\hbar\sum_{\sigma_1}G(11^+)$ (G Eq. 93) and $\epsilon_{\rm e}=1-vP_{\rm e}$ (G Eq. 95). Equations (G.84)–(G.87) with $W\to W_{\rm e}$ are Hedin's equations (1965) for electrons in the field of clamped nuclei. With $\Gamma(123)=\delta(12)\delta(13)$ and $G$ replaced by the Kohn–Sham $G_0$, $P_{\rm e}=-i\hbar\sum_\sigma G(12)G(21^+)$ is the RPA $\chi^0$ and $W_{\rm e}$ is the $W$ of a GW or BSE calculation. In a gapped crystal at long wavelength and at phonon frequencies, $W_{\rm e}\simeq v/\epsilon^\infty$.

Bridge to EPP · your $W$ is $D^{\rm e}_{11}$ (§B.9)

EPP reaches $W_{\rm e}$ from the other side. At $c\to\infty$ only the Coulomb part of its electron-dressed photon propagator $D^{\rm e}$ (E.388) survives. Its scalar slot, written $D^{\rm e}_{11}$ (the index 1 labels that scalar, Coulomb slot, not the $x$ component), obeys (G.94) with $P_{\rm e}$ replaced by the RPA bubble without local fields or xc (E.435), and EPP names it $W^{\rm e}\equiv D^{\rm e}_{11}$ (E.437). So the $W$ of a GW code is one projection of EPP's photon propagator. §X.1 sets up the correspondence and §B.9 takes the limit step by step.

Letting the nuclei move: $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$

Now treat the electronic part exactly and the nuclear part on top of it. Subtracting (G.94) from (G.90) gives $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}P_{\rm n}W$, and since $P_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}=\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle/\delta\varphi$,

$$W(12)=W_{\rm e}(12)+\int d(34)\,W_{\rm e}(13)\frac{\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle}{\delta\varphi(4)}v(42). \tag{G.96}$$

The remaining step, which Giustino calls "a delicate passage", needs the nuclear response to a probe of all charges. A second probe $J$ that couples only to the nuclei, $\hat H_2(t)=\int d\br\,\hat n_{\rm n}(\br t)J(\br t)$, gives the reciprocity relation $\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle/\delta\varphi(2)=\delta\langle\hat n(2)\rangle/\delta J(1)$ (G Eq. 99): pushing on all charges and watching the nuclei is the same as pushing on the nuclei and watching all charges. When the nuclei move the electrons follow, so the total charge response is the nuclear response screened by $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$ (G Eq. 100). What is left is the nuclear density–density correlation function

$$\mathcal D(12)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\big\langle\hat T[\hat n_{\rm n}(1)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle][\hat n_{\rm n}(2)-\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(2)\rangle]\big\rangle, \tag{G.103}$$

in which the nuclei already interact through $W_{\rm e}$. Assembling the pieces gives the main result of Giustino §IV.B, first derived by Hedin and Lundqvist (1969):

$$W(12)=W_{\rm e}(12)+W_{\rm ph}(12), \tag{G.104}$$
$$W_{\rm ph}(12)=\int d(34)\,W_{\rm e}(13)\,\mathcal D(34)\,W_{\rm e}(24). \tag{G.105}$$
Derivation: from (G.96) to (G.105), and the argument order in (G.100)

By reciprocity (G.99), $\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle/\delta\varphi(4)=\delta\langle\hat n(4)\rangle/\delta J(3)$. The total density changes through the nuclei and through the electrons that respond to the nuclear potential $v\,\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}\rangle$. With $\chi_{\rm e}$ the reducible electronic density response,

$$\delta\langle\hat n(1)\rangle=\int d3\,\Big[\delta(13)+\int d5\,\chi_{\rm e}(15)v(53)\Big]\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle=\int d3\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(31)\,\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle,$$

using $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(31)=\delta(31)+\int d5\,v(35)\chi_{\rm e}(51)$, which is (G.80) restricted to electrons, and the symmetry of $\chi_{\rm e}$ and $v$. With (G.102), $\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(1)\rangle/\delta J(2)=\mathcal D(21)$, this gives $\delta\langle\hat n(4)\rangle/\delta J(3)=\int d5\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(54)\mathcal D(35)$. Inserting it into (G.96) and using $\int d4\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(54)v(42)=W_{\rm e}(52)$, the first form of (G.86) for electrons,

$$W(12)=W_{\rm e}(12)+\int d(35)\,W_{\rm e}(13)\,\mathcal D(35)\,W_{\rm e}(52),$$

which is (G.105) after relabelling and $W_{\rm e}(52)=W_{\rm e}(25)$. The argument order $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(31)$ is what makes the last step work; the printed (G.100) has $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)$. The two agree when $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$ is symmetric, and the final results (G.104)–(G.105) are correct as printed.

The vertex is a screened gradient of the nuclear potential

Phonons appear when the point-nucleus density (G.67) is expanded in the displacements. With $\delta(\br-\bu)=\delta(\br)-\bu\cdot\nabla\delta(\br)+\frac12\bu\cdot\nabla\nabla\delta(\br)\cdot\bu$ (G Eq. 106),

$$\begin{aligned}\hat n_{\rm n}(\br)=n^0_{\rm n}(\br)&+\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}\cdot\nabla\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\\&-\frac12\sum_{\kappa p}Z_\kappa\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}\cdot\nabla\nabla\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\cdot\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}.\end{aligned} \tag{G.107}$$

The first term is the static lattice of point charges. The second is a lattice of point dipoles $Z_\kappa\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}$, the seed of polar coupling (§A.4). The third smears each nucleus over its zero-point motion. Keeping the dipole term in each density fluctuation,

$$\mathcal D(12)=\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}Z_\kappa\nabla_{1,\alpha}\delta(\br_1-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\,\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t_1t_2)\,Z_{\kappa'}\nabla_{2,\alpha'}\delta(\br_2-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}), \tag{G.108}$$

with the displacement–displacement correlation function, the phonon Green function of this page,

$$\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(tt')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\big\langle\hat T\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}(t)\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t')\big\rangle. \tag{G.109}$$

Inserting (G.108) into (G.105), the delta-function gradients act on $W_{\rm e}=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}v$, and $Z_\kappa v(\br,\boldsymbol\tau)=-V_\kappa(\br-\boldsymbol\tau)$ turns them into gradients of the nuclear potential:

$$\begin{aligned}W_{\rm ph}(12)=\sum_{\substack{\kappa\alpha p\\ \kappa'\alpha'p'}}\int d(34)\,&\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)\nabla_{3,\alpha}V_\kappa(\br_3-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\\&\times\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(t_3t_4)\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(24)\nabla_{4,\alpha'}V_{\kappa'}(\br_4-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}).\end{aligned} \tag{G.110}$$

$V_\kappa$ is the bare Coulomb potential of the nucleus, $-Z_\kappa e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r)$, or its ionic pseudopotential. This is the central identification of Giustino §IV: the electron–phonon vertex is $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$, the field of a displaced nucleus screened by the full many-body dielectric matrix. The screening is dynamical, since $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)$ depends on $t_1-t_3$. Hedin and Lundqvist replaced it by its static limit, which is the Born–Oppenheimer approximation for the vertex. Giustino keeps the frequency dependence, following Bardeen and Pines (1955) and Baym (1961), and stresses that the harmonic truncation of (G.107) is the only approximation so far (but see the note on (G.108) at the end of this section).

In the mode basis of §A.6 the vertex becomes $g_{\bq\nu}(\br,\omega)=\int d\br'\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\br,\br',\omega)\,g^{\rm b}_{\bq\nu}(\br')$, with the bare coupling $g^{\rm b}_{\bq\nu}=\Delta_{\bq\nu}V^{\rm en}$, the change of the electron–nuclei potential per unit amplitude of phonon $\bq\nu$ (G Eqs. 142–143). The DFPT vertex (G.38) of §A.3 is its static RPA+xc approximation. Symbolically $W_{\rm ph}=g\,\mathcal D\,g^*$, with screened vertices at both ends; the phonon self-energy of §A.6 will instead carry one bare and one screened vertex.

Subtlety · which "retardation"

Here "retardation" means Giustino's frequency-dependent electronic screening; the speed of light is infinite throughout this section, because $v(12)$ is instantaneous. EPP's second meaning, the finite speed of light, is set out in §X.1.

A polar insulator: $W_{\rm ph}$ is the Fröhlich interaction

In a homogeneous polar insulator at long wavelength, (G.104) becomes scalar algebra. The electrons give $W_{\rm e}=v/\epsilon^\infty$, flat at phonon frequencies. The lattice adds a pole: $W=v/\epsilon(\omega)$ with $\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon^\infty(\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega^2)/(\omega_{\rm TO}^2-\omega^2)$, the form EPP derives classically (E.159). With the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller relation $\epsilon^0/\epsilon^\infty=\omega_{\rm LO}^2/\omega_{\rm TO}^2$, the difference is

$$\begin{aligned}W_{\rm ph}(\bq,\omega)&=v(\bq)\Big(\frac1{\epsilon^\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon^0}\Big)\frac{\omega_{\rm LO}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{\rm LO}^2}=\frac1\hbar\,|g(\bq)|^2\,\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\rm LO}(\omega),\\ |g(\bq)|^2&=v(\bq)\,\frac{\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}}{2}\Big(\frac1{\epsilon^\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon^0}\Big),\qquad v(\bq)=\frac{e^2}{\varepsilon_0\Omega q^2},\end{aligned}$$

with $v(\bq)$ per unit cell of volume $\Omega$ and $\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\rm LO}=2\omega_{\rm LO}/(\omega^2-\omega_{\rm LO}^2)$ the adiabatic LO propagator (G Eq. 132). $|g|^2$ is the square of Fröhlich's vertex (G.7) of §A.4, so the phonon part of the screened interaction is Fröhlich's coupling squared times the LO propagator: the long-wavelength content of (G.110). The model is not printed in Giustino; it follows from (G.104) and the LST relation. The figure evaluates it with damping.

Jellium with mobile ions: where the attraction comes from

In a simple metal the same algebra gives the effective interaction of Bardeen and Pines (1955), the work Giustino follows in keeping the frequency dependence of the screening. Treat the ions as a classical plasma of charge $Ze$, mass $M$ and density $n_{\rm ion}$ inside an electron gas with static dielectric function $\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)$. Their polarization is that of free charges, $vP_{\rm n}=\Omega_p^2/\omega^2$, with $\Omega_p^2=n_{\rm ion}Z^2e^2/(\varepsilon_0M)$, so $\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)-\Omega_p^2/\omega^2$ and

$$W(q,\omega)=\frac{v(q)}{\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)}\,\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-\omega_q^2},\qquad W_{\rm ph}(q,\omega)=\frac{v(q)}{\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)}\,\frac{\omega_q^2}{\omega^2-\omega_q^2},\qquad \omega_q^2=\frac{\Omega_p^2}{\epsilon_{\rm e}(q)}.$$

Electronic screening turns the ion plasma oscillation into an acoustic phonon, $\omega_q\to\Omega_pq/k_{\rm TF}$ at small $q$, with $k_{\rm TF}$ the Thomas–Fermi wavevector (Bohm–Staver sound; the dispersion and its Kohn anomaly are in §A.6). Below $\omega_q$ the total interaction is negative: electrons that exchange less energy than a phonon attract each other, because the slow ionic cloud overscreens them. At $\omega\to0$ the ions screen perfectly and $W\to0$. This frequency-dependent attraction, retarded in Giustino's sense because the ions respond late, is the pairing interaction of Eliashberg theory (§A.9).

Phonons are nuclei interacting through $W_{\rm e}$

The phonon propagator needs its own equation of motion. Newton's equation for $\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\kappa p}$ (G Eq. 112) has the Coulomb force of the electrons and of all other nuclei on its right-hand side, with no Lorentz force and no radiation. A third probe, an external force $\mathbf F_{\kappa p}(t)$ coupled to the displacement, generates $\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=\delta\langle\Delta\hat\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\rangle/\delta F_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}$ (G Eq. 113), and differentiating Newton's equation gives a Dyson-like equation (G Eq. 116). In frequency space, with $f(\omega)=\int dt\,f(t)e^{i\omega t}$,

$$\sum_{\kappa''\alpha''p''}\big[M_\kappa\omega^2\delta_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa''\alpha''p''}-\Pi^{\rm ph}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa''\alpha''p''}(\omega)\big]\mathcal D_{\kappa''\alpha''p'',\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\delta_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}, \tag{G.118}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ph}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\int d\br\,d\br'\,\big[&Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)\\&+\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}\nabla_\alpha\langle\hat n(\br)\rangle v(\br,\br')\big]Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'_{\alpha'}\delta(\br'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}).\end{aligned} \tag{G.119}$$

The first term couples two point nuclei through the electron-screened $W_{\rm e}(\omega)$. The second is Baym's "static force", from the equilibrium charge density. Holding the crystal fixed in the laboratory frame imposes the many-body acoustic sum rule

$$\sum_{\kappa'p'}\Pi^{\rm ph}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega=0)=0\quad\text{for any }\alpha,\alpha', \tag{G.120}$$

and with it the self-energy takes its compact form:

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ph}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\sum_{\kappa''p''}Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa''}&\frac{\partial^2}{\partial r_\alpha\partial r'_{\alpha'}}\bigg|_{\br=\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p},\,\br'=\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa''p''}}\\&\times\big[\delta_{\kappa'p',\kappa''p''}W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)-\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',0)\big].\end{aligned} \tag{G.121}$$
Derivation: from (G.119) and (G.120) to (G.121)

Integrate the first term of (G.119) by parts twice. Each $\nabla\delta$ moves onto $W_{\rm e}$ with a minus sign, so the term is $A_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}(\omega)\equiv Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa'}\,\partial^2W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)/\partial r_\alpha\partial r'_{\alpha'}$ at $\br=\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}$, $\br'=\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}$ (Cartesian indices implied). The static term is local in the nucleus, $\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}S_{\kappa p}$, and does not depend on $\omega$. The sum rule (G.120) at $\omega=0$ fixes it: $S_{\kappa p}=-\sum_{\kappa''p''}A_{\kappa p,\kappa''p''}(0)$. Hence

$$\Pi^{\rm ph}_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}(\omega)=A_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}(\omega)-\delta_{\kappa p,\kappa'p'}\sum_{\kappa''p''}A_{\kappa p,\kappa''p''}(0),$$

which is (G.121). On the diagonal, $\kappa'p'=\kappa p$, the first term and the $\kappa''p''=\kappa p$ part of the sum combine into $Z_\kappa^2\partial\partial'[W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)]$ at coincident points, and the rest of the sum, $-\sum_{\kappa''p''\neq\kappa p}A_{\kappa p,\kappa''p''}(0)$, is the static restoring force from all the other nuclei. The singular bare $v$, a nucleus interacting with itself, cancels in that difference because it does not depend on frequency.

So the phonon self-energy is the mixed second derivative of the electron-screened interaction between two point nuclei, and its frequency dependence is that of $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\omega)$. The second term of (G.121) is the "self" force constant that enforces the sum rule. At $\omega=0$, (G.121) gives the Born–Oppenheimer force constants of DFPT, dressed by the exact $\epsilon_{\rm e}(0)$; §A.6 starts from this split, $\Pi^{\rm ph}=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)$, and solves (G.118). The quadratic term of (G.107) survives in the mean nuclear density,

$$\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(\br t)\rangle=n^0_{\rm n}(\br)-\frac{i\hbar}{2}\sum_{\kappa p,\alpha\alpha'}Z_\kappa\frac{\partial^2\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})}{\partial r_\alpha\partial r_{\alpha'}}\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa\alpha'p}(t^+t), \tag{G.122}$$

a nucleus smeared by its zero-point motion, since $i\hbar\mathcal D(t^+t)=\langle\Delta\hat\tau_\alpha\Delta\hat\tau_{\alpha'}\rangle$. It enters $V_{\rm tot}$ and produces the Debye–Waller self-energy of §A.7.

Bridge to EPP · one Dyson equation instead of two (§X.1, §B.7)

Giustino needs two coupled Dyson equations, (G.90) for $W$ and (G.118) for $\mathcal D$. EPP needs one, $D=D^0+D^0\Pi_{jj}D$ (E.379), for the propagator of the total electromagnetic field, with current–current self-energies in place of density responses. §X.1 maps the two structures term by term, and §B.7 derives the phonon self-energy that replaces (G.121).

The Hedin–Baym equations

Equations (G.79), (G.84), (G.85), (G.87), (G.92)–(G.95), (G.110), (G.118), (G.121) and (G.122) close on themselves. Giustino collects them in his Table I and calls them the Hedin–Baym equations: the electronic ones are Hedin's (1965), the nuclear ones Baym's (1961). They hold at zero temperature in the harmonic approximation.

Three properties matter. The set is nonperturbative: it fixes the exact propagators self-consistently, not order by order in a coupling constant. It does not assume Born–Oppenheimer: $\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega)$ and the vertex keep their frequency dependence, so the same equations cover insulators, doped semiconductors and metals. And setting $\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=0$ removes $W_{\rm ph}$, $\Pi^{\rm ph}$ and the zero-point smearing, which returns Hedin's equations for clamped nuclei. The price is the harmonic approximation; going beyond it (Gillis 1970) is formidable. Table I contains no symbol $g$. The coupling lives in $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\nabla V_\kappa$ inside (G.110) and in $Z_\kappa\nabla\delta$ contracted with $W_{\rm e}$ in (G.121), which is why Giustino calls the electronic dielectric matrix $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)$ "absolutely central". Bridge to EPP · what gets replaced (§X.1)

Every bare Coulomb line in Table I is the instantaneous $v(12)$, coupled to charge densities. EPP keeps it as the longitudinal photon, adds the transverse photon and couples both to currents (§X.1, §B.4). For $c\to\infty$ it recovers $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}\mathcal DW_{\rm e}$, but only for the dipolar, Born-charge part of the vertex, since its Lagrangian has no short-range coupling (§B.9).

Note on the source · G Eqs. 100, 108 and 127

(G.100). The total-density response should read $\int d3\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(31)\,\delta\langle\hat n_{\rm n}(3)\rangle/\delta J(2)$, not $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(13)$. Only this order turns (G.96) into (G.105) (see the derivation above). The orders coincide for a symmetric $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$, and (G.104), (G.105), (G.110) are correct as printed. The same caveat applies to the first term of (G.115).

(G.108). The text says the harmonic truncation of (G.107) is the sole approximation, but (G.108) also keeps only the linear term of (G.107) in each density fluctuation. The dropped cross terms are three- and four-displacement correlators, i.e. two-phonon processes, so (G.108) and (G.110) are one-phonon expressions.

(G.127). The identity used in §A.6 to reach the DFPT force constants (G.128) is printed as

$$\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\br)\rangle}{\partial\tau^0_{\kappa\alpha p}}=-Z_\kappa\int d\br'\,\big[\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\br,\br';0)-\delta(\br,\br')\big]\nabla'_\alpha\delta(\br'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p}).$$

It should carry $+Z_\kappa$ and $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\br',\br;0)$. Check: for perfect screening, $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}\to0$, the electrons must follow the nucleus, $\partial n_{\rm e}/\partial\tau_\alpha=-Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})$, which the corrected sign gives. With the printed sign, (G.126) would yield $Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa'}\partial\partial'(2v-W_{\rm e})$ instead of (G.121).

Giustino also uses the letter $D$ for the nuclear density correlator (G.103), the displacement correlator (G.109), its matrix $\mathbf D(\omega)$ and the dynamical matrix $D^{\rm dm}$; the arguments tell them apart. Strictly, he notes, "phonon Green function" should be reserved for $-(i/\hbar)\langle\hat T\hat a_{\bq\nu}\hat a^\dagger_{\bq'\nu'}\rangle$.
