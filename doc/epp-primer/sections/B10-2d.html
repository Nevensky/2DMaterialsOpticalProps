Two-dimensional crystals: no LO–TO splitting at Γ

A polar monolayer talks to the electromagnetic field only at $z=0$. By the end you can derive EPP's 2D phonon self-energy, reduce it to the strictly two-dimensional screened interaction $W^{\rm e,2D}=v_Q/\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}$, and explain why the LO branch then starts at the TO frequency and rises linearly, with the same screening length $r_0$ that sets Rytova–Keldysh exciton binding in 2D BSE.

Section B.9 derived the bulk electrostatic result, E Eq. 454. With static electronic screening, $W^{\rm e}(\bq,0)=4\pi/(\bq\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\bq)$, the factor $q^2$ in $|\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2$ cancels against the Coulomb kernel:

$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\,\frac{|\hat\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma(\bq)|^2}{\hat\bq\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\hat\bq}\qquad(\text{3D, cf. E.454})$$

The shift depends on the direction of $\bq$ but not on its length, so it survives at $\Gamma$. That is the LO–TO splitting. EPP §8 repeats the construction for a single polar layer. Units stay Gaussian, so a sheet has the Coulomb kernel $v_Q=2\pi/Q$. Born charges carry the dimension of charge, $\bQ=(Q_x,Q_y)$ is an in-plane wave vector, and all symbols are EPP's own: nothing is renamed in this section.

Physical picture: where does the depolarization field go?

An LO phonon is a polarization wave, $P\propto\cos Qx$, along its own wave vector. Its bound charge $-\partial_xP$ piles up where the dipoles converge. In bulk these charges form infinite planes, and the field between charged planes does not depend on their spacing: it is the capacitor field $-4\pi P/\epsilon_\infty$ at every wavelength, which stiffens the LO mode equally for every $q$. In a monolayer the bound charge sits on a line inside the sheet. Its field lines bulge out into the vacuum or the substrate, to a distance of about $1/Q$, before closing on the opposite charge. The longer the wavelength, the weaker the in-plane field. At $Q\to0$ only the short-range springs remain, so LO and TO are degenerate.

A sheet of point dipoles

EPP builds the ionic photon self-energy from Born-charge point dipoles on the atomic sites (§B.5). The printed draft puts every dipole of a monolayer in the plane $z=0$ and at its cell origin. The correction (§Z.3) places atom $a$ at its in-plane position $\boldsymbol\rho^0_a$ and height $z_a$. Repeating the lattice sums with 2D wave vectors (EPP pp. 64 and 66–67) then gives paramagnetic and diamagnetic parts confined to the atomic planes, as matrices in the in-plane reciprocal-lattice vectors $\bG,\bG'$ (as in §Z.3, a primed tag marks a corrected equation and a page tag such as p.101′ a corrected unnumbered display):

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')={}&-\frac{\hbar}{cS_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega_\sigma(\bQ)}{2}F_\sigma(\bQ,\omega)\\&\times\sum_{ab}j^a_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ+\bG)\,j^{b*}_{\beta\sigma}(\bQ+\bG')\,\delta(z-z_a)\,\delta(z'-z_b)\end{aligned}\tag{E.300$'$}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')={}&-\frac{1}{S_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\\&\times e^{-i(\bG-\bG')\cdot\boldsymbol\rho^0_a}\,\delta(z-z_a)\,\delta(z'-z_a)\end{aligned}\tag{E.305$'$}$$

The atom-resolved vertex $j^a_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ+\bG)=\sum_\mu Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\bQ)\,e^{-i(\bQ+\bG)\cdot\boldsymbol\rho^0_a}/\sqrt{m_a}$ carries the in-plane basis phase. For a planar sheet, $z_a=0$, the atom sums in E.300′ collapse to $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ+\bG)\,j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\bQ+\bG')\,\delta(z)\delta(z')$ with $j=\sum_aj^a$. At $\bG=\bG'=0$, the only element EPP keeps, E.300′ and E.305′ are then the printed E.300 and E.305, with the phase-corrected vertex $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ)=\sum_{a\mu}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}e_{a\mu\sigma}(\bQ)\,e^{-i\bQ\cdot\boldsymbol\rho^0_a}/\sqrt{m_a}$ (E p.86a′ with in-plane positions). Everything below is written for a planar sheet with this $j$, and E Eqs. 455–472 keep their printed form. The diamagnetic term cancels the static paramagnetic one exactly as in 3D, by completeness of the phonon eigenvectors (E Eqs. 306–310). The strictly 2D electronic bubble of a layer (E Eq. 285) has the same sheet form. The ionic self-energy of the sheet is therefore the bulk one, renormalized from volume to area:

$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')=\frac{\Omega_{\rm uc}}{S_{\rm uc}}\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}(\bQ,\omega)\,\delta(z)\delta(z')\tag{E.455}$$

Here $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}(\bQ,\omega)$ is the bare ionic bubble of E Eq. 391, which carries $1/\Omega_{\rm uc}$; the prefactor replaces it by $1/S_{\rm uc}$. The δ functions pin both ends of every ionic bubble to $z=0$. The resummation of §B.7 therefore goes through with two replacements (E Eqs. 456–457): $D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\to D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0)$ and $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}(\bq,\omega)\to(\Omega_{\rm uc}/S_{\rm uc})\,\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}(\bQ,\omega)$. The phonon self-energy of a 2D crystal is

$$\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bQ,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0)\,J_{\beta\sigma'}(\bQ,\omega),\tag{E.458}$$
$$J_{\mu\sigma}(\bQ,\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2cS_{\rm uc}\,\omega_\sigma(\bQ)}}\;\omega\,j_{\mu\sigma}(\bQ).\tag{E.459}$$

The real change is in the photon line. It is evaluated in the plane of the sheet and obeys a $z$-resolved Dyson equation (unnumbered, EPP p. 100), in which the electronic bubble may spread over the thickness of the layer:

$$\begin{aligned}D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')&=D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')\\&\quad+\sum_{\mu\nu}\int_{-l/2}^{l/2}dz_1dz_2\,D^0_{\alpha\mu}(\bQ,\omega,z,z_1)\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\mu\nu}(\bQ,\omega,z_1,z_2)\,D^{\rm e}_{\nu\beta}(\bQ,\omega,z_2,z')\end{aligned}$$

This is where crystal local-field effects along $z$ enter, the same effects that a supercell RPA calculation of a monolayer has to handle.

Subtlety: “Ω → S” is not enough, and out-of-plane dipoles are dropped

EPP p. 86 calls the transition to 2D trivial: $\Omega\to S$, $\Omega_{\rm uc}\to S_{\rm uc}$, $\bq\to\bQ$. That holds for the self-energies, which carry $\delta(z)\delta(z')$, but not for the photon line. A sheet exchanges photons with itself only at $z=z'=0$, so it sees the vacuum propagator of E Eq. 375 integrated over $q_z$ (derived, not printed in EPP), for in-plane $\mu,\nu$:

$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bQ,\omega;0,0)=\int\frac{dq_z}{2\pi}\,D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bQ,q_z,\omega)=\frac{2\pi c}{\omega^2\beta}\left(\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\mu\nu}-Q_\mu Q_\nu\right),\qquad\beta=\sqrt{Q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}}$$

Its longitudinal projection is $-2\pi c\beta/\omega^2$, which tends to $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}Q^2v_Q$ for $c\to\infty$. The kernel $v_Q=2\pi/Q$ thus comes from the $q_z$ integral, not from a change of normalization. The $zz$ component contains a contact term $\propto\delta(z-z')$, the depolarization field of a sheet of out-of-plane dipoles. EPP discards it as the “divergent local term” before E Eq. 463 and keeps only $\alpha=x,y$ from there on, although E Eq. 300′ contains out-of-plane dipoles. Everything below applies to in-plane modes (the in-plane LO/TO pair of hBN or the E′ mode of MoS₂), and everything except the last subsection and its figure tab is electrostatic. ZO-type modes with $Z^*_{zz}$ receive a $Q$-independent depolarization shift instead.

The electrostatic limit: a strictly 2D screened interaction

For $c\to\infty$ only the longitudinal sector survives, as in bulk. The partial Fourier transform of E Eq. 438 gives

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')\equiv-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\left[i\bQ+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right]\left[-i\bQ+\frac{\partial}{\partial z'}\right]W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,z,z').\tag{E.460}$$

The brackets are the 3-vectors $(iQ_x,iQ_y,\partial_z)$ and $(-iQ_x,-iQ_y,\partial_{z'})$, of which components $\mu$ and $\nu$ are taken. EPP then switches off the electronic local fields along $z$ and squeezes the electronic bubble into the sheet, $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')\approx\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,\omega)\delta(z)\delta(z')$, with the 2D RPA polarizability (unnumbered, EPP p. 101, here corrected; SBZ is the surface Brillouin zone):

$$\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,\omega)=\frac{2e^2}{S}\sum_{\mathbf K\in{\rm SBZ}}\sum_{nm}\frac{f_{n\mathbf K}-f_{m\mathbf K+\bQ}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\mathbf K}-E_{m\mathbf K+\bQ}+i\eta}\,\left|\rho_{n\mathbf K,m\mathbf K+\bQ}\right|^2\tag{E p.101$'$}$$

The printed display has $2/S$ in place of $2e^2/S$. With the dimensionless number-density vertices $\rho_{n\mathbf K,m\mathbf K+\bQ}$ it is the number bubble, while E Eqs. 461–462 and 470 need the charge–charge correlator, $e^2$ times it (cf. E Eq. 350 and §B.9). It is the same lost factor $e$ as in the continuity equation of §B.8 (§Z.3).

Summing the RPA chain of sheet bubbles gives the screened Coulomb interaction between heights $z$ and $z'$:

$$W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,z,z')=v_Q\,e^{-Q|z-z'|}+v_Q^2\,e^{-Q(|z|+|z'|)}\,\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,\omega),\tag{E.461}$$
$$\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}=\frac{\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}}{1-v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}},\tag{E.462}$$

with $v_Q=2\pi/Q$. The first term is the bare Coulomb interaction after the in-plane Fourier transform. The second is the potential of the charge induced in the sheet, which decays away from it as $e^{-Q|z|}$. For in-plane components no $z$-derivatives appear in E Eq. 460, and at $z=z'=0$ (E Eq. 463)

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0)\equiv-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,Q_\mu Q_\nu\,W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0);\qquad\mu,\nu=x,y.\tag{E.464}$$

Inserted into E Eq. 458 with the vertices of E Eq. 459 (this is E Eq. 465), it gives

$$\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}(\bQ,\omega)=\frac{\hbar\,W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0)}{2S_{\rm uc}\,\omega_\sigma(\bQ)}\left|\sum_{\alpha=x,y}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ)\,Q_\alpha\right|^2.\tag{E.466}$$

The 2D phonon propagator keeps the bulk form of E Eq. 405 (E Eq. 467), so the renormalized frequencies solve

$$\omega^2=\omega^2_\sigma(\bQ)+\frac{W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0)}{S_{\rm uc}}\left|\sum_{\alpha=x,y}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ)\,Q_\alpha\right|^2.\tag{E.468}$$

In the plane, E Eqs. 461–462 collapse to the form every 2D RPA practitioner knows:

$$W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,z=0,z'=0)\equiv W^{\rm e,2D}(\bQ,\omega)=\frac{v_Q}{\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,\omega)},\tag{E.469}$$
$$\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,\omega)=1-v_Q\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,\omega).\tag{E.470}$$

With the bubble of E p.101′ and $v_Q=2\pi/Q$, E Eq. 470 is dimensionless (with the printed $2/S$ it is not). For a 2D electron gas it gives Stern's $\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(Q,0)=1+2m^*e^2/(\hbar^2Q)$, valid for $Q\le2k_F$ at $T\to0$ without a background dielectric constant. With a static electronic response, $\omega=0$ inside $W$ (E Eq. 471), EPP's final result is

$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\bQ)=\omega^2_\sigma(\bQ)+\frac{1}{S_{\rm uc}}\,\frac{v_Q}{\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,\omega=0)}\left|\sum_{\alpha=x,y}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bQ)\,Q_\alpha\right|^2.\tag{E.472}$$
Derivation: from E.461 to E.469, and from E.465 to E.468

The in-plane screened interaction. At $z=z'=0$ both exponentials in E Eq. 461 equal 1. With E Eq. 462,

$$W^{\rm e}(\bQ,\omega,0,0)=v_Q+v_Q^2\,\frac{\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}}{1-v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}}=v_Q\,\frac{1-v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}+v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}}{1-v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}}=\frac{v_Q}{1-v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}},$$

which is E Eqs. 469–470. E Eq. 461 is the solution of $W=v+v\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}W$ when the bubble is a δ function in $z$ and $v(\bQ,z,z')=v_Qe^{-Q|z-z'|}$ is the in-plane transform of $1/|\br-\br'|$.

The self-energy. E Eq. 465 is E Eq. 458 for $\sigma=\sigma'$ with E Eq. 459 inserted: $J^*_{\alpha\sigma}J_{\beta\sigma}=\frac{\hbar\omega^2}{2cS_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}j^*_{\alpha\sigma}j_{\beta\sigma}$. Now insert E Eq. 464:

$$\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}=-\frac{\hbar\omega^2}{2cS_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}\sum_{\alpha\beta=x,y}j^*_{\alpha\sigma}\left(-\frac{c}{\omega^2}Q_\alpha Q_\beta W^{\rm e}\right)j_{\beta\sigma}=\frac{\hbar W^{\rm e}}{2S_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma}\Big|\sum_{\alpha}j_{\alpha\sigma}Q_\alpha\Big|^2 .$$

The factors $\omega^2$ and $c$ cancel, as in bulk (E Eq. 452). The poles of E Eq. 467 satisfy $\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+2\omega_\sigma\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}/\hbar$, which gives E Eq. 468.

The 3D check. The same steps with $\Omega_{\rm uc}$, $v(q)=4\pi/q^2$ and $\epsilon_\infty$ give the bulk shift at the top of this section. Only the Coulomb kernel and the cell measure differ.

Why LO meets TO at Γ

Put the two results side by side, for a mode with in-plane dipole $\mathbf j_\sigma$:

$$\begin{aligned}\text{3D:}\quad&\tilde\omega^2_\sigma-\omega^2_\sigma=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\,\frac{q^2\,|\hat\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2}{q^2\;\hat\bq\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\hat\bq}\;\xrightarrow{\;q\to0\;}\;\text{const},\\[4pt]\text{2D:}\quad&\tilde\omega^2_\sigma-\omega^2_\sigma=\frac{2\pi}{S_{\rm uc}}\,\frac{Q^2\,|\hat\bQ\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2}{Q\;\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,0)}\;\xrightarrow{\;Q\to0\;}\;0.\end{aligned}$$

The vertex $|\bQ\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2$ supplies $Q^2$ in both cases. The 3D kernel removes $q^2$; the sheet kernel removes only one power of $Q$. For an insulator $\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,0)$ tends to a finite value as $Q\to0$, so the LO branch leaves the TO frequency with a finite slope (derived from E Eq. 472):

$$\hbar\,\frac{d\tilde\omega_{\rm LO}}{dQ}\bigg|_{Q=0}=\frac{\pi\hbar\,|\hat\bQ\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma|^2}{S_{\rm uc}\,\omega_\sigma\,\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(0,0)}$$

TO modes, with $\hat\bQ\cdot\mathbf j_\sigma=0$, are not shifted in either case. The first figure shows the same counting in real space: without electronic screening the 2D shift is the 3D one times $Qd/2$, for a layer of thickness $d$ with $\Omega_{\rm uc}=S_{\rm uc}d$.

Agreement with Sohier et al. 2017 is in form

EPP's comparisons with Sohier et al., Nano Lett. 17, 3758 (2017) (E Eq. 454 "probably identical" to their Eq. 2, E Eq. 472 "consistent with" their Eq. 3) are agreements in form, not term-by-term identities; see the note in §B.9. Two points from the literature, not from EPP: Sohier et al. take their screening from DFPT, which includes local fields and exchange–correlation, rather than the strictly 2D RPA of E Eq. 470; and in a 3D-periodic supercell the periodic images restore a spurious splitting unless the Coulomb interaction is truncated in $z$.

Bridge to Giustino

Giustino's treatment of polar coupling is three-dimensional. The LO–TO splitting it points to (the Gonze–Lee nonanalytic term, cited in Giustino §VI.A.3 but not printed) carries $\epsilon^\infty$, and its long-range vertex is the Verdi–Giustino form $\propto(\bq\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa\nu})/(\bq\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\bq)$ (Giustino §VI.A.3, Eq. G.180; §A.4). Both rest on the 3D kernel $4\pi/q^2$. EPP's 2D result follows from the single replacement $4\pi/(\bq\cdot\epsilon_\infty\cdot\bq)\to2\pi/[Q\,\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,0)]$. Applied to the electrostatic vertex of §B.8, the same replacement turns the $1/q$ Fröhlich divergence into a coupling that tends to a finite value, $\propto\hat\bQ\cdot Z^*\cdot\mathbf e/\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(\bQ,0)$, as $Q\to0$ (derived, not in EPP). In Giustino's language (his phonon self-energy is written $\Pi^{\rm ph}$ on this page), E Eq. 472 is the long-range part of the adiabatic phonon self-energy $\Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega=0)$ (Eq. G.125, §A.6), the part DFPT adds through $Z^*$ and $\epsilon^\infty$, now evaluated with a two-dimensional $W_{\rm e}$.

The same $r_0$ that sets your exciton binding

For a gapped layer the static sheet bubble is quadratic at small $Q$, $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,0)\approx-\alpha_{\rm 2D}Q^2$, where $\alpha_{\rm 2D}$ is the in-plane sheet polarizability (a length in Gaussian units, with the $e^2$ of E p.101′). E Eq. 470 then gives $\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}=1+r_0Q$ with $r_0=2\pi\alpha_{\rm 2D}$, and $W^{\rm e,2D}=2\pi/[Q(1+r_0Q)]$ is the Rytova–Keldysh interaction of 2D BSE and Wannier-exciton models. A substrate below and a capping layer above, with dielectric constants $\epsilon_{\rm a}$ and $\epsilon_{\rm b}$, add the environment. This is standard electrostatics and a literature parameterization; EPP itself treats a freestanding sheet:

$$\epsilon_{\rm 2D}(Q)=\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q,\qquad\epsilon_{\rm env}=\tfrac12(\epsilon_{\rm a}+\epsilon_{\rm b})$$

Some papers write $\epsilon_{\rm env}(1+r_0'Q)$ with $r_0'=r_0/\epsilon_{\rm env}$. For a diatomic in-plane mode $|\hat\bQ\cdot\mathbf j_{\rm LO}|^2=Z^{*2}/\mu$, and E Eq. 472 becomes

$$\begin{aligned}(\hbar\tilde\omega_{\rm LO})^2&=(\hbar\omega_{\rm T})^2+\frac{C\,Q}{\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q},\\ C&=\frac{2\pi e^2Z^{*2}\hbar^2}{S_{\rm uc}\,\mu}=0.3782\,\frac{Z^{*2}}{S_{\rm uc}[\text{Å}^2]\;\mu[\text{u}]}\ \text{eV}^2\,\text{Å},\end{aligned}$$

with $Z^*$ in units of $|e|$ and $e^2=14.40$ eV Å. Two regimes meet at $Q^*=\epsilon_{\rm env}/r_0$:

  • $Q\ll Q^*$: a linear rise with slope $C/(2\hbar\omega_{\rm T}\epsilon_{\rm env})$. Encapsulation divides the slope by $\epsilon_{\rm env}$.
  • $Q\gg Q^*$: the sheet's own electrons dominate the screening, and $(\hbar\tilde\omega_{\rm LO})^2\to(\hbar\omega_{\rm T})^2+C/r_0$. This plateau does not depend on the environment.

These are the two regimes of the Keldysh potential in real space: $1/(\epsilon_{\rm env}r)$ for $r\gg r_0/\epsilon_{\rm env}$, logarithmic inside. An exciton of radius $a_X$ samples $W$ around $Q\sim1/a_X$; an LO phonon of wave vector $Q$ samples the same function at the same $Q$. If your BSE workflow already gives you $r_0$, the LO dispersion needs only $Z^*$ and the mode eigenvector on top. With hBN numbers ($S_{\rm uc}=5.43$ Å², $Z^*=2.7$, $\mu=6.10$ u, $\hbar\omega_{\rm T}=170$ meV, $r_0=10$ Å; literature values, illustrative) one finds $C=0.0832$ eV² Å, an initial slope of 0.245 eV Å, $\hbar\tilde\omega_{\rm LO}=181.8$ meV at $Q=0.1$ Å$^{-1}$, and a plateau at 193 meV, close to the bulk LO near 200 meV.

Why the plateau looks like bulk, and where the model stops

A layer of thickness $d$ cut from a bulk crystal has $r_0\approx d(\epsilon_\infty-1)/2$ (a common literature estimate). Then $C/r_0=4\pi|\mathbf j|^2/[\Omega_{\rm uc}(\epsilon_\infty-1)]$ with $\Omega_{\rm uc}=S_{\rm uc}d$: the plateau is the bulk splitting of E Eq. 454 with $\epsilon_\infty-1$ in place of $\epsilon_\infty$ (derived). It is also where the strictly 2D model is least trustworthy, because $Qd$ approaches 1 and the finite thickness neglected in E Eq. 461 starts to matter. EPP's $W$ is RPA without local fields or exchange–correlation; a production calculation would use the macroscopic, $z$-truncated $\epsilon$ of the layer.

Doped sheets: plasmon–phonon modes in one sample

E Eq. 471 sets $\omega=0$ inside $W$. As in bulk (§B.9), that fails when free carriers put a plasmon near the phonon. Keep the dynamical $W$ of E Eq. 468 and describe the carriers by a Drude sheet, $\epsilon_{\rm 2D}(Q,\omega)=\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q-\omega_{\rm pl}^2(Q)/\omega^2$ with $\omega_{\rm pl}^2=2\pi ne^2Q/m^*$ (Gaussian, $n$ per area). E Eq. 468 becomes a quadratic in $\omega^2$ (derived):

$$(\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q)\,\omega^4-\omega^2\Big[(\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0Q)\,\omega_{\rm T}^2+\omega_{\rm pl}^2+C'Q\Big]+\omega_{\rm pl}^2\,\omega_{\rm T}^2=0,\qquad C'=C/\hbar^2$$

The 2D plasmon rises as $\sqrt Q$ from zero, so a single doped sample shows the whole anticrossing as a function of $Q$, where bulk GaAs needs a series of densities. At small $Q$ the upper branch is LO-like and starts at $\omega_{\rm T}$. Beyond the crossing it turns plasmon-like, and the lower branch is pushed below $\omega_{\rm T}$: below the plasmon frequency the carriers overscreen, $\epsilon_{\rm 2D}\lt0$, which reverses the sign of the shift. Even the weakly polar MoS₂-like sheet, whose undoped LO shift is a fraction of a meV, shows an anticrossing of about 4 meV: at resonance the splitting grows as the square root of the coupling, $\sqrt{C'Q/\epsilon_{\rm 2D}}$, not linearly. The Drude form is the local limit of the 2D Lindhard function. It holds while the plasmon lies well above the intraband electron–hole continuum, $\omega\gg v_FQ$, and it drops both the $(v_FQ/\omega)^2$ correction to the plasmon dispersion and Landau damping. At the crossing of both presets $\hbar v_FQ\lesssim0.4\,\hbar\omega_{\rm T}$ (our estimate), so the curves are qualitatively right there.

Finite $c$: when the sheet radiates

Keeping $c$ finite (retardation in EPP's sense) means keeping $\beta$ in the sheet propagator. For a sheet inside a homogeneous medium $\epsilon_{\rm env}$, the longitudinal pole condition gives (derived with the same $q_z$ integral, now in the medium; not in EPP)

$$\omega^2=\omega_{\rm T}^2+\frac{C'\beta}{\epsilon_{\rm env}+r_0\beta},\qquad\beta=\sqrt{Q^2-\epsilon_{\rm env}\,\omega^2/c^2}.$$

Outside the light cone $\beta\lt Q$, and the shift falls to zero at the light line. Inside it $\beta$ is imaginary and the in-plane polar modes radiate. At $Q=0$ the radiative width (FWHM) is $\hbar\gamma_0=C/(\hbar c\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm env}})$, about 42 μeV for freestanding hBN. It varies as $\sqrt{1-x^2}$ for the p-polarized, LO-like mode and as $1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ for the s-polarized, TO-like mode, with $x=cQ/(\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm env}}\,\omega_{\rm T})$. The same two laws govern the radiative widths of in-plane 2D excitons (literature, quantum-well excitons). All of it happens at $Q\lesssim10^{-4}$ Å$^{-1}$, where the electrostatic shift is itself only tens of μeV. The smallness of this window is why E Eq. 472 is safe for phonon physics.

Note on the source

E Eq. 455 is introduced as “your $\Pi^{\rm ion,0}_{jj}$” but written without the superscript 0; both it and E Eq. 391, to which it refers, are the bare ionic bubble (notation switch of EPP p. 87). The “divergent local term” dropped before E Eq. 463 exists only in the $zz$ component (see the subtlety box above). The thickness $l$ in the $z$-resolved Dyson equation and “SBZ” are not defined (read: layer thickness, surface Brillouin zone). Typo: “the the 2D dielectric function” (p. 102). Two slips are corrected above (§Z.3): E Eqs. 300 and 305 put every dipole at its cell origin in the plane $z=0$ and so lack the in-plane basis phase (E Eqs. 300′, 305′), and the 2D bubble on p. 101 lacks the factor $e^2$ (E p.101′).

This is where the EPP manuscript ends. Section Z.1 collects what EPP adds to Giustino's picture and lists what is still open, including the out-of-plane modes and the full finite-$c$ sheet problem set aside here.
