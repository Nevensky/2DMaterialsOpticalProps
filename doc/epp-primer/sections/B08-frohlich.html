B.8EPP §7.2 · Eqs. 411–432 · correction (428′), (428a), (448a)

The electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex

Open the electron loop inside the phonon self-energy and an electron–phonon vertex appears that nobody put in by hand: one photon exchanged between an ionic and an electronic current. It depends on frequency, it comes in a bare and a screened version, and for $c\to\infty$, once the factor $e$ that the draft loses in its continuity equation is restored (E.428′), the screened one becomes the Verdi–Giustino vertex with $\epsilon^\infty$ replaced by the dynamical RPA tensor $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$.

A vertex made of a photon

In Giustino's Hamiltonian (G Eq. 1) the coupling $g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)$ is an input: the matrix element of a screened potential change (G Eq. 38). The EPP Lagrangian has no such term (§B.2). Electrons couple to the vector potential through $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\bA$, ions through the polarization current of their Born-charge dipoles, $\frac1c\dot\bp\cdot\bA$. An electron can feel a phonon only by exchanging a photon with it, so the electron–phonon vertex is a composite: ionic current vertex, photon propagator, electronic current vertex. Only the long-range polar channel arises this way; the short-range deformation-potential part of Giustino's $g$ has no counterpart in the EPP Lagrangian (E.134).

§B.7 summed these exchanges into the phonon self-energy $\Omega^{\rm e}=-J^*D^{\rm e}J$ (E.399) and split it into an electron-free part $\Omega^0$ and an electron-induced part $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ (E.400–402); this section opens $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$. Notation as in the rest of Part B: $D^0$ is the free photon propagator (E.375), $D^{\rm e}$ the one dressed by electrons only (E.388), $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj}$ and $\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj}$ the irreducible and reducible electronic photon self-energies, and $J_{\mu\sigma}(\bq,\omega)=\sqrt{\hbar/2c\Omega_{\rm uc}\omega_\sigma(\bq)}\;\omega\,j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)$ the ionic current vertex (E.392), built from the mode current $j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)=\sum_{a\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}e_{a\nu\sigma}(\bq)/\sqrt{m_a}$ (E.295) with atomic masses $m_a$ and phonon eigenvectors $e_{a\nu\sigma}$. Numbers use $e^2/\varepsilon_0=180.95$ eV Å.

Physical picture

A polar phonon is an oscillating dipole $\bp=Z^*\bQ$, that is, a current $\dot\bp=-i\omega\bp$, and a current radiates. The longitudinal part of its field is the Coulomb potential of a polarization wave; the transverse part is light. An electron absorbs the field through a transition $n\bk\to m\bk+\bq$, which also carries a current. Both ends bring a factor $\omega$ and the propagator knows the speed of light, so the vertex depends on frequency and on $c$, unlike the adiabatic $g(\bk,\bq)$.

Opening the electron bubble

The electron-induced self-energy contains the reducible electronic correlator between two bare photon lines:

$$\begin{aligned}\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\alpha_1\beta_1}&J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^0_{\alpha\alpha_1}(\bq,\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm e}_{jj\alpha_1\beta_1}(\bq,\omega)\\&\times D^0_{\beta_1\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)\end{aligned}\tag{E.411}$$

Expanding $\Pi^{\rm e}=\Pi^{\rm e,0}+\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}+\dots$ and regrouping the chain onto the left photon line gives

$$\begin{aligned}\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\alpha_1\beta_1}&J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\alpha_1}(\bq,\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\alpha_1\beta_1}(\bq,\omega)\\&\times D^0_{\beta_1\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)\end{aligned}\tag{E.412}$$

One photon line is now screened, the other bare, and the electronic bubble between them is irreducible.

Derivation: from (E.411) to (E.412)

Suppress the Cartesian indices; every product is a $3\times3$ matrix product at fixed $(\bq,\omega)$:

$$\begin{aligned}D^0\Pi^{\rm e}D^0&=D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0+\dots\\&=\big(D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0+\dots\big)\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0=D^{\rm e}\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0,\end{aligned}$$

using the Dyson series of $D^{\rm e}$ (E.388). Nothing is approximated: the same chain of bubbles is read from a different end.

Inserting the RPA current correlator (E.383, §B.5) and splitting each transition into vertex × propagator × vertex defines the dynamical electron current vertex and the bare electron–hole propagator; the latter is boxed but unnumbered on EPP p. 92:

$$J^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{c\Omega}}\;\frac{\hbar\omega}{E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}}\;j^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\tag{E.414}$$
$$\chi^0_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)=2\,\frac{E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}}{\hbar\omega}\;\frac{f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}+i\eta}$$

Here $E_{n\bk}$ and $f_{n\bk}$ are band energies and occupations, $\Omega=N\Omega_{\rm uc}$ is the crystal volume ($N$ cells), $j^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$ is the paramagnetic current matrix element of E.384 (it contains $e$), and the 2 counts spin. The product $J^\mu\chi^0[J^\nu]^*$ reproduces the summand of E.383 exactly.

Do not implement $J^{\rm e}$ and $\chi^0$ separately

EPP's $\chi^0$ is not the Lindhard function. With $\Delta E=E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}$ and $\Delta E/[\hbar\omega(\hbar\omega+\Delta E)]=1/\hbar\omega-1/(\hbar\omega+\Delta E)$ it splits into the familiar density bubble plus a term $\propto(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq})/\hbar\omega$ that cancels only for a complete band sum (longitudinally it is $\sum(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq})|\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}|^2=0$). The vertex $J^{\rm e}\propto1/\Delta E$ diverges for intraband transitions as $q\to0$ and for degenerate bands; only the product $J^{\rm e}\chi^0J^{\rm e*}$ is regular. In a truncated-band code use E.383 directly or, longitudinally, the charge vertices of E.428′.

Two vertices, one bubble

Grouping each photon line with the two current vertices at its ends defines the bare and the screened electrodynamic electron–phonon vertices,

$$g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=i\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}\big[J^\alpha_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\big]^*\,D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.415}$$
$$g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=i\sum_{\alpha\beta=xyz}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J^\beta_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\tag{E.416}$$

and the electron-induced self-energy takes the shape of Giustino's phonon self-energy, vertex × bubble × vertex (EPP p. 92, unnumbered):

$$\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)=\sum_{\bk\in{\rm BZ},nm}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)\;\chi^0_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\;g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma'}(\bk,\bq,\omega)$$

The two factors of $i$ supply the minus sign of E.412–413. Read from left to right (EPP Fig. 14): phonon $\sigma$ emits through its Born-charge current $J^*$; the photon crosses the polarizable electrons ($D^{\rm e}$, longitudinal and transverse); an electron makes the transition $n\bk\to m\bk+\bq$ ($\chi^0$); the pair recombines into a bare photon ($D^0$), which phonon $\sigma'$ absorbs.

Why only one dressed line? Write $x=D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}$ for one electronic bubble hung on a bare line (schematically, as a scalar). Then $D^0\Pi^{\rm e}D^0=D^0(x+x^2+x^3+\dots)$ contains every chain of $k$ bubbles exactly once. Dressing both lines gives $D^{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^{\rm e}=D^0x/(1-x)^2$, that is $D^0(x+2x^2+3x^3+\dots)$, which contains the $k$-bubble chain $k$ times: double counting. Leaving both lines bare, $D^0\Pi^{\rm e,0}D^0=D^0x$, keeps only the one-bubble term: screening to first order, never resummed (derived here, not printed in EPP; steps of this kind are marked [derived] below). The correct count is either two bare lines around the reducible $\Pi^{\rm e}$ (E.411) or one bare and one screened line around the irreducible $\Pi^{\rm e,0}$ (E.412). In the static long-wavelength limit of an insulator, $1-x$ is the electronic dielectric constant $\epsilon$ (E.432), so the three pairings multiply the bare dipolar shift $\Omega^0$ by $1+(1/\epsilon-1)$, $1+(1-\epsilon)/\epsilon^2$ and $1+(1-\epsilon)$. The figure turns these factors into LO frequencies.

Bridge to Giustino: one bare vertex, one screened

Giustino's nonperturbative phonon self-energy (G §V.A.3, see §A.6) has the same structure, (G.141): the bare coupling $g^{\rm b}=\Delta_{\bq\nu}V^{\rm en}$ (G Eq. 142) at one end, the dynamically screened $g^{\rm cc}=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b,*}$ (G Eq. 144) at the other, and the irreducible $P_{\rm e}$ between them. $g^{\rm dyn,0}$ and $g^{\rm dyn}$ are their electrodynamic, long-range counterparts, and $D^{\rm e}=(\hat\epsilon^{\rm e})^{-1}D^0$ (E.431) mirrors $g=\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b}$ (G Eq. 143). Two differences: EPP needs no static subtraction, because its bare phonons hold only short-range forces, and it keeps the $\omega$ dependence of the screened vertex, which Giustino drops in his approximation (iv) on the way to (G.145). Giustino notes that most ab initio calculations replace $g^{\rm b}$ by $g$ in (G.145), for nonadiabatic frequencies as well as in the lifetime formula (G.146), and asks for the effect of the bare vertex to be tested (G §V.A.3, p. 015003-25). For the real part, the frequency shift, the replacement is the miscount of the "two screened" curve above, applied to the nonadiabatic part; for the width it is harmless in a consistent RPA as long as $\epsilon(\omega_{\bq\nu})\approx\epsilon(0)$ (§A.6).

Which vertex is screened, and at which frequency

"One bare, one screened" holds for self-energies with an electron bubble between the vertices; Fan–Migdal, with no bubble there, has two screened vertices (§A.7). The pairing is exact, but freezing the screened vertex at $\omega=0$, as (G.145) does, overestimates linewidths by about $\epsilon(\bq,0)$ in metals (§A.6). EPP keeps the dynamic form through $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$.

From currents to charges: the continuity identity

To compare with the electrostatic vertices of Part A, the current vertex has to become a charge vertex. EPP evolves the density and current operators with the band Hamiltonian $H^{\rm e}_0=\sum_{n\bk}E_{n\bk}c^+_{n\bk}c_{n\bk}$ (E.421), imposes continuity (E.422), Fourier transforms and compares coefficients (E.423–427). The density $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ of E.420 counts electrons, while the current $\bj_{\rm e}$ of E.419 carries their charge, so continuity reads $e\dot\rho_{\rm e}=-\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}$ (E p.94′), E.422 becomes $e[\rho_{\rm e},H^{\rm e}_0]_-=-i\hbar\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}$ (E.422′), and the left sides of E.423–425 carry the same $e$. With the charge vertex $\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=\langle\phi_{n\bk}|e^{-i\bq\cdot\br}|\phi_{m\bk+\bq}\rangle$ (E.106 at $\bG=0$, E.426) the result is

$$e\,\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=-\sum_\mu\hbar q_\mu\,\frac{j^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}}{E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}}\tag{E.428$'$}$$

E.428′ corrects the printed E.428, whose left side lacks the factor $e$ (§Z.3). It is $e\,\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\bj=0$ for a single transition: $\bq\cdot\bj=\omega_{\rm tr}\,e\rho$ with $\omega_{\rm tr}=(E_{m\bk+\bq}-E_{n\bk})/\hbar$. For the dynamical vertex it gives $\sum_\mu q_\mu J^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)=-\sqrt{1/c\Omega}\;\omega\,e\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$ [derived]: the longitudinal part of the electron current vertex is $\omega$ times the charge vertex, and the singular $1/\Delta E$ disappears.

Familiar from optics codes

This is the identity behind the equivalence of velocity- and length-gauge dipoles in RPA and BSE codes, $\langle v|\mathbf p|c\rangle=im\,\omega_{vc}\langle v|\br|c\rangle$ with $\omega_{vc}=(E_v-E_c)/\hbar$, with the same fine print: it holds for a local band Hamiltonian, and nonlocal pseudopotentials or scissor-shifted energies add commutator terms.

Note on the source: the missing $e^2$, resolved

The draft writes continuity on p. 94 as $\dot\rho_{\rm e}=-\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}$, the rate of a number density set equal to the divergence of a charge current, and E.422–428 inherit the slip: as printed, E.428 equates the dimensionless number-density vertex $\rho$ (E.106, E.426) with $-\hbar\bq\cdot\bj/\Delta E$, while $j^\mu$ (E.384) contains $e$. A free-electron check, $\phi=e^{i\bk\cdot\br}/\sqrt\Omega$, gives $-\hbar\bq\cdot\bj/\Delta E=e$, not 1. The correction (§Z.3) keeps $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ a number density, as the diamagnetic $A^2$ terms require, and writes $e\rho$ wherever a charge is meant: E p.94′, E.422′, the left sides of E.423–425 and of the displays between them, E.428′, and the vertices E p.95′ and E p.98′ below. As printed, those two vertices have the dimension of a potential (energy per charge), not of an energy. The author saw the symptom; a blue margin note in Croatian (p. 96) reads "U odnosu na Giustino-a fali ti taj $e^2$. Vjerojatno je u interakciji D ili u $Z^*$…", that is, "Compared with Giustino you are missing that $e^2$. It is probably in the interaction D or in $Z^*$. Also, his notation for this matrix element is $g^0_{mn\sigma}$ and ours $g^0_{nm\sigma}$, but that is irrelevant." One $e$ sits in EPP's charge-dimensioned Born charge ($Z^*_{\rm EPP}=|e|Z^*_{\rm G}$), the other is the $e$ of E.428′; $D$ is innocent, and the note can be deleted. The same slip puts $e^3$ into the p. 96 transcription of Verdi–Giustino (corrected as E p.96′ below) and drops $e^2$ from the 2D bubble on p. 101 (§B.10). The correction also fixes three smaller slips in this passage: the Heisenberg evolution on p. 93 gets its $\hbar$, $c_{n\bk}(t)=e^{iH^{\rm e}_0t/\hbar}c_{n\bk}e^{-iH^{\rm e}_0t/\hbar}$; the "retarded limit" before E.418 becomes the non-retarded (instantaneous) limit; and the claim before E.417 that the bare vertex reduces to E.131 now reads: up to the overall sign $e/|e|$ and the relabelling $n\leftrightarrow m$, $\bk\to\bk+\bq$, to the unscreened ($\hat\epsilon^\infty=1$), $\bG=0$ form of E.131 in Gaussian units.

The electrostatic limit: Verdi–Giustino with $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$

The Dyson equation for $D^{\rm e}$ (E.388) can be written as a dielectric problem,

$$\sum_\alpha\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\mu\alpha}(\bq,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\nu}(\bq,\omega)=D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.431}$$
$$\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=\delta_{\mu\nu}-\sum_\alpha D^0_{\mu\alpha}(\bq,\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\alpha\nu}(\bq,\omega)\tag{E.432}$$

As $c\to\infty$ only the longitudinal sector survives (derivation in §B.9), and $D^{\rm e}$ becomes the screened Coulomb interaction in vector form:

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{\sum_{\alpha\beta}q_\alpha\,\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,q_\beta}\tag{E.446}$$

Inserting E.375 and E.446 into the two vertices and using E.428′ gives the unnumbered displays of EPP pp. 95 and 98, corrected:

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=\frac{4\pi i\,e}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_a\sum_{\mu\nu=xyz}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\;\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}\,e_{a\nu\sigma}(\bq)}{q^2}\;\big[\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big]^*\tag{E p.95$'$}$$
$$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=\frac{4\pi i\,e}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_a\sum_{\mu\nu=xyz}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\;\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}\,e^*_{a\nu\sigma}(\bq)}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\;\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\tag{E p.98$'$}$$

E p.95′ and E p.98′ correct the printed displays on pp. 95 and 98, which lack the factor $e$ of E.428′ and so have the dimension of a potential rather than an energy (§Z.3). Compare with the Verdi–Giustino long-range vertex (G Eq. 180, §A.4), $\propto(\bq+\bG)\cdot\bZ^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa\nu}/[(\bq+\bG)\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot(\bq+\bG)]$ times a plane-wave matrix element. With $\bG=0$, the basis phase dropped ($\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa=0$), $\epsilon^\infty=1$, Gaussian units, in which $e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\to e^2$, and EPP's Born charges $Z^*=|e|Z^*_{\rm G}$, it reads

$$g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)=\frac{4\pi i\,|e|}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_a\sum_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_a\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\;\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}\,e_{a\nu\sigma}(\bq)}{q^2}\;\langle\phi_{m\bk+\bq}|e^{i\bq\cdot\br}|\phi_{n\bk}\rangle\tag{E p.96$'$}$$

E p.96′ corrects the printed transcription on p. 96, whose $e^2$ becomes $e^3$ with EPP's charge-dimensioned $Z^*$ (§Z.3). Since $\langle\phi_{m\bk+\bq}|e^{i\bq\cdot\br}|\phi_{n\bk}\rangle=[\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}]^*$, the two corrected vertices are, in the two new equations of the correction,

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=\frac{e}{|e|}\,g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)=-g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)\tag{E.428a}$$
$$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=-\frac{e}{|e|}\,\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\,\big[g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)\big]^*\tag{E.448a}$$

The bare vertex is Verdi–Giustino with $\epsilon^\infty=1$ and $\bG=0$, up to the overall sign $e/|e|$ and the relabelling $n\leftrightarrow m$, $\bk\to\bk+\bq$. The sign is a convention of the factorization E.415–416 (the factor $i$ and the order of the vertices). It cannot be absorbed in the polarization vector, because E p.95′ and E p.96′ are linear in the same $e_{a\nu\sigma}(\bq)$, but it is not observable: only the product $g^{\rm dyn}\chi^0g^{\rm dyn,0}$ enters E.413, and that product is fixed. Where the vertex is added to Hamiltonian matrix elements, such as a short-range $g^{\rm S}$ in Verdi–Giustino's $g=g^{\rm S}+g^{\rm L}$, use $g^0_{mn\sigma}=-\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}$.

The screened vertex is Verdi–Giustino (complex conjugated, for real $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}$) with $\epsilon^\infty$ replaced by $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$: the RPA tensor at the frequency at which the vertex is used, so the screening is nonadiabatic. Of Verdi–Giustino's $e^2$, one $e$ is in EPP's $Z^*$ and the other is the $e$ of E.428′. For GaAs, E p.98′ with $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\to\epsilon^\infty$ and G Eq. 180 both give $\sqrt N|g|=304$ meV at $q=0.1$ Å$^{-1}$, with $\omega_{\rm LO}$ in both zero-point amplitudes (inputs of the light-line figure below; literature values, illustrative). This closes a loop opened in §B.1, where $\epsilon^\infty$ was put into the dipole potential by hand (EPP p. 23, E.112–120: "later we will remove it"); here it is generated by $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj}$. EPP calls the screened result "the conjugated complex part" of the vertex; E.428a and E.448a together give the first relation below, and inserting it into the vertex form of E.413 (p. 92) gives the second [derived]:

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}=-\big[g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}\big]^*\,\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq},\qquad \Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}\to-\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\bq}\sum_{\bk nm}\big|g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}\big|^2\chi^0_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$$

The electronic correction is minus the bare vertex squared, times the bubble, times the screening factor. For an insulator it is negative: the electrons soften the bare dipolar stiffening $\Omega^0$, as they must.

What "almost identical" hides

  • No local fields and no exchange–correlation: the screening is the head of the RPA dielectric matrix, not DFPT's macroscopic $\epsilon^\infty$ (§B.9).
  • The basis phase of G Eq. 180 is missing (source note in §B.1).
  • The zero-point amplitude contains the bare short-range $\omega_\sigma$, which is TO-like for a polar mode, whereas Verdi–Giustino use the physical $\omega_{\bq\nu}$. When $\Omega^{\rm e}$ is frequency independent, the phonon Dyson equation (E.405) gives the LO pole a quasiparticle weight $\omega_\sigma/\omega_{\rm LO}$, which turns $|g|^2\propto1/\omega_\sigma$ into $1/\omega_{\rm LO}$ [derived] (derivation in §X.2).

Nonadiabaticity matters when electrons can respond at the phonon frequency. In an undoped semiconductor the gap lies far above $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}$, so $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega_{\rm LO})\approx\epsilon^\infty$ and static Verdi–Giustino screening is accurate. With free carriers it is not.

Across the light line: LO is Coulomb, TO needs photons

At finite $c$ the vertices contain the full propagator. Contracting E.375 with an ionic current along $\hat\bq$ (an LO mode in a cubic crystal) or perpendicular to it (TO) separates the two cases cleanly. Normalize each contraction $K$ by the bare Coulomb value $-4\pi c/\omega^2$.

Derivation: LO and TO kernels from (E.375)

The free propagator of the total field is

$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\frac{-q_\mu q_\nu+\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\delta_{\mu\nu}}{q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}-i\eta}\tag{E.375}$$

For a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf t}\perp\bq$ [derived]:

$$\hat q_\mu D^0_{\mu\nu}\hat q_\nu=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2},\qquad \hat t_\mu D^0_{\mu\nu}\hat t_\nu=\frac{4\pi}{c}\,\frac{1}{q^2-\omega^2/c^2-i\eta},\qquad \hat q_\mu D^0_{\mu\nu}\hat t_\nu=0 .$$

So $K^0_{\rm LO}=1$ and $K^0_{\rm TO}=\omega^2/(\omega^2-c^2q^2)$. For electrons with a constant isotropic dielectric constant $\epsilon$, take $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj,\mu\nu}=(\epsilon-1)\,\omega^2\delta_{\mu\nu}/4\pi c$, which makes the longitudinal part of E.432 equal to $\epsilon$. Solving E.388 gives $K_{\rm LO}=1/\epsilon$ and $K_{\rm TO}=\omega^2/(\epsilon\omega^2-c^2q^2)=\epsilon^{-1}\,[1-(q/q_c)^2]^{-1}$ with $q_c=\sqrt\epsilon\,\omega/c$. For an LO current, $D^0\cdot\hat\bq=-(4\pi c/\omega^2)\hat\bq$ picks out $\hat\bq\cdot J^{\rm e*}$, and the corollary of E.428′ turns E.415 into E p.95′ at every $q$, not only for $c\to\infty$.

The LO kernel contains no $c$: longitudinal photons are the instantaneous Coulomb field at every $q$, so the $c\to\infty$ form of the LO vertex is exact, inside the light cone too. The TO kernel equals the LO one deep inside the light cone, resonates at the light line of the electronic medium, $q_c=\sqrt{\epsilon^\infty}\,\omega/c$ (the light-cone wave vector of §X.1), and decays as $(q_c/q)^2$ outside. TO phonons therefore couple to electrons, through transverse electronic currents, only inside or near the light cone, $q\lesssim q_c$. For GaAs $q_c\approx6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$, four orders of magnitude below the zone boundary: invisible on the k-grids of transport calculations, central in optics, where it becomes the phonon-polariton physics of §B.7.

When the LO/TO split is clean

The clean split needs $\bj_\sigma$ purely along or across $\hat\bq$ and an isotropic $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}$; otherwise both kernels contribute. Also, at finite $c$ the transverse part of E.432 is not the optical dielectric function: for a constant electronic $\epsilon$ it equals $(q^2-\epsilon\omega^2/c^2)/(q^2-\omega^2/c^2)$ [derived], which tends to 1 as $c\to\infty$. Only the longitudinal part enters E.445–446.

In short, EPP derives the polar electron–phonon vertex instead of postulating it. Its static electrostatic limit, with the factor $e$ of E.428′, is the Verdi–Giustino vertex; the additions are frequency-dependent RPA screening, bare/screened bookkeeping that cannot double count, and a photon-mediated coupling of TO phonons near the light cone. §B.9 takes $c\to\infty$ in the whole self-energy and recovers Giustino's LO–TO splitting.
