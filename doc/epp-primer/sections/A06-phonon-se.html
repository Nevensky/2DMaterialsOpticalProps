Phonon self-energy: adiabatic vs nonadiabatic

DFPT phonons are built from the zero-frequency value of the many-body phonon self-energy. The frequency-dependent remainder, $\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)$, shifts and broadens them. It contains one bare and one screened electron–phonon vertex, with the static screening subtracted.

§A.5 showed that the nuclei interact through the electronically screened Coulomb interaction $W_{\rm e}$, with a phonon self-energy given by G Eq. (121). Here that result becomes the formulas that ab initio codes evaluate. Physically, a vibrating nucleus drags a cloud of screening electrons with it. If the electrons respond instantly, the screening is static and the phonon is the one DFPT computes. If they lag behind, the screening acquires a dynamical part, which shifts the phonon frequency and lets the phonon decay into electron–hole pairs. On this page Giustino's phonon Green's function $D$ is written $\mathcal D$ and his phonon self-energy $\Pi^{\rm ph}$; $\Pi^{\rm A}$ and $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ keep their names. Units are SI; in the mode basis $\Pi$ is a frequency and $\hbar\Pi$ an energy.

Phonons are poles of a propagator

A phonon is a resonance of the displacement–displacement correlation function $\mathcal D_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)$. Inverting the nuclear equation of motion gives

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}(\omega)=\left[\mathbf M\omega^2-\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega)\right]^{-1}, \tag{G.123}$$

where $\mathbf M$ is the diagonal matrix of nuclear masses $M_\kappa$. The resonances satisfy $\Omega_\nu(\omega)=\omega$, where $\Omega_\nu^2(\omega)$ are the eigenvalues of $\mathbf M^{-1/2}\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega)\mathbf M^{-1/2}$ (G Eq. 124). This is the dynamical-matrix problem of §A.1 with the force constants replaced by $\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega)$. Because $\Pi^{\rm ph}$ is complex and frequency dependent, the roots can leave the real axis (finite lifetimes) and one "eigenmode" can have several roots.

The adiabatic split: $\Pi^{\rm A}$ is what DFPT computes

Giustino partitions the self-energy at zero frequency:

$$\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega)=\Pi^{\rm A}+\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega),\qquad \Pi^{\rm A}=\Pi^{\rm ph}(\omega=0). \tag{G.125}$$

Setting $\omega=0$ is the Born–Oppenheimer adiabatic approximation (Keating 1968): the electronic dielectric matrix $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)$ is replaced by $\epsilon_{\rm e}(\br,\br',0)$ over the whole phonon range, so the electrons follow the nuclei instantaneously. With the acoustic sum rule, $\Pi^{\rm A}$ takes the Hellmann–Feynman form

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm A}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}=&\int\!d\br\,\frac{\partial\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\br)\rangle}{\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\frac{\partial V^{\rm en}(\br)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}+\int\!d\br\,\langle\hat n_{\rm e}(\br)\rangle\frac{\partial^2V^{\rm en}(\br)}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}\\&+\frac{\partial^2U_{\rm nn}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}\partial\tau_{\kappa'\alpha'p'}}.\end{aligned} \tag{G.128}$$

These have the form of the DFPT interatomic force constants (§A.3): an induced-density term, an unperturbed-density term and the ion–ion term. The difference is that here the density response is governed by the exact many-body $\epsilon_{\rm e}(0)$ and the exact density, whereas DFPT uses $\epsilon^{{\rm H}xc}=1-(v^{\rm C}+f^{xc})\chi^0$ at clamped nuclei. In practice $\Pi^{\rm A}$ is replaced by the DFT force constants. Since $W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)=W_{\rm e}(\br',\br,-\omega)$, $\Pi^{\rm A}$ is real and symmetric: adiabatic phonons have real frequencies and live forever. In the eigenmode basis the adiabatic propagator is diagonal,

$$\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu\nu'}(\omega)=\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}}\,\delta_{\nu\nu'}, \tag{G.132}$$

which is the textbook free-boson form $\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu\nu}=1/(\omega-\omega_{\bq\nu}+i\eta)-1/(\omega+\omega_{\bq\nu}-i\eta)$ of G Eq. (134). Giustino stresses that this form "rests on the adiabatic approximation".

Physical picture: who is faster?

The adiabatic condition is $|\varepsilon_{m\bk+\bq}-\varepsilon_{n\bk}|\gg\hbar\omega_{\bq\nu}$ for the electronic transitions that the phonon couples (Giustino §VII). Insulators with eV gaps satisfy it easily. In a metal, intraband transitions cost about $\hbar v_Fq$; they are slower than the phonon when $q\lesssim\omega/v_F$, the window identified by Maksimov and Shulga. In a doped semiconductor with $\varepsilon_F\sim\hbar\omega$ the condition fails for all wave vectors up to a few $k_F$, not only near $\Gamma$ (still a small part of the zone, since $k_F$ is small). For metals the static response of the electron gas is even singular as $q,\omega\to0$, which DFPT codes tame by smearing the occupations (de Gironcoli 1995).

The Dyson equation: shifts, widths, new poles

Everything beyond the adiabatic reference goes into a Dyson equation:

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}(\omega)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}^{\rm A}(\omega)+\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}^{\rm A}(\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm NA}(\omega)\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal D}(\omega). \tag{G.135}$$

In the mode basis the inverse propagator is

$$\mathcal D^{-1}_{\bq\nu\nu'}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}\left[\delta_{\nu\nu'}\left(\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}\right)-2\omega_{\bq\nu}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu'}(\omega)\right]. \tag{G.136}$$

$\Pi^{\rm NA}$ acts in four ways: (i) the real part of its diagonal shifts the frequencies; (ii) the imaginary part broadens the lines; (iii) off-diagonal elements couple adiabatic modes; (iv) its frequency dependence can create new poles, for example coupled plasmon–phonon modes in degenerate polar semiconductors, "the electronic analog of photon polaritons". Keeping only the diagonal element of a nondegenerate mode, the complex zeros $\tilde\Omega_{\bq\nu}=\Omega_{\bq\nu}-i\gamma_{\bq\nu}$ of (G.136) satisfy

$$\tilde\Omega^2_{\bq\nu}=\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+2\omega_{\bq\nu}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu}(\tilde\Omega_{\bq\nu}), \tag{G.137}$$

whose imaginary and real parts give the linewidth and the renormalized frequency:

$$\gamma_{\bq\nu}=-\frac{\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\Omega_{\bq\nu}}\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu}(\Omega_{\bq\nu}-i\gamma_{\bq\nu}), \tag{G.138}$$
$$\Omega^2_{\bq\nu}=\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+\gamma^2_{\bq\nu}+2\omega_{\bq\nu}\,{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu}(\Omega_{\bq\nu}-i\gamma_{\bq\nu}). \tag{G.139}$$

For small corrections these reduce to the Allen–Grimvall forms $\gamma_{\bq\nu}\simeq-{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu}(\omega_{\bq\nu})$ and $\Omega_{\bq\nu}\simeq\omega_{\bq\nu}+{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu}(\omega_{\bq\nu})$. The phonon lifetime is $\tau^{\rm ph}_{\bq\nu}=(2\gamma_{\bq\nu})^{-1}$.

Derivation: from (G.137) to (G.138)–(G.139), and why $\tau=1/2\gamma$

Write $\tilde\Omega^2=(\Omega-i\gamma)^2=\Omega^2-\gamma^2-2i\Omega\gamma$. The real part of (G.137) gives $\Omega^2-\gamma^2=\omega^2+2\omega\,{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}$, which is (G.139). The imaginary part gives $-2\Omega\gamma=2\omega\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}$, which is (G.138). Near $\omega\approx\Omega>0$,

$$\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\omega^2-\tilde\Omega^2}\simeq\frac{\omega_{\bq\nu}/\Omega}{\omega-\Omega+i\gamma},$$

so the spectral function $-\frac1\pi{\rm Im}\,\mathcal D$ is a Lorentzian of half width at half maximum $\gamma$. In the time domain the amplitude decays as $e^{-i\Omega t-\gamma t}$ and the vibrational energy as $e^{-2\gamma t}$, hence $\tau^{\rm ph}=1/2\gamma$. Strictly, $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ must be evaluated at the complex frequency $\Omega-i\gamma$, which requires continuing the retarded self-energy below the real axis; in practice, and in the figure below, it is evaluated on the real axis.

Inside $\Pi^{\rm NA}$: one bare vertex, one screened vertex

Combining G Eqs. (119) and (125), only the frequency-dependent part of the screened interaction survives in the nonadiabatic self-energy:

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa'\alpha'p'}(\omega)=\int\!d\br\,d\br'\,&Z_\kappa\nabla_\alpha\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})\left[W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)-W_{\rm e}(\br,\br',0)\right]\\&\times Z_{\kappa'}\nabla'_{\alpha'}\delta(\br'-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa'p'}).\end{aligned} \tag{G.140}$$

The nuclear point charges interact through $W_{\rm e}$, and only the dynamical part of the screening makes phonons shift and decay. Inserting $W_{\rm e}=v+vP_{\rm e}W_{\rm e}$ (G Eq. 94), the gradients of $v$ and of $W_{\rm e}$ turn into electron–phonon couplings, and in the mode basis

$$\begin{aligned}\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu,\bq'\nu'}(\omega)=&\int\!d\br\,d\br'\,g^{\rm b}_{\bq\nu}(\br)\,P_{\rm e}(\br,\br',\omega)\,g^{\rm cc}_{\bq'\nu'}(\br',\omega)\\&-\int\!d\br\,d\br'\,g^{\rm b}_{\bq\nu}(\br)\,P_{\rm e}(\br,\br',0)\,g^{*}_{\bq'\nu'}(\br',0),\end{aligned} \tag{G.141}$$

with the bare and dressed coupling functions

$$g^{\rm b}_{\bq\nu}(\br)=\Delta_{\bq\nu}V^{\rm en}(\br), \tag{G.142}$$
$$g_{\bq\nu}(\br,\omega)=\int\!d\br'\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}(\br,\br',\omega)\,g^{\rm b}_{\bq\nu}(\br'). \tag{G.143}$$

$g^{\rm cc}$ is defined like (G.143) with $g^{{\rm b},*}$ in place of $g^{\rm b}$ (G Eq. 144); since $\epsilon_{\rm e}$ is real at $\omega=0$, $g^{\rm cc}(\br,0)=g^*(\br,0)$. The bare vertex is the variation of the ionic (pseudo)potential, long ranged in metals; the dressed one is screened by the full electronic $\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}$ and is short ranged. Equation (G.141) is nonperturbative: it relies only on the harmonic approximation, and it agrees with Keating (1968) and with Scalapino's electron-gas result. Its structure is the main message of this section: the irreducible polarization $P_{\rm e}=GG\Gamma$ sits between a bare vertex on one side and a screened vertex on the other, and its static value is subtracted.

Why exactly one screened vertex? The lowest-order diagram that starts and ends on a phonon line has two bare vertices, $\Pi\simeq g^{\rm b}G_0G_0g^{\rm b}$. Dressing it means summing all chains of proper polarization insertions joined by $v$: $g^{\rm b}P g^{\rm b}+g^{\rm b}PvPg^{\rm b}+\dots=g^{\rm b}P\,\epsilon_{\rm e}^{-1}g^{\rm b}=g^{\rm b}Pg$. The whole chain can be absorbed into one end, which is the Dyson equation of the dressed vertex, $g=g^{\rm b}+vGG\Gamma g$ (G Fig. 1d; see §A.3). So one bare and one screened vertex around the irreducible bubble is exact, and exact RPA once $P\to\chi^0$. Screening the other end as well counts chains more than once: the two geometric series multiply, $\sum_{a,b}P(vP)^{a+b}=\sum_n(n+1)P(vP)^n$, so a chain with $n$ Coulomb links appears $n+1$ times. Leaving both ends bare keeps only the first term, screening to first order in $P$. The electron self-energy is different: in Fan–Migdal both ends of the internal phonon line sit on the electron line and each end is screened, so it has two screened vertices (§A.7).

From (G.141) to the working formula

Five approximations turn (G.141) into a Brillouin-zone sum: (i) $\Gamma=1$, the GW-type vertex; (ii) Kohn–Sham $G$ at clamped nuclei; (iii) RPA$+xc$ screening from DFT; (iv) the frequency dependence of the screened coupling is dropped, $g^{\rm cc}(\br,\omega)\simeq g^*(\br,0)$, an approximation that is "ubiquitous in the literature but it is never mentioned explicitly"; (v) spin degeneracy and time-reversal symmetry. The result is

$$\begin{aligned}\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq\nu\nu'}(\omega)=2\sum_{mn}\int\!\frac{d\bk}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,&g^{\rm b}_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)\,g^*_{mn\nu'}(\bk,\bq)\\&\times\left[\frac{f_{m\bk+\bq}-f_{n\bk}}{\varepsilon_{m\bk+\bq}-\varepsilon_{n\bk}-\hbar(\omega+i\eta)}-\frac{f_{m\bk+\bq}-f_{n\bk}}{\varepsilon_{m\bk+\bq}-\varepsilon_{n\bk}}\right].\end{aligned} \tag{G.145}$$

The factor 2 is spin, $\eta\to0^+$, $g_{mn\nu}$ is the screened DFPT matrix element of G Eq. (38) and $g^{\rm b}_{mn\nu}$ the same with the bare ionic potential; at finite temperature $f$ becomes Fermi–Dirac. The first term in brackets is the bare polarizability $\chi^0(\bq,\omega)$ in the Kohn–Sham basis, the second its static value. The subtraction $\chi^0(\omega)-\chi^0(0)$ removes double counting: the static bubble is already inside $\Pi^{\rm A}$, because the DFT phonons were computed with static screening. Most calculations also replace $g^{\rm b}$ by $g$ and keep only the imaginary part. With ${\rm Im}\,1/(x-i0^+)=\pi\delta(x)$ and $\tau=1/2\gamma$ this gives the golden-rule linewidth

$$\frac{1}{\tau^{\rm ph}_{\bq\nu}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\,2\sum_{mn}\int\!\frac{d\bk}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\,|g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)|^2\left(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}\right)\delta\!\left(\varepsilon_{m\bk+\bq}-\varepsilon_{n\bk}-\hbar\omega_{\bq\nu}\right). \tag{G.146}$$

A phonon decays by creating an electron–hole pair of energy $\hbar\omega_{\bq\nu}$. All the calculations reviewed in Giustino §VII use $g^{\rm b}\to g$ (his footnote 8).

Subtlety: Eqs. (G.145) and (G.146) differ by $\epsilon(\bq,0)$

Equation (G.145) has the numerator $g^{\rm b}g^*$, Eq. (G.146) has $|g|^2$. With $g=\epsilon^{-1}g^{\rm b}$ they differ by the factor $\epsilon(\bq,0)$, which in a metal at small $q$ is about $(k_{\rm TF}/q)^2\gg1$. In a consistent RPA treatment the exact result of (G.141) is ${\rm Im}[g^{\rm b}P(\omega)g^{\rm cc}(\omega)]=|g(\omega)|^2\,{\rm Im}P(\omega)$ (derivation below). So the "common practice" $|g|^2$ is right when $\epsilon(\omega_{\bq\nu})\approx\epsilon(0)$, while pairing the bare vertex with a statically screened one, approximation (iv), overestimates the width by about $\epsilon(\bq,0)$. The bare/screened bookkeeping and the frequency dependence of the screened vertex have to be treated together. Giustino flags the replacement as something "it will be important to assess".

Derivation: ${\rm Im}[g^{\rm b}Pg^{\rm cc}]=|g|^2\,{\rm Im}P$ in the RPA

Take a homogeneous, $\bq$-diagonal RPA: $\epsilon(\omega)=1-vP(\omega)$, $g(\omega)=g^{\rm b}/\epsilon(\omega)$ and $g^{\rm cc}(\omega)=g^{{\rm b}*}/\epsilon(\omega)$. Then $g^{\rm b}P(\omega)g^{\rm cc}(\omega)=|g^{\rm b}|^2P/\epsilon$ and

$${\rm Im}\frac{P}{1-vP}=\frac{{\rm Im}\left[P\,(1-vP^*)\right]}{|\epsilon|^2}=\frac{{\rm Im}P}{|\epsilon(\omega)|^2},$$

because $vPP^*$ is real. Hence

$${\rm Im}[g^{\rm b}Pg^{\rm cc}]=\frac{|g^{\rm b}|^2\,{\rm Im}P(\omega)}{|\epsilon(\omega)|^2}=|g(\omega)|^2\,{\rm Im}P(\omega).$$

With approximation (iv), $g^{\rm cc}(\omega)\to g^*(0)$, the same product becomes $|g^{\rm b}|^2{\rm Im}P(\omega)/\epsilon(0)=\epsilon(0)\,|g(0)|^2\,{\rm Im}P(\omega)$: the cancellation between ${\rm Re}P\,{\rm Im}\,\epsilon^{-1}$ and ${\rm Im}P\,{\rm Re}\,\epsilon^{-1}$ is lost.

A doped 2D semiconductor: the nonadiabatic window

The figure below evaluates (G.145) for the simplest system where adiabaticity fails: one dispersionless optical phonon $\omega_0$ (adiabatic, so it already contains $\Pi^{\rm A}$) coupled by a constant intraband matrix element to a single parabolic 2D band holding $n$ electrons per unit area. With $g^{\rm b}g^*\to|g|^2$ as in (G.146), $\int d\bk/\Omega_{\rm BZ}=A_{\rm cell}\int d^2k/(2\pi)^2$ turns the bracket of (G.145) into the 2D Lindhard function, $\chi^0=N_0\tilde\chi$ with $N_0=m^*/\pi\hbar^2$ (both spins), so that

$$\hbar\Pi^{\rm NA}(Q,\omega)=G\left[\tilde\chi(Q,W)-\tilde\chi(Q,0)\right],$$

where $G=N_0A_{\rm cell}|g|^2$ is the coupling strength, $Q=q/k_F$, $W=(\hbar\omega+i\eta)/\varepsilon_F$, and the closed form (retarded, $T=0$, checked against direct $\bk$ integration) is

$$\tilde\chi(Q,W)=-\left[1+\frac{s(W-Q^2)+s(-W-Q^2)}{2Q^2}\right],\qquad s(z)=z\sqrt{1-4Q^2/z^2},$$

using the principal square root; statically $\tilde\chi(Q,0)=-1$ for $Q\le2$. The phonon spectral function follows from the diagonal of (G.136),

$$B(Q,\omega)=-\frac1\pi\,{\rm Im}\,\frac{2\omega_0}{\omega^2-\omega_0^2+2i\eta\omega-2\omega_0\Pi^{\rm NA}(Q,\omega)},$$

written with the retarded denominator; $\eta$ adds a small intrinsic width so that undamped modes stay visible.

Near the upper continuum edge the first-order and self-consistent solutions differ strongly: the hardened pole can sit just above the edge and stay sharp, as the spectral map shows, while the first-order formula already samples the continuum at $\omega_0$. For $\varepsilon_F\gg\hbar\omega_0$ the width approaches the Landau-damping limit $\hbar\gamma\approx G\,\hbar\omega_0/[\varepsilon_FQ\sqrt{4-Q^2}]$, valid for $\hbar\omega_0\ll\varepsilon_FQ$ and $Q$ not close to 2. Mode coupling (iii) and plasmon–phonon poles (iv) are absent: the model has one mode and uses the bare bubble $\chi^0$ without the Coulomb chain, hence no plasmon.

Physical picture: two limits that do not commute

At $\bq\to0$ the intraband part of the bracket in (G.145) depends on the order of limits. Static first: the static bubble tends to $\partial f/\partial\varepsilon=-\delta(\varepsilon_{\bk}-\varepsilon_F)$, a Fermi-surface softening contained in the adiabatic phonon, which the second term of the bracket removes. Dynamic first: at $\bq=0$ the dynamical term vanishes, because a vibration faster than $v_Fq$ cannot rearrange the Fermi sea. The net result is a stiffening, $\hbar\,{\rm Re}\,\Pi^{\rm NA}_{\bq=0}\simeq N_F\langle|g|^2\rangle_{\rm FS}>0$ with $N_F$ counting both spins, the approximation of Saitta et al. (2008) quoted by Giustino. In the figure it is the hardening by $\approx G$ at small $Q$.

Where nonadiabatic effects have been seen

In the Raman G band of graphene ($E_{2g}$, 1585 cm⁻¹, $\bq=0$; Giustino §VII) electrons near the Dirac points make "virtual" transitions with energies comparable to the phonon; the measured frequency versus gate doping follows the nonadiabatic calculation while the adiabatic one stays flat (Pisana et al. 2007; Giustino Fig. 8). This is a nonadiabatic Kohn anomaly: it sits at small $\bq$, where the static and dynamic limits of (G.145) differ (callout above). The $2k_F$ anomaly of the jellium model below is an adiabatic one. The renormalization is typically about 1%, but reaches about 300 cm⁻¹ in graphite intercalation compounds (Saitta et al. 2008) and 7% at small $\bq$ in CaC$_6$ (Calandra, Profeta and Mauri 2010). In MgB$_2$ the calculation overshoots (761 vs 600 cm⁻¹). Saitta et al. suggested that finite electron lifetimes, absent from the Kohn–Sham $G$ of approximation (ii), partly wash out the effect. Linewidths from (G.146), or Allen's double-delta simplification (G Eq. 185), range from about 1 meV (Nb) to 30 meV (MgB$_2$).

Jellium: adiabatic phonons and the Kohn anomaly

The simplest $\Pi^{\rm A}$ belongs to point ions of valence $Z$ and mass $M$ in an electron gas. In G Eq. (121) at $\omega=0$ the ions interact through $W_{\rm e}(q,0)=v(q)/\epsilon_{\rm e}(q,0)$. Treating the ions as a uniform fluid (no lattice) with one ion per volume $\Omega$, the longitudinal mode obeys (cf. G Eqs. 121, 128; Bardeen–Pines)

$$M\omega_q^2=\frac{Z^2e^2}{\varepsilon_0\Omega}\,\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm e}(q,0)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\omega_q^2=\frac{\Omega_p^2}{\epsilon_{\rm e}(q,0)},\qquad\Omega_p^2=\frac{Z^2e^2}{\varepsilon_0\Omega M}:$$

the bare ionic plasma oscillation, screened statically. With Thomas–Fermi screening, $\epsilon=1+k_{\rm TF}^2/q^2$, the mode is acoustic with the Bohm–Staver velocity $v_s=\Omega_p/k_{\rm TF}=(Zm_e/3M)^{1/2}v_F$. With the Lindhard function of §A.2, $\epsilon_{\rm e}(q,0)$ is continuous at $q=2k_F$ but its slope diverges logarithmically, because $2k_F$ is the largest momentum that a zero-energy electron–hole pair can carry across the Fermi sphere. The phonon inherits the singularity: $d\omega/dq$ diverges at $2k_F$. This is the Kohn anomaly (Kohn 1959), the reciprocal-space face of the Friedel oscillations that Giustino §VI.A.2 mentions for metals. It is an adiabatic, static-screening feature that DFPT reproduces and smearing blunts. Nonadiabatic corrections are small here: $\omega/qv_F\approx v_s/v_F=(Zm_e/3M)^{1/2}\sim10^{-3}$–$10^{-2}$, Migdal's small parameter.

Bridge to EPP: same Dyson equation, same bare–screened pair, photon lines instead of Coulomb lines

EPP §7 dresses its bare phonon propagator $F^0=\mathcal D^{\rm A}/\hbar$ by emission and reabsorption of photons and obtains a Dyson equation of exactly this form, $F=F^0+F^0\Omega^{\rm e}F$ (E.398), whose pole condition is (G.137) with $\Omega^{\rm e}=\hbar\Pi$ (§B.7). Its electron-induced part has one photon line dressed by electrons and one bare, and factorizes into bare vertex, bubble and screened vertex, the structure of (G.141) (§B.8). The match is structural, not term by term (§B.8 discusses EPP's modified bubble and its singular intraband current vertex), and double counting is avoided in a different way (last row of the table):

Note on the source

Giustino writes the Saitta approximation as an average "over the wave vectors $\bk$ in the Brillouin zone". Derived from (G.145), the intraband static term gives $\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bk}-\varepsilon_F)$, so it is a Fermi-surface average, and $N_F$ must count both spins given the explicit factor 2 in (G.145). Elsewhere (e.g. G Eq. 192) Giustino's $N_F$ is per spin; in that convention the formula reads $2N_F\langle|g|^2\rangle_{\rm FS}$. G Eq. (127), used to obtain (G.128), has a sign and argument-order slip that leaves (G.128) unaffected; see the note at the end of §A.5.
