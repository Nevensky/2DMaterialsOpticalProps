B.5EPP §§4.5–4.7, §5 · Eqs. 246–311

Propagators and the RPA photon self-energy

Free phonons and photons get their propagators, and matter enters the photon line through one current–current self-energy. By the end you can write the ionic part exactly, explain why gauge invariance fixes the diamagnetic term, and recognise the result as the Gonze–Lee infrared dielectric tensor.

§B.4 ended with the free Hamiltonian $H_0$ (E.236) of Kohn–Sham electrons, bare phonons and transverse photons, and an interaction $V'$ (E.237′). Units are Gaussian, $Z^*$ carries the dimension of charge, and $f(\omega)=\int dt\,e^{i\omega t}f(t)$ (E.255). Names follow the page convention: EPP's photon propagator $D$, photon self-energy $\Pi$ (written $\Pi_{jj}$ from EPP §6 on) and phonon propagator $F$; Giustino's phonon Green's function is $\mathcal D$.

Temperature: the "$n_B=0$" rule

Everything below uses $T=0$ time-ordered propagators. EPP §4.5 argues that this costs nothing for bosons. After the continuation $i\omega_n\to\omega+i\eta$ (E.246), the Matsubara phonon propagator becomes the retarded one,

$$F^r_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{(\omega+i\eta)^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)},\tag{E.247}$$

which contains no temperature: for a harmonic field the commutator $[Q(t),Q(0)]$ is a c-number. The real-time time-ordered function at finite $T$ does depend on $T$,

$$F^T_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=2\,\frac{\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{\hbar}\left[\frac{1+n_{\bq\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}-\frac{n_{\bq\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)-i\eta}\right],\tag{E.249}$$

but only through its imaginary part, $-i\pi(1+2n_{\bq\sigma})\,\delta(\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2)$ times $2\omega_\sigma/\hbar$. EPP's rule follows: in time-ordered perturbation theory set $n_{\bq\sigma}=0$ in every boson line, keep the Fermi functions $f(T)$ in the electron bubbles, and make the $i\eta$ retarded at the end.

Where the rule holds, and where it does not

It holds for what EPP §§5–7 compute. Free boson lines and the ionic photon self-energy below (one harmonic phonon line) have $T$-independent retarded forms. Self-energies built from electron bubbles carry their temperature dependence in $f(T)$, as in Giustino's G Eq. 145. It fails when a boson line runs inside a fermion self-energy: Giustino's Fan–Migdal self-energy (G Eq. 158) carries $n_{\bq\nu}$ in its numerators, and that produces the temperature shift of band gaps (§A.7, §A.8). The figures below use retarded denominators $\omega^2-\omega_0^2+i\gamma\omega$; the time-ordered $+i\eta$ form has the wrong sign of ${\rm Im}$ at $\omega\lt 0$.

The free phonon line

With Mahan's definition and the dimensionless displacement $Q_{\bq\sigma}=a_{\bq\sigma}+a^\dagger_{-\bq\sigma}$,

$$F_\sigma(\bq;t-t')=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right|T\{Q_{\bq\sigma}(t)\,Q_{-\bq\sigma}(t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right\rangle,\tag{E.251}$$

the two time orderings give $e^{\mp i\omega_\sigma(t-t')}$ (E.254), and the Fourier transform adds them into

$$\boxed{F_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}}\tag{E.256}$$

that is, $\frac1\hbar\big[\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_\sigma+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_\sigma-i\eta}\big]$: the ordering $t>t'$ gives the pole at $+\omega_\sigma$, the ordering $t\lt t'$ the pole at $-\omega_\sigma$. Because $Q$ is dimensionless, $F$ has units of inverse energy.

Bridge to Giustino: the same phonon line

Giustino's adiabatic mode propagator is $\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{\bq\nu}+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_{\bq\nu}-i\eta}=\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+i\eta}$ (G Eqs. 132, 134; §A.6), so $\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu}=\hbar F_\sigma$. One difference matters. EPP's $\omega_\sigma(\bq)$ are bare frequencies from short-range force constants, without LO–TO splitting; the photon line regenerates it in §B.7.

The free transverse photon line

The photon propagator is defined with the opposite sign and an extra $1/c$,

$$D^0_{\mu\nu}(\br,\br';t-t')=\frac{i}{\hbar c}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right|T\{A_\mu(\br,t)\,A_\nu(\br',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phot}_0\right\rangle,\tag{E.257}$$

a choice that makes $D$ the response of the vector potential to an external current, $\bA=D\,\bj^{\rm ext}$ (EPP p. 83). Split $\bA=\bA_t+\bA_l$ (E.258); the cross terms vanish (E.259). The transverse operators oscillate at $\omega_\bq=cq$, the time integral gives the same pole pair as for phonons, and the polarization sum is the transverse projector, $\sum_{\alpha=1,2}e_{\mu\alpha}(\bq)e_{\nu\alpha}(\bq)=\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$:

$$\boxed{D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-4\pi c\left[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2}\right]\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\bq+i\eta},\qquad\omega_\bq=qc.}\tag{E.261}$$

It is the Green's function of $(\omega^2/c^2-q^2)\bA_t=-\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj_t$. Its static limit, $\frac{4\pi}{cq^2}(\delta_{\mu\nu}-\hat q_\mu\hat q_\nu)$, is Biot–Savart. Between currents $j\sim\rho v$ it is weaker than Coulomb's $4\pi/q^2$ between charges by $(v/c)^2$, unless $cq\lesssim\omega$: the light cone of §X.1. (The printed intermediate step (E.260) duplicates a factor $2\omega_{\bq\alpha}$; (E.261) is correct. Clerical slips of this kind are collected in §Z.2.)

The longitudinal photon line vanishes; Coulomb does not

The longitudinal operators do not appear in $H_0$: the longitudinal field energy was moved into $V'$ as the Coulomb term (E.235′). In the interaction picture they are frozen, $\omega_{\bq3}=0^+$ (E.262), the same steps give a propagator proportional to $\omega_{\bq3}$, and

$$\boxed{D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=0.}\tag{E.263}$$

The retarded version vanishes too (E.267–E.268), because a static $\bA_l$ commutes with itself. Strictly, (E.263) holds for $\omega\neq0$: a time-independent $\bA_l$ inserted directly into (E.259) gives a term $\propto\delta(\omega)$.

This does not remove the longitudinal field. In the Heisenberg picture $\bA_l$ evolves, with $\bE^l=-\frac1c\dot\bA^l$ tied to the charges by Gauss's law (E.269), and that physics sits in the Coulomb term of $V'$. §B.6 rewrites that Coulomb term, through continuity, as the temporal-gauge propagator $D^{0l}$ of (E.374) between currents, and explains why (E.263) and (E.374) do not contradict each other.

Dressing the transverse photon

EPP §5 first drops the Coulomb term of $V'$ (E.270), so that only $\bA_t$ propagates. The ionic terms then take the form of the electronic ones once an ionic density tensor and an ionic current are defined,

$$\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\br)=\frac{m_0}{e^2}\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\,\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia}),\tag{E.271$'$, 315$'$}$$
$$j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\br)=\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\,\pi_{a\mu}(\br)\,\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia}).\tag{E.272$'$}$$

$m_0$ is an arbitrary reference mass that cancels. (E.272′) is the polarization current $Z^*\dot Q$ of point dipoles at the atomic sites $\bR^0_{ia}=\bR^0_i+\br^0_a$, with $\dot Q\to\pi/m_a$. The printed (E.271) and (E.272) put every dipole of a cell at its origin $\bR^0_i$; the corrected forms follow the basis-phase correction of §Z.3, under which the momentum field (E.274′) carries $e^{i\bq(\br-\br^0_a)}$, so that $\pi_{a\mu}(\bR^0_{ia})=\pi_{ia\mu}$; the rest of $\dot\bP$, $-\frac1c Z^*Z^*\bA/m_a$, is the ionic diamagnetic current, as $\bp\to\bp-\frac ec\bA$ for electrons. The interaction becomes four look-alike terms (E.273). Expanding the exact propagator (E.275) in the interaction picture (E.276) and summing the RPA diagrams gives

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}D^t_{\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)={}&D^{0t}_{\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega)\\&+\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int d^3\br_1\!\int d^3\br_2\,D^{0t}_{\mu\alpha}(\br,\br_1,\omega)\,\Pi^{\rm RPA}_{\alpha\beta}(\br_1,\br_2,\omega)\,D^t_{\beta\nu}(\br_2,\br',\omega)\end{aligned}}\tag{E.277}$$
=+

"RPA" means that the self-energy is the lowest-order irreducible insertion of each species: one bubble plus one contact term,

$$\Pi^{\rm RPA}_{\alpha\beta}(\br,\br',\omega)=\Pi^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\br,\br',\omega)+\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\br,\br',\omega),\tag{E.278}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\br,\br',\omega)={}&\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{mc}\,\rho^{\rm e}(\br)\,\delta(\br-\br')\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}}_{\Pi^{\rm e,dia}}\\&+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm el}_0\right|T\{j^{\rm e}_\alpha(\br,t)\,j^{\rm e}_\beta(\br',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm el}_0\right\rangle}_{\Pi^{\rm e,para}},\end{aligned}\tag{E.279}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\br,\br',\omega)={}&\underbrace{-\frac{e^2}{m_0c}\,\rho^{\rm ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\br)\,\delta(\br-\br')}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}}\\&+\underbrace{\frac{i}{\hbar c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i\omega(t-t')}\left\langle\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right|T\{j^{\rm ion}_\alpha(\br,t)\,j^{\rm ion}_\beta(\br',t')\}\left|\Phi^{\rm phon}_0\right\rangle}_{\Pi^{\rm ion,para}}.\end{aligned}\tag{E.280}$$

In (E.279) $\rho^{\rm e}$ means $\langle\rho^{\rm e}\rangle$; EPP says so only for the ions, whose contact term it derives from the two equivalent contractions of the $A\rho^{\rm ion}A$ vertex (factor 2, p. 59).

Physical picture: why two terms per species

Every charged species couples to $\bA$ twice: the paramagnetic vertex $-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA$ absorbs or emits one photon, the $A^2$ vertex scatters two at one point. The contact term alone is the inertial response of free charges, as in a plasma. The bubble adds the restoring forces (springs for ions, gaps for electrons). In a slowly varying field bound charges only polarize, so in an insulator the two terms cancel exactly at $\omega=0$.

Bridge to Giustino: $W=v+vPW$ for photons

(E.277)–(E.278) have the algebra of Giustino's $W=v+vPW$ (G Eq. 90, §A.5): $D^{0t}\leftrightarrow v$, $\Pi\leftrightarrow P$, $D^t\leftrightarrow W$. Resumming the electrons first gives $D^t=D^t_{\rm e}+D^t_{\rm e}\Pi^{\rm ion}D^t$ with $D^t_{\rm e}=D^{0t}+D^{0t}\Pi^{\rm e}D^t_{\rm e}$, the transverse counterpart of his $W_{\rm e}$ (G Eq. 94) and of $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm e}\mathcal DW_{\rm e}$ (G Eqs. 104–105). The vertices are now currents instead of densities, and the ions enter through the pre-screened Born charge $Z^*$ instead of the nuclear charge $Z_\kappa$. The ionic part is exact for harmonic phonons, because $j^{\rm ion}$ is linear in phonon operators. The electronic bubble has no vertex corrections (no excitons); a BSE current–current response in its place would give exciton polaritons.

Gauge invariance fixes the diamagnetic term

In the temporal gauge $\bE=\frac{i\omega}{c}\bA$, so a static, uniform $\bA$ carries no field at all. It is a pure gauge, and in a normal (non-superconducting) system it must drive no current: $\Pi(\bq\to0,\omega=0)=0$. The diamagnetic term is frequency independent, so it must equal minus the static paramagnetic term. EPP uses this (proved in its Lecture 9 notes for quasi-2D crystals) to write the electronic self-energy of a crystal without any explicit diamagnetic term,

$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}_{\alpha\beta\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega=0)}\tag{E.281}$$

In optics language, $\bj=\Pi\bA$ and $\bE=\frac{i\omega}{c}\bA$ give $\sigma=-\frac{ic}{\omega}\Pi$ and $\epsilon(\omega)=1+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi(\omega)$ (not written in EPP), so $-4\pi c\,\Pi$ has units of frequency squared. At $\bq=0$ a free electron gas has only the contact term, $\Pi=-ne^2/mc$, which gives the Drude $\epsilon=1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2$ with $\omega_p^2=4\pi ne^2/m$. The paramagnetic part in a crystal is

$$\Pi^{\rm para,e}_{\alpha\beta\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{2}{c\Omega}\sum_{\bk\in{\rm BZ}}\sum_{nm}\frac{f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}+i\eta}\;j^\alpha_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\bG)\left[j^\beta_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\bG')\right]^*,\tag{E.282}$$
$$j^\alpha_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\br)=\frac{e\hbar}{2im}\left\{\phi^*_{n\bk}(\br)\,\partial_\alpha\phi_{m\bk+\bq}(\br)-\left[\partial_\alpha\phi^*_{n\bk}(\br)\right]\phi_{m\bk+\bq}(\br)\right\},\tag{E.284}$$

with $j^\alpha(\bG)=\int d\br\,e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\cdot\br}j^\alpha(\br)$ over the crystal (E.283). The factor 2 is spin, $\Omega$ is the crystal volume, and $\phi_{n\bk}$ is normalized over the crystal. For a strictly 2D sheet in a supercell of period $l$, EPP takes $\Pi^{\rm RPA,e,2D}_{\alpha\beta}(\bQ,\omega)=l\,\Pi^{\rm RPA,e}_{\alpha\beta,\bG=\bG'=0}(\bQ,\omega)$ (after E.285; used in §B.10).

Bridge to your RPA code: Adler–Wiser with currents

(E.282) is the Adler–Wiser $\chi^0_{\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)$ of RPA and GW codes, with the density vertices $\langle n\bk|e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\cdot\br}|m\bk+\bq\rangle$ replaced by current vertices and an overall factor $-1/c$. In the optical limit $j^\alpha_{n\bk,m\bk}(\bG=0)=\frac{e}{m}\langle n\bk|\hat p_\alpha|m\bk\rangle$, the momentum matrix elements of velocity-gauge optics. Giustino's $P_{\rm e}$ (G Eq. 92) is the density version; §B.6 shows that continuity turns one into the other.

What (E.281) buys in practice

Exactly, "dia + para" and (E.281) agree. In practice band sums are truncated, and with nonlocal pseudopotentials the velocity operator is $\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,\br]$ rather than $\bp/m$, so the computed $\Pi^{\rm para}(0)$ no longer cancels $ne^2/mc$ exactly. A naive "dia + para" then leaves $-(1-S)\,\omega_p^2/\omega^2$ in $\epsilon$, with $S$ the fraction of the f-sum that the bubble captures: for $S\lt 1$, a Drude term in an insulator. (E.281) enforces $\Pi(0)=0$ by construction. It also sets to zero the true static transverse response at finite $q$, the orbital magnetic susceptibility of order $q^2$, which is negligible for optics.

The ionic self-energy, exactly

Inserting (E.272′) into (E.280) leaves a momentum–momentum correlator of point ions (E.286). The momentum (E.274′) is linear in $a,a^\dagger$, so only the $aa^\dagger$ and $a^\dagger a$ contractions survive, the occupation factors cancel in the principal part, and after the lattice sums $\bG$ and $\bG'$ enter only through the basis phases of the atoms. The result is compact in terms of the ionic current vertex

$$\boxed{j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG)=\sum_{a\mu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}}{\sqrt{m_a}}\,e_{a\mu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_a}\equiv\sum_aj^a_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG)}\tag{E.295$'$}$$

the Born-charge dipole that mode $\sigma$ carries per unit mass-weighted amplitude, in units of charge per $\sqrt{\rm mass}$, at the wave vector $\bq+\bG$. The structure factor $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_a}$ is the basis phase of a dipole at its atom (§B.1); the printed (E.295) lacks it, because its dipoles sit at the cell origin (§Z.3). The vertex vanishes for nonpolar modes, and for acoustic modes at $\bq\to0$, $\bG=0$ because the Born charges obey $\sum_aZ^*_a=0$. With the conjugate vertex $j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\bq+\bG')$, which carries $e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{i(\bq+\bG')\br^0_b}$ (E.296′),

$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}\,j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG)\,j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\bq+\bG')}\tag{E.297$'$}$$
$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{\hbar}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{2}\,F_\sigma(\bq,\omega)\,j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG)\,j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\bq+\bG')}\tag{E.299$'$}$$

(E.299′) is "vertex, phonon line, vertex", the third diagram above; the $\bG\bG'$ dependence sits in the vertices. Here $\sigma$ labels the $3s$ branches and $\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu$ are Cartesian ((E.287) still uses $\alpha,\beta$ for branches). The 2D sheet version (E.300′) has $S_{\rm uc}$, in-plane $\bG$ and atom-resolved vertices at the heights $z_a$ of the atoms, which for a planar sheet reduce to $\delta(z)\delta(z')$ (§B.10).

Derivation: from (E.286) to (E.297′)

With $a_{\bq\sigma}(t)=a_{\bq\sigma}e^{-i\omega_\sigma t}$, the surviving contractions force $\bq'=-\bq$ and equal branches. The time integrals $\int d\tau\,e^{i\omega\tau}\theta(\pm\tau)e^{\mp i\omega_\sigma\tau}$ produce the pole pair, and with $e_{a\mu\sigma}(-\bq)=e^*_{a\mu\sigma}(\bq)$ (E.288) the pairs recombine into (EPP p. 62)

$$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta}(\br,\br',\omega)={}&-\frac{1}{\hbar c}\frac1N\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\sum_{\bq\in{\rm BZ}}\sum_{ia\mu}\sum_{jb\nu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{b\beta\nu}}{\sqrt{m_am_b}}\,\hbar\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)\,e_{a\mu\sigma}(\bq)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{i\bq(\br-\br^0_a)}e^{-i\bq(\br'-\br^0_b)}\\&\times\left\{\frac{1+n_{\bq\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}-\frac{n_{\bq\sigma}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)-i\eta}\right\}\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia})\,\delta(\br'-\bR^0_{jb}).\end{aligned}\tag{E.289}$$

This is (E.289) corrected in place by the basis-phase rule of §Z.3: the $\delta$ functions sit at the atoms, and the phases $e^{i\bq(\br-\br^0_a)}$, $e^{-i\bq(\br'-\br^0_b)}$ come from (E.274′); on the support of the $\delta$ functions their product is $e^{i\bq(\bR^0_i-\bR^0_j)}$. The printed form has $\delta(\br-\bR^0_i)\,\delta(\br'-\bR^0_j)$ and $e^{i\bq(\br-\br')}$. The bracket is that of (E.249); $n_{\bq\sigma}=0$ gives (E.290′). The lattice sums use $\sum_i\delta(\br-\bR^0_i-\br^0_a)=\Omega_{\rm uc}^{-1}\sum_\bG e^{i\bG\cdot(\br-\br^0_a)}$. Together with $\sum_\bq\to\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^3}\int d\bq$ (E.291) they give the prefactor $\frac{\Omega}{N}\frac{N^2}{\Omega^2}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}$ and a bracket whose only $\bG,\bG'$ dependence is the pair of structure factors $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_a}\,e^{i(\bq+\bG')\br^0_b}$ (E.293′). Comparing with the crystal form $f(\br,\br')=\sum_{\bG\bG'}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d\bq}{(2\pi)^3}f_{\bG\bG'}(\bq)e^{i(\bq+\bG)\cdot\br}e^{-i(\bq+\bG')\cdot\br'}$ (E.294) reads off (E.297′). The diamagnetic term (E.302′) goes through the same lattice sum, plus the relabelling $\bG+\bG'\to\bG$, to (E.304′).

The response is that of $s$ interpenetrating gratings of point dipoles, one per sublattice, each displaced by $\br^0_a$ from the Bravais lattice. In the printed form, with every atom of the cell at $\bR^0_i$, the bracket does not depend on $\bG,\bG'$ at all. The Bragg components are reliable only for $|\bq+\bG|$ small compared with the inverse size of the charge induced around an atom, because an atom is not a point dipole and quadrupoles enter at the next order; in practice the $\bG$ sum is cut off smoothly, as Verdi and Giustino do with $e^{-a|\bq+\bG|^2}$. From E.380 on EPP keeps only $\bG=\bG'=0$, the macroscopic response.

The diamagnetic part follows from (E.271′) and (E.280). It is local, $\propto\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia})\delta(\br-\br')$ (E.302′), and after the same lattice sum it is constant in $\bq$ and $\omega$; it depends on $\bG$ and $\bG'$ only through the structure factor $e^{-i(\bG-\bG')\br^0_a}$:

$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\rm uc}c}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}\,e^{-i(\bG-\bG')\br^0_a}}\tag{E.304$'$}$$

Its diagonal $\bG=\bG'$ is (E.304) as printed. For isotropic Born charges $-4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\bG\bG}=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_aZ^{*2}_a/m_a\equiv\Omega_p^2$, the ionic plasma frequency of ions with the springs cut; for a diatomic crystal it is the $\omega_p^2$ defined with (E.152) in §B.3.

Completeness: the diamagnetic term is minus the static bubble

Set $\omega=0$ in (E.297′). There are no real excitations at zero frequency, so $\eta\to0$ and every mode contributes its full strength (E.306):

$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega=0)=\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG)\,j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\bq+\bG').\tag{E.307$'$}$$

Insert the vertices (E.308′) and use the completeness of the dynamical-matrix eigenvectors, $\sum_\sigma e_{a\mu\sigma}(\bq)e^*_{b\nu\sigma}(\bq)=\delta_{ab}\delta_{\mu\nu}$ (E.28). Only $a=b$ survives, so the $\bq$-dependent phases cancel:

$$\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega=0)=\frac{1}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{a\mu}\frac{Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}}{m_a}\,e^{-i(\bG-\bG')\br^0_a},\tag{E.309$'$}$$
$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm ion,dia}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega=0)}\tag{E.310}$$

(E.310) and (E.311) below hold as printed once the subscripts $\bG\bG'$ are added, as matrix identities; on the diagonal $\bG=\bG'$ they are the printed equations. Neither the force constants nor $\bq$ survive. Read as a statement about modes, (E.309′) at $\bG=\bG'=0$ is an oscillator-strength sum rule, $\sum_\sigma j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq)j^*_{\beta\sigma}(\bq)=\sum_{a\mu}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}/m_a$: springs move infrared strength between modes but cannot change its total. It is the ionic Thomas–Reiche–Kuhn rule.

Note on the source: the argument in §5.2.2

EPP first motivates (E.310) with $\nabla\cdot\bj^{\rm ion}=0$ (E.301), because "there are no free charges". But the ions carry the bound charge $-\nabla\cdot\bP$, used in §6 (E.313), and (E.301) with (E.350) would force $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}=0$. The right reason is gauge invariance; the result stands because §5.2.3 proves it through completeness. At exactly $\bq=0$ the acoustic terms of (E.306) are $0/0$: take $\omega\to0$ first.

The infrared dielectric tensor

Combining (E.297′), (E.304′) and (E.310),

$$\boxed{\Pi^{\rm ion,RPA}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)-\Pi^{\rm ion,para}_{\alpha\beta,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega=0)}\tag{E.311}$$

has the same structure as (E.281). With $\frac{\omega_\sigma^2}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}+1=\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2-\omega_\sigma^2}$ it reads, in the form EPP writes out in §6,

$$\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj\mu\nu,\bG\bG'}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3s}\frac{j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq+\bG)\,j^*_{\nu\sigma}(\bq+\bG')}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}.\tag{E.352$'$}$$

From E.380 on only its $\bG=\bG'=0$ element is used, with the vertex $j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)$ of E p.86a′, which keeps the basis phase $e^{-i\bq\br^0_a}$. It vanishes at $\omega=0$, and for $\omega\gg\omega_\sigma$ it tends to the contact term: far above the phonons the lattice responds as free ions. Now add the electrons far below the gap, where $4\pi c\,\Pi^{\rm e}\approx(\epsilon^\infty-1)\omega^2$, and use $\epsilon=1+4\pi c\,\Pi/\omega^2$. At $\bq\to0$ and $\bG=\bG'=0$, where the basis phases drop out, this gives (derived, not printed in EPP)

$$\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=\epsilon^\infty_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\sigma}\frac{j_{\alpha\sigma}\,j^*_{\beta\sigma}}{\omega_\sigma^2-\omega^2-i\eta},$$

the infrared dielectric tensor of Gonze and Lee (1997), the DFPT reference Giustino cites for the LO–TO splitting (§VI.A.3), with $j_{\alpha\sigma}j^*_{\beta\sigma}$ their mode oscillator strength $S_{\sigma,\alpha\beta}$ (up to the $e^2$ inside EPP's $Z^*$). The ingredients are standard DFPT output, but in EPP the formula is derived: the poles are the bare short-range frequencies and $\epsilon^\infty$ comes from $\Pi^{\rm e}$. For one polar mode its zero is $\omega_{\rm LO}^2=\omega_{\rm TO}^2+\Omega_p^2/\epsilon^\infty$ (§B.3, §B.7).

Bridge to Giustino: the photon self-energy is the dual of the phonon self-energy

Giustino's phonon self-energy has the same structure, $\Pi^{\rm ph}\propto Z_\kappa Z_{\kappa'}\partial\partial'[W_{\rm e}(\omega)-W_{\rm e}(0)]$ (G Eq. 121, §A.5), enforced by the acoustic sum rule (G Eq. 120): translation invariance kills the static response to a rigid shift of the lattice, as gauge invariance here kills the static response to a uniform $\bA$. The $\chi^0(\omega)-\chi^0(0)$ in Giustino's nonadiabatic $\Pi^{\rm NA}$ (G Eq. 145, §A.6) looks alike but serves another purpose, avoiding double counting of the adiabatic part. Through continuity (§B.6), (E.352′) also fixes the ionic density response $\Pi^{\rm ion}_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rm ion}_{jj}\cdot\bq$ (at $\bG=\bG'=0$; E.354a for the full matrix) that drives the LO phonon and the Fröhlich coupling.
