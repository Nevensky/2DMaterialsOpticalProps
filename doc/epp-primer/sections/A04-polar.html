Polar materials: Fröhlich, Verdi–Giustino and the LO–TO splitting

Why the electron–phonon vertex of a polar crystal diverges as $1/q$, how Born charges and $\epsilon^\infty$ fix its strength, why it must be removed before Wannier interpolation, and why the same electrostatic kernel splits LO from TO phonons.

In a polar crystal a displaced ion drags a net charge along. The dipole per cell created by displacing the sublattice of atom $\kappa$, at zero macroscopic electric field, defines the Born effective charge tensor $\bZ^*_\kappa$, dimensionless (in units of $e$) in Giustino's convention. A phonon that moves atom $\kappa$ by $\Delta\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$ therefore puts a dipole $e\bZ^*_\kappa\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$ in every cell, with phase $e^{i\bq\cdot\bR_p}$. Giustino §II.A.3 states the consequence: in ionic crystals "the atomic displacements can generate long-ranged electric fields; these fields provide a new scattering channel for electrons and holes." Notation: $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity, $\epsilon^\infty$ and $\epsilon^0$ are the high-frequency (clamped-ion) and static relative permittivities, and units are SI as in Giustino.

A wave of dipoles and its field

In a longitudinal wave the dipoles point along $\bq$. Their heads converge on some planes and diverge from others, so sheets of bound charge $-\nabla\cdot\bP$ appear half a wavelength apart (figure below). Their macroscopic potential solves Poisson's equation in a medium whose electrons screen with $\epsilon^\infty$, so $\phi(\bq)\propto\bq\cdot\bP/(\bq\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot\bq)$, which grows as $1/q$. A transverse wave has $\bq\cdot\bP=0$: no bound charge and no electrostatic potential. In real space one displaced ion is a dipole whose potential falls off as $1/r^2$. In a nonpolar insulator the screened potential of a displaced atom is at least quadrupolar, $R^{-3}$ (Giustino §VI.A.2, citing Pick, Cohen and Martin 1970), and that one power of $R$ decides everything below. In a metal the Fermi sea screens even the dipole field and the coupling stays finite (§A.2).

Why polar crystals break Wannier interpolation

Converged Brillouin-zone integrals of electron–phonon quantities can need $10^6$ wave vectors, and every DFPT calculation is "roughly as expensive as one total energy minimization" (Giustino §VI). The way out is to rotate $g$ into maximally localized Wannier functions $\mathrm w_{mp}$ (G Eqs. 175–176) and represent it in real space:

$$ g_{mn\kappa\alpha}(\bR_p,\bR_{p'})=\Big\langle \mathrm w_{m0}(\br)\Big|\frac{\partial V^{\rm KS}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha}}(\br-\bR_{p'})\Big|\mathrm w_{n0}(\br-\bR_p)\Big\rangle_{\rm sc}, \tag{G.177}$$

the amplitude for an electron to hop from Wannier function $n$ in cell $p$ to $m$ in cell 0 when atom $\kappa$ of cell $p'$ moves along $\alpha$ ("sc" denotes an integral over the Born–von Kármán supercell). A generalized Fourier transform returns it to any $(\bk,\bq)$,

$$ g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)=\sum_{pp'}e^{i(\bk\cdot\bR_p+\bq\cdot\bR_{p'})}\sum_{m'n'\kappa\alpha}U_{mm'\bk+\bq}\,g_{m'n'\kappa\alpha}(\bR_p,\bR_{p'})\,U^\dagger_{n'n\bk}\,\mathrm u_{\kappa\alpha,\bq\nu}, \tag{G.178}$$

with $U_{\bk}$ the unitary rotations that define the Wannier functions, $\mathrm u_{\kappa\alpha,\bq\nu}=(\hbar/2M_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu})^{1/2}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\bq)$, and the inverse (G.179) evaluated on a coarse DFPT grid. This works if $g(\bR_p,\bR_{p'})$ has died off inside the Wigner–Seitz supercell of the coarse grid. In $\bR_p$ it decays like the Wannier functions, exponentially in insulators. In $\bR_{p'}$ it decays like the screened potential of the displaced atom, and in a polar material that is the dipole of the figure: $|g(0,\bR_{p'})|\sim|\bR_{p'}|^{-2}$. Then $g(\bk,\bq)\sim|\bq|^{-1}$ near Γ, and no finite real-space cutoff captures it. "Physically this singularity corresponds to the 'Fröhlich electron-phonon coupling'" (Giustino §VI.A.3).

The Verdi–Giustino long-range vertex

Verdi and Giustino (2015) and Sjakste et al. (2015) split $g=g^{\mathcal S}+g^{\mathcal L}$ and obtain the long-range part from electrostatics: the potential of all Born charges displaced according to mode $\nu$, screened by the electronic permittivity tensor,

$$ \begin{aligned} g^{\mathcal L}_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)=i\,\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_\kappa\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2N_pM_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu}}\Big)^{1/2} &\sum_{\bG\neq-\bq}\frac{(\bq+\bG)\cdot\bZ^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa\nu}(\bq)}{(\bq+\bG)\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot(\bq+\bG)}\\ &\times\big\langle\psi_{m\bk+\bq}\big|e^{i(\bq+\bG)\cdot(\br-\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa)}\big|\psi_{n\bk}\big\rangle_{\rm sc}. \end{aligned} \tag{G.180}$$

Here $\Omega$ is the unit-cell volume and $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$ the position of atom $\kappa$ in the cell. Each factor has a plain meaning:

  • $\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\big[(\bq+\bG)\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot(\bq+\bG)\big]^{-1}$ is the instantaneous, longitudinal Coulomb interaction per cell, screened by clamped-ion electrons: the long-wavelength, static limit of $W_{\rm e}$.
  • $(\bq+\bG)\cdot\bZ^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa\nu}$ projects the mode's dipole on the wave vector; only its longitudinal part makes a potential. For acoustic modes the acoustic sum rule $\sum_\kappa\bZ^*_\kappa=0$ cancels the leading term, so the basis phase $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa}$ inside the matrix element matters there (Giustino footnote 7).
  • For small $\bq+\bG$ the matrix element is interpolated as $[U_{\bk+\bq}U^\dagger_{\bk}]_{mn}$, which tends to $\delta_{mn}$: the long-range coupling is intraband and forward-scattering.
  • $\bZ^*_\kappa$ and $\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty$ are DFPT outputs, responses to a uniform electric field (§A.3). Eq. (G.180) has no free parameter.

In practice one subtracts (G.180) from the coarse-grid DFPT $g$, Wannier-interpolates the smooth remainder $g^{\mathcal S}$, and adds (G.180) back on the fine grid. The $\bG$ sum is damped by $e^{-a|\bq+\bG|^2}$, and "the results are independent of the choice of the cutoff parameter $a$", because what the cutoff removes is short-ranged and ends up in $g^{\mathcal S}$. Giustino's Fig. 7 shows anatase TiO2: the remainder is smooth and stays below about 150 meV, while the total diverges on both sides of Γ. The laboratory below reproduces this logic in one dimension.

From Born charges back to Fröhlich

Giustino writes that the 1950 Fröhlich replacement (G.7), shown in §A.2, "is derived in Sec. VI.A.3". That section gives only (G.180) and refers to Verdi and Giustino, so the reduction is written out here. Take a cubic diatomic crystal with one polar LO mode, $\bZ^*_1=-\bZ^*_2=Z^*$, $\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty=\epsilon^\infty$, keep $\bG=0$ and the intraband term, and set $N_p\to1$. The sum over the two atoms gives $Z^*q/\sqrt\mu$, with $\mu$ the reduced mass, and

$$ |g^{\mathcal L}(q)|=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{Z^*}{\epsilon^\infty q}\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2\mu\omega_{\rm LO}}\Big)^{1/2}=\frac1q\Big[\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}}{2}\Big(\frac1{\epsilon^\infty}-\frac1{\epsilon^0}\Big)\Big]^{1/2}.$$

The second form follows from two relations between the same ingredients, the Born-charge LO–TO splitting and the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller (LST) relation (derivation below). It is exactly (G.2) with $V_0$ from (G.7), including the overall factor $i$.

Derivation: from (G.180) to the Fröhlich vertex

1. Keep $\bG=0$ and let $\bq\to0$ in the matrix element, which becomes $\delta_{mn}$. Insert the LO eigenvector of a cubic diatomic crystal, $\be_1=(M_2/M)^{1/2}\hat\bq$, $\be_2=-(M_1/M)^{1/2}\hat\bq$ with $M=M_1+M_2$:

$$\sum_\kappa\frac{\bq\cdot\bZ^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa}}{\sqrt{M_\kappa}}=Z^*q\Big(\sqrt{\frac{M_2}{MM_1}}+\sqrt{\frac{M_1}{MM_2}}\Big)=\frac{Z^*q}{\sqrt\mu},\qquad \mu=\frac{M_1M_2}{M},$$

so that, with $\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}=\frac{e^2}{\varepsilon_0\Omega}$,

$$g^{\mathcal L}=\frac{i}{q}\,\frac{e^2}{\varepsilon_0\Omega}\,\frac{Z^*}{\epsilon^\infty}\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2\mu\omega_{\rm LO}}\Big)^{1/2}\delta_{mn}.$$

2. The same eigenvector, projected on the nonanalytic force constants of the next subsection, gives $\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2=e^2Z^{*2}/(\varepsilon_0\Omega\mu\epsilon^\infty)$. Use it to eliminate $Z^{*2}/\mu$:

$$|g^{\mathcal L}|^2=\frac{e^2}{\varepsilon_0\Omega q^2}\,\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\rm LO}}\,\frac{\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2}{\epsilon^\infty}.$$

3. LST, $\omega_{\rm TO}^2/\omega_{\rm LO}^2=\epsilon^\infty/\epsilon^0$, turns the last factor into $\omega_{\rm LO}^2(1/\epsilon^\infty-1/\epsilon^0)$, which gives the Fröhlich form above.

4. Compare (G.2), $-i(\hbar/2M_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu})^{1/2}\,\bq\cdot\be\,V_0$, with $V_0$ from (G.7): the mass cancels, $-i\times(-1)=+i$, and the magnitude is the same. With the GaAs inputs of the explorer below, the Fröhlich route gives $|g|q=0.0321$ eV Å$^{-1}$ and the Born-charge route 0.0305 eV Å$^{-1}$. The Born charge implied by LST is 2.19 (2.18 if the measured $\hbar\omega_{\rm TO}=33.3$ meV is used instead, the value quoted in §B.1 and §B.7), against the literature value 2.07 (illustrative), so the two input sets are slightly inconsistent.

The strength is set by $1/\epsilon^\infty-1/\epsilon^0$, the part of the screening that comes from the ions. Fast perturbations are screened by electrons alone, slow ones by electrons and ions, and the difference is what an electron feels from the LO phonon. The dimensionless polaron constant $\alpha=(1/\epsilon^\infty-1/\epsilon^0)(\mathrm{Ry}\,m^*/m_e\hbar\omega_{\rm LO})^{1/2}$ (standard, not printed in Giustino) is 0.07 for the GaAs preset of the explorer and about 5 for its LiF and NaCl presets, which assume $m^*=m_e$ (illustrative). Giustino calls the $1/q$ coupling "the physical basis for the phenomenon of electron self-trapping in polarons" (§II.A.3), and self-trapping is strongest at large $\alpha$.

Physical picture · the Fröhlich vertex is the ionic part of $W$

Square the vertex and attach the LO phonon propagator of Giustino's Eq. (132), written in energy units. With $v(q)=e^2/(\varepsilon_0\Omega q^2)$ and LST one finds (derived here)

$$\frac{v(q)}{\epsilon^\infty}+|g^{\mathcal L}(q)|^2\,\frac{2\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}}{(\hbar\omega)^2-(\hbar\omega_{\rm LO})^2}=\frac{v(q)}{\epsilon(\omega)},\qquad \epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon^\infty\,\frac{\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega^2}{\omega_{\rm TO}^2-\omega^2}.$$

The electronically screened Coulomb line plus one-phonon exchange is the fully screened interaction, with $v/\epsilon^0$ at $\omega=0$ and $v/\epsilon^\infty$ at high frequency. This is the polar case of $W=W_{\rm e}+W_{\rm ph}$, which §A.5 derives in general.

The same kernel splits LO from TO

Giustino does not write the phonon-side partner of (G.180). Section VI.A.3 only notes that the long-range split "is analogous to that in use for calculating the splitting between longitudinal-optical (LO) and transverse-optical (TO) phonons in polar materials (Gonze and Lee, 1997)". Here is that term for orientation, from Gonze and Lee; the review does not print it. The interatomic force constants of a polar crystal have a nonanalytic part at Γ,

$$ \widetilde C^{\rm NA}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}(\bq\to0)=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{(\bq\cdot\bZ^*_\kappa)_\alpha\,(\bq\cdot\bZ^*_{\kappa'})_\beta}{\bq\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon^\infty\cdot\bq}, $$

with the same Born charges and the same screened Coulomb kernel as (G.180). It depends on the direction $\hat\bq$ but not on $|\bq|$, so the limit $\bq\to0$ depends on how Γ is approached. It is added to the analytic, short-range force constants before the dynamical matrix of §A.1 is diagonalized. For the cubic diatomic crystal the LO mode is pushed up and the TO mode, with $\bq\cdot\bZ^*\cdot\be=0$, is untouched:

$$ \omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{Z^{*2}}{\mu\,\epsilon^\infty},\qquad \frac{\omega_{\rm LO}^2}{\omega_{\rm TO}^2}=\frac{\epsilon^0}{\epsilon^\infty}\ \ (\text{LST}).$$

LST is the $\omega=0$ value of the $\epsilon(\omega)$ in the box above, whose pole is $\omega_{\rm TO}$ and whose zero is $\omega_{\rm LO}$, where a longitudinal polarization wave needs no external charge. Eliminating $Z^*$ gives a compact statement: $|g^{\mathcal L}|^2=\frac{v(q)}{\epsilon^\infty}\,\frac{\hbar(\omega_{\rm LO}^2-\omega_{\rm TO}^2)}{2\omega_{\rm LO}}$. The Fröhlich coupling and the LO–TO splitting are one number read off two objects, the vertex and the dynamical matrix. A crystal without LO–TO splitting has no Fröhlich coupling.

Where electrostatics stops being exact

Every factor in (G.180) is electrostatic. The kernel is the instantaneous Coulomb interaction and $\epsilon^\infty$ is a static electronic response, so neither meaning of "retardation" is present: not the frequency dependence of electronic screening (Giustino's sense, §A.2) and not the finite speed of light (EPP's sense). The second matters only inside the light cone, $q\lesssim q_c=\sqrt{\epsilon^\infty}\,\omega_{\rm LO}/c$. For GaAs $q_c\approx6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$, four orders of magnitude below the zone boundary and well below the inverse polaron radius of 0.025 Å$^{-1}$. Inside the cone a TO phonon, dark for electrostatics, still carries a polarization current that radiates a transverse field. It mixes with light into a phonon-polariton, and whether LO and TO differ at $\bq=0$ depends on the order of the limits $\bq\to0$ and $c\to\infty$. §X.1 measures the light cone, and §B.7 recovers the splitting as a photon-mediated phonon self-energy.

Bridge to EPP · the static vertex (E.131′)

EPP §3 derives the same vertex, (E.131), from the potential of Born-charge point dipoles in an anisotropic dielectric, and calls it "exactly equal" to Verdi and Giustino's Eq. (4). With each dipole placed at its atom, the corrected (E.131′) is (G.180) (§Z.3). §B.1 displays it in EPP's notation and lists the bookkeeping differences.

The real difference is the status of $\epsilon^\infty$. EPP puts it in "only ad hoc and later we will remove it to avoid double-counting". In the full theory the bare dipole couples to the photon field, the RPA photon self-energy regenerates electronic screening (§B.5), and $\bq\cdot\epsilon^\infty\cdot\bq$ becomes $\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq$, with a transverse channel added near the light cone (§B.8). Eq. (G.180) is the $c\to\infty$, statically screened limit of that vertex, up to complex conjugation and a sign convention (E.448a), once the charge $e$ lost in EPP's continuity equation is restored (E.428′, §Z.3).

Note on the sources · basis phase and citation

Like Eq. (4) of Verdi and Giustino (2015), EPP's printed (E.131) lacks the basis phase $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\cdot\mathbf r^0_a}$ that (G.180) restores (Giustino's footnote 7), because EPP places every Born dipole of a cell at the cell origin. The correction in §Z.3 puts each dipole at its atom and gives (E.131′), which carries the phase and matches (G.180) with $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa\to\mathbf r^0_a$; without it the vertex depends on how atoms are assigned to cells and couples spuriously to acoustic modes (E.131a, E.131b in §B.1). EPP also cites the 2015 PRL as by Giustino alone. See the source note in §B.1.

Subtlety · what the reduction assumes

The Fröhlich form needs one polar LO mode, isotropic $\bZ^*$ and $\epsilon^\infty$, the $\bG=0$ term and intraband scattering. Eq. (G.180) needs none of these and sums over all modes, which matters in TiO2 with its several LO branches. The classic Fröhlich model adds parabolic bands and a dispersionless LO phonon. The multipole expansion behind (G.180) also has a next term: dynamical quadrupoles give a direction-dependent but finite long-range contribution, treated analytically in work after this review. In a two-dimensional crystal the dipole sheet gives a coupling that stays finite as $q\to0$ (§B.10).
