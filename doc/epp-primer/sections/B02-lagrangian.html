B.2EPP §4.1 · Eqs. 132–143

One Lagrangian for electrons, ions and fields

EPP puts band electrons, harmonic ions and the full electromagnetic field into one classical Lagrangian. It has no electron–phonon term: electrons and ions interact only by sharing the vector potential. Its Euler–Lagrange equations give the Lorentz force for the electrons and, for the ions, a lattice driven by the electric field through the Born charges.

In §B.1 EPP built two electron–phonon vertices by hand: the bare rigid-ion vertex $g^0$ (E.107) and the electrostatic polar vertex $g^{\mathcal L}$ (E.131′, §Z.3). In the second, the dipole field of a displaced ion was screened by an $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ that the author himself calls ad hoc. EPP §4 changes strategy. It no longer computes the potential that a displaced ion produces. Electrons, ions and the electromagnetic field become three dynamical systems in one Lagrangian, and the field carries every interaction between them.

Physical picture

Treat the electromagnetic field as the medium through which everything talks. A moving electron acts on the field through its current. A displaced ion acts on it through the current of its induced dipole. Apart from the frozen crystal potential felt by the electrons and the short-range springs between ions, nothing acts on anything else directly. What Giustino calls the Coulomb interaction is the part of the field that follows the charges instantly (the longitudinal part). The rest is light, which travels at $c$. An electron–phonon interaction is then a two-step process: electron to field, field to ion.

The temporal gauge

EPP §4 opens by setting the scalar potential to zero, $\Phi=0$. Both fields then come from the vector potential alone:

$$\bE=-\frac1c\frac{\partial\bA}{\partial t}, \tag{E.132}$$
$$\bB=\nabla\times\bA. \tag{E.133}$$

This is the temporal (Weyl) gauge. It is convenient for three reasons.

  • One field carries everything. Split $\bA=\bA_l+\bA_t$ into a curl-free part along $\bq$ and a divergence-free part transverse to it (cf. E.218). The longitudinal field $\bE_l=-\frac1c\dot\bA_l$ is the Coulomb field of the charges, and $\bA_t$ carries radiation. Coulomb and light become two polarizations of one object. That is what later allows a single photon propagator, longitudinal plus transverse, $D^0=D^{0l}+D^{0t}$ (§B.6).
  • Electrons and ions couple in the same way. With no scalar potential there is no $\rho\Phi$ term. Every coupling has the form "current times $\bA$". Electronic and ionic currents therefore enter the photon self-energy on an equal footing (§B.5).
  • Coulomb is derived, not postulated. Giustino takes the instantaneous interaction $v(\br-\br')\delta(t-t')$ as given. Here the Coulomb energy comes out of the energy of the longitudinal field. The Coulomb gauge $\nabla\cdot\bA=0$ describes the same physics in a different bookkeeping: $\bA$ is purely transverse, and $\Phi$ has no dynamics of its own, being fixed at every instant by Poisson's equation. The temporal gauge instead keeps the Coulomb field inside $\bA$, next to the photons.

The price is that Gauss's law is not one of the Euler–Lagrange equations. It is a constraint on the initial state that the dynamics preserves. §B.4 deals with it, and that is where $\frac1{8\pi}\int\bE_l^2$ turns into the Coulomb energy.

Conventions. From here on EPP uses Gaussian units, a signed electron charge ($e<0$) and Born charges with the dimension of charge (see the Part B introduction). The PDF writes dot products without a dot ($\mathbf v_e\bA$); we insert them.

The Lagrangian, term by term

For valence electrons at positions $\br_e$ with velocities $\mathbf v_e$, and ions displaced by $Q_{ia\mu}$ (cell $i$, atom $a$, direction $\mu$) from their sites, EPP writes

$$\begin{aligned}L={}&\underbrace{\sum_e\left[\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}-V_{\rm cr}(\br_e)\right]}_{(1)}+\underbrace{\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q^2_{ia\mu}}{2}}_{(2)}-\underbrace{\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}}_{(3)}\\&+\underbrace{\int\frac{d\br}{8\pi}\left\{\bE^2(\br)-\bB^2(\br)\right\}}_{(4)}+\underbrace{\frac ec\sum_e\mathbf v_e\cdot\bA(\br_e)}_{(5)}+\underbrace{\frac1c\sum_{ia}\dot{\bp}_{ia}\cdot\bA(\bR^0_{ia})}_{(6)},\end{aligned} \tag{E.134}$$

where the dipole of atom $a$ in cell $i$ and the frozen crystal potential are

$$p_{ia\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}, \tag{E.135}$$
$$V_{\rm cr}(\br)=\sum_{ia}v_a(\br-\bR^0_{ia}). \tag{E.136}$$
TermWhat it describesWhat it becomes (§B.4)
(1)Noninteracting valence electrons in the frozen local pseudopotential $v_a$ (nucleus plus core) at the equilibrium sitesBand states, taken as Kohn–Sham $E_{n\bk}$ in $H_0$ (E.236)
(2)+(3)Harmonic ions with force constants $k$ (E.5)Bare phonons $\hbar\omega_\alpha(\bq)$ from short-range force constants
(4)The free electromagnetic field, all polarizationsTransverse photons $\hbar cq$ (E.214) and the Coulomb energy $\frac1{8\pi}\int\bE_l^2$ (E.235′)

(6)The ionic polarization current $\dot\bp$ coupled to $\bA$Ion–photon vertices $Z^*\pi\cdot\bA$ and $Z^*Z^*AA$; electron–ion and ion–ion Coulomb

EPP reads term (5) as "the electron–electron interaction" and term (6) as "the electron–phonon interaction", and remarks on the symmetry of the two "current times vector potential" bindings. Taken literally, each term couples one kind of matter to the field. They describe interactions between particles only because the same $\bA$ appears in both.

Why the ions couple through their Born dipoles

In this model the static charge of the ions is already inside $V_{\rm cr}$, which acts on the electrons directly. The field does not see an ion at rest at its site. It sees only how the charge distribution changes when the ion moves, and to linear order that change is a dipole per atom, $\bp=\hat Z^*\bQ$ (E.135). Here $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}=\partial p_\mu/\partial R_{a\nu}=\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\partial P_\mu/\partial R_{a\nu}$ is the Born effective charge of EPP §3 (E.110, §B.1). A changing dipole is a current. Summed over the crystal, $\dot\bP(\br)=\sum_{ia}\dot\bp_{ia}\,\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia})$ (E.167′) is the polarization current, and term (6) couples it to $\bA$ exactly as term (5) couples the electron current. A displaced ion behaves like a particle that carries a tensor charge $Z^*$. Three consequences follow.

  • Velocity form equals dipole energy. Since $\bA$ is taken at the fixed site $\bR^0_{ia}$, $\frac1c\dot\bp\cdot\bA=\bp\cdot\bE+\frac1c\frac{d}{dt}(\bp\cdot\bA)$. A total time derivative does not change the equations of motion, so term (6) is equivalent to the Lagrangian term $+\bp\cdot\bE$, i.e. to the familiar dipole energy $U=-\bp\cdot\bE$. If you compute optical spectra, this is the same equivalence as between velocity ($\bp\cdot\bA$) and length ($\br\cdot\bE$) gauge optical matrix elements.
  • Acoustic sum rule. $\sum_aZ^*_a=0$, so a rigid translation of a cell carries no dipole. Acoustic modes at $\bq\to0$ do not couple to the field.
  • The Born charge is the right object. $Z^*$ is the response at zero macroscopic field (the transverse charge). It does not include the electronic screening of the dipole's own long-range field. In EPP that screening has to come from the electrons' own coupling to $\bA$, term (5), instead of the $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ put in by hand in §B.1. EPP regenerates it from the electronic photon self-energy (§B.5, §B.9).

There is no electron–phonon term

No term of (E.134) contains both $\br_e$ and $Q_{ia\mu}$. There is no $g$. Electrons and ions interact only by sharing $\bA$, through two channels:

  • Longitudinal. A longitudinal polarization wave carries bound charge $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\bP$. The electron charge and the ionic bound charge meet in the energy of $\bA_l$, which §B.4 shows is their instantaneous Coulomb energy (E.235′). This is the bare, unscreened Fröhlich coupling, i.e. (E.131′) with $\hat\epsilon^\infty\to1$.
  • Transverse. An electron current emits a photon through term (5), and a polarization current absorbs it through term (6). This channel is retarded in EPP's sense of the word (finite $c$) and has no counterpart in Giustino. It matters only near the light cone, $q\lesssim q_c=\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega/c\approx6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$ for a GaAs phonon (§X.1).

The same field also carries the electron–electron interaction (term 5 twice) and the ion–ion interaction (term 6 twice). The latter is the dipole–dipole force behind the LO–TO splitting (§B.7). The polar, long-range part of Giustino's vertex $g$ reappears only later, as a composite: electron current, photon dressed by the electrons, ionic current (§B.8).

What the Lagrangian leaves out

  • Short-range coupling. $V_{\rm cr}$ is frozen at the equilibrium sites, so the deformation-potential vertex of EPP §2 (E.107–E.108) is absent. Only the polar, long-range channel is rebuilt from the field. A complete theory would add a screened short-range $g^{\mathcal S}$.
  • Double counting. $k$ must be the short-range (analytic) force constants, because the dipole–dipole part is regenerated through $\bA_l$. The same holds for the electron–electron Coulomb term and the Hartree–xc part already inside DFT bands (discussed in §B.4).
  • Dipole approximation. Each atom carries a point dipole, with $\bA$ taken at its equilibrium site $\bR^0_{ia}$. The printed (E.134) and (E.141)–(E.143) take it at the cell origin $\bR^0_i$, while $V_{\rm cr}$ uses $\bR^0_{ia}$; that drops the basis phase $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\cdot\br^0_a}$, the factor Giustino keeps in the Verdi–Giustino vertex (his footnote 7). This section shows the corrected forms (§Z.3). The difference is negligible at photon wave vectors but matters at finite $q$ in cells with more than one atom, for local fields and for acoustic modes (§B.1). Ions also carry no quadrupoles and feel no magnetic force.
  • Model electrons. Nonrelativistic and spinless, with a local pseudopotential.

Electrons: the Lorentz force

EPP §4.1 checks that term (5) is the correct coupling. The Euler–Lagrange equation for electron $e$ is

$$\nabla_eL-\frac{d}{dt}\nabla_{\mathbf v_e}L=0. \tag{E.137}$$

Because $\bA_e\equiv\bA(\br_e,t)$ is evaluated at the moving electron, its total time derivative has a convective part. After a vector identity, the result (unnumbered, EPP p. 27) is the Lorentz equation,

$$m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\br_e)+e\bE_e+\frac ec\,\mathbf v_e\times\bB_e .$$

In the temporal gauge, $e\bE_e$ contains the Coulomb force of all other charges, through $\bE_l$, as well as the radiation field. The canonical momentum $\partial L/\partial\mathbf v_e=m_e\mathbf v_e+\frac ec\bA_e$ differs from the kinetic one. That difference is the origin of the minimal coupling $(\bp-\frac ec\bA)^2/2m_e$ of §B.4 (E.182).

Derivation: from (E.137) to the Lorentz equation

Inserting (E.134) into (E.137), only terms (1) and (5) depend on $\br_e$ and $\mathbf v_e$:

$$\begin{aligned}\nabla_eL&=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}(\br_e)+\frac ec\nabla_e(\mathbf v_e\cdot\bA_e),\\ \frac{d}{dt}\nabla_{\mathbf v_e}L&=m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}+\frac ec\frac{d\bA_e}{dt}.\end{aligned} \tag{E.138}$$

The electron moves, so $\frac{d\bA_e}{dt}=\frac{\partial\bA_e}{\partial t}+(\mathbf v_e\cdot\nabla_e)\bA_e$. Then

$$m_e\frac{d\mathbf v_e}{dt}=-\nabla_eV_{\rm cr}-\frac ec\frac{\partial\bA_e}{\partial t}+\frac ec\Big[\nabla_e(\mathbf v_e\cdot\bA_e)-(\mathbf v_e\cdot\nabla_e)\bA_e\Big].$$

With $\mathbf a\times(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)=\mathbf b(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)-(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)\mathbf c$, where $\nabla_e$ acts only on $\bA$ and $\mathbf v_e$ is an independent variable, the bracket is $\mathbf v_e\times(\nabla_e\times\bA_e)$. With (E.132)–(E.133), $-\frac1c\partial_t\bA_e=\bE_e$ and $\nabla_e\times\bA_e=\bB_e$, which gives the Lorentz equation.

Ions: a lattice driven by the electric field

For the ions, EPP §4.2 writes

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q_{ia\mu}}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}}=0. \tag{E.139}$$

Term (6) depends on $\dot Q$, not on $Q$, so it enters through the canonical momentum. Its time derivative is $\dot\bA$ at a fixed site, which (E.132) turns into an electric force:

$$m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}+\sum_{jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}E_\nu(\bR^0_{ia}). \tag{E.143$'$}$$

Each ion is a harmonic oscillator driven by the field through its Born charge. In EPP's words, the field "couples to the dipole moment $p_{ia\mu}$ and not to the coordinate $Q_{ia\mu}$". The generalized force $F_{a\mu}=\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}E_\nu$ is the standard alternative definition of the Born charge, $Z^*_{\nu\mu}=\partial F_\mu/\partial E_\nu$. Note the transposed index order relative to (E.135). Since $\bR^0_{ia}$ does not move, there is no convective term and no magnetic force on the ions; the dipole approximation drops forces of order $v/c$.

Derivation: (E.139) to (E.143′)

Only term (3) depends on $Q$. With the symmetry $k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}=k_{jb\nu,ia\mu}$,

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q_{ia\mu}}=-\sum_{jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}. \tag{E.140}$$

Terms (2) and (6) depend on $\dot Q$. Since $\dot p_{ia\nu}=\sum_\mu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}\dot Q_{ia\mu}$, we get $\partial L/\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}=m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}+\frac1c\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}A_\nu(\bR^0_{ia})$, which is the ionic canonical momentum (E.183′) of §B.4. Its time derivative at the fixed site is

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}}=m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}+\frac1c\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}\dot A_\nu(\bR^0_{ia}), \tag{E.141}$$ $$m_a\ddot Q_{ia\mu}=-\sum_{jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{jb\nu}-\frac1c\sum_\nu Z^*_{a\nu\mu}\dot A_\nu(\bR^0_{ia}). \tag{E.142}$$

With $-\frac1c\dot A_\nu=E_\nu$ (E.132) this is (E.143′).

Take a diatomic cell at $\bq\to0$ with $Z^*_a=-Z^*_b\equiv Z^*$ and the lattice-summed spring $k$ of §B.3. Dividing each of the two equations (E.143′) by its mass and subtracting, the relative coordinate $u=Q_a-Q_b$ obeys $M\ddot u+ku=Z^*E$, with reduced mass $M$ and bare optical frequency $\omega_{\rm O}^2=k/M$. Add a phenomenological damping $\gamma$ (anharmonic decay, which E.134 does not contain) and drive with $E(t)=\mathrm{Re}\,E_0e^{-i\omega t}$. Then

$$u(\omega)=\frac{Z^*E_0/M}{\omega_{\rm O}^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega},\qquad \dot P=-i\omega\,\frac{Z^*u}{\Omega_{\rm uc}} .$$

Below resonance $u$ follows $E$ in phase. At resonance it lags by $90^\circ$, so the polarization current $\dot P$ is in phase with $E$ and the field does work on the lattice. Above resonance $u$ is in antiphase. In the Lorentz oscillator model, a bound valence electron obeys the same equation with $Z^*\to e$, $M\to m_e$ and a resonance in the eV range (this model is not part of E.134, whose electrons are band electrons). A free carrier has no restoring force at all. The figure compares the three.

Bridge to Giustino

Giustino starts from electrons and nuclei interacting through the instantaneous Coulomb interaction, with no vector potential (G §IV.A, Eqs. 62–67, §A.5). His working Hamiltonian (G Eq. 1, §A.2) has bands, phonons and an explicit vertex $g$ computed by DFPT (G Eq. 38). That $g$ already contains the Coulomb field of the displaced ions, screened by the electrons. EPP's Lagrangian has no $g$. The field is a dynamical variable whose longitudinal part reproduces Giustino's Coulomb interaction (§B.4) and whose transverse part adds retardation in EPP's sense, the finite speed of light. (In Giustino, "retardation" means the frequency dependence of the electronic screening.) Light does appear in Giustino, but only as an external classical field coupled through the electron velocity (G Eq. 200, §A.9). That is the same $\frac ec\mathbf v\cdot\bA$ form as term (5), without the field's own dynamics.

Note on the source

  • "Thus in this way we have plausibly closed the scheme" (p. 26) holds for the polar channel only, since the short-range coupling is absent. The remark on the symmetry of terms (5) and (6) refers to an external "Lecture 9" that is not part of the manuscript.
  • (E.141)–(E.143) carry the transposed charge $Z^*_{a\nu\mu}$, while (E.135) has $Z^*_{a\mu\nu}$. Both are correct.
  • As printed, (E.134) and (E.141)–(E.143) take $\bA$ and $\bE$ at the cell origin $\bR^0_i$, i.e. every dipole of the cell at one point. Following the basis-phase correction (§Z.3), this section takes them at the atomic site $\bR^0_{ia}$: (E.134), (E.141) and (E.142) are corrected in place and keep their numbers, (E.143′) replaces (E.143), and the canonical momentum becomes (E.183′). For a field that is uniform over a cell, as in the figure above and in §B.3, nothing changes.
  • The vector identity printed on p. 27 lacks its parentheses (the derivation above uses the correct form). This slip and the unannounced changes of units and of the sign of $e$ are listed in Z.2.

The ionic equation (E.143′) is tested on a diatomic polar crystal in §B.3, where it yields $\epsilon(\omega)$, LO and TO modes and the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller relation. The field's own Euler–Lagrange equations come next, in §B.4: the Ampère–Maxwell law with source $\bj_{\rm e}+\dot\bP$, Gauss's law from continuity, the canonical momenta and the quantized photons.
