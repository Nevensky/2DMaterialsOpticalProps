Z.3Appendix · corrections to the EPP draft

Corrections to three results

Three issues in the EPP draft go beyond typos: the Born-charge dipoles sit at the cell origin instead of at the atoms, E.410 is read as the LO–TO splitting, and the electrostatic Fröhlich vertices lack a factor $e$. For each, the corrected equations, what they change downstream and what they leave alone, and the GaAs numbers that test them.

Of the slips listed in §Z.2, three change equations or the reading of a displayed result. Each correction was derived from the manuscript's own definitions, checked by two independent skeptical reviewers against the page images, revised until every objection was answered, and tested numerically: dimensions, limits, GaAs, random three-atom cells and Giustino's (G.180). Where two corrections touch the same display, the merged form is given at the end. Every section of this primer that uses an affected equation shows the corrected form and links here.

Units are the manuscript's. EPP §3 is in SI units and writes the electron charge as $-e$ with $e\gt0$. From §4 on the units are Gaussian and $e=-|e|$ is the signed electron charge. Born charges carry the charge, $Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}=|e|\,Z^*_{\kappa,\mu\nu}$, with Giustino's dimensionless $Z^*_\kappa$, and $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$ is a number density.

One tag convention is used for every corrected and every new equation, here and in the LaTeX file. E.n′ is the corrected form of the printed equation (n) and replaces it; a combined tag such as E.241′, 314′ replaces two printed equations with the same content. E p.N′ replaces an unnumbered display on page N (E p.Na′, b′, … when a page has several); the LaTeX file writes it (pN′). E.na, E.nb, … are new equations, inserted after (n). In the LaTeX file every tagged display is one equation* environment with one explicit \tag; these environments never step the equation counter, and the file itself contains no \stepcounter. When a primed display is pasted in place of a printed equation, \stepcounter{equation} goes directly after it (once at each place of a combined tag), so that later equations keep their printed numbers; page-tagged and lettered displays are pasted as they are. An equation whose only error is one symbol, or that a stated rule fixes, is corrected in place and keeps its number: the LaTeX file gives the rule instead of a display, and this page shows such equations corrected under their printed numbers.

1. Born dipoles sit at the atoms: the basis phase

The problem. EPP §3 places the dipole of atom a in cell $i$ at the cell origin $\bR^0_i$ (E.121–E.123), not at the atom, $\bR^0_{i{\rm a}}=\bR^0_i+\br^0_{\rm a}$ (E.94). The normal modes of E.7, E.83 and E.242 attach the Bloch phase to the cell, as Giustino's do, so every vertex built from these dipoles loses the basis phase $e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_{\rm a}}$ that §2 keeps for the pseudopotential (E.101) and Giustino keeps in (G.180). Through the ionic current vertex the omission reaches every equation of §§5–8.

Corrected equations. The polar vertex of §3 (SI units) becomes

$$\begin{aligned}g^{\mathcal L}_{nm\alpha}(\bk,\bq)={}&\frac{4\pi i}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\frac{e}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{\bG}\sum_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_{\rm a}\omega_\alpha(\bq)}}\\&\times\frac{(q_\mu+G_\mu)Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}e_{{\rm a}\nu\alpha}(\bq)\,e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_{\rm a}}}{(\bq+\bG)\hat\epsilon^\infty(\bq+\bG)}\,\rho_{n\bk,m\bk-\bq}(-\bG),\end{aligned}\tag{E.131$'$}$$

with $e/4\pi\epsilon_0\to e$ in Gaussian units. This is (G.180). It follows from (E.121′)–(E.124′), in which the potential of the displaced atom carries $e^{i\bq(\br-\bR^0_i-\br^0_{\rm a})}$ and the left label of E.121 becomes $\nu$, the direction of the displacement. In §§4–6 one rule does the rest: $\bA(\bR^0_i)\to\bA(\bR^0_{i{\rm a}})$ and $\delta(\br-\bR^0_i)\to\delta(\br-\bR^0_{i{\rm a}})$ inside the atom sums. The polarization (Gaussian, like everything below) becomes

$$P_\mu(\br)=\sum_{i{\rm a}\nu}Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}Q_{i{\rm a}\nu}\,\delta(\br-\bR^0_{i{\rm a}}),\tag{E.241$'$, 314$'$}$$

and the ionic current vertex, which carries the ions into the photon self-energy (E.297′, E.352′), becomes

$$j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG)=\sum_{{\rm a}\mu}\frac{Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}}{\sqrt{m_{\rm a}}}\,e_{{\rm a}\mu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_{\rm a}}\equiv\sum_{\rm a}j^{\rm a}_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq+\bG).\tag{E.295$'$}$$

From E.380 on the manuscript keeps only $\bG=\bG'=0$. There the two unnumbered displays after E.385 are replaced by

$$j_{\mu\sigma}(\bq)=\sum_{{\rm a}\alpha}\frac{Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\alpha}}{\sqrt{m_{\rm a}}}\,e_{{\rm a}\alpha\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{-i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}\tag{E p.86a$'$}$$

and its conjugate (E p.86b′), with $e^*_{{\rm b}\beta\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{+i\bq\br^0_{\rm b}}$. With this vertex, every equation from E.385 to E.472 that contains $j_{\mu\sigma}$ only as a symbol keeps its printed form. Where the vertex is written out (E p.93a′, E p.93b′, E.418′, E.430′, E.447′, E.448′ and the merged vertices below), $e^{-i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}$ stands next to $e_{{\rm a}\mu\sigma}(\bq)$ and $e^{+i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}$ next to $e^*_{{\rm a}\mu\sigma}(\bq)$. The new (E.131a) states that only the product $e_{{\rm a}\nu\alpha}(\bq)\,e^{-i(\bq+\bG)\br^0_{\rm a}}$ is unchanged when an atom is assigned to a neighbouring cell, and (E.131b) expands the vertex for a long acoustic wave (both in §B.1). After E.454 a matrix form of the electrostatic result is added,

$$\begin{aligned}\tilde\Omega^2_{{\rm a}\mu,{\rm b}\nu}(\bq)={}&\tilde\Omega^2_{0,{\rm a}\mu,{\rm b}\nu}(\bq)\\&+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\sqrt{m_{\rm a}m_{\rm b}}}\sum_{\bG}\frac{[(\bq+\bG)\hat Z^*_{\rm a}]_\mu\,[(\bq+\bG)\hat Z^*_{\rm b}]_\nu}{(\bq+\bG)\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\cdot(\bq+\bG)}\,e^{i(\bq+\bG)(\br^0_{\rm a}-\br^0_{\rm b})},\end{aligned}\tag{E.454a}$$

with $[(\bq+\bG)\hat Z^*_{\rm a}]_\mu=\sum_\alpha(q_\alpha+G_\alpha)Z^*_{{\rm a}\alpha\mu}$ and $\tilde\Omega^2_0$ the bare short-range dynamical matrix. Projected on mode $\sigma$ at $\bG=0$ it is E.454. The full sum is the dipole–dipole matrix of Gonze and Lee once the on-site self term is removed by Ewald regularization; with every dipole at the cell origin its ${\rm a}\neq{\rm b}$ terms would not converge either. The other corrected displays (E.121′–E.124′, E.143′, E.167′, E.183′, E.186′, E.271′, 315′, E.272′, E.274′, the ionic self-energies of §5 from E.290′ to E.309′, E.352′ and the proof of E.351 on pp. 76–77) and the new E.354a and E.399a are indexed in the LaTeX file. The sign of E.15 and E.35 must be corrected with it (§Z.2, item 3): with the printed sign the eigenvectors come out complex conjugated, and the phase would double the spurious acoustic term instead of removing it.

What does not change. At $\bG=0$ only the relative phases $e^{-i\bq(\br^0_{\rm a}-\br^0_{\rm b})}=1+O(q|\br^0_{\rm a}-\br^0_{\rm b}|)$ are observable. Unchanged are the $q\to0$, $\bG=0$ limit for optical modes (Fröhlich's vertex, G Eq. 7), the uniform-field model of §4.2.1 (E.144–E.165), E.406–E.410 at $\bq=0$ together with E.410′ and E.410e below, the LO–TO splitting (the $q\to0$ limit of E.454), the polariton region $q\lesssim q_c$ (in GaAs $q_c|\br^0_{\rm As}-\br^0_{\rm Ga}|\approx10^{-4}$, and the oscillator strength changes by about $5\times10^{-9}$), the sum rule E.309 with E.310–E.311 on the diagonal $\bG=\bG'$, and all electronic and photon-propagator equations.

What changes, with GaAs numbers.

  • Finite $q$ in cells with more than one atom. With $|\br^0_{\rm As}-\br^0_{\rm Ga}|=2.45$ Å the phase difference along [111] is 0.24 rad at $q=0.1$ Å$^{-1}$ and 1.22 rad at 0.5 Å$^{-1}$. In a central-force model with eigenvectors computed at finite $q$, the LO coupling $|g^{\mathcal L}|^2$ and the dipolar shift of E.454 change by 0.5–0.8% at 0.1 Å$^{-1}$ and by 12–22% at 0.5 Å$^{-1}$, up or down depending on the direction and on the cell of the printed form.
  • Every $\bG\neq0$ element, where $e^{-i\bG\br^0_{\rm a}}$ survives even at $\bq=0$.
  • Acoustic modes. Without the phase the vertex contains a spurious term $\propto\sum_{\rm a}(\bq\cdot\br^0_{\rm a})\hat Z^*_{\rm a}\bu$, which gives $|g|^2\propto1/q$, the scaling of a piezoelectric coupling. It depends on the choice of cell and survives in centrosymmetric crystals. In GaAs it corresponds to a piezoelectric constant of 0.6–1.8 C/m² along [111], depending on the cell, and 1.04 C/m² along [100], where the true coupling vanishes by symmetry. The corrected vertex keeps only the internal-strain dipole: about 1.2 C/m² along [111] in the central-force model, and zero along [100]. In the pole condition E.410c below, the phase-less vertex would give LA[111], for a rigid translation, an extra dipolar term of 0.8, 2.5 or 5.9 km/s in $\sqrt{\Delta\omega^2}/q$ for three choices of cell, comparable to the sound velocity of 5.4 km/s; with E p.86a′ a rigid acoustic translation has $\bj_\sigma\propto\sum_{\rm a}\hat Z^*_{\rm a}\bu=0$.

Where the primer uses it. §B.1 derives the phase and tests the choice of cell in a figure; §A.4 discusses (G.180) and Giustino's footnote 7; the ionic self-energy of §B.5, the phonon self-energy of §B.7 and the vertices of §B.8 and §B.9 use E.295′ and E p.86a′; the Lagrangian and Hamiltonians of §B.2 and §B.4, the interaction of §B.6 and the sheet self-energies of §B.10 also put the dipoles at the atomic sites.

2. E.410 is the upper phonon polariton at $q\to0$, not the LO–TO splitting

The problem. EPP §7.1 presents E.410 as the LO–TO splitting, "completely analogous to the non-analytic dynamical matrix correction done by Quantum Espresso." The algebra of E.406–E.410 is right, but the limit is a different one: $\bq=0$ at finite $c$, deep inside the light cone, where the vacuum propagator E.408 is isotropic, and with the electrons left out. Every polar mode, transverse or longitudinal, is then shifted by the same unscreened $\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2$. That is the $q\to0$ end point of Huang's upper phonon polariton in a crystal without electrons.

Corrected and new equations. The right side of E.410 is unchanged; the left side now names the limit:

$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\bq\to0)\Big|_{c\ \text{finite}}=\omega^2_\sigma(\bq=0)+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_\alpha\big|j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq=0)\big|^2\tag{E.410$'$}$$

A new EPP §7.1.1 keeps $\bq$ finite and models the electrons of an insulator by $\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{jj\mu\nu}\simeq(\epsilon_\infty-1)\,\omega^2\delta_{\mu\nu}/4\pi c$. The Dyson equation for $D^{\rm e}$ is then solved in closed form (E.410a), the diagonal phonon self-energy follows (E.410b), and its pole condition is

$$\omega^2=\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\left[\frac{|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2}{\epsilon_\infty}+\frac{\omega^2\,|(1-\hat\bq\hat\bq)\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2}{\epsilon_\infty\,\omega^2-c^2q^2}\right],\tag{E.410c}$$

with $j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq)$ the phase-corrected vertex E p.86a′ (the full $3s\times3s$ form is E.410d). The longitudinal term contains no $c$. The transverse term contains $c$ and $q$ only through $cq$, so the limits $\bq\to0$ and $c\to\infty$ do not commute. For $\bq\to0$ at finite $c$,

$$\tilde\omega^2_\sigma=\omega^2_\sigma+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\epsilon_\infty}\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\sigma}|^2 ,\tag{E.410e}$$

while for $cq\gg\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega$

$$\begin{aligned}\tilde\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)&=\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}\,\epsilon_\infty}\,|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma|^2\\&=\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+\frac{W^{\rm e}(\bq,0)}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\Big|\sum_\alpha q_\alpha j_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq)\Big|^2 .\end{aligned}\tag{E.410f}$$

E.410f with $W^{\rm e}(\bq,0)=4\pi/(q^2\epsilon_\infty)$ is E.454, and with a tensor $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ it is the standard non-analytic term. For a cubic diatomic crystal E.410c gives one LO mode and Huang's two transverse branches (E.410g), with the crossover at $q_c=\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega/c$ (E.410h).

Two independent differences. E.410′ differs from the electrostatic E.454 in two ways, and they must be kept apart. Electronic screening sets the size of the shift. In GaAs it takes the $q\to0$ frequency from 55.6 meV (E.410′) to 35.9 meV (E.410e). This is what separates E.410 from the measured LO at 36.2 meV (the remaining 0.3 meV reflects the literature Born charge), and it comes from the electron-free approximation that §7.1 states ("Suppose that we neglect the interaction with electrons"). The order of limits decides only whether the transverse modes share the shift. The LO frequency is the same whichever limit is taken first; only the TO modes move, from 35.9 meV at $q\to0$ with finite $c$ to 33.3 meV with $c\to\infty$ first.

Downstream. No later equation uses E.410, so nothing downstream inherits a change. The correction retitles §7.1 "Lowest self-energy correction and the phonon polariton at $\mathbf q=0$", replaces its Quantum Espresso sentence, fixes the photon/phonon slips before E.406–E.407 and $\Omega^0_\sigma\to\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}$ in E.406, adds E.410′ and §7.1.1, retitles §7.3 "… and the LO–TO splitting", and adds after E.454 a paragraph identifying it as the standard non-analytic term, which shifts only modes with $\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\sigma\neq0$, reduced by $1/(\hat\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\cdot\hat\bq)$. Unchanged: E.406–E.409, E.411–E.454 apart from the other two corrections, the 2D equations of §8, and the classical picture of pp. 33–35. In GaAs $q_c=5.6\times10^{-5}$ Å$^{-1}$, about $5\times10^{-5}$ of $2\pi/a$; at $q=10\,q_c$ the TO mode lies only 0.03 meV below $\omega_{\rm TO}$. Outside $q\lesssim q_c$ retardation is negligible and E.454 applies. Visible backscattering Raman, at $q\approx10^{-2}$ Å$^{-1}$, sees the split TO and LO of E.410f; infrared spectroscopy and near-forward Raman scattering probe the polariton region.

Where the primer uses it. §B.7 derives E.410a–E.410h and plots the polariton branches against the order of limits; §B.9 derives E.454; §X.1 gives the light-cone argument from the propagator side; §A.4 has the electrostatic LO–TO splitting.

3. The charge lost in the continuity equation: a factor $e$

The problem. On p. 94 the draft writes continuity as $\dot\rho_{\rm e}=-\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}$. It sets the rate of a number density ($\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^+\Psi$, E.239, E.420) equal to the divergence of a charge current (E.419). E.428 then equates the dimensionless density vertex $\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$ with $-\hbar\bq\cdot\bj/\Delta E$, which contains $e$; for free electrons the right side is exactly $e$, not 1. Both electrostatic vertices obtained from E.428, the bare one on p. 95 and the boxed screened one on p. 98, therefore lack a factor $e$ and have the dimension of a potential. This is the $e^2$ "missing" in the author's margin note on p. 96: one $e$ is in EPP's $Z^*$, the other is the $e$ of E.428. Nothing is missing in $D$.

Corrected equations. Continuity for a number density reads

$$e\,\dot\rho_{\rm e}(\br,t)=-\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}(\br,t),\tag{E p.94$'$}$$

so E.422 becomes $e[\rho_{\rm e},H^{\rm e}_0]_-=-i\hbar\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}$ (E.422′), the left sides of E.423–E.425 and of the displays between them carry $e$, and the charge–current relation is

$$e\,\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=-\sum_\mu\hbar q_\mu\,\frac{j^\mu_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}}{E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}}.\tag{E.428$'$}$$

The fix keeps $\rho_{\rm e}$ a number density, as the diamagnetic $\bA^2$ terms (E.237, E.279, E.321) require, and writes $e\rho_{\rm e}$ wherever a charge is meant. The same convention corrects Gauss's law in §4,

$$\nabla\cdot\bE=4\pi\big(e\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion}\big),\tag{E.234$'$}$$

and likewise the Coulomb energy (E.235′) and the Coulomb terms of (E.237′) and (E.312′); the continuity display on p. 38 and E.179–E.180 are corrected in place. The same slip leaves the 2D electronic bubble on p. 101 without $e^2$. It enters E.435 and E.461–E.470 and is a charge–charge correlator:

$$\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}(\bQ,\omega)=\frac{2e^2}{S}\sum_{\mathbf K\in{\rm SBZ}}\sum_{nm}\frac{f_{n\mathbf K}-f_{m\mathbf K+\bQ}}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\mathbf K}-E_{m\mathbf K+\bQ}+i\eta}\,\big|\rho_{n\mathbf K,m\mathbf K+\bQ}\big|^2.\tag{E p.101$'$}$$

With it $v_Q\Pi^{\rm e,0}_{\rho\rho}$ is dimensionless ($v_Q=2\pi/Q$), and for a 2D electron gas E.470 gives Stern's $\epsilon^{\rm e}_{\rm 2D}(Q,0)=1+2m^*e^2/(\hbar^2Q)$ for $Q\le2k_F$.

What changes, what does not. Changed: the Heisenberg evolution after E.420 (E p.93c′, a missing $\hbar$), E p.94′, E.422′, E.428′, the vertices on pp. 95, 96 and 98 and the sentence at the top of p. 99 (merged forms below), E p.101′, and for the same convention E.234′, E.235′, E.237′, E.312′. Unchanged: the electrodynamic vertices E.414–E.417, E.429–E.430, E.447–E.448 and E.431–E.446, where the charge sits in $j^\mu$, and every result written with $j^\mu$, $j_{\alpha\sigma}$ or $W^{\rm e}$, including E.452–E.454 and E.469–E.472. With the corrected vertices $\sum g^{\rm dyn}\chi^0g^{\rm dyn,0}$ reproduces E.413 and $\Omega^0_{\sigma\sigma}+\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}$ reproduces E.452, the screened Gonze–Lee term; with the printed vertices both fail by $e^2$.

GaAs. The screened vertex E p.98′ with $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\to\epsilon_\infty$ and Giustino's (G.180) evaluated in SI give the same $\sqrt N|g|=304$ meV for the LO mode at $q=0.1$ Å$^{-1}$ (leading order in $q$, with $\hbar\omega_{\rm LO}=36.2$ meV in the zero-point amplitude). With finite-$q$ eigenvectors and the basis phase the common value is 303.0 meV along [111], for either cell, and 304.0 meV along [100]. The printed vertices have the dimension of energy per charge, so no such comparison is possible with them. Fröhlich's vertex with $1/\epsilon_\infty-1/\epsilon(0)$ from Lyddane–Sachs–Teller gives 319.8 meV; the ratio is $Z^*/Z^*_{\rm LST}=2.07/2.18$, a property of the input data.

Where the primer uses it. §B.8 (the continuity identity and the electrostatic limit), §B.9, §B.4 (Gauss's law and the Coulomb energy), §B.2 and §B.6 (the Coulomb terms of E.235′ and E.312′) and §B.10 (the 2D bubble).

Where the corrections meet: the vertices on pp. 95–99

The factor $e$ multiplies the prefactor of a vertex and the basis phase multiplies its atom summand. The two operations commute, and the final displays carry both. With E.428′ inserted in E.418′, the bare vertex on p. 95 is

$$\begin{aligned}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)={}&\frac{4\pi i\,e}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\rm a}\sum_{\mu\nu=xyz}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_{\rm a}\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\\&\times\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}e_{{\rm a}\nu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{-i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}}{q^2}\,\big[\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big]^*.\end{aligned}\tag{E p.95$'$}$$

The display and the margin note on p. 96 are replaced by Verdi and Giustino's long-range vertex with the basis phase of (G.180), written in EPP's notation at $\hat\epsilon^\infty=1$ and $\bG=0$, in Gaussian units, with $Z^*=|e|Z^{*\rm VG}$ and with their atomic positions $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa$ equal to the dipole positions $\br^0_{\rm a}$ of E.314′:

$$\begin{aligned}g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)={}&\frac{4\pi i\,|e|}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\rm a}\sum_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_{\rm a}\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\,\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}e_{{\rm a}\nu\sigma}(\bq)}{q^2}\\&\times\langle\phi_{m\bk+\bq}|e^{i\bq(\br-\br^0_{\rm a})}|\phi_{n\bk}\rangle.\end{aligned}\tag{E p.96$'$}$$

Since $\langle\phi_{m\bk+\bq}|e^{i\bq(\br-\br^0_{\rm a})}|\phi_{n\bk}\rangle=e^{-i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}[\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}]^*$,

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)=\frac{e}{|e|}\,g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)=-g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq).\tag{E.428a}$$

With E.428′ inserted in E.448′, the boxed screened vertex on p. 98 is

$$\begin{aligned}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)={}&\frac{4\pi i\,e}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\sum_{\rm a}\sum_{\mu\nu=x,y,z}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2Nm_{\rm a}\omega_\sigma(\bq)}}\\&\times\frac{q_\mu Z^*_{{\rm a}\mu\nu}e^*_{{\rm a}\nu\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\,\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq},\end{aligned}\tag{E p.98$'$}$$

and the sentence at the top of p. 99 ("This is again almost identical …") is replaced by

$$\begin{aligned}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)&=-\frac{e}{|e|}\,\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\,\big[g^0_{mn\sigma}(\bk,\bq)\big]^*\\&=-\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\,\Big[\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}(\bk,\bq,\omega)\Big]^*.\end{aligned}\tag{E.448a}$$

The signs, stated once. The two relations carry signs for different reasons. The second line of E.448a follows from E.415 and E.416 alone: both vertices carry the factor $+i$ and the propagator E.442 is real, so the screened vertex is minus the complex conjugate of the bare one, times $q^2/(\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\cdot\bq)$, for any complex $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}$ and either sign of $e$. The overall sign $e/|e|$ of E.428a, and of the first line of E.448a, compares a diagrammatic vertex with a Hamiltonian matrix element. It is a convention of the factorization E.411–E.416 (the factor $i$ and the order of the two vertices). It cannot be moved into the polarization vector, because E p.95′ and E p.96′ are linear in the same $e_{{\rm a}\nu\sigma}(\bq)$, and it cancels in the product $g^{\rm dyn}\chi^0g^{\rm dyn,0}$ of E.413, in $\Delta\Omega^{\rm e}$ and in $|g|^2$. Where E p.95′ is added to Hamiltonian matrix elements, such as a short-range $g^{\rm S}$ in Verdi–Giustino's $g=g^{\rm S}+g^{\rm L}$, use $g^0_{mn\sigma}=-\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\rm dyn,0}_{nm\sigma}$. For real $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}$ and electrons, E.448a is the complex conjugate of the Verdi–Giustino vertex with $\hat\epsilon^\infty$ replaced by the dynamical $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$, and the basis phase becomes $e^{+i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}$ with $e^*_{{\rm a}\nu\sigma}(\bq)$.

The phase in these relations. Both sides of E.428a and E.448a carry the same phase, so the relations hold exactly as they would without it, but only with $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa=\br^0_{\rm a}$ in E p.96′. A comparison at $\boldsymbol\tau_\kappa=0$, which dipoles at $\bR^0_i$ would force, is not the corrected one. The same holds for the GaAs check of 304 meV above.

E.410 at finite $q$. E.410b–E.410f use the vertex E p.86a′, so both strengths contain $e_{{\rm a}\beta\sigma}(\bq)\,e^{-i\bq\br^0_{\rm a}}$. At $\bq\to0$ the phase is 1, and 55.6, 35.9 and 33.3 meV are unchanged. At finite $\bq$ the strengths do not depend on the choice of cell, the acoustic branches get no dipolar shift beyond the internal-strain term of E.131b, and for $cq\gg\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}\,\omega$ E.410d in the atom basis is the $\bG=0$ term of E.454a.

The Hamiltonian. E.237 is touched by both corrections, through $\bA(\bR^0_{i{\rm a}})$ and $e\rho_{\rm e}$; its final form is (E.237′). E.235′ and E.312′ change by the factor $e$ only, since their $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\bP$ is built from E.241′, 314′.

Bridge to Giustino

All three corrections bring EPP closer to Giustino's review. (G.180) carries the basis phase, and his footnote 7 notes that Eq. 4 of Verdi and Giustino lacked it; the printed E.131, "exactly equal" to that Eq. 4, shares the omission (§A.4). With the factor $e$ and the phase restored, E p.98′ has the form of the Verdi–Giustino vertex, with the full $e^2$ and with $\epsilon^\infty\to\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega)$; the remaining differences (RPA screening without local fields, the bare $\omega_\sigma$ in the zero-point amplitude) are discussed in §B.8. And E.454 is the Gonze–Lee non-analytic term that Giustino cites in §VI.A.3, while E.410′ lives in the polariton region $q\lesssim q_c$, which his instantaneous Coulomb theory does not contain.

What the corrections do not fix

The dipoles are still points. The measured piezoelectric coupling of GaAs, $(2/\sqrt3)|e_{14}|\approx0.19$ C/m² for LA[111], is much smaller than the internal-strain term of the corrected vertex, because the clamped-ion electronic response largely cancels it; in the long-range vertex that response enters through dynamical quadrupoles, so a consistent $O(q)$ theory needs them. The $\bG$ sums of E.295′ and E.454a need a smooth cutoff and the removal of the on-site self term (§Z.2, item 29). From E.380 on only $\bG=\bG'=0$ is kept, which neglects the crystal local fields of electrons and ions. E.410a–E.410h model the electrons by a constant $\epsilon_\infty$, adequate for a gapped insulator only.
