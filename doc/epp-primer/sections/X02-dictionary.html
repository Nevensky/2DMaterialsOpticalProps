A dictionary between the two formalisms

Every object in Giustino's electron–phonon theory has an EPP counterpart, a generalization, or no counterpart yet. This section is the lookup table: concepts, symbols and units, and diagram glyphs, side by side.

The two papers describe the same crystal with different bookkeeping. Giustino's field theory has electrons, phonons and one interaction, the instantaneous Coulomb potential, which couples to charge densities (§A.5). EPP has electrons, bare short-range phonons and one field, the vector potential $\bA$, which couples to currents (§X.1). Three substitutions translate almost everything, and most of the tables below apply them over and over. They are derived in §X.1, §B.6 and §B.7; here they are collected for lookup.

Three substitutions

Lines. A Coulomb line $v(\bq)=4\pi/q^2$ (Gaussian units, charges in the vertices) becomes the longitudinal photon propagator, which acts on currents instead of charges:

$$D^{0l}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{4\pi c}{q^2}\,\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{\omega^2}. \tag{E.374}$$

Its electron-screened version is the $ll$ sector of the electron-dressed photon propagator $D^{\rm e}$. With $D^{\rm e}_{11}\equiv W^{\rm e}$ (Eq. E.437), the electrostatic limit reads

$$\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rm e}_{\mu\nu}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,q_\mu q_\nu\,W^{\rm e}(\bq,\omega). \tag{E.441}$$

Bubbles and vertices. Charge conservation, $\nabla\cdot\bj=i\omega\rho$, ties every density correlator to the current–current correlator. Giustino's polarization $P$ is therefore the longitudinal projection of EPP's photon self-energy, up to the factor $e^2$ (Giustino's densities count electrons; EPP's $\Pi^{\rm e}_{\rho\rho}$ correlates the charge density $e\rho^{\rm e}$, with $\rho^{\rm e}=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ a number density, §Z.3):

$$\Pi^i_{\rho\rho}(\br,\br',\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\,\Pi^i_{jj\mu\nu}(\br,\br',\omega);\qquad i={\rm e,\,ion}. \tag{E.350}$$

In $\bq$ space this is $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,\bq\cdot\Pi_{jj}\cdot\bq$. The same identity turns each density vertex into the longitudinal part of a current vertex, $e\rho_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=-\hbar\,\bq\cdot\bj_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}/(E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq})$ (Eq. E.428′; the printed E.428 loses the charge $e$, §Z.3).

Phonons. The phonon propagators agree up to a factor $\hbar$. Both are built from the dimensionless $a+a^\dagger$, but Giustino defines the mode-basis propagator as $-i\langle T\cdots\rangle$ (Eq. G.133), while EPP defines $F=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle TQQ\rangle$ with $Q=a+a^\dagger$ (Eq. E.251):

$$\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu\nu'}(\omega)=\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}}\,\delta_{\nu\nu'}, \tag{G.132}$$
$$F^0_\sigma(\bq,\omega)=2\,\frac{\omega_\sigma(\bq)}{\hbar}\,\frac{1}{\omega^2-\omega^2_\sigma(\bq)+i\eta}, \tag{E.390}$$

so $\mathcal D^{\rm A}_{\bq\nu}=\hbar F_\sigma$ in the mode basis. What dresses them differs. Giustino's phonon self-energy $\Pi^{\rm ph}$ couples point nuclei through $W_{\rm e}$ (Eq. G.121). EPP's phonon emits and reabsorbs an electron-screened photon through Born-charge currents:

$$\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma'}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\sigma}(\bq,\omega)\,D^{\rm e}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\,J_{\beta\sigma'}(\bq,\omega). \tag{E.399}$$

The displayed EPP equations are in Gaussian units with charge-dimensioned Born charges; Giustino's are SI. The notation table below gives the conversions.

Reading a diagram across

Take a Giustino diagram built from Coulomb lines, such as $W$, $W_{\rm ph}$ or the phonon self-energy. Replace each dashed Coulomb line by a dashed longitudinal-photon line and draw a wavy transverse line beside it. Replace each dot where a Coulomb line meets an electron (a density vertex) by a square (a current vertex). Attach the phonon to the field through a Born-charge current vertex $J$ instead of a bare nuclear charge. Sending $c\to\infty$ deletes the wavy lines again and returns Giustino's diagram, except that the Born charge $Z^*$ stands where the nuclear charge $Z_\kappa$ stood and only the long-range (dipolar) part of the coupling is kept.

Applying the conversions to one number

Two rules do most of the work. Gaussian charges become SI charges with $e^2\to e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0$, and EPP's Born charges already contain the electron charge, $Z^*_{\rm EPP}=|e|Z^*_{\rm G}$. The factor $N^{-1/2}$ sits inside EPP's $g$ and outside Giustino's. Both papers compute the long-range (Fröhlich) vertex, so it makes a good test. For a cubic diatomic crystal with Born charges $\pm Z^*$ and reduced mass $\mu$, take the longitudinal mode along $\bq$, keep $\bG=0$ and set the Bloch overlap to one. Giustino's Eq. (G.180) with $N_p$ dropped, and EPP's screened electrostatic vertex in its corrected form (E p.98′, with $\hat\epsilon^{\rm e}\to\epsilon^\infty$; the printed p. 98 vertex lacks the factor $e$, §Z.3), reduce to leading order in $q$ (basis phases set to 1) to

$$\begin{aligned}|g^{\rm G}(q)|&=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega}\,\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{Z^*_{\rm G}}{q\,\epsilon^\infty}\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2\mu\,\omega_{\rm LO}}\Big)^{1/2},\\ \sqrt N\,|g^{\rm dyn}(q)|&=\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{\rm uc}}\,\frac{|e|\,Z^*_{\rm EPP}}{q\,\epsilon^\infty}\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2\mu\,\omega_\sigma}\Big)^{1/2}.\end{aligned}$$

With $|e|Z^*_{\rm EPP}=e^2Z^*_{\rm G}$ and the Gaussian $e^2\equiv e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0$, the prefactors agree. One real difference remains: the frequency in the zero-point amplitude. Giustino's $\omega_{\bq\nu}$ is the DFPT phonon, which for the LO mode already contains the long-range shift. EPP's $\omega_\sigma$ is the bare short-range phonon, here equal to $\omega_{\rm TO}$. The figure computes both, together with Fröhlich's vertex (Eqs. G.2 and G.7) and EPP's unscreened vertex (E p.95′).

Derivation: why the dressed residue removes the factor $\sqrt{\omega_{\rm LO}/\omega_\sigma}$

This argument is derived on this page; neither paper discusses the normalization. In the electrostatic limit with static screening, $\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}$ is frequency independent (Eqs. E.452, E.454), and the diagonal Dyson solution (Eq. E.405) becomes

$$F_\sigma(\omega)=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_\sigma}{\omega^2-\tilde\omega^2},\qquad \tilde\omega^2=\omega_\sigma^2+\frac{2\omega_\sigma\Omega^{\rm e}_{\sigma\sigma}}{\hbar}=\omega_{\rm LO}^2 .$$

Rewrite it as the free propagator of a mode at $\tilde\omega$ times a weight:

$$F_\sigma(\omega)=\frac{\omega_\sigma}{\tilde\omega}\cdot\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\tilde\omega}{\omega^2-\tilde\omega^2}.$$

Any quantity of the form "vertex × phonon × vertex", such as the phonon-mediated interaction $W_{\rm ph}$ (or a Fan–Migdal self-energy, once EPP derives one), then contains $|g^{\rm dyn}|^2\,\omega_\sigma/\tilde\omega$ times the free propagator at $\tilde\omega$. Since $|g^{\rm dyn}|^2\propto1/\omega_\sigma$, the product scales as $1/\tilde\omega=1/\omega_{\rm LO}$, exactly like $|g^{\rm G}|^2$ multiplied by $\mathcal D^{\rm A}/\hbar$ at $\omega_{\rm LO}$. The physical check is classical: $W_{\rm ph}=W-W_{\rm e}=\frac{v}{(\epsilon^\infty)^2}\,\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{\rm LO}^2}$ for $\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon^\infty+\omega_p^2/(\omega_{\rm TO}^2-\omega^2)$, and both bookkeepings reproduce it.

Diagram glyphs

Giustino draws electrons, phonons, Coulomb lines and two kinds of e–ph vertex (his Figs. 1–2). EPP adds three kinds of photon line and separates charge vertices from current vertices (its Figs. 7–14). Below, both sets are drawn in this page's visual language: blue electrons, amber zigzag phonons, rose for the whole electromagnetic field (dashed: longitudinal; wavy: transverse; a wavy line over a dashed one: both polarizations, as in $D^0$; heavy or double: dressed, as in $D^{\rm e}$), dots for charge vertices and squares for current vertices. What EPP does not (yet) contain

Five pieces of Part A have no EPP counterpart yet: short-range (deformation-potential) coupling, which must still come from DFPT under EPP's double-counting rule (§B.1); electron self-energies; exchange–correlation and local fields; phonon-assisted optics; and out-of-plane dipoles in 2D. The "− missing in EPP" filter of the concept map lists them row by row, and §Z.1 discusses them.

Same word, different meaning

  • Retardation. Frequency-dependent screening (Giustino) versus finite speed of light (EPP): §X.1. EPP's "retarded limit $c\to\infty$" means the instantaneous limit.
  • LO–TO splitting. E.410′ is the $q\to0$ end point of the upper phonon polariton of a crystal without electrons, a shift shared by LO and TO; the readout $\hbar\tilde\omega(q=0)$ above gives about 57.5 meV for the GaAs-like preset, whose $Z^*\approx2.18$ is fixed by the measured splitting (55.6 meV with the literature $Z^*=2.07$). The electrostatic splitting is E.454. The two differ in two independent ways. Electronic screening, which E.410′ leaves out, accounts for 56 against 36 meV: with electrons the same limit gives 35.9 meV (E.410e). The order of limits only decides whether the TO modes share that shift (35.9 meV at $q\to0$, finite $c$) or stay at 33.3 meV ($c\to\infty$ first): §B.7, §Z.3.
  • Longitudinal photon. $D^{0l}=0$ (E.263) versus the Coulomb propagator (E.374): §B.6.
  • Screened vertices. One bare and one screened in phonon self-energies, two screened in Fan–Migdal: §A.6, §A.7, §B.8.
