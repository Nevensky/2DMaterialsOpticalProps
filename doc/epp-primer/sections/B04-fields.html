B.4EPP §§4.3–4.4 · Eqs. 166–237

Quantized fields: transverse photons, and Coulomb as the longitudinal photon

The Lagrangian of §B.2 becomes a quantum Hamiltonian. Two of the three field polarizations turn into photons with energy $\hbar cq$. The third has no restoring force, and its energy is exactly the instantaneous Coulomb interaction of electrons and ionic bound charges.

In §B.2 one Lagrangian (E.134) coupled band electrons, harmonic ions and the vector potential in the temporal gauge $\Phi=0$ and gave the ionic equation of motion; §B.3 turned that into the dielectric function of a polar crystal. Here the field itself becomes a quantum degree of freedom. The end product is the split $H=H_0+V'$ of (E.236)–(E.237′), on which every diagram of Part B is built.

Physical picture

Expand the field in plane waves. For each $\bq$ the amplitude is a 3-vector. The two components perpendicular to $\bq$ have a curl, hence a magnetic field, hence a restoring "spring": they oscillate at $\omega=cq$ and are the photons. The component along $\bq$ has no curl and no spring. Its electric field has a divergence instead, which Gauss's law ties to the charge density at the same instant. So the longitudinal field is not an independent degree of freedom, and its energy is the Coulomb energy of the charges. Giustino postulates that Coulomb interaction (§A.5); EPP derives it.

Conventions. Gaussian units, as in the rest of Part B, with $\bE=-\frac1c\partial_t\bA$ and $\bB=\nabla\times\bA$ in the temporal gauge (to convert to SI, $e^2\to e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0$ and $4\pi\to1/\varepsilon_0$ in Poisson's equation). Here $\Omega$ is the normalization volume of the whole crystal, not the cell volume $\Omega_{\rm uc}$. We write photon ladder operators as $b_{\bq\alpha}$ and keep $a$ for phonons; the PDF also uses $a$ for photons in Eqs. (208)–(217) and in §4.7.

Maxwell's equations from the field Lagrangian

EPP §4.3 rewrites the point-particle couplings of (E.134) with densities: the electronic current $\bj_{\rm e}(\br)=e\sum_e\mathbf v_e\,\delta(\br-\br_e)$ (E.166) and the ionic polarization of point Born dipoles, $\bP(\br)=\sum_{ia}\mathbf p_{ia}\,\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia})$ (E.167′), with each dipole at its atom (§Z.3) and $\mathbf p_{ia}=\hat Z^*_a\bQ_{ia}$ (E.135). The field part of the Lagrangian is then a local density,

$$\mathcal L_A(\br)=\frac{1}{8\pi c^2}\dot{\bA}^2(\br)-\frac{1}{8\pi}\big[\nabla\times\bA(\br)\big]^2+\frac1c\,\bj_{\rm e}(\br)\cdot\bA(\br)+\frac1c\,\dot{\bP}(\br)\cdot\bA(\br). \tag{E.169}$$

The Euler–Lagrange equation for $A_\mu(\br)$ (E.172–E.173) collects $\partial\mathcal L_A/\partial A_\mu=(j_{{\rm e}\mu}+\dot P_\mu)/c$, $\partial\mathcal L_A/\partial\dot A_\mu=-E_\mu/4\pi c$ and a curl of $\bB$ from the magnetic term. The result is the Ampère–Maxwell law with two sources:

$$\nabla\times\bB=\frac1c\dot{\bE}+\frac{4\pi}{c}\dot{\bP}+\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj_{\rm e}. \tag{E.175}$$

With $\mathbf D=\bE+4\pi\bP$ (E.176) this is the textbook macroscopic law $\nabla\times\bB=\frac1c\dot{\mathbf D}+\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj_{\rm e}$ (E.177). Its divergence, combined with continuity $\nabla\cdot\bj_{\rm e}=-e\dot\rho_{\rm e}$, gives $\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf D-4\pi e\rho_{\rm e})=0$ (E.179), which EPP integrates to Gauss's law:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=4\pi e\rho_{\rm e}. \tag{E.180}$$

Here $\rho_{\rm e}=\sum_e\delta(\br-\br_e)$ is the electron number density (its operator form is $\Psi^\dagger\Psi$, E.239) and $e\rho_{\rm e}$ the charge density, since $\bj_{\rm e}$ is a charge current. The printed continuity display, (E.179) and (E.180) write $\rho_{\rm e}$ for the charge density; following the factor-$e$ correction they are corrected in place and keep their numbers (§Z.3).

Faraday's law and $\nabla\cdot\bB=0$ are identities of the potentials. Note the division of labour: the ions are a polarizable background $\bP$ (ionic part only), while the valence electrons are free charges and currents whose polarization reappears as the photon self-energy $\Pi^{\rm e}$ in §B.5.

Gauss's law is a constraint, not an equation of motion

In the temporal gauge, varying $\bA$ yields only Ampère's law. Gauss's law is preserved by the dynamics (E.179) and must be imposed on the initial state (EPP silently sets the integration constant to zero). Quantum mechanically, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D-4\pi e\rho_{\rm e}=\nabla\cdot\bE-4\pi(e\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion})$ generates the residual gauge transformations $\bA\to\bA+\nabla\chi(\br)$ and annihilates physical states, so using Gauss's law inside $H$, as in (E.234′) below, is legitimate on that subspace. The Coulomb gauge reaches the same physics directly.

Legendre transform: four minimal couplings

The canonical momenta of electrons, ions and field are (EPP §4.4)

$$p_{e\mu}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial v_{e\mu}}=m_ev_{e\mu}+\frac ecA_\mu, \tag{E.182}$$
$$\pi_{ia\mu}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot Q_{ia\mu}}=m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}+\frac1c\sum_\alpha Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}A_\alpha(\bR^0_{ia}), \tag{E.183$'$}$$
$$p_{A_\mu}=\frac{\delta L}{\delta\dot A_\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial\dot A_\mu}=-\frac{1}{4\pi c}E_\mu. \tag{E.184}$$

Both matter momenta are minimally coupled: a displaced ion behaves as a particle of charge $Z^*$. Each ion feels $\bA$ at its own equilibrium site $\bR^0_{ia}=\bR^0_i+\br^0_a$; the printed (E.183), the Hamiltonians on pp. 38–39 and (E.186) take it at the cell origin $\bR^0_i$, and are shown here in the corrected forms (§Z.3; the unnumbered display below is corrected in place). The field momentum is minus the total electric field over $4\pi c$, transverse plus longitudinal. Inserting the momenta into the Legendre transform

$$H=\sum_e\bp_e\cdot\mathbf v_e+\sum_{ia}\pi_{ia}\cdot\dot\bQ_{ia}+\int d\br\,\bp_A\cdot\dot\bA-L, \tag{E.181}$$

all linear couplings cancel and only energies remain:

$$\begin{aligned}H={}&\sum_e\frac{m_e\mathbf v_e^2}{2}+\sum_eV_{\rm cr}(\br_e)+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{m_a\dot Q_{ia\mu}^2}{2}\\&+\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\frac{1}{8\pi}\int d\br\,\big[\bE^2+\bB^2\big].\end{aligned} \tag{E.185}$$

The interaction is hidden in the momenta, and the Coulomb interaction is hidden in $\int E^2$. In canonical variables (EPP p. 39, unnumbered),

$$\begin{aligned}H={}&\sum_e\frac{1}{2m_e}\Big[\bp_e-\frac ec\bA(\br_e)\Big]^2+\sum_eV_{\rm cr}(\br_e)+\sum_{ia\mu}\frac{1}{2m_a}\Big[\pi_{ia\mu}-\frac1c\sum_\alpha Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}A_\alpha(\bR^0_{ia})\Big]^2\\&+\frac12\sum_{ia\mu,jb\nu}k_{ia\mu,jb\nu}Q_{ia\mu}Q_{jb\nu}+\frac{1}{8\pi}\int d\br\,\big[\bE^2+\bB^2\big].\end{aligned}$$

Expanding the two squares gives $H=H_0'+V$. Here $H_0'$ contains the $\bA$-free kinetic and lattice terms plus the field energy, and the interaction is

$$\begin{aligned}V={}&-\frac{e}{2m_ec}\sum_e\big[\bp_e\cdot\bA(\br_e)+\bA(\br_e)\cdot\bp_e\big]+\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\sum_e\bA^2(\br_e)\\&-\frac1c\sum_{ia\alpha\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\pi_{ia\mu}A_\alpha(\bR^0_{ia})+\frac{1}{c^2}\sum_{ia\mu\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{2m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}A_\alpha(\bR^0_{ia})A_\beta(\bR^0_{ia}).\end{aligned} \tag{E.186$'$}$$

Every charged species couples twice, linearly through its current and quadratically through a contact term. For the ions the "charge" is the Born tensor and the current is the polarization current $Z^*\pi/m_a\approx\dot\bP$. The electronic product is symmetrized because $\bp_e$ does not commute with $\bA(\br_e)$ (here $\nabla\cdot\bA\neq0$); $\pi$ and $\bA$ commute.

Quantizing the field in the polarization triad

The free-field part of $H_0'$, written with $\bp_A=-\bE/4\pi c$, and its canonical commutator are

$$H^{0\prime}_{\rm ph}=\int\frac{d\br}{8\pi}\big[\bE^2+\bB^2\big]=\int\frac{d\br}{8\pi}\big\{16\pi^2c^2\bp_A^2+[\nabla\times\bA]^2\big\}, \tag{E.188}$$
$$\big[A_\mu(\br),p_{A\nu}(\br')\big]=\ii\hbar\,\delta(\br-\br')\,\delta_{\mu\nu}. \tag{E.189}$$

The delta function is the ordinary one for all three Cartesian components, a feature of the temporal gauge (the Coulomb gauge would have the transverse delta). With plane waves, $\bA(\br)=\Omega^{-1/2}\sum_\bq\bA_\bq e^{\ii\bq\cdot\br}$ (E.190), each $\bq$ carries a three-component amplitude. This is step 1 of the phonon recipe of §B.1, and EPP repeats steps 2 and 3: a rotation that decouples the Cartesian components, then ladder operators. The rotation is the orthogonal triad of its Fig. 6: $\be_3(\bq)=\hat\bq$, $\be_2=-\hat\bq\times\be_1$ and $\be_1=\hat\bq\times\be_2$ (E.197–E.199). Writing $\bA_\bq=\sum_\alpha\be_\alpha\tilde A_{\alpha\bq}$ and $\bp_{A\bq}=\sum_\alpha\be_\alpha\tilde p_{A\alpha\bq}$ (E.202–E.203), and using $\bq\times\be_3=0$, gives

$$\boxed{H^{0\prime}_{\rm ph}=2\pi c^2\sum_{\bq,\alpha=1,2,3}\tilde p_{A\alpha\bq}\,\tilde p_{A\alpha-\bq}+\sum_{\bq,\alpha=1,2}\frac{q^2}{8\pi}\tilde A_{\alpha\bq}\tilde A_{\alpha-\bq}} \tag{E.204}$$

This is the structural result of the section. The electric ("kinetic") term contains all three polarizations. The magnetic ("spring") term contains only the two transverse ones, because $[\bq\times\be_\alpha]\cdot[\bq\times\be_\beta]=q^2(\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\delta_{\alpha3}\delta_{\beta3})$.

For $\alpha=1,2$, (E.204) describes harmonic oscillators of frequency $cq$ ("mass" $1/4\pi c^2$, spring $q^2/4\pi$). The ladder operators $\tilde A_{\alpha\bq}=\sqrt{2\pi c\hbar/q}\,(b_{\bq\alpha}+b^\dagger_{-\bq\alpha})$ and $\tilde p_{A\alpha\bq}=-\ii\sqrt{\hbar q/8\pi c}\,(b_{\bq\alpha}-b^\dagger_{-\bq\alpha})$ (E.208–E.209) diagonalize them, and the field energy splits as $H^{0\prime}_{\rm ph}=H^0_{\rm Tph}+H^0_{\rm Lph}$ with

$$H^0_{\rm Tph}=\sum_{\bq,\alpha=1,2}\hbar qc\,\Big\{b^\dagger_{\bq\alpha}b_{\bq\alpha}+\frac12\Big\}, \tag{E.214}$$
$$H^0_{\rm Lph}=2\pi c^2\sum_\bq\tilde p_{A3\bq}\,\tilde p_{A3-\bq}. \tag{E.215}$$

Each transverse mode is an oscillator whose energy changes in quanta $\hbar cq$: a photon. The longitudinal term has no $\tilde A_3^2$ partner. Inserting (E.209) into it produces $b_{\bq3}b_{-\bq3}$ and $b^\dagger b^\dagger$ terms, so it is not diagonal, and EPP assigns it to the interaction. The field operator nevertheless keeps all three polarizations:

$$\bA(\br)=\sum_{\bq,\alpha=1,2,3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi c\hbar}{q\Omega}}\;\be_\alpha(\bq)\,\big\{b_{\bq\alpha}+b^\dagger_{-\bq\alpha}\big\}\,e^{\ii\bq\cdot\br}. \tag{E.216}$$

The reason is the commutator (E.189), which closes only through the completeness relation $\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}e_{\mu\alpha}e_{\nu\alpha}=\delta_{\mu\nu}$ (EPP p. 45). The transverse projector alone, $\delta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$, is nonlocal in real space, with a dipolar $1/r^3$ tail. The longitudinal projector cancels that tail. The same $1/r^3$ tensor will reappear below as the dipole–dipole interaction between ions.

Note on the source: triad conventions

(E.197)–(E.199) define a left-handed set, $\be_1\times\be_2=-\be_3$, as in Fig. 6; this is harmless. (E.200)–(E.201) give $\bq$ and $-\bq$ the same unit vector, so $\be_3(\bq)=\pm\hat\bq$ by half-space; only $e_{3\mu}(\bq)e_{3\nu}(-\bq)=q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$ enters later. "Unitary" in (E.196) means real orthogonal. The $\alpha=3$ ladder operators reuse the scale $cq$, which nothing in (E.215) fixes: they are bookkeeping, not normal modes (EPP §4.7 sets $\omega_{\bq3}=0^+$, E.262).

The longitudinal photon is the Coulomb interaction

What, then, is $H^0_{\rm Lph}$? EPP §4.4.3 inverts the triad and the Fourier transform (E.221–E.226), then uses $\bp_A=-\bE/4\pi c$ and $e_{3\mu}(\bq)=q_\mu/q$:

$$H^0_{\rm Lph}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum_{\mu\nu=x,y,z}\int d\br\int d\br'\,E_\mu(\br)\left\{\int\frac{d\bq}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2}\,e^{-\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\br')}\right\}E_\nu(\br'). \tag{E.227}$$

The kernel in braces is the longitudinal delta function, the projector onto curl-free fields. So (E.227) is simply $\frac{1}{8\pi}\int\bE_l^2$, the energy of the longitudinal electric field. Since $q_\mu q_\nu/q^2=\frac{1}{4\pi}q_\mu q_\nu\cdot\frac{4\pi}{q^2}$, the kernel is $\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu$ acting on the Coulomb propagator $V(\br-\br')=1/|\br-\br'|$ (E.229). Two integrations by parts move the derivatives onto the fields, and only $\nabla\cdot\bE$ survives. Gauss's law, with the electronic charge density $e\rho_{\rm e}$ and the ionic bound charge $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\bP$,

$$\nabla\cdot\bE=4\pi\big(e\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion}\big), \tag{E.234$'$}$$

then gives the central result of EPP §4.4:

$$\boxed{H^0_{\rm Lph}=\frac12\int d\br\int d\br'\,\frac{\big[e\rho_{\rm e}(\br)+\rho_{\rm ion}(\br)\big]\big[e\rho_{\rm e}(\br')+\rho_{\rm ion}(\br')\big]}{|\br-\br'|}} \tag{E.235$'$}$$

The printed (E.234) and (E.235) write $\rho_{\rm e}$ where the charge density $e\rho_{\rm e}$ is meant (note below; §Z.3).

Derivation: from (E.227) to (E.235′)

Because $\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu e^{-\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\br')}=q_\mu q_\nu e^{-\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\br')}$, with $\partial'_\nu=\partial/\partial x'_\nu$, the kernel can be written with the Coulomb propagator:

$$H^0_{\rm Lph}=\frac{1}{32\pi^2}\int d\br\int d\br'\sum_{\mu\nu}E_\mu(\br)\left\{\partial_\mu\partial'_\nu\int\frac{d\bq}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{4\pi}{q^2}e^{-\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\br')}\right\}E_\nu(\br'), \tag{E.228}$$ $$\int\frac{d\bq}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{4\pi}{q^2}e^{-\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\br')}=V(\br-\br')=\frac{1}{|\br-\br'|}. \tag{E.229}$$

With the derivatives acting only on $V$,

$$H^0_{\rm Lph}=\frac{1}{32\pi^2}\int d\br\,\bE(\br)\cdot\nabla_{\br}\int d\br'\,\big\{\nabla_{\br'}V(\br-\br')\big\}\cdot\bE(\br'). \tag{E.231}$$

Integrating by parts in $\br'$ (the surface term vanishes) turns the inner integral into $-\int d\br'\,V(\br-\br')\,\nabla_{\br'}\cdot\bE(\br')$, which is (E.232). The same step in $\br$ gives

$$H^0_{\rm Lph}=\frac{1}{32\pi^2}\int d\br\,\big[\nabla_{\br}\cdot\bE(\br)\big]\int d\br'\,V(\br-\br')\,\big[\nabla_{\br'}\cdot\bE(\br')\big]. \tag{E.233}$$

The transverse part of $\bE$ has dropped out, as it must. Inserting (E.234′) gives the prefactor $(4\pi)^2/32\pi^2=\frac12$ of (E.235′).

The same identity is quick to check in Fourier space, with the normalization of (E.190). With $\rho_\bq$ the Fourier component of the total charge density $e\rho_{\rm e}+\rho_{\rm ion}$, Gauss's law gives $\tilde E_{3\bq}=-4\pi\ii\rho_\bq/q$, so that, with $v(q)=4\pi/q^2$,

$$\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum_\bq|\tilde E_{3\bq}|^2=\sum_\bq\frac{2\pi|\rho_\bq|^2}{q^2}=\frac12\sum_\bq v(q)\,|\rho_\bq|^2 .$$

The energy of the longitudinal field is exactly the instantaneous Coulomb energy of all charges. No Coulomb term was put into the Lagrangian; it was hidden in $\frac{1}{8\pi}\int E^2$ all along. "Instantaneous" is literal: $\bE_l$ has no equation of motion of its own, so at each time it is fixed by the charges at that time. Expanding the square of (E.235′) gives three interactions, each of which Part B will dress:

  • Electron–electron, $\frac12\iint e^2\rho_{\rm e}\rho_{\rm e}'/|\br-\br'|$: the bare Coulomb interaction. RPA dressing turns it into $W^{\rm e}$.
  • Electron–ion, $e\iint\rho_{\rm e}\rho_{\rm ion}'/|\br-\br'|$: the energy of an electron in the field of the ionic dipoles. After phonon quantization this is the bare (unscreened, $\epsilon^\infty\to1$) Fröhlich vertex $\propto\ii\,e\,\bq\cdot\hat Z^*_a\be_{a\sigma}\,e^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br^0_a}/q^2$ ($\be_{a\sigma}$: phonon eigenvector; $e^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br^0_a}$: the basis phase of the dipole at its atom). This is the vertex of E p.95′ up to the overall sign convention discussed in §B.8; the printed p. 95 vertex lacks the factor $e$ (§Z.3). The factor that turns $q^2$ into $\bq\cdot\hat\epsilon^\infty\cdot\bq$ is not inserted by hand: it is generated by $\Pi^{\rm e}$ (§B.8, §B.9).
  • Ion–ion, $\frac12\iint\rho_{\rm ion}\rho_{\rm ion}'/|\br-\br'|$: the dipole–dipole interaction. At long wavelength $\bE_l=-4\pi\bP_\parallel$, which stiffens only the longitudinal optical mode by $\Delta\omega^2=\omega_p^2=4\pi Z^{*2}/(\Omega_{\rm uc}M)$, the unscreened LO shift of §B.3. For GaAs this unscreened shift would put the LO phonon near 56 meV instead of the measured 36 meV; dividing $\omega_p^2$ by $\epsilon^\infty=10.9$ restores 36 meV (numbers in §B.7). TO modes carry no bound charge and couple only to transverse photons, which is where polaritons come from (§B.6).

Link to Giustino: the Coulomb Hamiltonian, derived

Giustino §IV.A starts from $\hat H=\hat T_{\rm e}+\hat T_{\rm n}+\hat U_{\rm ee}+\hat U_{\rm nn}+\hat U_{\rm en}$ (G Eqs. 62–67), every interaction being the postulated instantaneous $v(\br,\br')$, with no vector potential (§A.5). (E.235′) is that structure, obtained as the longitudinal sector of electrodynamics. Two differences matter. EPP's ions enter only through the induced dipole density $-\nabla\cdot\bP$ with Born charges; the linear term of Giustino's nuclear density expansion (G Eq. 107), $Z_\kappa\Delta\boldsymbol\tau_{\kappa p}\cdot\nabla\delta(\br-\boldsymbol\tau^0_{\kappa p})$, is the same point-dipole density with bare charges $Z_\kappa$, and the static lattice charge stays in $V_{\rm cr}$. And (E.235′) keeps the self-interaction terms that G Eq. 64 removes with $-\delta(\br-\br')$ and G Eq. 65 excludes; normal ordering of the electron term and dropping on-site dipole terms, neither mentioned by EPP, must remove them. For the RPA/BSE practitioner: the bare $v$ in $W=v+v\chi v$ is the electron–electron part of $H^0_{\rm Lph}$, and the transverse photons of (E.214) are what a Coulomb-only theory leaves out.

Note on the source: number density or charge density

EPP defines $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ (E.239), a number density, and calls it the "electronic charge density operator" before (E.235). Gauss's law (E.234), the Coulomb energy (E.235) and the last term of (E.237) need the charge density, and so do the continuity display before (E.179) and (E.179)–(E.180), because $\bj_{\rm e}$ (E.166) is a charge current. The correction (§Z.3) keeps $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ and writes $e\rho_{\rm e}$ wherever a charge density is meant: (E.179)–(E.180) are corrected in place, and (E.234′), (E.235′) and (E.237′) replace the printed equations. The diamagnetic $A^2$ term of (E.237′) keeps the number density, as it must. The same convention gives (E.312′) in §B.6, and the same slip in the continuity equation on p. 94 removes a factor $e$ from the electrostatic Fröhlich vertices (E.428′, §B.8).

The final split: $H_0$ and $V'$

Moving the Coulomb term out of $H_0'$ and into the interaction, $H_0=H_0'-H^0_{\rm Lph}$ and $V'=V+H^0_{\rm Lph}$ (EPP §4.4.4), leaves a genuinely free Hamiltonian of Kohn–Sham electrons, bare phonons and transverse photons,

$$\begin{aligned}H_0={}&\sum_n\sum_{\bk\in{\rm BZ}}E_{n\bk}\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{n\bk}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3s}\sum_{\bq\in{\rm BZ}}\hbar\omega_\alpha(\bq)\Big(a^\dagger_{\bq\alpha}a_{\bq\alpha}+\frac12\Big)\\&+\sum_{\bq,\sigma=1,2}\hbar qc\,\Big\{b^\dagger_{\bq\sigma}b_{\bq\sigma}+\frac12\Big\},\end{aligned} \tag{E.236}$$

and an interaction that contains every coupling:

$$\begin{aligned}V'={}&-\frac1c\int d\br\,\bj_{\rm e}(\br)\cdot\bA(\br)+\frac{e^2}{2m_ec^2}\int d\br\,\rho_{\rm e}(\br)\,\bA^2(\br)\\&-\frac1c\sum_{ia\alpha\mu}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}\pi_{ia\mu}A_\alpha(\bR^0_{ia})+\frac{1}{2c^2}\sum_{ia\mu\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{m_a}Z^*_{a\alpha\mu}Z^*_{a\beta\mu}A_\alpha(\bR^0_{ia})A_\beta(\bR^0_{ia})\\&+\frac12\int d\br\int d\br'\,\frac{\big[e\rho_{\rm e}(\br)+\rho_{\rm ion}(\br)\big]\big[e\rho_{\rm e}(\br')+\rho_{\rm ion}(\br')\big]}{|\br-\br'|}.\end{aligned} \tag{E.237$'$}$$

Here $\rho_{\rm e}=\Psi^\dagger\Psi$ is the electron number density (E.239), with $\Psi(\br)=\sum_{n\bk}c_{n\bk}\phi_{n\bk}(\br)$ (E.240), $\bj_{\rm e}$ is the paramagnetic current (E.238), $\rho_{\rm ion}=-\nabla\cdot\bP$ with $P_\mu(\br)=\sum_{ia\nu}Z^*_{a\mu\nu}Q_{ia\nu}\delta(\br-\bR^0_{ia})$ (E.241′, 314′), and the $\bA$ in $V'$ is the full field (E.216), all three polarizations included. (E.237′) carries both corrections of §Z.3 that touch the printed (E.237): $\bA$ at the atomic sites $\bR^0_{ia}$, and $e\rho_{\rm e}$ in the Coulomb term. Note what is absent: no term is written as an electron–phonon vertex $g\,c^\dagger c\,(a+a^\dagger)$, and there is no Debye–Waller term (the ionic $A^2$ term is a two-photon, not a two-phonon, vertex). Electrons and phonons interact only through the field: electrostatically through the $e\rho_{\rm e}\rho_{\rm ion}$ cross term, which becomes the bare long-range Fröhlich vertex once $\bQ$ is quantized, and electrodynamically by exchanging a photon between $-\frac1c\bj_{\rm e}\cdot\bA$ and $-\frac1c Z^*\pi\cdot\bA/m_a$. The transverse photons bring retardation in EPP's sense (finite $c$), not Giustino's (frequency-dependent electronic screening). The first two terms of (E.236) are the first line of Giustino's (G.1) (§A.2).

Note on the source: two different $\bj_{\rm e}$

(E.166) defines the kinetic current $e\sum_e\mathbf v_e\delta(\br-\br_e)$, built from velocities and gauge invariant. The $\bj_{\rm e}$ in (E.237′) must instead be the paramagnetic current

$$\bj_{\rm e}(\br)=\frac{e\hbar}{2\ii m_e}\Big\{\Psi^\dagger(\br)\nabla\Psi(\br)-\big[\nabla\Psi^\dagger(\br)\big]\Psi(\br)\Big\}, \tag{E.238}$$

which is what $-\frac{e}{2m_ec}\sum_e(\bp_e\cdot\bA+\bA\cdot\bp_e)$ in (E.186′) becomes in second quantization. The two differ by the diamagnetic current: kinetic $=$ paramagnetic $-\frac{e^2}{m_ec}\Psi^\dagger\Psi\,\bA$. Using the kinetic current in (E.237′) would triple the $A^2$ term.

What $H_0$ already contains

EPP flags double counting on p. 40 but does not resolve it here. The Kohn–Sham $E_{n\bk}$ already contain the Hartree and xc mean field, to which (E.235′) adds the bare electron–electron interaction. The force constants in $H_0$ must be short-range, since the dipole–dipole part is regenerated here by $\rho_{\rm ion}V\rho_{\rm ion}$; the rule is set out in §B.1, which also explains the basis phase that the printed (E.167) and (E.241) drop and that (E.167′) and (E.241′, 314′) restore.
