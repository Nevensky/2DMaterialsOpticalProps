B.7Photon exchange · phonon Dyson equation · polaritons · order of limits

The phonon self-energy from photon exchange; phonon polaritons

A polar phonon carries an oscillating dipole, and a changing dipole is a current; the current creates a field, the electrons screen it, and the field acts back on the ions. This section rearranges the propagator of the field from Section B.6 into a Dyson equation for the phonons, whose self-energy is exactly this exchange of a photon. With the electrons switched off and $\bq\to0$, as in the EPP manuscript, every polar mode is shifted by the same amount: the result is the upper phonon polariton, a transverse mode whose frequency equals the longitudinal one in a cubic crystal, not the LO–TO splitting. Which of the two appears is decided by the order of the limits $q\to0$ and $c\to\infty$.

What you need: the free propagators $F^0$ of the phonons and $D^0$ of the field, the photon line $D^{\rme}$ dressed by the electrons, the ionic current vertex $j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)$ and the ionic self-energy $\Pi^{\ion}$ of Section B.5; the Dyson equation of the full field and its longitudinal and transverse modes, from Section B.6; the phonon Dyson equation and the shift of a mode by a self-energy, from Section A.6; Born charges and the LO–TO splitting, from Section A.4; and the dielectric function of a polar crystal, from Section B.3. A background box recaps Dyson equations as geometric series. Units are Gaussian, with the elementary charge $e$, the reduced Planck constant $\hbar$ and the speed of light $c$ written out.1

The picture: a phonon that feels its own field

Take a polar optical phonon of long wavelength, for example in GaAs. In every cell the gallium and arsenic atoms move against each other. They carry opposite Born charges, so each cell holds an oscillating dipole, $e\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\bu_{\ell\kappa}$, built from the Born charges $\mathbf Z^*_\kappa$ of Section A.4 and the displacements $\bu_{\ell\kappa}$ of the atoms, with the phase $\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}$ that the wave of wavevector $\bq$ has in cell $\ell$. An oscillating dipole carries a current, the time derivative of the dipole, and a current is a source of the electromagnetic field.

The field has two parts. The part along $\bq$ comes from bound charges: where the polarization $\bP$ (dipole per unit volume) changes along $\bq$, the charge $-\nabla\cdot\bP$ piles up. This is the Coulomb field, and it acts at once. The part across $\bq$ is a transverse field that travels at the speed of light, made of photons. shows why a longitudinal wave of dipoles makes the first kind and a transverse one does not. In the temporal gauge of Part B both parts are carried by the vector potential $\bA$, and the propagator of the field, $D$ of Section B.6, contains both. In this section "photon" means the whole field, its longitudinal part included.

The field crosses the crystal. Below the band gap the electrons cannot absorb it, but they polarize and screen it, by the factor $\varepsilon_\infty$$\,\approx10.9$ in GaAs, the electronic dielectric constant. The screened field then pushes on the Born charges again: on the same mode, or on another polar mode with the same wavevector. The phonon has emitted a photon and absorbed it again.

In Part B this loop carries all the long-range physics of a polar lattice. The bare phonons of the EPP manuscript come from the short-range force constants $\Phi^{\mathrm{sr}}$ alone (Section B.1), so they have no LO–TO splitting.2 Throughout Part B, the frequencies $\omega_{\bq\nu}$ and eigenvectors $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}(\bq)$ of Section A.1 denote these bare modes. The LO–TO splitting, the phonon polaritons and, in doped crystals, the coupling of LO phonons to plasmons must all come out of the exchange of photons.

Section B.6 summed the relevant diagrams to find how the crystal dresses light. This section reads the same diagrams the other way, to find how light dresses the lattice. The diagrams use a small vocabulary. A propagator is a line: zigzag for a phonon, wavy for the field, thin when free and heavy when dressed. A self-energy is a blob between two lines, called a bubble because the electronic one is a closed loop of an electron and a hole. A dot where a phonon line meets a field line is a vertex. A term of a series is a chain of lines and bubbles, read as a product of matrices. In this language the section takes three steps, which draws:

  1. collect all electronic bubbles into one dressed photon line, $D^{\rme}$;
  2. open each ionic bubble into a phonon line between two current vertices;
  3. recognize a Dyson equation for the phonons, whose self-energy is the exchange of one dressed photon.

Then it evaluates the result. With the electrons switched off and $\bq\to0$, as the EPP manuscript does, every polar mode shifts by the same amount. Keeping $\bq$ finite shows what that shift is, and why the order of the limits $q\to0$ and $c\to\infty$ matters.

Step 1: resum the electrons into the photon line

The starting point is the propagator $D$ of the field in the crystal: the response of the vector potential to an external current $\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$, the probe that Section B.6 places in the crystal, $\bA=D\,\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$. Section B.6 showed that it obeys one Dyson equation ,

$$D=D^0+D^0\,\Pi\,D,\qquad \Pi=\Pi^{\rme}+\Pi^{\ion} .$$
$D$, $D^0$
propagator of the field in the crystal ($D$) and in vacuum ($D^0$): $3\times3$ matrices in the Cartesian indices, functions of $(\bq,\omega)$, with the units of $c/\omega^2$
$\Pi^{\rme}$, $\Pi^{\ion}$
photon self-energy $\Pi$ of the electrons and of the ions: the current each induces in response to $\bA$, $\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}=\Pi\,\bA$; units inverse to those of $D$, so that $D^0\Pi$ is a pure number

Products are matrix products, $(D^0\Pi)_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_\gamma D^0_{\alpha\gamma}\Pi_{\gamma\beta}$, at fixed $\bq$ and $\omega$. As in Section B.6, only the macroscopic elements are kept, those that average over a cell (reciprocal lattice vectors $\bG=\bG'=0$); the crystal local fields are left out.

Dyson equations as geometric series skip if familiar

For numbers, $X=X_0+X_0SX$ is solved by $X=X_0/(1-X_0S)$, and iterating it gives the geometric series $X=X_0+X_0SX_0+X_0SX_0SX_0+\dots$, which converges where $|X_0S|\lt1$. The closed form holds everywhere, including where the series diverges. For matrices the same steps give $X=(X_0^{-1}-S)^{-1}$; the order of the factors must be kept.

For a propagator, $X_0$ is the free propagator and $S$ the self-energy. The poles of a propagator are the frequencies of the excitations it describes (Section A.5). $X$ has a pole where $X_0^{-1}-S$ is singular, which is in general not a pole of $X_0$: the Dyson equation moves the excitations. No finite piece of the series does, because each term only has the poles of $X_0$ and of $S$.

A self-energy that is a sum, $S=S_1+S_2$, can be resummed in two stages. Since $X^{-1}=X_0^{-1}-S_1-S_2=X_1^{-1}-S_2$ with $X_1^{-1}=X_0^{-1}-S_1$, the equation $X=X_0+X_0(S_1+S_2)X$ is equivalent to $X=X_1+X_1S_2X$ with $X_1=X_0+X_0S_1X_1$. First dress the line with $S_1$; then use the dressed line to resum $S_2$. Step 1 below does exactly this, with the electrons as $S_1$ and the ions as $S_2$.

Now regroup the series of $D$ so that the electrons are summed first.

  1. Iterate the Dyson equation into a series of chains:

    $$D=D^0+D^0\Pi D^0+D^0\Pi D^0\Pi D^0+\dots$$
    Inserting the right side into itself again and again pushes the unknown $D$ to ever higher order, leaving chains of free lines and self-energies. Each $\Pi$ is $\Pi^{\rme}+\Pi^{\ion}$, so a chain with three bubbles, for example, splits into $2^3=8$ chains, each a sequence of electronic and ionic bubbles joined by free lines.

  2. Sum the chains that contain no ionic bubble:

    $$D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+\dots=D^{\rme} .$$
    These chains are the iteration of the Dyson equation $D^{\rme}=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^{\rme}$ of the electron-dressed line .

  3. In every other chain, cut at the ionic bubbles. Before the first one, between consecutive ones and after the last stand stretches made only of free lines and electronic bubbles. Summing over all chains with a given number of ionic bubbles sums every stretch over all its lengths, which turns each stretch into $D^{\rme}$:

    $$D=D^{\rme}+D^{\rme}\,\Pi^{\mathrm{ion,scr}}\,D^{\rme}, \tag{B7.1}$$
    $$\begin{gathered}\Pi^{\mathrm{ion,scr}}=\Pi^{\ion}+\Pi^{\ion}D^{\rme}\Pi^{\ion}\\ +\Pi^{\ion}D^{\rme}\Pi^{\ion}D^{\rme}\Pi^{\ion}+\dots\\ =\Pi^{\ion}+\Pi^{\ion}D^{\rme}\,\Pi^{\mathrm{ion,scr}}\end{gathered} \tag{B7.2}$$
    The stretches are independent: how many electronic bubbles one stretch holds does not restrict any other, so the sum over all chains factorizes into one sum per stretch.

Written for inverses, the Dyson equations of $D$ and of $D^{\rme}$ read $D^{-1}=(D^0)^{-1}-\Pi^{\rme}-\Pi^{\ion}$ and $(D^{\rme})^{-1}=(D^0)^{-1}-\Pi^{\rme}$. $$\begin{gathered}D^{-1}=(D^{\rme})^{-1}-\Pi^{\ion},\\ D=D^{\rme}+D^{\rme}\,\Pi^{\ion}D ,\end{gathered}$$

Equation says how the ions enter the field. An external current produces, in the crystal with the ions held fixed, the field $D^{\rme}\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$. The ions answer with the current $\Pi^{\mathrm{ion,scr}}D^{\rme}\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$, and this current produces a field of its own through the leftmost factor $D^{\rme}$ (a product of propagators acts from right to left). The chain contains the response of the ions to the field they create themselves, screened by the electrons, over and over: the ions interact with each other through $D^{\rme}$. The EPP manuscript compares this with a system embedded in a polarizable medium, whose response includes its response to its own field screened by the medium. In Part A the same idea is Baym's: the nuclei interact through the Coulomb interaction screened by the electrons, $W^{\rme}$ (Section A.5). Here they interact through $D^{\rme}$, which carries the screened Coulomb interaction in its longitudinal part (Section B.9) and the transverse photons besides.

Step 2: open the ionic bubble

The ionic bubble describes the lattice as a set of charged oscillators. A field of wavevector $\bq$ drives the polar modes of the same $\bq$ through their Born charges, and the driven modes carry a current. Section B.5 wrote the result with the ionic current vertex , here its macroscopic element ($\bG=0$),

$$j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)=\sum_{\kappa\beta}\frac{eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{M_\kappa}}\,e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa} ,$$

the dipole along $\alpha$ that mode $\bq\nu$ puts in a cell per unit of its mass-weighted amplitude (charge per $\sqrt{\text{mass}}$). Here $\mathbf Z^*_\kappa$ is the dimensionless Born charge tensor, $M_\kappa$ the mass and $\btau_\kappa$ the position of atom $\kappa$ in its cell, and $e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)$ the eigenvector of the bare mode. The phase $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}$ is there because the dipole of atom $\kappa$ sits at the atom, $\btau_{\ell\kappa}=\bR_\ell+\btau_\kappa$, not at the corner of its cell.4 With this vertex the ionic self-energy is

$$\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\,\OV}\sum_\nu\frac{j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)\,j^*_{\beta\nu}(\bq)}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}} .$$

The sum runs over the $3N_\kappa$ bare branches, $\OV$ is the cell volume, and the positive infinitesimal $\eta\to0^+$ makes the function retarded: its poles lie just below the real frequency axis, so that a response follows its cause. The derivation below obtains this expression from a driven classical lattice, which shows where each factor comes from.

Derivation: the ionic bubble from a driven lattice

Apply an electric field $\bE$$\,\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\br-\omega t)}$ to the harmonic lattice of Section A.1 and compute the current of the ions to first order in $\bE$.

  1. The force on atom $\ell\kappa$ along $\beta$ is $F_{\ell\kappa\beta}=\sum_\alpha eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}E_\alpha\,\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\btau_{\ell\kappa}-\omega t)}$.

    The dipole $e\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\bu_{\ell\kappa}$ of the atom has the energy $-\sum_{\alpha\beta}eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}u_{\ell\kappa\beta}E_\alpha$ in the field (Section B.2). The force is minus its derivative with respect to $u_{\ell\kappa\beta}$, with the field taken at the atom.

  2. Add $F_{\ell\kappa\beta}$ to the spring forces in Newton's equation of Section A.1, and project on mode $\bq\nu$ with the normal coordinate $Q_{\bq\nu}$ of Section A.1,

    $$Q_{\bq\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_\ell}}\sum_{\ell\kappa\beta}\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}\sqrt{M_\kappa}\,e^*_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)\,u_{\ell\kappa\beta} .$$

    The projected equation of motion is

    $$\begin{gathered}\ddot Q_{\bq\nu}+\omega^2_{\bq\nu}Q_{\bq\nu}\\ =\sqrt{N_\ell}\,\sum_\alpha j^*_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)\,E_\alpha\,\ee^{-\ii\omega t}\end{gathered}$$
    The projection turns the spring forces into $-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}Q_{\bq\nu}$, as in Section A.1. In the force term the phase of the field, $\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}$, cancels the projection phase $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}$; the sum over the $N_\ell$ cells gives $N_\ell$, and what is left, $\sum_{\kappa\beta}eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}\,e^*_{\kappa\beta,\nu}\,\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$, is $j^*_{\alpha\nu}$. Modes of other wavevectors are not driven, because their lattice sums vanish.

  3. The steady state oscillates with the field:

    $$Q_{\bq\nu}=\frac{\sqrt{N_\ell}\,\sum_\alpha j^*_{\alpha\nu}E_\alpha}{\omega^2_{\bq\nu}-\omega^2}\,\ee^{-\ii\omega t} .$$
    This is the response of a driven undamped oscillator. Causality fixes how the pole at $\omega=\omega_{\bq\nu}$ is passed, $\omega\to\omega+\ii\eta$.

  4. The macroscopic polarization $\bP$ is the $\bG=0$ Fourier component of the dipole density $\sum_{\ell\kappa}e\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\bu_{\ell\kappa}\,\delta(\br-\btau_{\ell\kappa})$:

    $$\begin{aligned}P_\alpha&=\frac{1}{\OVSC}\sum_{\ell\kappa\beta}eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}\,u_{\ell\kappa\beta}\\&\qquad\times\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_{\ell\kappa}}\\&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_\ell}\,\OV}\sum_\nu j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)\,Q_{\bq\nu}\end{aligned}$$
    Mode $\bq\nu$ displaces the atoms by $u_{\ell\kappa\beta}=(N_\ell M_\kappa)^{-1/2}\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)\,Q_{\bq\nu}$ (Section A.1). The sum over cells gives $N_\ell$, and $\OVSC=N_\ell\OV$ is the volume of the crystal. The basis phase now appears as $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}$ and builds $j_{\alpha\nu}$, not its conjugate.

  5. Combine: $P_\alpha=\frac{1}{\OV}\sum_{\beta\nu}j_{\alpha\nu}j^*_{\beta\nu}E_\beta/(\omega^2_{\bq\nu}-\omega^2)$.

    The factors $\sqrt{N_\ell}$ cancel, as they must for a response per unit volume.

  6. Write current and field with $\bA$: the ionic current is $\bj^{\ion}$$\,=\partial_t\bP=-\ii\omega\bP$ and $\bE=-\frac1c\partial_t\bA=\frac{\ii\omega}{c}\bA$, so

    $$j^{\ion}_\alpha=\frac{\omega^2}{c\,\OV}\sum_{\beta\nu}\frac{j_{\alpha\nu}j^*_{\beta\nu}}{\omega^2_{\bq\nu}-\omega^2}\,A_\beta .$$
    A moving dipole is a current, and in the temporal gauge the field is the time derivative of $\bA$: each contributes one factor $\omega$. Comparing with $\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}=\Pi\bA$ gives $\Pi^{\ion}$ as quoted above.

The result vanishes at $\omega=0$. The ions feel only the electric field, and a static $\bA$ carries none. Section B.5 obtains the same expression from the quantized lattice, where the diamagnetic term does this job; a harmonic lattice responds like a classical one, so the two agree exactly.5

The bubble already has the shape "vertex, line, vertex": the oscillator factor $1/[(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}]$ is, up to a constant, the free phonon propagator. Two steps make this explicit.

  1. Write the oscillator factor with the free phonon propagator $F^0_\nu(\bq,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}}$ :

    $$\frac{1}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}}=\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}\,F^0_\nu(\bq,\omega) .$$
    This is the definition of $F^0$, the propagator of the phonon field $\phi_{\bq\nu}$, solved for the oscillator factor. $F^0$ has units of inverse energy.

  2. Share the remaining factor $\hbar\omega^2/(2c\OV\omega_{\bq\nu})$ equally between the two vertices.

    Equal shares make the vertex at one end of the phonon line the complex conjugate of the vertex at the other end ($\omega$ is real), so the bubble reads vertex, line, conjugate vertex, like the self-energies of Part A.

The result defines the dynamic ionic vertex and writes the bubble with $F^0$:6

$$J_{\alpha\nu}(\bq,\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2c\OV\omega_{\bq\nu}}}\;\omega\,j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq) \tag{B7.3}$$
$$\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)=-\sum_\nu J_{\alpha\nu}\,F^0_\nu\,J^*_{\beta\nu} . \tag{B7.4}$$

dynamic ionic vertex: the current along $\alpha$ that mode $\bq\nu$ carries per unit of its phonon field, at frequency $\omega$, normalized for the coupling to the field (units below)
$\omega_{\bq\nu}$
frequency of the bare mode $\bq\nu$ ($\omega_{\bq\nu}$), from the short-range force constants
$F^0_\nu$

The minus sign in appears because $F^0$, defined with the factor $-\ii/\hbar$ of Section A.5, is minus the response of an oscillator to a force: below its pole a driven oscillator follows the force, while $F^0$ is negative there. Each factor of $J$ has a meaning:

  • $\omega$: the current of an oscillating dipole is its time derivative, $-\ii\omega$ times the dipole, and the field that drives the dipole is the time derivative of $\bA$. A displacement that does not move carries no current and feels no force from $\bA$: $J_{\alpha\nu}(\bq,0)=0$.
  • $(\hbar/2\omega_{\bq\nu})^{1/2}$, together with the $1/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$ inside $j_{\alpha\nu}$: the zero-point amplitude $(\hbar/2M_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu})^{1/2}$ of Section A.1. The phonon field $\phi_{\bq\nu}$ is a displacement in units of this amplitude, so $J$ is the current per unit of $\phi_{\bq\nu}$.
  • $(c\OV)^{-1/2}$: the normalization to one cell of volume $\OV$, and the $1/c$ of the Gaussian coupling $-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA$ between a current and the vector potential, shared between the two vertices.

Only products such as $J^*DJ$ appear, and they are energies. The propagator $D$ has the units of $c/\omega^2$ and $J^*J$ those of $\hbar\omega^2|j|^2/(c\OV\omega_{\bq\nu})$, so $J^*DJ$ has the units of $\hbar|j|^2/(\OV\omega_{\bq\nu})$. The combination $4\pi|j|^2/\OV$ is the square of a frequency, a plasma frequency of the ions (Section B.3): for GaAs it is $(44.6\ \text{meV}/\hbar)^2$, and $J^*DJ$ is tens of meV.

Step 3: a Dyson equation for phonons

Insert the opened bubble into the chain . Take the term with $N$ dressed lines $D^{\rme}$; it contains $N+1$ ionic bubbles.

  1. Replace every bubble by $-\sum_\nu J_{\cdot\nu}F^0_\nu J^*_{\cdot\nu}$, where each dot stands for the Cartesian index at one end of the bubble. For $N=1$, written out:

    $$\begin{aligned}\big[\Pi^{\ion}D^{\rme}\Pi^{\ion}\big]_{\alpha\beta}&=\sum_{\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\gamma}D^{\rme}_{\gamma\delta}\Pi^{\ion}_{\delta\beta}\\&=\sum_{\nu\nu'}J_{\alpha\nu}F^0_\nu\Big[\sum_{\gamma\delta}J^*_{\gamma\nu}D^{\rme}_{\gamma\delta}J_{\delta\nu'}\Big]\\&\qquad\times F^0_{\nu'}J^*_{\beta\nu'}\end{aligned}$$
    The two minus signs of the bubbles cancel here. The Cartesian sums now sit between the two phonon lines, and the branch sums outside.

  2. The bracket connects branch $\nu'$ to branch $\nu$ through one dressed photon line. Define the phonon self-energy as minus this bracket, every factor taken at $(\bq,\omega)$:

    $$\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'}=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\nu}\,D^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}\,J_{\beta\nu'} \tag{B7.5}$$
    With this sign the $N=1$ term reads $-\sum_{\nu\nu'}J_{\alpha\nu}\big[F^0\Pi^{\ph}F^0\big]_{\nu\nu'}J^*_{\beta\nu'}$, the first correction to $-JF^0J^*$, with $F^0_{\nu\nu'}=F^0_\nu\delta_{\nu\nu'}$ a diagonal matrix in the branch indices.

  3. Count the signs for any $N$: the term carries $(-1)^{N+1}$ from its $N+1$ bubbles and $(-1)^N$ from writing each of its $N$ brackets as $-\Pi^{\ph}$. The product is $-1$ for every $N$, so

    $$\begin{gathered}\Pi^{\mathrm{ion,scr}}_{\alpha\beta}=-\sum_{\nu\nu'}J_{\alpha\nu}F_{\nu\nu'}J^*_{\beta\nu'},\\ F=F^0+F^0\Pi^{\ph}F^0\\ +F^0\Pi^{\ph}F^0\Pi^{\ph}F^0+\dots\end{gathered} \tag{B7.6}$$
    Every term has the same overall sign, so the branch matrices between the outer vertices form one series: $F^0(\Pi^{\ph}F^0)^N$ is the term with $N$ photons exchanged.

  4. Sum the geometric series:

    $$F=F^0+F^0\,\Pi^{\ph}\,F . \tag{B7.7}$$
    The series in is the iteration of this equation, which is equivalent to $F^{-1}=(F^0)^{-1}-\Pi^{\ph}$ (background box above).

Equations and are the central result of the section.7 The screened ionic correlator is vertex, dressed phonon line, conjugate vertex. The ionic current is linear in the displacements, so the screened ionic correlator is the correlator of two phonon fields between two vertices. This identifies the $F$ of as the propagator of the phonons in the full crystal: the phonon propagator of Section A.5, now dressed by the exchange of photons. It is the same Dyson equation as of Section A.6, with a different free propagator and a different self-energy.

The self-energy reads like the picture of . Phonon $\bq\nu'$ produces the current $J_{\beta\nu'}$. The current produces the field $\sum_\beta D^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}J_{\beta\nu'}$, longitudinal and transverse, screened by the electrons on its way. The field drives phonon $\bq\nu$ through $J^*_{\alpha\nu}$. Three consequences follow from the structure alone:

  • $D^{\rme}$ is a tensor, so one photon line can connect two different branches: the off-diagonal elements of $\Pi^{\ph}$ mix branches.
  • Only branches that carry a current take part. Nonpolar modes, and acoustic modes as $\bq\to0$, have $j_{\alpha\nu}=0$, the latter because the Born charges add up to zero .
  • $J\propto\omega$, so $\Pi^{\ph}$ depends on frequency even when the electrons respond instantly.

One branch at a time: the diagonal approximation

If the off-diagonal elements of $\Pi^{\ph}$ are dropped, $F_{\nu\nu'}\approx F_\nu\delta_{\nu\nu'}$, the matrix equation becomes one scalar equation per branch. This is the diagonal approximation. With the free propagator written out,

$$\begin{gathered}F_\nu(\bq,\omega)=\frac{F^0_\nu}{1-F^0_\nu\,\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}}\\ =\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}/\hbar}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}-2\omega_{\bq\nu}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}/\hbar}\end{gathered} \tag{B7.9}$$

A dressed mode is a pole of $F_\nu$, where the denominator vanishes:

$$\bar\omega^2_{\bq\nu}=\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\hbar}\,\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}\big(\bq,\bar\omega_{\bq\nu}\big) . \tag{B7.10}$$
$\bar\omega_{\bq\nu}$

The condition is self-consistent, because $\Pi^{\ph}$ depends on the frequency at which it is evaluated, and it can have more than one solution. When the self-energy is small, $\bar\omega^2-\omega^2\approx2\omega(\bar\omega-\omega)$ gives the shift $\bar\omega_{\bq\nu}-\omega_{\bq\nu}\approx\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}/\hbar$ of Section A.6 , and an imaginary part of $\Pi^{\ph}$ gives a width. In an insulator below its gap, $\Pi^{\ph}$ is real, and the dressed modes have no width in this theory.10

The diagonal approximation is exact when the bare modes are chosen so that $\Pi^{\ph}$ has no off-diagonal elements. In a cubic crystal with two atoms per cell this happens near $\Gamma$ if the currents of the three optical modes point along $\hat\bq=\bq/q$ and along two directions across it. The acoustic modes carry no current at $\Gamma$, so they decouple there. In other bases the off-diagonal elements are as large as the diagonal ones, and the matrix form is needed (question 3 of the check, and the derivation at the end of the finite-$\bq$ analysis).

Why the whole series is needed

No finite piece of the series moves a mode. Cut after $N$ exchanges, the series is $F_N=F^0+F^0\Pi^{\ph}F^0+\dots+F^0(\Pi^{\ph}F^0)^N$. For one branch and a constant self-energy the term with $N$ exchanges is proportional to $1/(\omega^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu})^{N+1}$: every term has its pole at the bare frequency, with a multiplicity that grows with the order. The series converges only where $|F^0_\nu\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}|\lt1$, that is, for $\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}\gt0$ as here, outside the window

$$\omega^2_{\bq\nu}-\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\hbar}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}\lt\omega^2\lt\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\hbar}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu} .$$

The dressed mode sits exactly on the upper edge of this window, where $F^0_\nu\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}=1$. No truncation reaches it; only the sum does. There is also no small parameter to justify a truncation. Without electrons the static self-energy of a GaAs optical mode at $\bq\to0$ is 29.8 meV (computed below), comparable to the energy of the bare mode itself, 33.3 meV. That energy is $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$, where $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ is the transverse optical frequency at $\Gamma$: in this theory the bare optical frequency equals it, as the finite-$\bq$ analysis below shows. The window then extends from zero frequency up to the dressed mode. shows the truncated series piling up structure at the bare frequency while the full sum moves the peak.

Derivation: the free propagator $D^0$ from Maxwell's equations

In the temporal gauge the electric field is $\bE=-\frac1c\partial_t\bA$ and the magnetic field is $\bB=\nabla\times\bA$ (Section B.2), so Ampère's law with Maxwell's displacement current, $\nabla\times\bB=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj+\frac1c\partial_t\bE$, becomes $\nabla\times\nabla\times\bA+\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t^2\bA=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj$. For a wave $\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\br-\omega t)}$, $\nabla\to\ii\bq$ and $\partial_t\to-\ii\omega$:

Along $\hat\bq$ the bracket is $-\omega^2/c^2$, so $\hat\bq\cdot\bA=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\hat\bq\cdot\bj$. With charge conservation this is Gauss's law: the longitudinal field is the Coulomb field of the charges that the current piles up. Across $\hat\bq$ the bracket is $q^2-\omega^2/c^2$, so the transverse part of $\bA$ is $-4\pi c/(\omega^2-c^2q^2)$ times the transverse part of $\bj$. What the result is, and what it is not

The EPP manuscript presents as the LO–TO splitting, the long-range correction that DFPT codes add to the dynamical matrix. The analogy holds in form: both shifts come from the long-range field of the Born dipoles acting back on short-range phonons. But is a different limit, and three of its features show it.

  • It is isotropic. The sum $\sum_\alpha|j_{\alpha\nu}|^2$ counts all three components of the mode current. The two transverse optical modes shift exactly as much as the longitudinal one. The LO–TO splitting depends only on the component of the current along $\hat\bq$ and leaves the TO modes where they are.
  • It is unscreened. Without electrons $\varepsilon_\infty=1$. The electrons divide the shift of $\omega^2$ by $\varepsilon_\infty\approx10.9$ in GaAs, which brings 55.6 meV down to 35.9 meV (below).
  • It is retarded, in the sense of a finite speed of light (Section X.1): holds only for $cq\ll\omega$, inside the light cone. There, as the next part shows, the shifted transverse modes are the $q\to0$ end of the upper phonon polariton.

Physical picture: a uniform oscillating polarization

At $\bq=0$ every cell oscillates in phase, and the crystal holds a uniform polarization $\bP(t)$. Every field is then uniform, and a uniform magnetic field has no curl, so Ampère's law, $\nabla\times\bB=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj+\frac1c\partial_t\bE$, reduces to $0=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj+\frac1c\partial_t\bE$ with $\bj=\partial_t\bP$. Integrated in time, it gives the oscillating field $\bE=-4\pi\bP$, whatever the direction of $\bP$ (with electrons, $-4\pi\bP/\varepsilon_\infty$). The field opposes the displacement of the ions and stiffens the motion by the same amount for longitudinal and transverse polarization. At finite $\bq$ with $cq\gg\omega$ the picture changes: a longitudinal wave of dipoles still leaves bound charges and the same field (Gauss's law), but a transverse one leaves none, and its field, limited by Faraday's law, is smaller by $(\omega/cq)^2$.

Keeping $\bq$ finite, and the electrons in, shows all three features at once. The order of limits

In , $c$ and $q$ enter only through the product $cq$ in the transverse term. The point $q=0$, $1/c=0$ is therefore a point where the result depends on the direction of approach, and the two orders of the limits give different answers. shows both: drag the speed of light far above its real value and the polariton region shrinks to $q=0$.

  • $q\to0$ at fixed $c$, that is $cq\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}\,\bar\omega$. On the phonon-like root, whose $\bar\omega$ stays finite, the transverse term tends to $|\hat\bq\times\bj_\nu|^2/\varepsilon_\infty$ and adds to the longitudinal one:
    $$\begin{gathered}\bar\omega^2_{\nu}(\bq\to0)=\omega^2_{\mathbf 0\nu}\\ +\frac{4\pi}{\OV\,\varepsilon_\infty}\sum_\alpha\big|j_{\alpha\nu}(\mathbf 0)\big|^2\end{gathered} \tag{B7.20}$$
    The shift is isotropic and does not depend on the direction from which $\bq$ approaches zero. In GaAs all three polar modes sit at 35.9 meV. Equation is the case $\varepsilon_\infty=1$. (The other root of each transverse mode is the photon-like lower polariton, whose frequency goes to zero.)
  • $c\to\infty$ at fixed $q$, then $q\to0$, that is $cq\gg\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}\,\bar\omega$. The transverse term vanishes as $\bar\omega^2/c^2q^2$, and only the longitudinal one remains:
    $$\begin{gathered}\bar\omega^2_{\bq\nu}=\omega^2_{\bq\nu}\\ +\frac{4\pi}{\OV\,\varepsilon_\infty}\big|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\nu(\bq)\big|^2\end{gathered} \tag{B7.21}$$
    The TO modes stay at $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$, the LO mode moves to $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$, and for a general mode the shift depends on the direction $\hat\bq$ even as $q\to0$. A function whose limit at a point depends on the direction of approach cannot be a smooth power series in the components of $\bq$ there: the shift is nonanalytic at $\Gamma$, in the sense of Section A.4. This is the LO–TO splitting of DFPT. For an anisotropic crystal $\varepsilon_\infty$ becomes $\hat\bq\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot\hat\bq$, and the shift is the dipole–dipole term of Gonze and Lee, which Section B.9 derives from the limit $c\to\infty$ .16

The result of the EPP manuscript and the electrostatic result therefore differ in two independent ways, and they must be kept apart. Screening sets the size of the shift: in GaAs it takes the $q\to0$ frequency of every polar mode from 55.6 meV to 35.9 meV. The order of limits decides only whether the transverse modes share the shift: it moves the transverse modes from 35.9 meV, as upper polaritons, to 33.3 meV, as TO phonons. The longitudinal frequency is the same whichever limit is taken first. So the gap between 55.6 meV and the measured 36.2 meV comes from leaving out the electrons, not from the order of limits. Its LO–TO splitting sits inside the adiabatic force constants, as the dipole–dipole term $\Phi^{\mathrm{dd}}$ of Section A.4, , built from $\mathbf Z^*_\kappa$ and $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$. The EPP formalism derives the same term as a self-energy, , and adds what electrostatics cannot see: the phonon polaritons for $q\lesssim q_{\mathrm{light}}$, and an isotropic limit at $q\to0$.

Part A also offers a consistency test. For a cubic crystal with two atoms per cell, the long-range vertex of Section A.4 equals Fröhlich's , whose strength is $1/\varepsilon_\infty-1/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}$, if and only if $\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}=(\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p})^2/\varepsilon_\infty$. That is the screened longitudinal shift of , the same at every $q$, so and both pass. The unscreened passes only inside its own model, with $\varepsilon_\infty=1$. The test checks the screening, not the order of limits.

What this model leaves out

The electrons are a constant $\varepsilon_\infty$. That is the adiabatic approximation for electronic screening, good in a crystal with a gap. With free carriers $\Pi^{\rme}$ gains a Drude term and $D^{\rme}$ a plasmon pole, which doping can bring near the phonon frequencies; the dynamic $D^{\rme}$ then couples LO phonons to plasmons (Section B.9).

Only macroscopic fields are kept, as in the main line of Section B.3. There is no Lorentz local field, so the TO frequency equals the bare one, and the electronic screening is that of the random-phase approximation without local fields. Section B.3 shows that a local field would shift only the bare frequency, which no experiment measures.

Real polaritons are damped, which the theory leaves out; adds a damping by hand, has none. Hexagonal BN is uniaxial; the hBN presets of the figures treat it as isotropic, with its in-plane constants.

At finite $\bq$ the basis phase in $j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)$ matters: it makes $|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\nu|^2$ and $|\hat\bq\times\bj_\nu|^2$ independent of how atoms are assigned to cells, and it gives a rigid acoustic translation no current, because the Born charges add up to zero. Without it, would give the acoustic branches a spurious dipolar shift. As $\bq\to0$ the phase is 1, so the results at $\Gamma$ do not change.

Check yourself: three short questions

  1. The vertex $J_{\alpha\nu}(\bq,\omega)$ vanishes at $\omega=0$. What does this do to the two parts of the self-energy as $\omega\to0$, and which part survives?

    Answer. $J^*J\propto\omega^2$. The transverse part of $D^{\rme}$ stays finite as $\omega\to0$, so the transverse term of $\Pi^{\ph}$ vanishes as $\omega^2/c^2q^2$: a static transverse wave of dipoles creates no field. The longitudinal part of $D^{\rme}$ grows as $1/\omega^2$ and cancels the $\omega^2$, leaving $\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}(\bq,0)=2\pi\hbar|\hat\bq\cdot\bj_\nu|^2/(\OV\omega_{\bq\nu}\varepsilon_\infty)$: a static longitudinal wave of dipoles creates the screened Coulomb field of its bound charges. This is the dipole–dipole part of the force constants.

  2. A GaAs-like crystal without electrons has $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}=33.3$ meV and $\hbar\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p}=44.6$ meV. Where are its polar optical modes for $q\to0$ at finite $c$, and at $q=10^{-2}$ Å⁻¹? Where is the crossover?

    Answer. For $q\to0$ the LO mode and the two upper polaritons sit at $\sqrt{33.3^2+44.6^2}=55.6$ meV, by ; the lower polaritons are photon-like, with frequencies going to zero. At $10^{-2}$ Å⁻¹, $\hbar cq=19.7$ eV is far above the phonon energies: the LO mode stays at 55.6 meV and the TO modes are back at 33.3 meV, by with $\varepsilon_\infty=1$. The crossover is $q_{\mathrm{light}}=\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}/c=1.7\times10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹.

  3. In a cubic crystal with two atoms per cell the three bare optical modes are degenerate. Why can the diagonal approximation be exact in one choice of these modes and wrong in another?

    Answer. $\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'}$ is proportional to $\sum_{\alpha\beta}j^*_{\alpha\nu}D^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}j_{\beta\nu'}$, and $D^{\rme}$ takes one value along $\hat\bq$ and another across it . With currents along $\hat\bq$ and along two perpendicular directions across it, every off-diagonal element vanishes and is exact. With two currents at $45^\circ$ to $\hat\bq$, their off-diagonal element is half the difference of the longitudinal and transverse parts, as large as the diagonal ones, and dropping it misplaces the modes. The determinant gives the same modes in every basis.
