The total propagator: connection formulas, plasmons and polaritons

Section B.4 left the electromagnetic interaction in two pieces: an instantaneous Coulomb line between charges and a photon line between currents. This section shows that charge conservation ties every charge to a current, so that the two lines are the longitudinal and transverse parts of one free propagator, the propagator of Maxwell's equations in vacuum. The whole perturbation series then sums into one Dyson equation, $D=D^0+D^0\Pi D$. In an isotropic medium its poles are plasmons, longitudinal optical phonons and polaritons.

What you need: the split of the Hamiltonian into a free part and the interaction $V'$, with its Coulomb term and its transverse photons (Section B.4); the free photon line $D^{0\mathrm T}$ and the photon self-energy $\Pi$ of electrons and ions, with its diamagnetic part (Section B.5); what a propagator and a self-energy are, and the Dyson structure that the photon propagator shares with the screened interaction $W$ (Section X.1 and Section A.5); the Born charges and the LO–TO splitting of a polar crystal (Section A.4) and its dielectric function (Section B.3). Three background boxes recap linear response, gauge transformations and Dyson series. Equations are in Gaussian units, and the key ones carry an SI footnote.

One interaction, two kinds of lines

A crystal answers an electromagnetic field in one way: its charges move, and an induced current flows. Charge piles up only where that current converges. Two descriptions of this single response are in use. Part A describes it through the induced charge, which acts on other charges through the instantaneous Coulomb interaction; the density response, the dielectric function $\varepsilon$ and the screened interaction $W$ of Sections A.3 and A.5 all belong to this description. Sections B.4 and B.5 describe it through the induced current, which acts on other currents through the transverse photon field.

The two descriptions overlap. A converging current is a growing charge, so part of the current response is the charge response in another form, and the Coulomb line must be part of the full electromagnetic line. This section makes both statements exact. The tool is charge conservation; the result is one equation for one propagator.

Physical picture: one current, two ways to look at it

A current wave $\bj\,\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\br-\omega t)}$ has a longitudinal part, along the wavevector $\bq$, and a transverse part, perpendicular to it. Only the longitudinal part carries charge: charge conservation gives $\rho=\bq\cdot\bj/\omega$. Coulomb's law sees the longitudinal part; light sees the transverse part. Once every charge is written as a longitudinal current, every interaction line becomes a $3\times3$ tensor, every response a current–current correlator, and the perturbation series sums into one Dyson equation. In an isotropic medium that equation splits again into a longitudinal channel, whose modes are plasmons and LO phonons, and a transverse channel, whose modes are polaritons.

Section B.4 split the Hamiltonian of electrons, ions and field into a free part $H_0$, made of Kohn–Sham electrons, ions on short-range springs and transverse photons, and the interaction $V'$ . Written with charge densities throughout, the interaction is1

In the diagrams of Sections X.1 and B.5, an interaction or a free propagator is drawn as a line between two vertices. The interaction contains two kinds of lines. The Coulomb term joins two charges through the kernel $1/|\br-\br'|$, the potential of a unit point charge. The kernel depends only on $\br-\br'$, so a Fourier transform turns it into the single function $4\pi/q^2$, which is the bare Coulomb energy $v(\bq)=4\pi e^2/q^2$ divided by $e^2$. The minimal coupling, the term $-\frac1c\int\bj\cdot\bA\,\dd^3r$, joins two currents through the free transverse photon line of Section B.5 :

The Coulomb line is instantaneous: $4\pi/q^2$ does not depend on $\omega$. The photon line is retarded: it has poles on the light cone, $\omega=\pm cq$, where free light propagates.

Place an external charge $\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}$ and an external current $\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$ in the crystal. In vacuum the charge would create a scalar potential through the Coulomb line, and the current a transverse vector potential through the photon line. In the crystal both sources also move the crystal's own charges, whose fields add to the potentials, so in general each potential responds to each source. Four functions, the sectors of the propagator, say how:

$$\begin{aligned}A_0&=D_{\rho\rho}\,\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}+\sum_\beta D_{\rho\beta}\,j^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta,\\ A_\alpha&=D_{\alpha\rho}\,\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}+\sum_\beta D^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}\,j^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta .\end{aligned}\tag{B6.3}$$
$A_0$
scalar potential of Section B.2, a potential and not a potential energy: an electron at $\br$ has the energy $-eA_0(\br)$

external charge and current densities, the probes
$D_{\rho\rho}$

$D_{\rho\beta}$

$D_{\alpha\rho}$

Arranged as a $4\times4$ matrix, with one scalar row and column and a $3\times3$ block, the sectors are free when the crystal is absent: $D_{\rho\rho}=v/e^2$ in the corner, $D^{\mathrm T}=D^{0\mathrm T}$ in the block, and nothing in between. A scalar potential fixed by the charges alone and a transverse vector potential fixed by the currents is the bookkeeping of the Coulomb gauge of textbook electrodynamics, the gauge with $\nabla\cdot\bA=0$.4 In $(\bq,\omega)$ space $D_{\rho\rho}$ and $D_{\alpha\rho}$ have units of length², like $4\pi/q^2$, and $D_{\rho\beta}$ and $D^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}$ units of length × time, like $4\pi c/\omega^2$. Wherever a sector meets one of the correlators defined next, the two share an index of the same kind, $\rho$ or Cartesian, and their product is dimensionless.

Induced charges and currents: four correlators

The potentials that act inside the crystal, the external ones plus those of the induced sources, move the crystal's charges. To first order in the potentials the induced charge and current are linear in them, which defines four response functions, the correlators:5

$$\begin{aligned}\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}&=\Pi_{\rho\rho}\,A_0+\sum_\beta\Pi_{\rho\beta}\,A_\beta,\\ j^{\mathrm{ind}}_\alpha&=\Pi_{\alpha\rho}\,A_0+\sum_\beta\Pi_{\alpha\beta}\,A_\beta .\end{aligned}\tag{B6.4}$$
$\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}$, $\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}$
charge and current induced in the crystal
$\Pi_{\rho\rho}$
charge–charge correlator: charge induced per unit scalar potential (units 1/length²)
$\Pi_{\rho\beta}$
charge–current correlator: charge induced per unit vector potential along $\beta$ (1/length²)
$\Pi_{\alpha\rho}$
current–charge correlator: current along $\alpha$ induced per unit scalar potential (1/(length × time))
$\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$
current–current correlator: current induced per unit vector potential, paramagnetic plus diamagnetic. It is the photon self-energy of Sections X.1 and B.5 (1/(length × time))

Each correlator has an electronic and an ionic part, labelled by a superscript: $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}=\Pi^{\mathrm e}_{\alpha\beta}+\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\beta}$, and likewise for the other three. No mixed term appears at this order: in $H_0$ electrons and ions move independently, so a bubble that starts on an electron and ends on an ion vanishes. Everything is written in $(\bq,\omega)$ space for a homogeneous medium, where each relation is a product. In real space and time it is a convolution, and in a crystal the products become matrices labelled by reciprocal lattice vectors (the local fields of the caveat at the end of the section).

Because $A_0$ and $\bA$ in are the total potentials, which already contain the fields of the induced sources, the correlators are irreducible response functions: they describe how the charges respond to the field they actually feel, not to the external field alone. In the random-phase approximation (RPA) used throughout Part B they are the bubbles of independent particles: Kohn–Sham electrons for $\Pi^{\mathrm e}$, bare phonons for $\Pi^{\ion}$. The charge–charge correlator of the electrons is the irreducible polarizability of Part A in charge units. An electron feels the potential energy $-eA_0$; the electrons respond with an induced number density $\chi^{\mathrm{irr}}$$\,(-eA_0)$, and each carries the charge $-e$, so

$$\Pi^{\mathrm e}_{\rho\rho}=e^2\,\chi^{\mathrm{irr}} .$$

The background box below gives the microscopic form of all four correlators. It explains why a charge source brings the prefactor $-\ii/\hbar$ and a current source $+\ii/\hbar c$, and why only $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ has a diamagnetic term.

Linear response and the four correlators skip if familiar

Perturb a system in equilibrium by $H'(t)=\int\dd^3r\,O(\br)\,\mathcal F(\br,t)$, where $\mathcal F$ is a classical field and $O$ the operator it couples to. To first order in $\mathcal F$ the expectation value of an operator $X$ changes by

$$\begin{gathered}\delta\langle X(\br,t)\rangle=\int\dd t'\int\dd^3r'\\ \times\,\mathcal R_{XO}(\br,\br';t-t')\,\mathcal F(\br',t'),\\ \mathcal R_{XO}(\br,\br';t)=-\frac{\ii}{\hbar}\,\theta(t)\,\big\langle[X(\br,t),O(\br',0)]\big\rangle .\end{gathered}$$

This is Kubo's formula. The operators evolve with the unperturbed Hamiltonian, the average is taken in its equilibrium state, and the step function $\theta(t)$, equal to 1 for $t\gt0$ and 0 otherwise, makes the response causal; in frequency space it becomes the $+\ii\eta$ of retarded functions.

A charge density couples to a scalar potential as $H'=\int\rho A_0\,\dd^3r$, so $O=\rho$ and $\mathcal F=A_0$. A current couples to a vector potential as $H'=-\frac1c\int\bj\cdot\bA\,\dd^3r$, so $O=j_\beta$ and $\mathcal F=-A_\beta/c$. Hence

$$\begin{gathered}\Pi_{\rho\rho}=\mathcal R_{\rho\rho},\qquad \Pi_{\alpha\rho}=\mathcal R_{j_\alpha\rho},\\ \Pi_{\rho\beta}=-\frac1c\,\mathcal R_{\rho j_\beta},\qquad \Pi_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac1c\,\mathcal R_{j_\alpha j_\beta}+\Pi^{\mathrm{dia}}_{\alpha\beta} .\end{gathered}$$

A charge source brings the prefactor $-\ii/\hbar$, a current source $+\ii/\hbar c$. The last term exists because the current operator itself contains $\bA$. With minimal coupling the velocity of an electron is $(\mathbf p+\frac ec\bA)/m_{\mathrm e}$, where $\mathbf p$ is its momentum operator, so even the unperturbed state carries the current $-\frac{e^2}{m_{\mathrm e}c}\,n_{\mathrm e}\bA$. That is the diamagnetic current; Section B.5 computed its response, the diamagnetic part $\Pi^{\mathrm{dia}}$, for electrons and ions . A scalar potential produces no such term. For a homogeneous medium the correlators depend only on $\br-\br'$ and $t-t'$, and a Fourier transform turns the convolution into the products of . Written with the Fourier components of the operators, each correlator carries one factor of the inverse crystal volume, $1/(N_\ell\OV)$, for $N_\ell$ cells of volume $\OV$.

The sectors at lowest order: lines and bubbles

The potentials in the crystal are the vacuum potentials of all sources, external and induced: $A_0=\frac{v}{e^2}\big(\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}+\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}\big)$ and $\bA=D^{0\mathrm T}\big(\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}+\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}\big)$. Inserting and solving order by order in the correlators gives each sector as a series.

  1. Zeroth order: no induced sources. Then $D_{\rho\rho}=v/e^2$, $D^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}=D^{0\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}$, and $D_{\rho\beta}=D_{\alpha\rho}=0$.

    In vacuum a charge creates only a scalar potential, by Coulomb's law, and a current only a transverse vector potential.

  2. First order: let the zeroth-order potentials induce sources, and add the vacuum potentials of those sources. An external charge, for example, creates $A_0=\frac{v}{e^2}\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}$. This induces $\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}=\Pi_{\rho\rho}\frac{v}{e^2}\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}$ and $j^{\mathrm{ind}}_\gamma=\Pi_{\gamma\rho}\frac{v}{e^2}\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}$, whose potentials are $\frac{v}{e^2}\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}$ and $\sum_\gamma D^{0\mathrm T}_{\alpha\gamma}j^{\mathrm{ind}}_\gamma$.

    Reading off the coefficients of $\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}$ gives the first-order terms of $D_{\rho\rho}$ and $D_{\alpha\rho}$. An external current gives those of $D_{\rho\beta}$ and $D^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}$ in the same way.
$$\begin{aligned}D_{\rho\rho}&=\tfrac{v}{e^2}+\tfrac{v}{e^2}\,\Pi_{\rho\rho}\,\tfrac{v}{e^2}+\dots,\\ D_{\rho\beta}&=\tfrac{v}{e^2}\sum_\gamma\Pi_{\rho\gamma}D^{0\mathrm T}_{\gamma\beta}+\dots,\\ D_{\alpha\rho}&=\sum_\gamma D^{0\mathrm T}_{\alpha\gamma}\Pi_{\gamma\rho}\,\tfrac{v}{e^2}+\dots,\\ D^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}&=D^{0\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm T}_{\alpha\gamma}\Pi_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm T}_{\delta\beta}+\dots\end{aligned}\tag{B6.5}$$

Each first-order term is a line, a bubble and a line. The kind of line at each end is fixed by the kind of source (on the right) and of potential (on the left), and the bubble has a matching vertex at each end: a dot for a charge and a square for a current in the diagrams of . The four sectors are objects of different kinds, a scalar, a row, a column and a $3\times3$ block. They cannot be added, and at higher orders every sector contains lines and bubbles of all kinds. The next two subsections remove this complication.6

Charge conservation: the connection formulas

The four correlators are not independent. The induced charge is the charge that the induced current has piled up, and that statement links every pair of them. Charge is conserved locally:

$$\begin{gathered}\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\bj=0,\\ -e\,\partial_t n_{\mathrm e}+\nabla\cdot\bj^{\mathrm e}=0,\\ \omega\,\rho(\bq,\omega)=\bq\cdot\bj(\bq,\omega) .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.6}$$

The first form is the continuity equation for any charge density and its current. The second is the same for the electrons, written with their number density: the factor $e$ turns the number of electrons per unit volume into a charge, and without it the equation would equate quantities of different dimensions.3 The third is the Fourier form: for a wave $\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\br-\omega t)}$, $\partial_t\to-\ii\omega$ and $\nabla\to\ii\bq$. For the ions continuity holds identically, because the bound charge $-\nabla\cdot\bP$ and the current $\partial_t\bP$ come from the same polarization. It holds for each species separately and for the induced parts alone, since the external sources are conserved by themselves.

  1. Take the sources induced by a scalar potential alone, $\bA=0$. Continuity, $\bq\cdot\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}=\omega\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}$, gives $\sum_\alpha q_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha\rho}A_0=\omega\,\Pi_{\rho\rho}A_0$, so

    $$\Pi_{\rho\rho}=\frac1\omega\sum_\alpha q_\alpha\,\Pi_{\alpha\rho} .$$
    Continuity holds whatever potential drives the charges, so the equality holds for every $A_0$, and the coefficients themselves must be equal.

  2. Take the sources induced by a vector potential alone, $A_0=0$. The same argument, for each component of an arbitrary $\bA$, gives

    $$\Pi_{\rho\beta}=\frac1\omega\sum_\alpha q_\alpha\,\Pi_{\alpha\beta} .$$
    These two steps act on the left index, the induced quantity. To reach the right index, the one that labels the source, a second fact is needed.

  3. Describe the same longitudinal field in two ways: by a scalar potential $A_0$ with $\bA=0$, or by the vector potential $\bA=-\frac c\omega\bq A_0$ with no scalar potential. Both give the same fields, so both induce the same current:

    $$\Pi_{\alpha\rho}=-\frac c\omega\sum_\beta\Pi_{\alpha\beta}\,q_\beta .$$
    With the electric field $\bE$$\,=-\nabla A_0-\frac1c\partial_t\bA$, the first description gives $\bE=-\ii\bq A_0$ and the second $\bE=\frac{\ii\omega}{c}\bA=-\ii\bq A_0$; in both the magnetic field $\bB=\ii\bq\times\bA$ vanishes. The crystal responds to the fields, not to the potentials that describe them: this is gauge invariance (background box below). It holds only for the full $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$, paramagnetic plus diamagnetic. A vector potential also drives the diamagnetic current, a scalar potential does not, and only the sum is the same in both descriptions.

  4. Insert step 3 into step 1:

    $$\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}q_\alpha\,\Pi_{\alpha\beta}\,q_\beta .$$
    Step 3 applied to the induced charge, combined with step 2, gives the same result, as it must: either way the charge is the divergence of a current driven by a longitudinal field.

Collected, these are the connection formulas:7

$$\begin{gathered}\Pi_{\rho\beta}=\frac1\omega\sum_\alpha q_\alpha\,\Pi_{\alpha\beta},\\ \Pi_{\alpha\rho}=-\frac c\omega\sum_\beta\Pi_{\alpha\beta}\,q_\beta,\\ \Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}q_\alpha\,\Pi_{\alpha\beta}\,q_\beta .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.7}$$

They hold for the electrons and for the ions separately. In SI units the factors $c$ disappear.1 In a crystal, where the correlators are matrices between plane waves with wavevectors $\bq+\bG$ and $\bq+\bG'$ ($\bG$ a reciprocal lattice vector), the $\bq$ on the left of $\Pi$ becomes $\bq+\bG$ and the $\bq$ on the right becomes $\bq+\bG'$.

Three consequences follow. First, only $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ is independent: the $4\times4$ matrix of correlators carries no more information than its $3\times3$ current block. Second, the charge response is the longitudinal part of the current response, $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{cq^2}{\omega^2}\,\hat\bq\cdot\Pi\cdot\hat\bq$, where $\hat\bq\cdot\Pi\cdot\hat\bq$ stands for $\sum_{\alpha\beta}\hat q_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha\beta}\hat q_\beta$; a transverse current carries no charge. Third, the dielectric function of Part A and the optical response are one object. With $\Pi^{\mathrm e}_{\rho\rho}=e^2\chi^{\mathrm{irr}}$ and $v=4\pi e^2/q^2$, the electronic test-charge dielectric function of Section A.5 becomes

$$\begin{gathered}\varepsilon^{\mathrm e}=1-v\chi^{\mathrm{irr}}=1-\frac{v}{e^2}\,\Pi^{\mathrm e}_{\rho\rho}\\ =1+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\hat\bq\cdot\Pi^{\mathrm e}\cdot\hat\bq .\end{gathered}$$

With the ionic bubble added, the same steps give the total $\varepsilon$. This is the longitudinal element of the formula that turns the photon self-energy into a dielectric function . The density response of GW, of the Bethe–Salpeter equation and of density-functional perturbation theory is the longitudinal current response of optics.

Gauge transformations skip if familiar

The fields are $\bE=-\nabla A_0-\frac1c\partial_t\bA$ and $\bB=\nabla\times\bA$. Changing the potentials to $A_0-\frac1c\partial_t\vartheta$ and $\bA+\nabla\vartheta$, for any function $\vartheta(\br,t)$, leaves both fields unchanged; such a change is a gauge transformation. Charges coupled through $\int\big(\rho A_0-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA\big)\dd^3r$ feel the change $\frac1c\int\vartheta\,\big(\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\bj\big)\,\dd^3r$, after an integration by parts and up to a total time derivative, which does not affect the motion. It vanishes when charge is conserved: gauge invariance and charge conservation are one statement. In quantum mechanics the transformation also multiplies the wavefunction of each electron by $\ee^{-\ii e\vartheta/\hbar c}$, and the diamagnetic part of the current is what makes the current transform correctly.

Step 3 uses the transformation with $\vartheta=\frac{\ii c}{\omega}A_0$ for a wave $\ee^{-\ii\omega t}$. Then $\partial_t\vartheta=cA_0$, the scalar potential is removed, and $\nabla\vartheta=-\frac c\omega\bq A_0$ appears as a longitudinal vector potential. Section B.2 uses the same freedom to choose the temporal gauge $A_0=0$ , and the subsection on the four sectors uses it to put the whole field into $\bA$.

Derivation: the ionic charge response, directly and through the connection formula

The ions give an exact test, because their bubble is known in closed form. Compute $\Pi^{\ion}_{\rho\rho}$ directly from the bound charge of the dipoles, then from Section B.5's $\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\beta}$ through .

  1. The Fourier component of the bound charge is $\rho^{\ion}(\bq)=-\ii\bq\cdot\bP(\bq)$, with $\bP(\bq)=\sum_{\ell\kappa}e\,\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\bu_{\ell\kappa}\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_{\ell\kappa}}$.

    $\rho(\bq)=\int\dd^3r\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}\rho(\br)$; the divergence becomes $\ii\bq\cdot$, and each point dipole contributes the phase of its own position $\btau_{\ell\kappa}$.

  2. Insert the displacement operator and sum over cells:

    $$\begin{gathered}\rho^{\ion}(\bq)=-\ii\sqrt{N_\ell}\sum_\nu\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}\Big)^{1/2}\\ \times\,\bq\cdot\bj_\nu(\bq)\,\phi_{\bq\nu},\\ \bq\cdot\bj_\nu(\bq)=\sum_{\kappa\alpha\beta}q_\alpha\,\frac{eZ^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{M_\kappa}}\\ \times\,e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa} .\end{gathered}$$
    $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_{\ell\kappa}}=\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}$. The lattice sum $\sum_\ell\ee^{\ii(\bq'-\bq)\cdot\bR_\ell}=N_\ell\delta_{\bq'\bq}$ keeps the phonons of wavevector $\bq$ and uses up the phase of the cell, while the phase of the atom inside the cell stays. The result contains the ionic current vertex $j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)$ of Section B.5 , contracted with $\bq$; $\phi_{\bq\nu}$ is the phonon field, $e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)$ the phonon eigenvector, $\omega_{\bq\nu}$ the phonon frequency and $M_\kappa$ the mass of atom $\kappa$.

  3. Use the microscopic form of $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ (first background box), per unit crystal volume $N_\ell\OV$. Different modes are independent, and for one mode the retarded correlator of the phonon field, $-\frac{\ii}{\hbar}\theta(t)\langle[\phi_{\bq\nu}(t),\phi^\dagger_{\bq\nu}(0)]\rangle$, has the Fourier transform

    $$F^0_\nu(\bq,\omega)=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}} .$$
    This is the free phonon propagator $F^0$ . The commutator of $\phi_{\bq\nu}(t)$ with $\phi^\dagger_{\bq\nu}(0)$ is the number $-2\ii\sin\omega_{\bq\nu}t$, so the result holds at any temperature.

  4. Collect the factors:

    $$\Pi^{\ion}_{\rho\rho}(\bq,\omega)=\frac1\OV\sum_\nu\frac{|\bq\cdot\bj_\nu(\bq)|^2}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}} .$$
    The factors $-\ii$ of $\rho^{\ion}$ and $+\ii$ of its conjugate multiply to 1, $N_\ell/(N_\ell\OV)=1/\OV$, and $\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}F^0_\nu$ is the fraction shown.

  5. Now the connection formula. Section B.5 found, for the macroscopic element $\bG=\bG'=0$ ,

    $$\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{\omega^2}{c\OV}\sum_\nu\frac{j_{\alpha\nu}(\bq)\,j^*_{\beta\nu}(\bq)}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}} ,$$

    and $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}q_\alpha\Pi^{\ion}_{\alpha\beta}q_\beta$ is exactly the result of step 4.

    The factor $\omega^2$ in front of $\Pi^{\ion}$ cancels the $1/\omega^2$ of the connection formula, and the two vertices combine into $|\bq\cdot\bj_\nu|^2$.

  6. Without the diamagnetic term the check fails. Section B.5's diamagnetic part is minus the static paramagnetic one , $\Pi^{\ion,\mathrm{dia}}_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{c\OV}\sum_\nu j_{\alpha\nu}j^*_{\beta\nu}$. Through the paramagnetic part alone gives the correct result minus $\frac{1}{\OV\omega^2}\sum_\nu|\bq\cdot\bj_\nu|^2$.

    That extra term is a charge response that diverges as $\omega\to0$, as if the insulator could carry a static current. The overall factor $\omega^2$ of the full $\Pi^{\ion}$ comes entirely from the diamagnetic subtraction.

The vertex $\bq\cdot\bj_\nu(\bq)$ is the sum over atoms of the long-range Fröhlich vertex of Section A.4 , at $\bG=0$ and with the factor $e$ and the masses $M_\kappa$ taken inside, phase of the atom included. The second tab of plots the three routes for one polar mode.8

What the connection formulas need

Three conditions, each of which a calculation can violate. First, $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ must be the full correlator, paramagnetic plus diamagnetic; the paramagnetic part alone adds a spurious static charge response, for the ions the term $-\frac{1}{\OV\omega^2}\sum_\nu|\bq\cdot\bj_\nu|^2$ of the derivation above, a Drude term of the wrong sign in an insulator. This is the familiar $1/\omega^2$ divergence of optical spectra computed from current vertices (the velocity gauge) without the diamagnetic or sum-rule term. Second, the current operator must obey continuity with the Hamiltonian that moves the charges. With nonlocal pseudopotentials the electron velocity is not $\mathbf p/m_{\mathrm e}$, and the current needs an extra term, the commutator of the pseudopotential with the position. Third, sums over bands must be complete, because the identity between the density and current forms uses completeness; truncated sums make an insulator look like a metal (Section B.5). When the three hold, a calculation with density vertices between Bloch states, $\langle m\bk+\bq|\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\br}|n\bk\rangle$ (the length gauge), and one with current vertices give the same dielectric function. The EPP manuscript checks the formulas explicitly only for the ionic correlator and infers the rest.

Longitudinal and transverse parts

The connection formulas single out the direction of $\bq$. Every vector splits into a part along $\hat\bq$ and a part perpendicular to it, by the two projectors $\mathcal P^{\mathrm L}_{\alpha\beta}=\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$ and $\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$, which add up to the unit matrix. For a vector, $\bA^{\mathrm L}=\hat\bq\,(\hat\bq\cdot\bA)$ is its longitudinal part and $\bA^{\mathrm T}=\bA-\bA^{\mathrm L}$ its transverse part. A tensor $X_{\alpha\beta}$, such as $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ or $D_{\alpha\beta}$, has two indices, so inserting $\mathcal P^{\mathrm L}+\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}$ on both sides splits it into four parts:

$$\begin{gathered}X_{\alpha\beta}=X^{\mathrm L}\,\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta+\hat q_\alpha X^{\mathrm{LT}}_\beta\\ +X^{\mathrm{TL}}_\alpha\,\hat q_\beta+X^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta} .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.8}$$
$X^{\mathrm L}$
longitudinal part, the scalar $\sum_{\alpha\beta}\hat q_\alpha X_{\alpha\beta}\hat q_\beta$, also written $\hat\bq\cdot X\cdot\hat\bq$
$X^{\mathrm{LT}}_\beta$, $X^{\mathrm{TL}}_\alpha$
mixed parts, vectors perpendicular to $\hat\bq$: $X^{\mathrm{LT}}_\beta=\sum_{\gamma\delta}\hat q_\gamma X_{\gamma\delta}\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\delta\beta}$ and $X^{\mathrm{TL}}_\alpha=\sum_{\gamma\delta}\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\gamma}X_{\gamma\delta}\hat q_\delta$
$X^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}$
transverse part, $\sum_{\gamma\delta}\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\gamma}X_{\gamma\delta}\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\delta\beta}$, a $2\times2$ block in the plane perpendicular to $\hat\bq$

In the polarization basis of Section B.4, with $\be_1$, $\be_2$ transverse and $\be_3=\hat\bq$ ($\be_\lambda(\bq)$), the longitudinal part is the entry $33$, the mixed parts are the entries $31,32$ and $13,23$, and the transverse part is the block $\lambda,\lambda'=1,2$. In an isotropic medium, one that looks the same in every direction and in a mirror, the only tensors that can be built from $\hat\bq$ are $\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$ and $\delta_{\alpha\beta}$. The mixed parts then vanish, and the transverse part is a scalar times $\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}$; that scalar is also written $X^{\mathrm T}$.

From four sectors to one tensor

Step 3 of the connection formulas showed that a scalar potential is a longitudinal vector potential in another form, and continuity says the same of a charge and a longitudinal current. Rewriting both ends of every sector in vector form turns the four sectors into four $3\times3$ tensors, which can be added. draws the result in the polarization basis. In the $4\times4$ matrix the row and the column $\lambda=3$ are empty, because there the vector potential is transverse; the conversion moves the scalar row and column $\rho$ into them.

  1. Put the whole field into the vector potential: replace $(A_0,\bA)$ by $\big(0,\ \bA-\frac c\omega\bq A_0\big)$.

    This is the gauge transformation of step 3 and of the background box. The fields do not change, and the new description is the temporal gauge of Section B.2, in which $\bA$ alone carries the field, longitudinal and transverse.

  2. Write the external charge as a current: $\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}=\bq\cdot\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}/\omega$.

    The external source obeys continuity by itself: an external charge can only appear where an external current brings it.

  3. Insert both into . The new vector potential is $\bA=D\,\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$, with one $3\times3$ propagator

    $$\begin{aligned}D_{\alpha\beta}={}&-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,q_\alpha q_\beta\,D_{\rho\rho}-\frac c\omega\,q_\alpha D_{\rho\beta}\\&+\frac1\omega\,D_{\alpha\rho}\,q_\beta+D^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta} .\end{aligned}$$

Comparing with , the factor $\hat q_\alpha$ on the left and the factor $\hat q_\beta$ on the right identify the blocks of the propagator with the sectors:

$$\begin{gathered}D^{\mathrm L}=-\frac{cq^2}{\omega^2}\,D_{\rho\rho},\\ D^{\mathrm{LT}}_\beta=-\frac{cq}{\omega}\,D_{\rho\beta},\\ D^{\mathrm{TL}}_\alpha=\frac{q}{\omega}\,D_{\alpha\rho},\end{gathered}\tag{B6.9}$$

The scalar sector carries the screened Coulomb interaction: an electron at $\br'$ creates the potential $-eD_{\rho\rho}$, so the energy between two electrons is $e^2D_{\rho\rho}$, which is $v$ in vacuum and, as shown below for an isotropic medium, the screened interaction $W$ of Part A in a crystal.

The zeroth order of $D_{\rho\rho}$ is the Coulomb line, and turns it into the free longitudinal line $D^{0\mathrm L}$:

$$\begin{gathered}D^{0\mathrm L}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\,q_\alpha q_\beta\,\frac{v(\bq)}{e^2}\\ =-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.10}$$

It is the Coulomb interaction, written as the longitudinal vector potential produced per unit longitudinal current, and it is the line that Section B.5 obtained from the longitudinal part of the Ampère–Maxwell equation, : charge conservation and the Maxwell equations give the same object. Inserting the connection formulas into the first-order sectors and converting with , each block becomes a line, a bubble and a line built from $D^{0\mathrm L}$, $D^{0\mathrm T}$ and $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ alone:

  1. Scalar sector. At first order,

    $$\begin{gathered}D^{\mathrm L}\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta=\sum_{\gamma\delta}\Big(-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\frac{v}{e^2}q_\alpha q_\gamma\Big)\,\Pi_{\gamma\delta}\\ \times\Big(-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\frac{v}{e^2}q_\delta q_\beta\Big)\\ =\sum_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm L}_{\alpha\gamma}\,\Pi_{\gamma\delta}\,D^{0\mathrm L}_{\delta\beta} .\end{gathered}$$
    The conversion factor $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}q_\alpha q_\beta$ and the factor $-\frac{c}{\omega^2}q_\gamma q_\delta$ of the connection formula for $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$ pair up with the two Coulomb lines, and each pair is a $D^{0\mathrm L}$ by .

  2. Mixed sectors: $\hat q_\alpha D^{\mathrm{LT}}_\beta=\sum_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm L}_{\alpha\gamma}\Pi_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm T}_{\delta\beta}$ and $D^{\mathrm{TL}}_\alpha\hat q_\beta=\sum_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm T}_{\alpha\gamma}\Pi_{\gamma\delta}D^{0\mathrm L}_{\delta\beta}$.

    Here one end of the bubble was a charge vertex, and it becomes a $D^{0\mathrm L}$ by the same pairing; the other end was already a current joined to a photon line.

  3. Sum the four blocks.

    The four products are the expansion of $(D^{0\mathrm L}+D^{0\mathrm T})\,\Pi\,(D^{0\mathrm L}+D^{0\mathrm T})$, and the zeroth orders add up to $D^{0\mathrm L}+D^{0\mathrm T}$.
$$\begin{gathered}D_{\alpha\beta}=D^0_{\alpha\beta}\\ +\sum_{\gamma\delta}D^0_{\alpha\gamma}\,\Pi_{\gamma\delta}\,D^0_{\delta\beta}+\dots,\\ D^0=D^{0\mathrm L}+D^{0\mathrm T},\\ \Pi=\Pi^{\mathrm e}+\Pi^{\ion} .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.11}$$

The rule behind this result is short. Every charge vertex becomes a current vertex, and every Coulomb line $v/e^2$ between charges becomes the line $D^{0\mathrm L}$ between currents. After the rule, lines of both kinds join the same kind of vertex, and the sectors add up line by line (step 2 of ).9

What $D^0$ is: the vacuum Maxwell propagator

The free propagator of is the sum of the line just found and the photon line of Section B.5:10

$$\begin{aligned}D^0_{\alpha\beta}={}&-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta\\&-\frac{4\pi c\,(\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta)}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-c^2q^2}\\ ={}&\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\;\frac{\omega^2\delta_{\alpha\beta}/c^2-q_\alpha q_\beta}{q^2-\omega^2/c^2} .\end{aligned}\tag{B6.12}$$

In the second form $\omega$ stands for $\omega+\ii\eta$. This is the propagator of Maxwell's equations in vacuum, in the temporal gauge.1 The proof takes three steps.

  1. In the temporal gauge $\bE=-\frac1c\partial_t\bA$ and $\bB=\nabla\times\bA$. The Ampère–Maxwell law of Section B.4 , $\nabla\times\bB=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj+\frac1c\partial_t\bE$, becomes $\nabla\times\nabla\times\bA+\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t^2\bA=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj$.

    Faraday's law and $\nabla\cdot\bB=0$ hold automatically for fields built from potentials. Gauss's law is the longitudinal part of this equation once the current obeys continuity.

  2. For a wave $\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\br-\omega t)}$ this reads

    $$\begin{gathered}\sum_\beta\Big[\Big(q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\Big)\delta_{\alpha\beta}\\ -q_\alpha q_\beta\Big]A_\beta=\frac{4\pi}{c}\,j_\alpha .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.13}$$
    $\nabla\times\nabla\times\bA=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\bA)-\nabla^2\bA$, which for the wave becomes $-\bq(\bq\cdot\bA)+q^2\bA$; and $\partial_t^2\to-\omega^2$.

  3. Project on $\hat\bq$ and perpendicular to it. The longitudinal part gives $-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\bA^{\mathrm L}=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj^{\mathrm L}$, so $\bA^{\mathrm L}=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\bj^{\mathrm L}$. The transverse part gives $\big(q^2-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\big)\bA^{\mathrm T}=\frac{4\pi}{c}\bj^{\mathrm T}$, so $\bA^{\mathrm T}=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2-c^2q^2}\bj^{\mathrm T}$.

    The operator in brackets has the eigenvalue $-\omega^2/c^2$ along $\hat\bq$ and $q^2-\omega^2/c^2$ in the transverse plane. Inverting each eigenvalue gives ; the prescription $\omega\to\omega+\ii\eta$ selects the solution that follows its source.

So $\bA=D^0\bj$ is the field that a given current produces in vacuum, and the second term of , $D^0\Pi D^0\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$, is the field of the current that this field induces in the crystal. The two parts of $D^0$ behave differently. The transverse part has poles on the light cone. The longitudinal part does not depend on $q$: it has no light cone, because Coulomb's law acts instantaneously at every distance. Its double pole at $\omega=0$, which needs the same causal prescription $\omega\to\omega+\ii\eta$, describes a static charge: in the temporal gauge a constant field $\bE=-\frac1c\partial_t\bA$ means a vector potential that grows linearly in time. The pole is harmless, because $D^{0\mathrm L}$ only ever acts on conserved currents, and continuity supplies the compensating powers of $\omega$. Two currents couple through $-\frac1c\,\bj^*\cdot D^0\cdot\bj'$; for the longitudinal line this is $\frac{4\pi}{\omega^2}(\hat\bq\cdot\bj)^*(\hat\bq\cdot\bj')=\frac{4\pi}{q^2}\rho^*\rho'$ by , the Coulomb energy of the two charges.

Photon propagators depend on the gauge; only their action on conserved currents does not, and the connection formulas guarantee that $D$ only acts on conserved currents. The two parts also differ in size. Their ratio is $|D^{0\mathrm T}|/|D^{0\mathrm L}|=\omega^2/|\omega^2-c^2q^2|$, which for a phonon of 36 meV is $3\times10^{-4}$ already at $q=10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹, a thousandth of the Brillouin zone. Outside a tiny region around $\bq=0$ the longitudinal, Coulomb part dominates, which is why the electrostatic theory of Part A works so well (Section X.1).

Is the free longitudinal line zero or not?

Both answers in this part of the primer are correct, and they concern different objects. Section B.5 found that the longitudinal field of $H_0$ has no free line of its own: in the interaction picture it has no restoring force and does not move, so its free propagator vanishes at every nonzero frequency. The line , which Section B.5 also wrote from the Maxwell equations , is not that object. It is the Coulomb term of $V'$, the energy of the longitudinal field that Gauss's law turned into an instantaneous interaction between charges , carried by the connection formulas from charge vertices to current vertices. A theory that kept the Coulomb line $v$ and added $D^{0\mathrm L}$ as well would count the Coulomb interaction twice.

One Dyson equation

At higher orders each sector of the $4\times4$ matrix mixes both kinds of lines and all four kinds of bubbles: a Coulomb line can join a charge bubble to a mixed bubble, which continues with a photon line into a current bubble, and so on. The rule of the previous subsection maps every such diagram to one built from current vertices joined by $D^{0\mathrm L}$ and $D^{0\mathrm T}$ (step 3 of ). The series then sums in four steps.

  1. A diagram of order $n$ is a chain of $n$ bubbles joined by $n+1$ lines.

    The correlators are irreducible, so no single line can cut one of them in two; everything between two bubbles is one free line.

  2. Apply the rule to every vertex and every line.

    The connection formulas apply to each bubble separately, because each holds for the full correlator of one species.

  3. Sum over the kind of each line, longitudinal or transverse, and over the species of each bubble, electronic or ionic.

    Each choice of line types belongs to exactly one sector of the old matrix, the one fixed by the kinds of its first and last lines, and the four sectors together contain every choice once. So each line becomes $D^0$ and each bubble $\Pi$.

  4. The term of order $n$ is $D^0(\Pi D^0)^n$, and the series is geometric.

    Factoring the first $D^0\Pi$ out of every term after the first leaves the whole series again (background box below).
$$\begin{gathered}D=D^0+D^0\,\Pi\,D,\\ D_{\alpha\beta}=D^0_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\gamma\delta}D^0_{\alpha\gamma}\,\Pi_{\gamma\delta}\,D_{\delta\beta} .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.14}$$

This is the Dyson equation for the total propagator $D$, with $D^0$ from and $\Pi=\Pi^{\mathrm e}+\Pi^{\ion}$ from Section B.5 and ; in the second line every function is taken at the same $(\bq,\omega)$. Section X.1 announced its form . The derivation shows that one $3\times3$ tensor equation carries the Coulomb physics and the photon physics together.11

The same equation follows in one line from classical electrodynamics. In the temporal gauge the induced current is $\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}=\Pi\bA$, longitudinal and transverse together, by with $A_0=0$. The field in the crystal is the vacuum field of all currents, $\bA=D^0\big(\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}+\Pi\bA\big)$, and writing $\bA=D\bj^{\mathrm{ext}}$ for every external current gives . Multiplied by the operator of , the same statement is Maxwell's equation in the medium, summed over $\beta$:

$$\begin{gathered}\Big[q^2\delta_{\alpha\beta}-q_\alpha q_\beta-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\Big]A_\beta\\ =\frac{4\pi}{c}\,j^{\mathrm{ext}}_\alpha,\\ \varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\Pi_{\alpha\beta} .\end{gathered}\tag{B6.15}$$

Here $\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}$ is the dielectric tensor of the crystal at wavevector $\bq$. The induced current is the time derivative of an induced polarization, $\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}=-\ii\omega\bP^{\mathrm{ind}}$; with $\bA=\frac{c}{\ii\omega}\bE$ this gives $\bP^{\mathrm{ind}}=\frac{c}{\omega^2}\Pi\bE$, and $\boldsymbol\varepsilon=1+4\pi\frac{c}{\omega^2}\Pi$. A collective mode of the crystal is a field that exists without an external source: a pole of $D$, where the bracket in has a zero eigenvalue.1

$D$ propagates the total field, longitudinal and transverse, through a crystal of electrons and phonons. Its longitudinal block contains the screened Coulomb interaction of Part A, its transverse block the light in the crystal, and its mixed blocks the coupling of the two: it is the screened interaction $W$ with photons added. The approximation is the RPA. $\Pi$ is the sum of the lowest-order bubbles of independent electrons and of bare phonons, with no mixed electron–ion bubbles, no exchange–correlation corrections to the electron bubble and, in the $(\bq,\omega)$ form used here, no crystal local fields.

Dyson equations and geometric series skip if familiar

A Dyson equation is the sum of a geometric series of diagrams. If every term repeats the same block, $D=D^0+D^0\Pi D^0+D^0\Pi D^0\Pi D^0+\dots$, factoring $D^0\Pi$ out of every term after the first leaves the whole series again: $D=D^0+D^0\Pi D$. For numbers this is the familiar sum $a+ar+ar^2+\dots=a/(1-r)$, here $D=D^0/(1-D^0\Pi)$. For matrices, $D=(1-D^0\Pi)^{-1}D^0$, or $D^{-1}=(D^0)^{-1}-\Pi$. The sum has poles where $1-D^0\Pi$ cannot be inverted, although no single term of the series has a pole there: collective modes appear only after resummation. The repeated block $\Pi$, the self-energy, collects the diagrams that cannot be split in two by cutting one free line; in the RPA it is one bubble. Section A.5 uses the same structure for the screened interaction, $W=v+v\chi^{\mathrm{irr}}W$ , and Section X.1 sets the two Dyson equations side by side .

An isotropic medium: plasmons, LO phonons and polaritons

In an isotropic medium, or in a cubic crystal at wavelengths much longer than the cell, the longitudinal and transverse parts of every tensor stay separate. A longitudinal wave has its electric field along $\bq$: it carries charge and no magnetic field, and it acts through Coulomb's law. A transverse wave has its electric field across $\bq$: it carries no charge, and it is light. The Dyson equation then splits into one equation for each channel, and the modes of each channel follow from its own dielectric function.

The simplest model: a polar insulator

Take a cubic polar insulator with two atoms per cell, such as GaAs, at photon energies far below the band gap and at wavevectors far below the size of the Brillouin zone.

  • Electrons. An insulator carries no static current, so $\Pi^{\mathrm e}$ vanishes at $\omega=0$ (Section B.5), and for $\hbar\omega$ far below the gap its leading term is proportional to $\omega^2$: $\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi^{\mathrm e}_{\alpha\beta}=(\varepsilon_\infty-1)\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}$, with $\varepsilon_\infty$ the electronic dielectric constant.
  • Ions. The three optical modes of the bare phonons, which have short-range force constants only, share one frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ near $\bq=0$, and the acoustic modes carry no dipole there. In each optical mode the two sublattices move against each other (Section A.1). With Born charges $\pm Z^*$ on the two atoms, each optical mode has a current vertex $\bj_\nu$ with $|\bj_\nu|^2=e^2Z^{*2}/M_{\mathrm r}$, and the three modes add up to $\sum_\nu j_{\alpha\nu}j^*_{\beta\nu}=\frac{e^2Z^{*2}}{M_{\mathrm r}}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}$.

Inserted into the self-energies of Section B.5 and into , they give one dielectric function for every direction:1

Two channels and their modes

Now take a general isotropic medium, with any longitudinal and transverse parts $\Pi^{\mathrm L}$ and $\Pi^{\mathrm T}$. The Dyson equation splits by channel in three steps.13

  1. Write the self-energy and the free propagator in the isotropic form of : $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}=\Pi^{\mathrm L}\mathcal P^{\mathrm L}_{\alpha\beta}+\Pi^{\mathrm T}\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}$, and $D^0_{\alpha\beta}=D^{0\mathrm L}\mathcal P^{\mathrm L}_{\alpha\beta}+D^{0\mathrm T}\mathcal P^{\mathrm T}_{\alpha\beta}$ with the scalars $D^{0\mathrm L}=-4\pi c/\omega^2$ and $D^{0\mathrm T}=-4\pi c/[(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-c^2q^2]$.

Longitudinal modes are Coulomb's law dressed by matter. They do not depend on $c$: $\Pi$ carries a factor $1/c$ from the coupling $-\frac1c\bj\cdot\bA$, so $c\Pi$, and with it $\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}$, is independent of the speed of light.   • In a free-electron metal at small $q$ and nonzero $\omega$ only the diamagnetic term of Section B.5 survives, $\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\Pi^{\mathrm e}=-\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/\omega^2$, so $\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}=1-\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/\omega^2$ vanishes at the electronic plasma frequency $\omega_{\mathrm p}$: the plasmon, a longitudinal oscillation of the electron density. As $q\to0$ the lower branch is light in the static medium, $\omega_{\mathrm{LP}}\simeq cq/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}}$ with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}$ the static dielectric constant, and the upper branch starts at $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$; at $q=0$ there is no direction that could tell longitudinal from transverse. For large $q$ the lower branch becomes the TO phonon, $\omega_{\mathrm{LP}}\to\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$, and the upper branch becomes light in the electronic medium, $\omega_{\mathrm{UP}}\simeq cq/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$. Between $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ the dielectric function is negative and no transverse wave propagates: this is the Reststrahlen band of Sections A.5 and B.3, where the crystal reflects infrared light almost completely.

The polaritons therefore live at wavevectors of the order of $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}/c$, a region invisible on the scale of the Brillouin zone. Outside it the transverse branch is the TO phonon at the bare $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$, the longitudinal branch sits at $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$, and the electrostatic picture of Part A holds. The two limits $q\to0$ and $c\to\infty$ do not commute. With $c\to\infty$ first, the transverse mode stays at $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ down to $q=0$; with $q\to0$ first, the upper transverse branch ends at $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$. Derivation: the polariton branches, their limits and their phonon content

  1. Write as $\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_\infty\,\dfrac{\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega^2}{\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}-\omega^2}$.

    The zero of $\varepsilon$ is $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$, so $(\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p})^2=\varepsilon_\infty(\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}})$. At $\omega=0$ this gives the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller relation $\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}/\varepsilon_\infty=\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}/\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}$ .

    This is a quadratic equation in $\omega^2$, and its two roots are .

  3. Small $q$: the product of the roots is $c^2q^2\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}/\varepsilon_\infty$ and their sum tends to $\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}$, so $\omega_{\mathrm{UP}}\to\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ and $\omega^2_{\mathrm{LP}}\simeq c^2q^2\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}/(\varepsilon_\infty\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}})=c^2q^2/\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}$.

    The small root is the product divided by the large root; the last equality is the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller relation of step 1.

  4. Large $q$: the sum of the roots grows as $c^2q^2/\varepsilon_\infty$, while the product grows only as $c^2q^2\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}/\varepsilon_\infty$. So $\omega_{\mathrm{UP}}\simeq cq/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{LP}}\to\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$.

    Again the small root is the product divided by the large one.

  5. Phonon content. In a polariton wave of electric amplitude $E_0$, the time-averaged energy of the ion motion is $\frac{E_0^2}{16\pi}\,\frac{(\omega^{\ion}_{\mathrm p})^2(\omega^2+\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}})}{(\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}-\omega^2)^2}$, and that of the field together with the electrons is $\frac{E_0^2}{16\pi}\big(\varepsilon_\infty+\varepsilon(\omega)\big)$. The first divided by the sum of both is the phonon fraction shown in .

    The ions move as driven oscillators with an amplitude proportional to $E_0/(\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}-\omega^2)$, and their kinetic plus potential energy is proportional to $\omega^2+\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}$. The field contributes $\varepsilon_\infty E_0^2$, electric energy including the electronic polarization, plus the magnetic part $(c^2q^2/\omega^2)E_0^2=\varepsilon E_0^2$ on the branch. At $q\to0$ the upper branch is $(\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}}+\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}})/(2\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}})=92$% phonon in GaAs, and the lower branch is $(\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega^2_{\mathrm{TO}})/(2\omega^2_{\mathrm{LO}})=8$% phonon.

The modes are poles of $D$, so they appear as peaks in its imaginary part, the spectral function of the field. maps it in both channels for four media: vacuum, a free-electron metal, a model insulator and the polar crystal.

What the isotropic model leaves out

The response is taken as local. That is accurate here: the wavevectors that matter, a few times $\omega/c$, are tiny compared with the Fermi wavevector or the Brillouin zone; at larger $q$ plasmons disperse and $\Pi^{\mathrm L}\neq\Pi^{\mathrm T}$. The electrons of the polar crystal enter only through a constant $\varepsilon_\infty$. Anisotropic crystals mix the two channels off their principal axes. In uniaxial hBN the mixed block is proportional to the difference between the in-plane and out-of-plane components of $\Pi$, and it vanishes only along and perpendicular to the axis; inside the Reststrahlen bands those two components have opposite signs, and the mixing produces the hyperbolic phonon polaritons of hBN, which no isotropic model can show. Crystal local fields, the elements of $\Pi$ between different reciprocal lattice vectors, mix the channels too. A two-dimensional sheet mixes them by its geometry, and needs the free propagator evaluated in the plane of the sheet (Section B.10).

Check yourself: three short questions

  1. An induced current is purely transverse, $\bq\cdot\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}=0$. What charge does it carry, and which part of $\Pi_{\alpha\beta}$ enters $\Pi_{\rho\rho}$?

    Answer. None: $\rho^{\mathrm{ind}}=\bq\cdot\bj^{\mathrm{ind}}/\omega=0$ by . The connection formula contracts $\Pi$ with $\bq$ on both sides, so only the longitudinal part enters, $\Pi_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{cq^2}{\omega^2}\Pi^{\mathrm L}$. A transverse current is invisible to Coulomb's law and acts on other charges only through the photon line.

  2. In vacuum $\Pi=0$. Where are the poles of $D^{\mathrm L}$ and $D^{\mathrm T}$, and what does that say about longitudinal waves in empty space?

    Answer. $D^{\mathrm T}=-4\pi c/[(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-c^2q^2]$ has poles at $\omega=\pm cq$: light. $D^{\mathrm L}=-4\pi c/\omega^2$ has a pole only at $\omega=0$. No longitudinal wave propagates in vacuum, and the longitudinal field is the static Coulomb field of charges; a longitudinal mode needs a medium where $\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}$ can vanish.

  3. Double the speed of light in GaAs. What happens to $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$, and to the lower polariton at $q=10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹?

    Answer. $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ does not change, because $\varepsilon^{\mathrm L}$ contains $c$ only through $c\Pi$, which is independent of $c$. At $q=10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹ the lower polariton is mostly light, $\omega_{\mathrm{LP}}\simeq cq/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm s}}$, so it nearly doubles: by it goes from 5.5 to 10.9 meV. As $c\to\infty$ the whole polariton region shrinks to $q=0$, and only the flat TO and LO branches of Part A remain.
