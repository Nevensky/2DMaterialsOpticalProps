Conventions used on every page

One notation for electrons, phonons and photons, chosen for clarity. The two sources this primer follows use different symbols for several quantities; their versions appear only in footnotes and in the source notes of each section's glossary.

Each kind of label has its own letters, and they keep their meaning on every page.

How symbols are written

  • Vectors and Cartesian tensors are bold, their components italic with indices: the displacement $\bu_{\ell\kappa}$ has components $u_{\ell\kappa\alpha}$.
  • Labels that abbreviate a word are set upright as a whole: $k_{\mathrm B}$ (Boltzmann), $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ (longitudinal optical, Section A.4), and superscripts such as ion (ionic), ph (phonon) and e (electrons only) on quantities of later sections, for example $\Pi^{\ion}$. Running indices stay italic.
  • Euler's number, the imaginary unit and the differential are upright, $\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}$ and $\dd^3r$, so that the exponential is never confused with the elementary charge $e$ or with the phonon eigenvector components $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}$.
  • A tilde means mass-scaled: $\tPhi$ is the force-constant matrix divided by square roots of masses.
  • Hats mark operators only where an operator and an ordinary number of the same name appear together. A hat also marks the unit vector of a vector, $\hat\bq=\bq/q$; a vector that is a unit vector by definition, such as a bond direction $\bn$, needs none.
  • A comma separates index groups with different roles: in $e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}$ the pair $\kappa\alpha$ labels a component and $\nu$ the mode; in $\Phi_{\ell\kappa\alpha,\ell'\kappa'\beta}$ the two groups label the two atoms.

Units and numbers

Equations are written in Gaussian units with $e$, $\hbar$, the electron mass $m_{\mathrm e}$ and the speed of light $c$ kept explicit, so atomic units follow by setting $e=\hbar=m_{\mathrm e}=1$. The elementary charge $e$ is positive and the electron carries $-e$. Key equations with charges or fields have a footnote with their SI form. Numbers are given in eV or meV, ångström (Å), atomic mass units (amu) and kelvin.

Signs and Fourier transforms

Oscillations go as $\ee^{-\ii\omega t}$, and a function of time is transformed as $f(\omega)=\int\dd t\,\ee^{\ii\omega t}f(t)$. Waves on the lattice go as $\ee^{+\ii\bq\cdot\bR_\ell}$, where $\bR_\ell$ is the position of cell $\ell$. Section A.1 introduces the lattice conventions where they are first needed.

The two sources

Part A follows F. Giustino, “Electron-phonon interactions from first principles”, Rev. Mod. Phys. 89, 015003 (2017). Part B follows the manuscript “Theory of the Electron-Phonon-Photon coupling” (draft v2), called the EPP manuscript. Neither is needed to read this page.
