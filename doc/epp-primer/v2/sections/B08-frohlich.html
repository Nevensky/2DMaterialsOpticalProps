The electrodynamic Fröhlich vertex

In Part B no term of the Lagrangian couples an electron to a phonon directly: the two interact by exchanging a photon, which the current of the vibrating Born-charge dipoles emits and an electronic transition absorbs. This section opens the electron bubble inside the phonon self-energy of Section B.7 and reads off the electron–phonon vertex that this exchange produces. The vertex depends on frequency and comes in a bare and a screened version. For an infinite speed of light it becomes the polar vertex of Part A, screened at the phonon frequency; at the real speed of light it also couples transverse optical phonons, but only near the light cone.

What you need: Section B.7, which wrote the phonon self-energy as the exchange of a photon between two ionic currents; the free photon propagator, the electronic photon self-energy and the electronic and ionic current vertices of Section B.5; the relation between charge and current correlators of Section B.6; the polar vertex of Section A.4; the bare and screened vertices of the phonon self-energy in Section A.6; and the light cone of Section X.1. A vertex made of a photon

In Part A the electron–phonon matrix element $g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)$ is an input. It is the matrix element, between the Bloch states $\psi_{n\bk}$ and $\psi_{m\bk+\bq}$, of the change of the self-consistent potential when the atoms are displaced in the pattern of phonon $\bq\nu$ (Sections A.2 and A.3), computed for nuclei that move infinitely slowly. In polar crystals its long-range part $g^{\mathrm{lr}}$ dominates at small $q$. Section A.4 obtained it as the potential energy of an electron in the macroscopic electric field of the Born-charge dipoles of a lattice wave ().

The Lagrangian of Part B (Section B.2) contains no such term. Electrons couple to the vector potential $\bA$ through their current, and ions couple to it through the current of their Born-charge dipoles. Apart from the static potential of the ions at their equilibrium sites, nothing couples electrons to ions directly: even the Coulomb interaction is carried by the field, as its longitudinal part. An electron can therefore feel a phonon only by exchanging a photon with it. The electron–phonon vertex becomes a composite object, read from the phonon to the electron:

  1. an ionic current vertex, where the vibrating dipoles emit or absorb the photon;
  2. a photon propagator, which carries the photon, with a longitudinal part (the Coulomb field) and a transverse part (light);
  3. an electronic current vertex, where an electron makes a transition from $n\bk$ to $m\bk+\bq$.

Only the long-range polar channel arises this way. The short-range part $g^{\mathrm{sr}}$ of $g$, the deformation potential that every phonon has whether or not the atoms carry Born charges, has no counterpart in Part B. The potential of the ions is frozen at their equilibrium sites (Section B.2), so a displaced atom acts on the electrons only through the field of its Born-charge dipole.

Physical picture: a current talks to a current

A polar phonon is an oscillating dipole. An atom of Born charge $e\mathbf Z^*_\kappa$ displaced by $\bu$ carries the dipole $\mathbf p=e\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\bu$, and when $\bu$ oscillates as $\ee^{-\ii\omega t}$ the dipole carries the current $\dot{\mathbf p}=-\ii\omega\,\mathbf p$. A current radiates. The part of its field along the wavevector $\bq$ is the Coulomb field of the polarization charge of the wave; the part across $\bq$ is light. An electron absorbs this field by making a transition, and a transition carries a current too. Both ends bring a factor $\omega$, and the photon line contains the speed of light, so the vertex depends on $\omega$ and on $c$, unlike the static $g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)$ of Part A.

Opening the electron bubble

The dressed line $D^{\rme}$ is a chain: a bare line, an electron–hole bubble (one factor $\Pi^{\rme}$, drawn as the closed loop of an electron and the hole it leaves), a bare line, another bubble, and so on, summed to all orders. Every term beyond the first contains at least one bubble, that is, at least one electronic transition. The vertex that couples a phonon to one electronic transition appears when the chain is cut open at one bubble. draws the result: a single bubble between two photon lines, one dressed and one bare.

Reading a Feynman diagram skip if familiar

A Feynman diagram is a picture of a product. Each line is a propagator, each dot is a vertex, and the value of the diagram is the product of the factors it shows, with every internal index summed: here the Cartesian indices at the dots, and the bands and wavevectors of a closed electron loop.

In this section a zigzag line is a phonon propagator, a thin wavy line is the free photon line $D^0$ and a heavy wavy line is the dressed line $D^{\rme}$. A dot where a phonon line meets a photon line is an ionic vertex $J_{\alpha\nu}$; a dot where two electron lines meet a photon line is an electronic vertex. A closed loop of two electron lines, one for the electron put into $m\bk+\bq$ and one for the empty state it leaves in $n\bk$, is an electron–hole bubble.

Lines in a chain multiply as matrices, because the index at the end of one factor is summed with the index at the start of the next. A Dyson equation such as $D^{\rme}=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^{\rme}$ says that the dressed line is the sum of all chains of bare lines and bubbles. Inserting the equation into its own right side generates the chains one at a time, $D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+\dots$, a geometric series that sums to $(1-D^0\Pi^{\rme})^{-1}D^0$.

In formulas, the electron bubble is opened in four steps.

  1. Split the dressed line into its first term and the rest, $D^{\rme}=D^0+(D^{\rme}-D^0)$. The self-energy splits accordingly:

    $$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'}&=\Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu'}+\Delta\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'},\\ \Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu'}&=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\nu}\,D^0_{\alpha\beta}\,J_{\beta\nu'},\\ \Delta\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'}&=-\sum_{\alpha\beta}J^*_{\alpha\nu}\\ &\qquad\times\big(D^{\rme}-D^0\big)_{\alpha\beta}\,J_{\beta\nu'} .\end{aligned}\tag{B8.1}$$
    $\Pi^{\ph,0}$, the electron-free part, is the self-energy of a crystal without electrons, whose ions exchange photons through empty space; Section B.7 evaluated it at $\bq\to0$. $\Delta\Pi^{\ph}$, the electron-induced part, contains every diagram with at least one electronic bubble. Both are energies, at the same $(\bq,\omega)$.

  2. Iterate the Dyson equation of $D^{\rme}$:

    $$D^{\rme}-D^0=D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+\dots$$
    Inserting $D^{\rme}=D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^{\rme}$ into its own right side again and again adds one bubble at a time. All products are $3\times3$ matrix products at the same $(\bq,\omega)$.

  3. Take the last bubble and the bare line after it out of every term, on the right:

    $$\begin{gathered}D^{\rme}-D^0\\ =\big(D^0+D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0+\dots\big)\,\Pi^{\rme}D^0\\ =D^{\rme}\,\Pi^{\rme}\,D^0 .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.2}$$
    The bracket is the same series again, so it sums to $D^{\rme}$. Nothing is approximated: the chain of bubbles is read from its right end instead of its left end.

  4. Insert into $\Delta\Pi^{\ph}$ of :

    $$\begin{aligned}\Delta\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu'}&=-\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}J^*_{\alpha\nu}\,D^{\rme}_{\alpha\gamma}\\ &\qquad\times\Pi^{\rme}_{\gamma\delta}\,D^0_{\delta\beta}\,J_{\beta\nu'} .\end{aligned}\tag{B8.3}$$
    The Cartesian indices $\gamma,\delta$ are summed at the two ends of the bubble and $\alpha,\beta$ at the ionic vertices.

The electron-induced self-energy is one dressed photon line, one bare photon line, and between them a single electronic bubble $\Pi^{\rme}$. The bubble is irreducible in the sense of Sections B.5 and B.7: it cannot be cut into two pieces by cutting a single photon line, while the whole chain $D^{\rme}-D^0$ can. Which line carries the dressing is a matter of reading direction; that exactly one line carries it is not, as the counting below shows.3

One transition at a time

To find the vertex of a single transition, split each term of $\Pi^{\rme}$ into a factor for each end of the bubble and a factor for the bubble itself. Since $\OVSC=N_\ell\OV$, with $N_\ell$ the number of cells,

$$\begin{gathered}\Pi^{\rme}_{\gamma\delta}(\bq,\omega)=\frac{1}{N_\ell}\sum_{\bk nm}J_\gamma^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\\ \times\,\mathcal B_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\,\big[J_\delta^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\big]^* ,\end{gathered}\tag{B8.4}$$

with

$$\begin{gathered}J_\gamma^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{c\OV}}\\ \times\frac{\omega}{\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}}\,j_\gamma^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq},\\ \mathcal B_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)=\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}}{\omega}\\ \times\frac{2\,\big(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}\big)}{\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}-\hbar\omega-\ii\eta} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.5}$$
$J_\gamma^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)$
dynamic electronic current vertex of the transition between $n\bk$ and $m\bk+\bq$, built like the ionic $J_{\alpha\nu}$: the current vertex times $-\omega/\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$, with the normalization $(c\OV)^{-1/2}$, and the same units as $J_{\alpha\nu}$. The factor $1/\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is what later turns its component along $\bq$ into a charge. $\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$ is the vector of its components
$\mathcal B_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)$
pair factor of the bubble: the occupation difference over the energy denominator of the transition, times $\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/\omega$ and times 2 for the two spin directions (eV⁻¹)

Multiplying out confirms the split. In $J\,\mathcal B\,J^*/N_\ell$ the normalizations give $\frac{1}{c\OV N_\ell}=\frac{1}{c\OVSC}$ and the frequency factors give $\frac{\omega^2}{\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}^2}\cdot\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}}{\omega}=\frac{\omega}{\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}}$; with the occupation factor $2(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq})/(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}-\hbar\omega-\ii\eta)$ of $\mathcal B$ they make one term of $\Pi^{\rme}$. The split puts one factor $\omega$ into each vertex. The electronic vertex then has the same structure as the ionic one, and its projection on $\bq$ will turn out to be $-\omega$ times the charge of the transition, divided by $\sqrt{c\OV}$ ().4

Do not evaluate the two factors separately

The pair factor is not one term of the density response $\chi^0$ of Section A.3. Partial fractions split it into two terms:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathcal B_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=\frac{2\big(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}\big)}{\hbar\omega}\\ -\frac{2\big(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq}\big)}{\hbar\omega+E_{n\bk}-E_{m\bk+\bq}+\ii\eta} .\end{gathered}$$

The second term is minus one term of $\chi^0$. The first cancels only in the sum over transitions: in a crystal with time-reversal symmetry, the transition from $m,-\bk-\bq$ to $n,-\bk$ has the same $J$ and the opposite occupation difference. Meanwhile the vertex $J^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\propto1/\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$ diverges for transitions with $\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}\to0$, within one band as $q\to0$ or between degenerate bands, while $\mathcal B$ vanishes there, as $\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$ times an occupation difference that itself vanishes with $\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$. Only the product $J\,\mathcal B\,J^*\propto(f_{n\bk}-f_{m\bk+\bq})/\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$ stays finite. The two factors $\ii$ multiply to $-1$, the sign of . Read from right to left, tells a story: phonon $\nu'$ radiates a photon through its ionic current; an electron absorbs this bare photon in the transition $n\bk\to m\bk+\bq$; the electron and the hole recombine into a photon, which the polarizable electrons dress on its way to phonon $\nu$.

The direction follows the momentum $\bq$, which the conjugations track. The ionic vertex $J_{\beta\nu'}$ takes $\bq$ from phonon $\nu'$. The conjugated electronic vertex next to the bare line gives it to the electron, $n\bk\to m\bk+\bq$. The unconjugated electronic vertex takes it back, $m\bk+\bq\to n\bk$, because $J^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$ multiplies the operator $c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk+\bq}$, which moves an electron from $m\bk+\bq$ to $n\bk$ (step 1 of the continuity identity below). Finally $J^*_{\alpha\nu}$ hands $\bq$ to phonon $\nu$. The labels $mn$ are those of Part A's $g_{mn\nu}(\bk,\bq)$ for the transition $n\bk\to m\bk+\bq$, the band of $\bk+\bq$ first; the screened vertex, which describes the reverse transition, keeps them. Like $g_{mn\nu}$ of Part A, the vertices carry no factor $N_\ell^{-1/2}$; the $1/N_\ell$ in front of the sum supplies it for both.5

Three things are new compared with Part A. The coupling is derived instead of postulated: it is the exchange of one photon. It depends on frequency, through the factors $\omega$ of the two current vertices and through the photon lines. And it comes in two versions, bare and screened, of which the self-energy contains one each.

Why one line is dressed and one is bare

Two screened vertices, or two bare ones, would count the chains of bubbles wrongly. To see this, count how often each chain appears. Treat every factor schematically as a number and write $x=D^0\Pi^{\rme}$ for one bubble hung on a bare line, so that $D^{\rme}=D^0(1+x+x^2+\dots)=D^0/(1-x)$. The exact electron-induced line of is $D^{\rme}-D^0=D^0(x+x^2+x^3+\dots)$: each chain of $k$ bubbles appears once. Written with $x$, the three ways of placing one bubble between two lines are $D^0x/(1-x)$, $D^0x/(1-x)^2$ and $D^0x$. Expanded in powers of $x$, they read

$$\begin{aligned}D^{\rme}\Pi^{\rme}D^0&=D^0(x+x^2+x^3+\dots),\\ D^{\rme}\Pi^{\rme}D^{\rme}&=D^0(x+2x^2+3x^3+\dots),\\ D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^0&=D^0\,x .\end{aligned}\tag{B8.8}$$

One bare and one screened line count every chain exactly once. Two screened lines count a chain of $k$ bubbles $k$ times, once for each bubble that can play the central one: this is double counting, in the sense of Section B.1. Two bare lines around a single bubble keep only the first term: screening to first order, never resummed. Two bare lines are correct only around the whole chain of bubbles, which is before the regrouping.6

The miscounts have a size. For a longitudinal optical (LO) mode, whose ionic current points along $\bq$, only the longitudinal line acts, and the number $x$ becomes $-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\hat\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rme}\cdot\hat\bq$. The connection formula of Section B.6 () turns this into $\frac{4\pi}{q^2}\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}$, with $\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}$ the charge–charge correlator of the electrons. In the random-phase approximation $\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}=e^2\chi^0$ (Section B.6), so $x=v(\bq)\chi^0(\bq,\omega)$, with $v(\bq)$$\,=4\pi e^2/q^2$ the Coulomb interaction. Then $1-x=1-v\chi^0$ is the electronic dielectric function $\varepsilon^{\rme}$ of Section A.3, which in an insulator tends to $\varepsilon_\infty$ at long wavelength and low frequency. For an LO mode, the electron-free part plus the electron-induced part built with each pairing is $\Pi^{\ph,0}$ times a factor $\zeta$:

$$\begin{gathered}\text{one bare, one screened:}\quad \zeta=1+\frac{x}{1-x}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_\infty},\\ \text{two screened:}\quad \zeta=1+\frac{x}{(1-x)^2}=\frac{\varepsilon_\infty^2-\varepsilon_\infty+1}{\varepsilon_\infty^2},\\ \text{two bare:}\quad \zeta=1+x=2-\varepsilon_\infty .\end{gathered}$$

To turn $\zeta$ into a frequency, take a cubic crystal with two atoms per cell, Born charges $\pm Z^*$ and reduced mass $M_{\mathrm r}$, at $\bq\to0$ but outside the light cone of Section X.1 ($cq\gg\omega$), as in Part A. Its LO mode mixes with no other mode, so the diagonal element of the phonon Dyson equation is enough, $F_\nu=F^0_\nu/(1-F^0_\nu\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu})$. With the free phonon line $F^0_\nu$$\,=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{(\omega+\ii\eta)^2-\omega^2_{\bq\nu}}$ () the denominator vanishes where $\omega^2=\omega^2_{\bq\nu}+2\omega_{\bq\nu}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}/\hbar$.

Two inputs fix the numbers. First, the bare optical frequency $\omega_{\bq\nu}$ is the transverse optical frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$. In a cubic crystal the short-range forces give the three optical modes one frequency at $\bq\to0$, and photon exchange does not shift the transverse ones there: a transverse current does not couple to the longitudinal line, and the transverse line is negligible outside the light cone (Section B.7). Second, the LO mode has $|\hat\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu|^2=e^2Z^{*2}/M_{\mathrm r}$, the squared dipole of the optical mode per unit mass-weighted amplitude (Sections A.4 and B.7), so that $2\omega_{\bq\nu}\Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu}/\hbar=4\pi e^2Z^{*2}/(\OV M_{\mathrm r})$, the square of the unscreened ionic plasma frequency $\omega_{\mathrm p}^{\ion}$. Hence

$$\begin{gathered}\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2=\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^2+\big(\omega_{\mathrm p}^{\ion}\big)^2\,\zeta(\varepsilon_\infty),\\ \big(\omega_{\mathrm p}^{\ion}\big)^2=\frac{4\pi e^2Z^{*2}}{\OV M_{\mathrm r}} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.9}$$

With one bare and one screened vertex, $\zeta=1/\varepsilon_\infty$ and is the LO–TO splitting of Section A.4 (). For GaAs ($Z^*=2.07$, $\OV=45.2$ Å³, $M_{\mathrm r}=36.1$ amu, $\varepsilon_\infty=10.9$, $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}=33.3$ meV; literature values, illustrative) $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm p}^{\ion}=44.6$ meV, and the three pairings give $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}=35.9$ meV with one bare and one screened vertex, close to the measured 36.2 meV; 54.1 meV with two screened vertices; and an imaginary frequency with two bare vertices, a spurious lattice instability that sets in above $\varepsilon_\infty\approx2.6$.7 plots the three cases against $\varepsilon_\infty$ for three materials.

The same count in Part A

Section A.6 found the same structure for the nonadiabatic phonon self-energy of Part A (). One end of its electron bubble carries the bare vertex, the matrix element of the bare change of the electron–nucleus potential $\Delta_{\bq\nu}V^{\mathrm{en}}$; the other carries the screened vertex $g$, the matrix element of $\Delta_{\bq\nu}V^{\mathrm{KS}}$. The electrodynamic vertices are their long-range counterparts. The free line $D^0$ plays the role of the bare potential and the dressed line $D^{\rme}$ that of the screened one, and $D^{\rme}=(\mathbf S^{\rme})^{-1}D^0$ of below mirrors the screening of the potential change in density-functional perturbation theory, $\Delta V^{\mathrm{KS}}=(\varepsilon^{\rme})^{-1}\Delta V^{\mathrm{en}}$ ().

Two differences remain. Part B needs no static subtraction: its bare phonons hold only short-range forces, whereas the phonons of density-functional perturbation theory already contain the static self-energy, so that Part A adds only the difference $\Pi^{\ph}(\omega)-\Pi^{\ph}(0)$. And Part B keeps the frequency dependence of the screened vertex, which the review neglects on the way to its working formula; it describes this approximation as ubiquitous in the literature but never stated explicitly. The review also notes that most calculations replace the bare vertex by the screened one. For the frequency shift this is the “two screened” miscount of , applied to the nonadiabatic part.8

Which vertex is screened, and at which frequency

“One bare, one screened” holds for self-energies with an electron bubble between the two vertices, as here. The Fan–Migdal electron self-energy of Section A.7 has no bubble between its vertices, and both of them are screened. The pairing is exact; what can go wrong is the frequency. If the screened vertex is frozen at $\omega=0$ while the bubble is taken at the phonon frequency, the linewidth of a phonon in a metal comes out too large by a factor of about $\varepsilon^{\rme}(\bq,0)$ (Section A.6). The vertices of keep the screening at the frequency where they are used.

From currents to charges: the continuity identity

The vertices are written with currents. The polar vertex of Part A is written with charges: the charge density of the electron feels the potential of the polarization wave. Charge conservation connects the two. Averaged over a unit cell, the charge density that belongs to the transition $n\bk\to m\bk+\bq$ is a wave $\propto\ee^{\ii(\bq\cdot\br-\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}t)}$. Where the charge grows, current must flow in. Only the current along $\bq$ can bring it, because only that current crosses the crests of the wave; a current across $\bq$ flows along the crests and piles up no charge. So the longitudinal part of the current vertex is fixed by the charge of the transition, while the transverse part is free. shows the charge and the current of one transition.

In second quantization a one-body operator, whose single-particle version $o$ has the matrix elements $\langle\psi_{n\bk}|o|\psi_{m\bk'}\rangle$ between Bloch states, is written $\hat O=\sum\langle\psi_{n\bk}|o|\psi_{m\bk'}\rangle\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk'}$, summed over both pairs of labels. Here $c^\dagger_{n\bk}$, $c_{n\bk}$ create and annihilate an electron in $\psi_{n\bk}$. The number density at the point $\br$ is the case $o=\delta(\br-\hat\br)$, with $\hat\br$ the position operator, so $\hat n(\br)=\sum\psi^*_{n\bk}(\br)\,\psi_{m\bk'}(\br)\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk'}$, and the charge density of the electrons is $-e\,\hat n(\br)$.

In the Heisenberg picture an operator evolves as $\hat O(t)=\ee^{\ii\hat H t/\hbar}\,\hat O\,\ee^{-\ii\hat H t/\hbar}$. For a band Hamiltonian of independent electrons, $\hat H=\sum_{n\bk}E_{n\bk}c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{n\bk}$, this gives $c_{n\bk}(t)=\ee^{-\ii E_{n\bk}t/\hbar}c_{n\bk}$ and $c^\dagger_{n\bk}(t)=\ee^{\ii E_{n\bk}t/\hbar}c^\dagger_{n\bk}$. The pair $c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk'}$ therefore oscillates as $\ee^{-\ii(E_{m\bk'}-E_{n\bk})t/\hbar}$, at the frequency of the transition from $n\bk$ to $m\bk'$.

Define the charge vertex of the transition, the matrix element of the electronic charge density weighted by $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}$, next to the current vertex of Section B.5:

$$\begin{gathered}\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=-e\,\langle\psi_{n\bk}|\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}|\psi_{m\bk+\bq}\rangle,\\ j_\alpha^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=-\frac{e\hbar}{2\ii m_{\mathrm e}}\int\dd^3r\\ \times\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}\big[\psi^*_{n\bk}\,\partial_\alpha\psi_{m\bk+\bq}\\ -(\partial_\alpha\psi^*_{n\bk})\,\psi_{m\bk+\bq}\big] .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.10}$$
$\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$
charge vertex of the transition (a charge; $-e$ for $q\to0$ within one band); the matrix element, $\int\dd^3r\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}\psi^*_{n\bk}\psi_{m\bk+\bq}$, and the integral of $j_\alpha$ run over the supercell, where the Bloch states $\psi_{n\bk}$ are normalized
$j_\alpha^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$
current vertex: the paramagnetic current density of an electron of charge $-e$ and mass $m_{\mathrm e}$, with $\partial_\alpha=\partial/\partial r_\alpha$ (charge × velocity)

Both vertices carry the electron's charge. The identity that links them follows from the conservation of that charge, in five steps.

  1. Write the charge and current density operators of the electrons in the Bloch basis and transform them with $\int\dd^3r\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}$:

    $$\begin{gathered}\hat\rho^{\rme}(\bq)=\sum_{\bk nm}\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk+\bq},\\ \hat\bj^{\rme}(\bq)=\sum_{\bk nm}\mathbf j^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk+\bq} .\end{gathered}$$
    These are one-body operators (background box). The integral of $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}\psi^*_{n\bk}\psi_{m\bk'}$ vanishes unless $\bk'=\bk+\bq$, up to a reciprocal-lattice vector: crystal momentum is conserved, as for $g$ in Section A.2. $\hat\rho^{\rme}$, $\hat\bj^{\rme}$ are the electronic charge and current densities of Section B.2.

  2. Let the operators evolve with the band Hamiltonian of the electrons, $\hat H^{\rme}_0=\sum_{n\bk}E_{n\bk}\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{n\bk}$. Each pair operator then oscillates at its transition frequency:

    $$c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk+\bq}(t)=\ee^{-\ii\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}t}\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk+\bq} .$$
    The background box gives $c_{m\bk+\bq}(t)=\ee^{-\ii E_{m\bk+\bq}t/\hbar}c_{m\bk+\bq}$ and $c^\dagger_{n\bk}(t)=\ee^{\ii E_{n\bk}t/\hbar}c^\dagger_{n\bk}$. $\hat H^{\rme}_0$ is the electronic part of the free Hamiltonian $H_0$ of Section B.4. It is used here only to connect the two vertices, which are built from its eigenstates.

  3. Impose charge conservation, $\partial_t\hat\rho^{\rme}+\nabla\cdot\hat\bj^{\rme}=0$, in Fourier form:

    $$\partial_t\hat\rho^{\rme}(\bq)+\ii\bq\cdot\hat\bj^{\rme}(\bq)=0 .$$
    Integrating $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}\,\nabla\cdot\hat\bj^{\rme}$ by parts gives $\ii\bq\cdot\hat\bj^{\rme}(\bq)$; the surface terms cancel under the Born–von Kármán condition. The equation balances a charge against a charge current, because both densities carry the factor $-e$. For a band Hamiltonian with a local potential the paramagnetic current obeys it exactly.

  4. Insert steps 1 and 2:

    $$\begin{gathered}\sum_{\bk nm}\big(-\ii\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}\,\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}+\ii\bq\cdot\mathbf j^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big)\\ \times c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{m\bk+\bq}=0 .\end{gathered}$$
    The time derivative of each pair operator brings down $-\ii\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$.

  5. Set each coefficient to zero:

    $$\bq\cdot\mathbf j^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}\,\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq} .\tag{B8.11}$$
    The pair operators with different labels are linearly independent, so the sum vanishes only if every coefficient does. Written out, with $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}=E_{m\bk+\bq}-E_{n\bk}$, the identity reads $\hbar\sum_\alpha q_\alpha\,j_\alpha^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}=(E_{m\bk+\bq}-E_{n\bk})\,\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$.

Equation is the continuity identity, the continuity equation for a single transition. Free electrons check it. With plane waves $\psi_\bk=\ee^{\ii\bk\cdot\br}/\sqrt{\OVSC}$ of energy $E_\bk=\hbar^2k^2/2m_{\mathrm e}$ the vertices are $\rho^{\,\bk,\bk+\bq}=-e$ and $\mathbf j^{\,\bk,\bk+\bq}=-e\hbar(2\bk+\bq)/2m_{\mathrm e}$, and $E_{\bk+\bq}-E_\bk=\hbar^2(2\bk\cdot\bq+q^2)/2m_{\mathrm e}$; then $\hbar\,\bq\cdot\mathbf j^{\,\bk,\bk+\bq}$ and $(E_{\bk+\bq}-E_\bk)\,\rho^{\,\bk,\bk+\bq}$ both equal $-e\hbar^2(2\bk\cdot\bq+q^2)/2m_{\mathrm e}$. The factor $e$ that Part A's polar vertex needs on the electron's side sits in the charge vertex.9

For the dynamic vertex the identity gives

$$\begin{gathered}\bq\cdot\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}(\omega)\\ =-\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{c\OV}}\;\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.12}$$

The projection of the dynamic electronic vertex on $\bq$ is $-\omega$ times the charge of the transition, divided by $\sqrt{c\OV}$, and the singular factor $1/\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}$ has disappeared. The ionic vertex obeys the same rule. The polarization charge of a lattice wave with polarization density $\bP$ is $-\ii\bq\cdot\bP$ and its current is $\partial_t\bP=-\ii\omega\bP$, so the current along $\bq$ is again $\omega$ times a charge, and $\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu(\bq)$ is, up to the factor $\ii$, the polarization charge of mode $\nu$.

Familiar from optics codes

The identity is the one that makes the velocity and length forms of optical matrix elements agree in calculations of absorption spectra: at $\bq\to0$, $\langle v|\hat{\mathbf p}|c\rangle=\ii m_{\mathrm e}\omega_{vc}\langle v|\hat\br|c\rangle$ with $\hbar\omega_{vc}=E_v-E_c$ for a valence state $v$ and a conduction state $c$. The fine print is the same too. It holds for a band Hamiltonian with a local potential; nonlocal pseudopotentials and scissor-shifted energies add commutator terms.

The electrostatic limit: the polar vertex of Part A, screened at the phonon frequency

Let the speed of light go to infinity at fixed $\bq\neq0$ and $\omega$. Transverse photons then disappear and only the instantaneous Coulomb interaction remains, screened by the electrons. Section B.9 takes this limit in the whole self-energy; here it is taken in the two vertices. First the photon lines:

  1. The free line becomes longitudinal:

    $$D^0_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\;\to\;-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta .$$
    The transverse part tends to $4\pi(\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta)/(cq^2)$ and falls as $1/c$, while the longitudinal part grows as $c$. In a product $J^*DJ$, where each vertex carries $c^{-1/2}$, the longitudinal part gives a finite contribution and the transverse part none.

  2. Write the Dyson equation of $D^{\rme}$ as a dielectric problem. Moving $D^0\Pi^{\rme}D^{\rme}$ to the left and factoring out $D^{\rme}$ gives $\mathbf S^{\rme}D^{\rme}=D^0$, so that $D^{\rme}=(\mathbf S^{\rme})^{-1}D^0$, with

    $$\begin{gathered}S^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\\ =\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\sum_\gamma D^0_{\alpha\gamma}\,\Pi^{\rme}_{\gamma\beta} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.13}$$
    All factors are at the same $(\bq,\omega)$, and the relation holds at any $c$. The dressed line is the bare line divided by a screening tensor $\mathbf S^{\rme}$, as the screened potential change of Part A is the bare one divided by $\varepsilon^{\rme}$.

  3. With the longitudinal free line of step 1, $\hat\bq$ is an eigenvector of $\mathbf S^{\rme}$ with eigenvalue $\hat\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\hat\bq$, and

    $$\begin{gathered}\lim_{c\to\infty}D^{\rme}_{\alpha\beta}(\bq,\omega)\\ =-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\frac{q_\alpha q_\beta}{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.14}$$
    With $D^0\propto\hat\bq\hat\bq$, the product $D^0\Pi^{\rme}$ applied to $\hat\bq$ gives $\hat\bq$ times the number $-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\hat\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rme}\cdot\hat\bq$. Inverting $\mathbf S^{\rme}$ on $\hat\bq$ therefore divides by $\hat\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\hat\bq=q^{-2}\,\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq$.

  4. Identify the denominator. By the connection formula of Section B.6,

    $$\begin{aligned}\frac{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq}{q^2}&=1+\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\,\hat\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rme}\cdot\hat\bq\\ &=1-v(\bq)\,\chi^0(\bq,\omega) .\end{aligned}$$
    The connection formula gives $\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rme}\cdot\bq$, and $\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}=e^2\chi^0$ in the random-phase approximation. The right side is the random-phase dielectric function of Section A.3 (), without crystal local fields, at the frequency $\omega$.

So for an infinite speed of light the dressed line is the Coulomb interaction screened by the electrons, written as a photon line: equals $-\frac{c}{e^2\omega^2}\,q_\alpha q_\beta\,W^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)$ with $W^{\rme}$$\,=4\pi e^2/(\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq)$, the screened interaction of Section A.5. Section B.9 reaches the same result through the four sectors of the propagator ().10

Now the vertices. The steps are the same for both; the bare one first.

  1. Insert the longitudinal free line into :

    $$\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}=-\frac{4\pi\ii c}{\omega^2q^2}\,\big(\bq\cdot\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big)^*\,\big(\bq\cdot\bJ_\nu\big) .$$
    The dyad $q_\alpha q_\beta$ contracts with the two currents, so each enters only through its component along $\bq$: only the longitudinal parts of the currents talk to the Coulomb line.

  2. Use at the electronic end and the definition of $J_{\beta\nu}$ at the ionic end:

    $$\begin{aligned}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}&=\frac{4\pi\ii}{\OV q^2}\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}\Big)^{1/2}\\ &\quad\times\big[\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu(\bq)\big]\big(\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big)^* .\end{aligned}$$
    $(\bq\cdot\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq})^*=-\omega\,(\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq})^*/\sqrt{c\OV}$ for real $\omega$ and $\bq$, and $\bq\cdot\bJ_\nu=(\hbar/2c\OV\omega_{\bq\nu})^{1/2}\,\omega\,\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu$. The factor $c/\omega^2$ of the line cancels the $\omega^2/c$ of the two vertices: the frequency and the speed of light drop out.

  3. Write out $\mathbf j_\nu$ and $(\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq})^*=-e\langle\psi_{m\bk+\bq}|\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot\br}|\psi_{n\bk}\rangle$, and combine the phase $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}$ of each atom with the plane wave:

    $$\begin{gathered}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}=-\frac{4\pi\ii e^2}{\OV\,q^2}\\ \times\sum_\kappa\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2M_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu}}\Big)^{1/2}\\ \times\bq\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa\nu}(\bq)\\ \times\langle\psi_{m\bk+\bq}|\ee^{\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\btau_\kappa)}|\psi_{n\bk}\rangle .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.15}$$
    $\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu=e\sum_\kappa\bq\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa\nu}(\bq)\,\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}/\sqrt{M_\kappa}$, with $\be_{\kappa\nu}$ the eigenvector of atom $\kappa$ as a 3-vector. The factor $e^2$ is one $e$ from the Born charge and one from the charge vertex.

  4. Repeat with the dressed line in the screened vertex. The denominator $q^2$ becomes $\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq$, and the complex conjugation moves from the electronic end to the ionic end:

    $$\begin{gathered}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn}}_{mn\nu}\\ =-\frac{4\pi\ii e^2}{\OV\,\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\\ \times\sum_\kappa\Big(\frac{\hbar}{2M_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu}}\Big)^{1/2}\\ \times\bq\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\be^*_{\kappa\nu}(\bq)\\ \times\langle\psi_{n\bk}|\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot(\br-\btau_\kappa)}|\psi_{m\bk+\bq}\rangle .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.16}$$
    In the screened vertex conjugates the ionic vertex and not the electronic one, so the eigenvector appears as $\be^*_{\kappa\nu}$ with the phase $\ee^{+\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}$, and the plane-wave matrix element is the one of $\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$ itself.

Compare with the polar vertex of Section A.4, the Verdi–Giustino vertex: $\frac{4\pi\ii e^2}{\OV}\sum_\kappa(\hbar/2M_\kappa\omega_{\bq\nu})^{1/2}$ times a sum over reciprocal-lattice vectors $\bG$ of $(\bq+\bG)\cdot\mathbf Z^*_\kappa\cdot\be_{\kappa\nu}/[(\bq+\bG)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\cdot(\bq+\bG)]$ times $\langle\psi_{m\bk+\bq}|\ee^{\ii(\bq+\bG)\cdot(\br-\btau_\kappa)}|\psi_{n\bk}\rangle$. The two limits are related to it, and to each other, by

$$\begin{aligned}\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}&=-\,g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{mn\nu}\Big|_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\to1,\ \bG=0},\\ \lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn}}_{mn\nu}&=-\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)\cdot\bq}\\ &\quad\times\Big[\lim_{c\to\infty}g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}\Big]^* .\end{aligned}\tag{B8.17}$$

The bare vertex is the Verdi–Giustino vertex with the electrons removed ($\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty\to1$) and only its macroscopic $\bG=0$ term kept. The screened vertex is minus the complex conjugate of the bare one, divided by the screening; for real $\mathbf S^{\rme}$ it is the complex conjugate of the $\bG=0$ term of the Verdi–Giustino vertex, with $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$ replaced by $\mathbf S^{\rme}(\bq,\omega)$. The screening is the random-phase dielectric function at the frequency at which the vertex is used, so it is nonadiabatic; for an insulator well below its gap it reduces to $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_\infty$. The dielectric constant that the EPP manuscript put into its dipole potential by hand in its early chapters is here produced by the electronic bubbles.11

The overall minus sign in the first line of is a convention of the factorization : the factor $\ii$ and the order of the two vertices. It cannot be moved into the eigenvector, because both sides are linear in the same $e_{\kappa\beta,\nu}(\bq)$, but it is not observable: only the product in enters, and $|g|^2$ is unaffected. Where the vertex is added to a Hamiltonian matrix element, as in $g=g^{\mathrm{sr}}+g^{\mathrm{lr}}$ of Section A.4, use $g^{\mathrm{lr}}=-\lim g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}$. The second line follows from the definitions alone, whatever the sign convention for the charge, because both vertices carry $+\ii$ and the longitudinal line is real.12

In numbers, for the LO mode of GaAs at $q=0.1$ Å⁻¹, with $\mathbf S^{\rme}\to\varepsilon_\infty=10.9$, $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}=36.2$ meV in the zero-point amplitude (the derivation “which frequency sits in the zero-point amplitude” below explains why the physical LO frequency, not the bare one, belongs there), eigenvectors and phases taken at $\bq\to0$ and an intraband plane-wave matrix element of 1, the screened vertex and the Verdi–Giustino vertex both give $|g|=304$ meV. The bare vertex is $\varepsilon_\infty$ times larger, 3.31 eV. Fröhlich's formula () gives about 320 meV; the difference reflects the input data, since the Born charge that reproduces the measured LO–TO splitting is 2.18 rather than 2.07.13

Inserting into the vertex form gives the electron-induced self-energy of a mode in the electrostatic limit:

$$\begin{gathered}\Delta\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}\to-\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq}\\ \times\frac1{N_\ell}\sum_{\bk nm}\big|g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}\big|^2\,\mathcal B_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.18}$$

It is minus the bare vertex squared, times the bubble, times the screening factor. For an insulator it is negative: the electrons soften the bare dipolar stiffening $\Pi^{\ph,0}$, as they must. Added to $\Pi^{\ph,0}$ it reduces the total to $\Pi^{\ph,0}\,q^2/(\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq)$, the bare dipolar shift divided by the electronic dielectric function, which is the factor $\zeta=1/\varepsilon_\infty$ used in .

Derivation: the electrons divide the dipolar shift by $\varepsilon^{\rme}$

  1. Electron-free part. With the longitudinal line,

    $$\begin{aligned}\Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu}&=\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2q^2}\,|\bq\cdot\bJ_\nu|^2\\ &=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\OV\omega_{\bq\nu}q^2}\,|\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu|^2 .\end{aligned}$$
    The definition of $J_{\beta\nu}$ supplies $\hbar\omega^2/(2c\OV\omega_{\bq\nu})$, which cancels the $c/\omega^2$ of the line.

  2. Electron-induced part. The bare limit and the bubble give

    $$\begin{gathered}\big|g^{\mathrm{dyn},0}_{mn\nu}\big|^2=\frac{16\pi^2}{\OV^2q^4}\,\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}\\ \times|\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu|^2\,\big|\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big|^2,\\ \frac{1}{N_\ell}\sum_{\bk nm}\big|\rho^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\big|^2\,\mathcal B_{n\bk,m\bk+\bq}\\ =-\OV\,\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho} .\end{gathered}$$
    The second relation is the longitudinal part of rewritten with and the connection formula: $\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}=-\frac{c}{\omega^2}\bq\cdot\Pi^{\rme}\cdot\bq$.

  3. Combine with :

    $$\Delta\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}=\Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu}\,\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq}\,\frac{4\pi}{q^2}\,\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho} .$$
    Collect the factors of steps 1 and 2.

  4. Use $\frac{4\pi}{q^2}\Pi^{\rme}_{\rho\rho}=1-\frac{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq}{q^2}$ from step 4 of the photon-line derivation. Then

    $$\Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu}+\Delta\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}=\Pi^{\ph,0}_{\nu\nu}\,\frac{q^2}{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq} .$$
    The two pieces add to the electron-free shift divided by the longitudinal dielectric function. The pole condition then reads $\omega^2=\omega_{\bq\nu}^2+\frac{4\pi}{\OV}\,\frac{|\bq\cdot\mathbf j_\nu|^2}{\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq}$, the dipolar shift that Section B.9 identifies with the Gonze–Lee term ().
Derivation: which frequency sits in the zero-point amplitude

The electrodynamic vertices contain the bare frequency $\omega_{\bq\nu}$, which for a polar optical mode is the transverse frequency, whereas the Verdi–Giustino vertex contains the physical frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$. The phonon propagator reconciles them. If $\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}$ does not depend on frequency, as in an insulator well below its gap, and the mode does not mix with others, as the LO mode of a cubic crystal with two atoms per cell, the diagonal propagator

$$F_\nu=\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\omega^2-\omega_{\bq\nu}^2-2\omega_{\bq\nu}\Pi^{\ph}_{\nu\nu}/\hbar}$$

has its pole at $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$, and near it

$$\begin{aligned}F_\nu&\simeq\frac1\hbar\,\frac{2\omega_{\bq\nu}}{(\omega-\omega_{\mathrm{LO}})(\omega+\omega_{\mathrm{LO}})}\\ &\simeq\frac{\omega_{\bq\nu}}{\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}}\;\frac1\hbar\,\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}} .\end{aligned}$$

A free phonon of frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$ would give $\frac1\hbar\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}}$ near its pole. The dressed LO phonon is that free phonon with the weight $\omega_{\bq\nu}/\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$. Any process that exchanges it carries $|g|^2$ times this weight, and $|g|^2\propto1/\omega_{\bq\nu}$ through the zero-point amplitude, so the product is $\propto1/\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$: the electrodynamic vertex with its weight acts as the Verdi–Giustino vertex with $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}$.

What the electrostatic limit leaves out

  • No crystal local fields and no exchange and correlation: $\bq\cdot\mathbf S^{\rme}\cdot\bq/q^2$ is the $\bG=\bG'=0$ element of the random-phase dielectric matrix $\varepsilon^{\rme}_{\bG\bG'}$ of Section A.3, not the macroscopic dielectric constant $\varepsilon_\infty$ of density-functional perturbation theory, which contains both (Section B.9).
  • Only the $\bG=0$ term of the Verdi–Giustino sum appears, since Part B keeps only the macroscopic field from Section B.5 on.
  • The basis phase $\ee^{-\ii\bq\cdot\btau_\kappa}$, which the ionic vertex of Section B.5 carries, matters at finite $q$. In a central-force model of GaAs it changes the LO coupling $|g|^2$ by 0.5–0.8% at 0.1 Å⁻¹ and by 12–22% at 0.5 Å⁻¹, up or down depending on the direction and on the cell used for the uncorrected form, and it removes a spurious coupling to acoustic phonons that would depend on the choice of the cell. As $\bq\to0$ only the relative phases of the atoms enter and the LO vertex is unchanged.14

Screened at the phonon frequency, not at zero

The screening in depends on the frequency at which the vertex is used. This is retardation in the sense of Part A, the delay of the electrons' response, not the finite speed of light. It matters when the electrons can respond at phonon frequencies. In an undoped semiconductor the gap (1.4 eV in GaAs) lies far above $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}=36$ meV, so $\mathbf S^{\rme}(\bq,\omega_{\mathrm{LO}})\approx\varepsilon_\infty$ and static screening is accurate. With free carriers it is not.

Take GaAs doped with a density $n_{\mathrm c}$ of conduction electrons of effective mass $m^*=0.067\,m_{\mathrm e}$, and model their response by the Lindhard function of a free-electron gas: $\varepsilon^{\rme}(q,\omega)=\varepsilon_\infty-v(q)\chi^0(q,\omega)$, with $\chi^0$ the density response of the conduction electrons alone. Two wavevectors set the behaviour. The Thomas–Fermi wavevector $k_{\mathrm{TF}}$ of Sections A.2 and A.6, with $k_{\mathrm{TF}}^2=6\pi e^2n_{\mathrm c}/E_{\mathrm F}$ and $E_{\mathrm F}$ the Fermi energy of the carriers, sets their static screening: $\varepsilon^{\rme}(q,0)\approx\varepsilon_\infty+k_{\mathrm{TF}}^2/q^2$ at small $q$, so that $k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$ is the inverse screening length of static carriers in the crystal. The wavevector $\omega/v_{\mathrm F}$, with $v_{\mathrm F}=\hbar k_{\mathrm F}/m^*$ the Fermi velocity and $k_{\mathrm F}=(3\pi^2n_{\mathrm c})^{1/3}$ the Fermi wavevector, separates two regimes. For $q\gg\omega/v_{\mathrm F}$ the carriers cross a wavelength in less than a period and screen as if the field were static. For $q\ll\omega/v_{\mathrm F}$ they cannot follow, and $\varepsilon^{\rme}\to\varepsilon_\infty-\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/\omega^2$ with the plasma frequency of the carriers $\omega_{\mathrm p}$, $\omega_{\mathrm p}^2=4\pi n_{\mathrm c}e^2/m^*$.15

Static screening would therefore suppress the polar vertex at small $q$: $1/\varepsilon^{\rme}(q,0)\approx q^2/k_{\mathrm{TF}}^2$ turns its $1/q$ into $q$. At the phonon frequency the vertex keeps its $1/q$ form, screened by $\varepsilon_\infty-\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/\omega^2$. At $n_{\mathrm c}=10^{17}$ cm⁻³, $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}=0.022$ Å⁻¹ and $k_{\mathrm{TF}}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}=0.015$ Å⁻¹, so the two treatments differ over the whole range $q\lesssim0.02$ Å⁻¹. Near $n_{\mathrm c}\approx7\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³ the factor $\varepsilon_\infty-\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2$ crosses zero: the plasmon of the carriers, which the bound electrons screen down to $\omega_{\mathrm p}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_\infty}$, is resonant with the LO phonon, the coupled modes of Section B.9 (). compares the two screenings.

Across the light line: LO is Coulomb, TO needs photons

At the real speed of light both parts of the photon line act. A longitudinal ionic current, that of an LO mode in a cubic crystal, couples to the longitudinal line, which is the Coulomb interaction at every wavevector. A transverse ionic current, that of a transverse optical (TO) mode, couples only through the transverse line, that is, through light. Light in a medium with dielectric constant $\varepsilon$ travels along the light line $\omega=cq/\sqrt\varepsilon$ of the $(q,\omega)$ plane, and the region $q\lesssim\sqrt\varepsilon\,\omega/c$ inside it is the light cone (Section X.1). The transverse line is as strong as the Coulomb line only inside the light cone and near its edge.

To compare the two, contract the photon line with a unit vector along $\bq$ (an LO current) and with a unit vector $\mathbf t$ perpendicular to $\bq$ (a TO current), and divide by the Coulomb value $-4\pi c/\omega^2$. Call the results the LO and TO kernels $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{LO}}$ and $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{TO}}$.

  1. Free line:

    $$\begin{gathered}\hat\bq\cdot D^0\cdot\hat\bq=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2},\qquad \hat\bq\cdot D^0\cdot\mathbf t=0,\\ \mathbf t\cdot D^0\cdot\mathbf t=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2-c^2q^2} .\end{gathered}$$
    $\hat\bq$ sees only $D^{0\mathrm L}$ and $\mathbf t$ only $D^{0\mathrm T}$, since $(\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta)\hat q_\beta=0$ and $\hat q_\beta t_\beta=0$. So $\mathcal K^0_{\mathrm{LO}}=1$ and $\mathcal K^0_{\mathrm{TO}}=\omega^2/(\omega^2-c^2q^2)$.

  2. Model the electrons of an insulator by a constant dielectric constant $\varepsilon$: $\Pi^{\rme}_{\gamma\delta}=(\varepsilon-1)\,\omega^2\delta_{\gamma\delta}/4\pi c$.

    This makes the longitudinal element of equal to $1+(\varepsilon-1)=\varepsilon$, as for a medium whose polarization follows the field instantly.

  3. Solve the Dyson equation of $D^{\rme}$ separately along $\hat\bq$ and across it, where $D^0$ and $\Pi^{\rme}$ are numbers: $D^{\rme}=D^0/(1-D^0\Pi^{\rme})$ in each subspace. This gives

    $$\begin{gathered}\mathcal K_{\mathrm{LO}}=\frac1\varepsilon,\\ \mathcal K_{\mathrm{TO}}(q)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,\frac{1}{1-q^2/q_{\mathrm{light}}^2},\\ \frac{\mathcal K_{\mathrm{TO}}(q)}{\mathcal K_{\mathrm{LO}}}=\frac{q_{\mathrm{light}}^2}{q_{\mathrm{light}}^2-q^2},\\ q_{\mathrm{light}}=\frac{\sqrt\varepsilon\,\omega}{c} .\end{gathered}\tag{B8.19}$$
    Along $\hat\bq$, $1-D^0\Pi^{\rme}=1+(\varepsilon-1)=\varepsilon$. Across it, $1-D^0\Pi^{\rme}=1+\frac{(\varepsilon-1)\,\omega^2}{\omega^2-c^2q^2}$, which equals $\frac{\varepsilon\omega^2-c^2q^2}{\omega^2-c^2q^2}$; so $\mathbf t\cdot D^{\rme}\cdot\mathbf t=\frac{-4\pi c}{\varepsilon\omega^2-c^2q^2}$.

Here $q_{\mathrm{light}}$ is the wavevector of the light line at the frequency $\omega$ (Å⁻¹); for $\omega=\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}$ and $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_\infty$ it is the crossover wavevector of Section B.7. Equation separates the two cases cleanly.16

  • The LO kernel contains no $c$. The longitudinal photon is the instantaneous Coulomb field at every wavevector, inside the light cone too, so the $c\to\infty$ form of the LO vertex is exact. For an LO current, $D^0\cdot\hat\bq=-\frac{4\pi c}{\omega^2}\hat\bq$ picks out $\hat\bq\cdot\bJ^{\,n\bk,m\bk+\bq}$, which turns into the charge vertex at any $c$. The Fröhlich $1/q$ runs straight through the light cone.
  • The TO kernel lives near the light cone. It equals the LO kernel deep inside the cone ($q\ll q_{\mathrm{light}}$), where the photon line is isotropic; it is resonant at the light line $q=q_{\mathrm{light}}$, where losses keep it finite; and outside it falls as $-(q_{\mathrm{light}}/q)^2/\varepsilon$, which vanishes as $c\to\infty$. TO phonons therefore couple to electrons, through transverse electronic currents, only for $q\lesssim q_{\mathrm{light}}$. Unlike the LO vertex, the TO vertex has no universal form, because it needs the transverse current matrix elements of the bands.
  • How small the cone is. For GaAs at $\hbar\omega=\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}=33.3$ meV, $\omega/c=1.7\times10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹ and $q_{\mathrm{light}}=\sqrt{10.9}\,\omega/c=5.6\times10^{-5}$ Å⁻¹, about $5\times10^{-5}$ of the zone-boundary wavevector $2\pi/a=1.1$ Å⁻¹. The TO channel is invisible on the wavevector grids of transport calculations, whose finest spacings are of order $10^{-2}$ Å⁻¹ and rarely below $10^{-3}$ Å⁻¹, and it is central in optics, where it becomes the phonon polaritons of Section B.7.

When the LO–TO split is clean

The clean separation needs an ionic current purely along or purely across $\hat\bq$ and an isotropic electronic screening tensor; otherwise both kernels contribute to one mode. At finite $c$ the transverse element of the tensor is also not the optical dielectric function. For a constant $\varepsilon$ it equals $(c^2q^2-\varepsilon\omega^2)/(c^2q^2-\omega^2)$, which tends to 1 as $c\to\infty$. Only the longitudinal element enters the electrostatic limit.

In short, Part B derives the polar electron–phonon vertex instead of postulating it. Its electrostatic limit, with the charge restored in the continuity identity and the Born dipoles at the atoms, is the Verdi–Giustino vertex of Part A. The derivation adds three things: screening by the random-phase dielectric function at the phonon frequency, a bookkeeping of bare and screened vertices that cannot double count, and a photon-mediated coupling of TO phonons near the light cone.

Check yourself: three short questions

  1. With two screened photon lines around one bubble, how many times is the chain of three bubbles counted? With one bare and one screened line?

    Answer. Three times, because $x/(1-x)^2=\sum_kk\,x^k$: any of the three bubbles can be the central one. With one bare line only the bubble next to it can, so the chain is counted once, $x/(1-x)=\sum_kx^k$.

  2. Show that a longitudinal ionic current sees the Coulomb interaction at every $q$, with no trace of the speed of light.

    Answer. The transverse part of $D^0$ contains the projector $\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\hat q_\alpha\hat q_\beta$, which annihilates $\hat\bq$, so $\hat q_\alpha D^0_{\alpha\beta}\hat q_\beta=\hat q_\alpha D^{0\mathrm L}_{\alpha\beta}\hat q_\beta=-4\pi c/\omega^2$ exactly, at every $q$. The remaining $c$ cancels against the factors $c^{-1/2}$ of the two vertices, and $\omega^2$ against their factors $\omega$, leaving $4\pi/(\OV q^2)$ times the two charges: the Coulomb interaction.

  3. GaAs is doped with $n_{\mathrm c}=10^{18}$ cm⁻³ electrons ($m^*=0.067\,m_{\mathrm e}$, $\varepsilon_\infty=10.9$). Compare the screening of the LO vertex at $q=0.002$ Å⁻¹ at the phonon frequency and at zero frequency.

    Answer. Here $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm p}=143$ meV, $k_{\mathrm F}=0.031$ Å⁻¹ and $\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}/v_{\mathrm F}=0.010$ Å⁻¹, so $q$ lies in the dynamic regime. At the phonon frequency $\varepsilon^{\rme}\approx\varepsilon_\infty-\omega_{\mathrm p}^2/\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^2=10.9-15.7\approx-5$, a screening factor $1/|\varepsilon^{\rme}|\approx0.2$. Statically, $k_{\mathrm{TF}}^2/q^2\approx1250$ and $\varepsilon^{\rme}\approx1260$, a factor of about $8\times10^{-4}$. Static screening would underestimate the vertex about 250-fold.
